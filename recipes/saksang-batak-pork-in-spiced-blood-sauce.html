Saksang (Batak Spiced Pork Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

Saksang, pork stewed with andaliman, torch ginger and coconut milk, is a celebrated dish of the Batak people of North Sumatra, often served at festivals.

Method

  1. Grind the shallots, garlic, chilies, ginger, galangal, turmeric, andaliman, candlenuts and coriander into a paste. Andaliman, a citrusy, numbing pepper from North Sumatra, is what makes saksang taste Batak.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the pork for 8 to 10 minutes until it renders fat and browns.
  3. Add the paste, lemongrass, torch ginger and lime leaves and fry for 5 minutes until fragrant.
  4. Add the coconut milk, water and salt, cover and simmer over low heat for 45 to 60 minutes, until the pork is tender and the sauce thick and dark. (Traditionally fresh pork blood is stirred in at the end to thicken and darken the sauce; it is left out here.)
  5. Uncover and simmer for 10 minutes to reduce further, then serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on high heat splits the coconut milk and toughens the pork.
  • Sichuan pepper is a substitute; use less, as it numbs more.

Authentic tips

  • Traditionally thickened with pork blood; omit for a more accessible version.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Sichuan pepper replaces andaliman; use half the amount.
  • Chicken replaces pork for a halal version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
