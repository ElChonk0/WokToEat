Saksang (Batak Spiced Pork Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

Saksang, pork stewed with andaliman, torch ginger and coconut milk, is a celebrated dish of the Batak people of North Sumatra, often served at festivals.

  • Cook 90 min

Method

  1. Pound the chilies, garlic, shallot, ginger, lemongrass, turmeric and andaliman into a coarse paste.
  2. Fry the paste in a heavy pot for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the pork and cook 10 minutes until it changes color.
  4. Pour in the coconut milk plus 200 ml water, cover and simmer on low for 70 minutes.
  5. Uncover, season with salt and simmer 10 minutes until thick and the oil separates.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on high heat splits the coconut milk and toughens the pork.
  • Sichuan pepper is a substitute; use less, as it numbs more.

Authentic tips

  • Traditionally thickened with pork blood; omit for a more accessible version.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Sichuan pepper replaces andaliman; use half the amount.
  • Chicken replaces pork for a halal version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
