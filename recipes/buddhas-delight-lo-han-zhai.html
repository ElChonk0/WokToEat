Buddha's Delight 罗汉斋

▾

Surprise me with

Lo han zhai, a vegetarian braise of mushrooms, tofu, bean thread and vegetables, is a traditional Cantonese dish eaten by Buddhist monks and at Lunar New Year, when many families avoid meat on the first day.

Method

  1. Soak the shiitake, wood ear, bean curd sheet and bean thread noodles separately in warm water (40 to 50 C / 105 to 120 F) for about 10 minutes, until soft. Keep the shiitake liquid. Cut everything into bite-size pieces and snip the noodles shorter.
  2. Mash the red bean curd smooth and mix it with the mushroom sauce, soy sauces, wine, sugar, salt and pepper; lumps of bean curd would leave salty pockets. Mix the cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  3. Heat the frying oil to 160 C (320 F) and fry the bean curd sheet pieces for 8 to 10 seconds until they puff and float, then the tofu cubes for 2 to 3 minutes until golden. Drain and pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil.
  4. Blanch the wood ear and baby corn in boiling water for 20 to 30 seconds, then the carrot for 1 minute, and drain.
  5. Heat the wok with the reserved oil over medium heat and fry the garlic and ginger for 20 seconds. Add the shiitake, napa stems and king oyster mushroom for 30 to 40 seconds, then splash in the wine.
  6. Add the water and soaking liquid, the blanched vegetables, napa leaves, fried bean curd and tofu. Cover and cook for 4 to 5 minutes until the cabbage is tender.
  7. Stir in the sauce, nestle the noodles into the center and cover for 1.5 minutes. Add the peas, then stir in the slurry a little at a time until the sauce is glossy. Finish with the sesame oil.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the cabbage into mush.
  • Discarding the mushroom soaking liquid, which carries the flavor.

Authentic tips

  • Traditional recipes include up to 18 ingredients for the 18 luohan; use what you have.
  • Fermented bean curd added to the sauce gives a deeper, savory note.

Substitutions

  • Use any mix of seasonal vegetables.
  • Add lotus seeds or ginkgo nuts for a festive version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps three days in the fridge and tastes better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the cabbage softens.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable broth
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
