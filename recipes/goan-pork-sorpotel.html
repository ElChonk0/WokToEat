Goan Pork Sorpotel

▾

Surprise me with

A rich, tangy Goan specialty traditionally made with pork meat and offal, sorpotel is slow-cooked in a spiced vinegar sauce and is a centerpiece dish at Goan Catholic celebrations, especially Christmas.

Method

  1. Boil the pork and liver in salted water for 20 minutes, then drain, keeping 300 ml (1.25 cups) of the stock. Cool and dice the meat into very small cubes, about 1 cm (1/2 in).
  2. Grind the chilies, garlic, ginger, cumin, pepper, cinnamon, cloves and turmeric with half the vinegar into a fine paste.
  3. Render the fattiest pieces of pork in the oil in a heavy pot over medium heat for 5 minutes. Add the onions and fry for 10 minutes until golden, then the paste for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant.
  4. Add all the meat, the green chilies, remaining vinegar, tamarind, sugar and reserved stock. Simmer uncovered over low heat for 45 to 60 minutes, stirring often, until thick and dark.
  5. Cool and refrigerate. Sorpotel is famously best after 2 to 3 days, reheated daily so the vinegar and spices mature.
  6. Serve with sannas (steamed rice cakes) or pao. It is a Goan Christmas and wedding classic.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the marinating time, which doesn't allow the vinegar and spices to properly penetrate the meat.
  • Using too little vinegar, which is essential to this dish's characteristic tangy depth.

Authentic tips

  • Sorpotel is traditionally even better a day or two after cooking, once the flavors have had time to fully develop.
  • This dish is intensely flavored and rich — traditionally served in smaller portions alongside sannas (steamed rice cakes).

Substitutions

  • This simplified version omits traditional offal for accessibility; purists may wish to add pork liver.
  • No palm vinegar: cider vinegar works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months.
