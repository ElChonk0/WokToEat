Rawon (East Javanese Black Beef Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Rawon, beef soup colored black by keluak nuts, is a signature dish of East Java, served with rice, bean sprouts and salted egg.

Method

  1. Crack the keluak nuts and soak the flesh in hot water for 10 minutes; discard any that smell sour. Keluak gives rawon its black color and earthy, chocolate-like depth.
  2. Simmer the beef in the water for 1.5 hours, skimming, until nearly tender.
  3. Blend the keluak, shallots, garlic, ginger, turmeric, candlenuts and coriander into a smooth paste. Then heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat and fry the spice paste (bumbu) with the lemongrass, galangal and leaves, stirring often, for 8 to 10 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried bumbu tastes sharp and grassy; this step is the base of Indonesian cooking.
  4. Scrape the paste into the beef, season with salt and sugar and simmer for 30 to 45 minutes more, until the beef is tender and the soup is dark and aromatic.
  5. Serve in bowls with rice, topped with raw bean sprouts, half a salted egg, fried shallots, sambal and krupuk.

Common mistakes

  • Using whole keluak without removing the toxic raw fruit; buy prepared, processed keluak.
  • Boiling hard makes the beef tough.

Authentic tips

  • Keluak gives rawon its black color and nutty flavor.
  • Serve with crackers (krupuk) and sambal.

Substitutions

  • Black peppercorns plus ground dark mushrooms are a poor but possible stand-in for keluak.
  • Short rib replaces brisket.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, bean sprouts and sambal
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
