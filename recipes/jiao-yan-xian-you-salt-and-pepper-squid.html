Jiāo Yán Xiān Yóu (Salt-and-Pepper Squid) 椒盐鲜鱿

▾

Surprise me with

Salt-and-pepper squid is a Hong Kong dai pai dong and Cantonese banquet staple: squid lightly coated and fried, then tossed with chili, garlic and pepper salt.

  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbSquid, cleaned and scored
  • 60 g1/2 cupCornstarch
  • 10 g2 tspSalt and white pepper
  • 20 g2 tbspChilies, sliced
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic, minced
  • 30 g2 tbspScallion
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsNeutral oil for frying

Method

  1. Clean the squid and cut into 2 cm rings, or score the body in a crosshatch and cut into pieces; pat very dry with paper towels.
  2. Toss the squid in the cornstarch and shake off the excess.
  3. Heat the oil to 190 C (375 F) and fry in small batches for 60-90 seconds until pale golden and crisp; drain on a rack.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil and stir-fry the garlic, chilies and scallion for 20 seconds.
  5. Return the squid with the salt and white pepper and toss over high heat for 10 seconds; serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Frying longer than 90 seconds makes squid rubbery.
  • Crowding the pot drops the oil temperature and gives greasy, soft squid.

Authentic tips

  • Dry the squid thoroughly or it will spatter dangerously.
  • Toast the salt with a little white pepper in a dry pan first for a more fragrant seasoning.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or firm tofu take the same coating.
  • Plain black pepper and salt with no chili is milder for children.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten within minutes; leftovers keep 1 day but lose their crispness.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Cold lager
