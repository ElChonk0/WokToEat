Jiāo Yán Xiān Yóu (Salt-and-Pepper Squid) 椒盐鲜鱿

▾

Surprise me with

Salt-and-pepper squid is a Hong Kong dai pai dong and Cantonese banquet staple: squid lightly coated and fried, then tossed with chili, garlic and pepper salt.

Method

  1. Cut the squid tubes open and score the inside in a fine crosshatch without cutting through, then cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) triangles; cut the tentacles into short pieces. Scoring keeps squid tender and makes it curl attractively. Pat dry and mix with the marinade pepper, wine, egg yolk and starch.
  2. Whisk the flour, batter starch, baking powder, salt and water into a thick batter, about as thick as mashed potato, then stir in the batter oil. Coat the squid just before frying.
  3. Heat the frying oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 175 C (350 F). Fry the squid in batches for 2.5 to 3 minutes, until curled and golden. Drain on a rack.
  4. Raise the oil to 195 C (380 F) and fry it all again for 30 to 40 seconds; any longer and squid turns rubbery. Drain, keeping 1 tablespoon of the oil in the wok.
  5. Over high heat fry the garlic for 20 seconds, then the chilies, green onion and bell pepper for 15 seconds each. Add the squid and toss for 40 seconds, splash in a little wine, then sprinkle with the garlic salt and pepper and toss for 30 seconds more. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Frying longer than 90 seconds makes squid rubbery.
  • Crowding the pot drops the oil temperature and gives greasy, soft squid.

Authentic tips

  • Dry the squid thoroughly or it will spatter dangerously.
  • Toast the salt with a little white pepper in a dry pan first for a more fragrant seasoning.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or firm tofu take the same coating.
  • Plain black pepper and salt with no chili is milder for children.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten within minutes; leftovers keep 1 day but lose their crispness.

Freezing: Not recommended.
