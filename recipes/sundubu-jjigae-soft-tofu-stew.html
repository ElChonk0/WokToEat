Sundubu Jjigae (Soft Tofu Stew)

This soft tofu stew is a staple of Korean home cooking, traditionally served bubbling hot in its own earthenware pot (ttukbaegi) with a raw egg cracked in at the table to finish cooking in the residual heat.

Method

  1. Heat sesame oil in a pot and sauté garlic and onion until fragrant.
  2. Stir in gochugaru and gochujang and fry 30 seconds to bloom their color and flavor in the oil.
  3. Add the stock, zucchini, and mushrooms; bring to a simmer for 5 minutes.
  4. Gently spoon in the soft tofu in large, rough chunks — don't dice it neatly, since it's meant to break apart slightly into the broth.
  5. Simmer another 5 minutes.
  6. If serving with egg, crack one egg directly into each individual serving bowl and ladle the hot stew over top — the heat gently cooks the egg at the table.
  7. Garnish with green onion and serve immediately with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the tofu too much once it's in the pot — it's meant to stay in soft, irregular pieces, not be broken into a smooth paste.
  • Letting the stew sit too long after adding the egg, which overcooks it past the soft, just-set texture that makes this dish special.

Authentic tips

  • An earthenware pot keeps the stew bubbling at the table longer, which is part of the appeal; any heavy pot works too, and an induction stove needs an induction-rated ttukbaegi or a Dutch oven.
  • Traditional versions use anchovy stock, with clams or pork; kelp and dried shiitake give a vegetarian broth with similar depth.

Substitutions

  • Add small clams or shrimp for a seafood version, classic in many Korean households.
  • Ground pork browned with the garlic and onion at the start makes a heartier stew.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheats well, though the tofu continues to soften — best eaten within 2 days.

Freezing: Tofu doesn't freeze well in this format; not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, served in a stone bowl
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
