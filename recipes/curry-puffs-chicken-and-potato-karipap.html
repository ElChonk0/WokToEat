Curry Puffs (Chicken and Potato Karipap)

▾

Surprise me with

Curry puffs, or karipap, are fried or baked pastries filled with spiced potato and meat, sold across Malaysia and Singapore and descended from the Indian samosa.

Method

  1. Make the filling: fry the onion in the oil over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes, add the chicken for 2 minutes, then the potatoes, curry powder and chili powder for 5 minutes. Add the water, sugar and salt, cover and simmer for about 10 minutes until the potatoes are soft. Uncover to dry it out, stir in the flour until it clumps and cool completely; hot or wet filling makes the pastry tear.
  2. Make the water dough by rubbing the butter into the flour, salt and sugar and mixing in the cold water to a soft, smooth dough. Separately rub the butter and lard into the oil-dough flour until it forms a smooth, soft dough. If it crumbles, add a little more fat, not water. Rest both, covered, for 30 minutes.
  3. Flatten the water dough into a disc and wrap it around the oil dough, pinching to seal. Rest for 15 minutes.
  4. Roll it out into a rectangle about 35 x 25 cm (14 x 10 in), roll it up tightly like a jelly roll, turn it 90 degrees, roll it flat again and roll it up once more. Rest for 15 minutes. These layers are what make the flaky, spiral crust.
  5. Cut the log into 20 pieces. Roll each into a round about 13 cm (5 in) across, put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center, fold it into a half-moon and pinch the edge shut, then crimp it by folding the edge over itself in small pleats (the traditional rope edge).
  6. Heat the frying oil in a Dutch oven to 160 C (320 F) and fry the puffs in batches over medium heat for 5 to 7 minutes, turning, until golden and the layers flake. Keep the oil moderate so the pastry cooks through before browning. Drain on a rack. They can also be brushed with egg and baked at 190 C (375 F) for 25 to 30 minutes.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Filling that is still warm softens the pastry and makes the puffs burst; cool it fully.
  • Overheated oil browns the pastry before it cooks through; keep it near 170 C.

Authentic tips

  • Dice the filling small so the puffs close neatly.
  • Bake instead at 200 C (400 F) for 25 minutes, brushing with yolk.

Substitutions

  • Sardines replace chicken for the classic sardine puff.
  • Sweet potato replaces potato.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days at room temperature; recrisp in a 180 C oven for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months uncooked; fry from frozen for 7 minutes.

  • Side dish: Sambal belacan
