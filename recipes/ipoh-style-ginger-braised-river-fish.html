Ipoh-Style Ginger-Braised River Fish

▾

Surprise me with

Ipoh is known for river fish served in Cantonese-style dai pai dong, and a ginger and soy braise is a common way to cook the sweet, firm fish; this is a home version of that style, not a recipe from a specific stall.

Ingredients

  • 900 g2 lbRiver fish (tilapia or red tilapia), whole and cleaned
  • 20 g2 tbspCornstarch
  • 100 ml1/3 cupNeutral oil, for frying
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, cut in strips
  • 4 cloves4 clovesGarlic, sliced
  • 40 g1.5 ozShallots, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspFermented soybean paste (tau cheo)
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 200 ml3/4 cupWater
  • 40 g1.5 ozSpring onions, cut in 4 cm pieces
  • 15 g1 tbspRed chilies, sliced
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry inside and out, make two or three slashes on each side and dust it lightly with cornstarch.
  2. Heat the oil in a wide skillet or flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry the fish for 3 to 4 minutes per side, until the skin is golden and crisp. Lift it out and pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil.
  3. Fry the ginger strips over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes until golden and fragrant, then the garlic and shallots for 1 minute and the tau cheo for 30 seconds. Plenty of ginger is the signature here; it tames the muddiness of river fish.
  4. Splash in the wine, then add the soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and water and bring to a simmer.
  5. Return the fish, spoon the sauce over and braise for 5 to 6 minutes, turning once carefully, until it is cooked through and the sauce has thickened slightly. Add the spring onions, chilies and sesame oil for the last minute and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet fish splatters and sticks; dry it completely.
  • Turning the fish too early tears the skin; let it release naturally.

Authentic tips

  • Use a non-stick or well-seasoned pan for the fish.
  • River fish can taste muddy, so soak it in salted water for 10 minutes first.

Substitutions

  • Sea bass or snapper replaces river fish.
  • Fish fillets shorten the simmer to 3 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
