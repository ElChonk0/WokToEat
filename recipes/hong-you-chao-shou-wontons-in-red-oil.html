Hong You Chao Shou (Wontons in Red Oil) 红油抄手

Chao shou, Sichuan's word for wontons, are dressed in a sauce of chili oil, soy sauce, garlic and vinegar and are a staple of Chengdu street food and breakfast.

Method

  1. Mix the pork with the ginger, scallion and 1 tablespoon of the soy sauce, stirring in one direction until the filling is sticky; add 2 tablespoons of water gradually for a juicier filling.
  2. Place a heaping teaspoon of filling in each wrapper, brush the edges with water, fold into a triangle and pinch the corners together.
  3. Combine the remaining soy sauce, chili oil, vinegar, garlic, sugar and Sichuan pepper oil in a large bowl.
  4. Boil the wontons in plenty of water for 4-5 minutes, until they float and the wrappers are translucent.
  5. Lift them directly into the sauce, toss gently and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling makes the wontons burst; a heaping teaspoon is enough.
  • Letting wontons sit in the water after they are cooked makes the wrappers gluey.

Authentic tips

  • Add a splash of the cooking water to loosen the sauce.
  • Make the sauce first so the wontons are never waiting.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken thigh replaces the pork.
  • Minced shrimp makes a lighter filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; dress fresh and re-boil the wontons for 1 minute.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked wontons on a tray for 2 months; boil from frozen for 5 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
