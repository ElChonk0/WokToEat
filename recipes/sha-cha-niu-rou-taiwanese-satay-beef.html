Shā Chá Niú Ròu (Taiwanese Sha Cha Beef) 沙茶牛肉

Sha cha sauce, a savory paste of dried fish, shrimp and spices introduced by Chaoshan migrants, is a defining flavor of Taiwanese stir-fries and hot pot.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 500 g1.1 lbBeef sirloin, sliced
  • 45 g3 tbspSha cha sauce
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and scallion
  • 200 g7 ozWater spinach or onion
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspCornstarch
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the beef thin against the grain and toss with the soy sauce and cornstarch; leave 10 minutes.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat and sear the beef 1 minute; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, garlic, scallion and sha cha sauce and fry 30 seconds.
  4. Add the vegetables and stir-fry 2 minutes.
  5. Return the beef and toss 30 seconds.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough.
  • Burning the sha cha makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Sha cha contains dried fish and shrimp, so it is not vegetarian.
  • Lamb or pork also work.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or pork take the same sauce.
  • Sha cha contains dried fish and shrimp; check labels.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
