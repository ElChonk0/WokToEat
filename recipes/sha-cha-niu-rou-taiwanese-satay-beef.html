Shā Chá Niú Ròu (Taiwanese Sha Cha Beef) 沙茶牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Sha cha sauce, a savory paste of dried fish, shrimp and spices introduced by Chaoshan migrants, is a defining flavor of Taiwanese stir-fries and hot pot.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the marinade soy sauce, rice wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and sear the beef for 1 minute until it just loses its pink. Lift it out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the garlic and green onion whites for 20 seconds, then the water spinach stems for 30 seconds and the leaves until just wilted.
  4. Add the sha cha sauce, soy sauce, sugar and water and toss for 20 seconds. Sha cha is a savory paste of dried fish, shrimp, garlic and spices brought by Chaozhou migrants.
  5. Return the beef with the green onion greens and toss for 30 seconds until glossy. Serve at once with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough.
  • Burning the sha cha makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Sha cha contains dried fish and shrimp, so it is not vegetarian.
  • Lamb or pork also work.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or pork take the same sauce.
  • Sha cha contains dried fish and shrimp; check labels.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
