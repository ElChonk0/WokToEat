Soto Daging (Indonesian Beef Soup)

A fragrant, turmeric-tinted beef soup found across Indonesia in countless regional variations, soto daging combines a light spiced broth with beef, vegetables, and often rice or noodles for a complete meal.

Method

  1. Blend shallot, garlic, and turmeric into a paste.
  2. Fry the paste with ginger and lemongrass in oil until fragrant.
  3. Add beef and stir to coat, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  4. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer, cover, and cook 1 to 1.25 hours until the beef is tender.
  5. Cook rice vermicelli separately; divide among bowls with beef and broth, topped with bean sprouts, boiled egg, and fried shallot.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the simmer, which doesn't give the beef enough time to become tender.
  • Skipping the fried shallot garnish, which adds essential crunch and aroma central to soto's presentation.

Authentic tips

  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half.
  • Every region in Indonesia has its own soto variation — feel free to adjust spices and garnishes to taste.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can replace beef for soto ayam, with a shorter cooking time.
  • Rice can substitute for vermicelli noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth and beef keep 4 days refrigerated; store noodles separately.

Freezing: The broth and beef freeze excellently for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or lontong, sambal and lime
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
