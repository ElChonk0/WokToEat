Babi Guling (Balinese Spit-Roast Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

Babi guling, pork stuffed with a base genep spice paste and spit-roasted until crisp, is Bali's signature festive dish for Hindu ceremonies.

Method

  1. Pound or blend the shallots, garlic, turmeric, ginger, galangal, kencur, chilies, candlenuts, coriander, pepper and shrimp paste into base genep, the Balinese spice paste. Fry it in the coconut oil for 8 minutes until fragrant, then mix in the lemongrass, lime leaves and half the salt. Cool.
  2. Score the meat side of the belly. Rub the skin with the remaining salt and leave the pork uncovered in the refrigerator overnight to dry the skin.
  3. Spread the spice paste and the greens over the meat side, roll the belly up with the skin outside and tie it tightly with string every 3 cm (1.25 in).
  4. Roast on a rack at 160 C (325 F) for 2 hours, brushing the skin with turmeric water every 30 minutes, until the meat is very tender. The turmeric gives the golden skin.
  5. Raise the oven to 230 C (450 F) and roast for 20 to 30 minutes more, until the skin puffs into crackling. Watch it; it goes from golden to burnt quickly.
  6. Rest for 15 minutes, then slice and serve the meat, crackling and stuffing with rice and sambal matah.

Common mistakes

  • Wet skin does not crisp.
  • Too little salt on the skin gives chewy crackling.

Authentic tips

  • A real suckling pig is whole spit-roasted; belly gives the same flavors at home.
  • Serve with lawar (a coconut-vegetable salad).

Substitutions

  • Pork belly plus the spice paste is a home-friendly alternative.
  • Cassava leaves or spinach work for the stuffing.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; crisp the skin in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, lawar and sambal matah
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh or arak
