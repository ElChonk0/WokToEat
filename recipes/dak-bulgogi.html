Dak Bulgogi 닭불고기

A chicken version of Korea's iconic marinated grilled beef bulgogi, using pear and soy for a sweet, tenderizing marinade before a quick sear — a common, budget-friendly weeknight variation on the classic.

  • Prep 15 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 10 min

Method

  1. Combine soy sauce, grated pear, garlic, sesame oil, sugar, and gochujang for the marinade.
  2. Coat chicken thoroughly and marinate at least 1 hour, or overnight for deeper flavor.
  3. Heat a pan or grill over high heat and cook chicken and onion together, stirring occasionally, 8–10 minutes until well charred and cooked through.
  4. Garnish with green onion and sesame seeds before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Over-marinating past 24 hours, since the pear's enzymes can start to break down the meat's texture too much.
  • Cooking over too gentle a heat, which misses the char that gives bulgogi its signature smoky edge.

Authentic tips

  • Grated Asian pear is both a sweetener and natural tenderizer — a genuinely distinctive part of the marinade, not just a flavor note.
  • High, quick heat is essential; this dish should sear and char, not stew in its own liquid.

Substitutions

  • No Asian pear: a regular pear or a small amount of grated apple works reasonably well.
  • Serve in lettuce wraps with ssamjang for a traditional Korean BBQ-style presentation.
  • No grill: spread the meat in a single layer on a foil-lined sheet pan and broil on the top rack for 5 to 6 minutes, flip, and cook 3 to 4 minutes more until charred at the edges; work in two batches so it browns instead of steaming. Broil the onion on the same pan.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; great in a rice bowl the next day.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with lettuce leaves, ssamjang and kimchi
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Barley tea
