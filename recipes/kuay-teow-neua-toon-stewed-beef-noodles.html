Kuay Teow Neua Toon (Thai Stewed Beef Noodle Soup) ก๋วยเตี๋ยวเนื้อตุ๋น

▾

Stewed beef noodles, brisket simmered with Chinese spices and served in a clear broth, are a Thai-Chinese noodle shop staple of Bangkok.

Method

  1. Blanch the brisket and bones in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse to keep the broth clear.
  2. Lightly toast the cinnamon and star anise in a dry Dutch oven, then add the meat, bones, water and pounded cilantro root paste. Bring to a boil, skim, and simmer gently, partly covered, for 2 to 2.5 hours, until the brisket is tender.
  3. Add the daikon, soy sauces, fish sauce and sugar and simmer for 30 minutes more, until the daikon is soft. Discard the bones and taste; the broth should be savory and lightly sweet.
  4. Blanch the noodles, bean sprouts and Chinese broccoli for 10 to 30 seconds in boiling water and divide among bowls.
  5. Ladle over the beef, daikon and hot broth and finish with fried garlic and its oil, green onion and cilantro. Season at the table with chili vinegar, chili flakes and sugar, the Thai way.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanch clouds the broth.
  • Boiling hard toughens the brisket.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with a dip of chili, vinegar and fish sauce.
  • Make ahead for a deeper flavor.

Substitutions

  • Short rib or chuck replace brisket.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the simmer to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 5 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth and beef for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili vinegar and fish sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
