White Cut Chicken–Style Thigh with Ginger-Scallion Oil 白切鸡

▾

Surprise me with

White cut chicken is prized in Cantonese cooking for showcasing the pure flavor of well-raised chicken through gentle poaching alone; the ginger-scallion oil dip does the rest of the flavor work.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, bone-in skin-on
  • 5 slices5 slicesGinger, sliced, for poaching
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, for poaching
  • 5 g1 tspSalt, for the poaching water
  • 40 g1/4 cupGinger, finely minced, for the oil
  • 4 stalks4 stalksGreen onion, white and light green parts, finely minced, for the oil
  • 6 g1 tspSalt, for the oil
  • 75 ml5 tbspNeutral oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspSesame oil

Method

  1. Take the thighs out of the refrigerator 30 minutes ahead. Bring a pot of water large enough to cover them generously to a boil with the poaching ginger, green onion and salt.
  2. Lower in the thighs, let the water return to a boil, then turn the heat down until only a few bubbles break the surface. Cover and poach for 15 minutes. A gentle heat keeps the meat silky; a hard boil tightens it and tears the skin.
  3. Turn off the heat and leave the thighs covered in the hot water for 10 minutes more. They are done when a chopstick pushed into the thickest part by the bone gives clear juices, not pink, or a thermometer reads 74 C (165 F).
  4. Meanwhile, put the minced ginger and green onion in separate small piles. Heat the oil in a small pan until it shimmers, about 180 C (350 F). Add the ginger and sizzle for 15 seconds, then the green onion for 20 to 30 seconds, just until fragrant and still bright green. Pour into a heatproof bowl and stir in the salt and sesame oil. Taste: it should be salty, as a little goes a long way.
  5. Lift the thighs straight into a large bowl of ice water and leave them for 5 minutes, spooning the water over them. The ice bath tightens the skin into its classic springy, gelatinous layer and stops the cooking.
  6. Pat the thighs dry, chop through the bone into strips and serve at room temperature with the ginger-scallion oil for dipping.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the chicken hard throughout, which produces tough, stringy meat instead of the intended silky texture.
  • Pouring the hot oil over the aromatics too soon, before it's hot enough to properly bloom their flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The ice bath step is what gives white cut chicken its characteristic tender, slightly bouncy skin texture.
  • Reserve the poaching liquid — it makes an excellent light broth base for a future soup.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs work but skip the ice bath skin benefit somewhat; still delicious.
  • No fresh ginger for the oil: ginger paste from a jar works in a pinch, though flavor is milder.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; serve cold, sliced, over rice.

Freezing: The cooked chicken freezes acceptably for a month; make the ginger-scallion oil fresh when serving.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain steamed rice or oil rice
  • Soup: Light pork and watercress soup
  • Dessert: Chilled grass jelly
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
