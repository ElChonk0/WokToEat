Beef Mechado

▾

Surprise me with

A Spanish-influenced Filipino braise similar to kaldereta but typically milder and without liver spread, mechado studs beef with pork fat (traditionally) and braises it in a tomato-soy sauce until tender.

Method

  1. If larding, push strips of pork fat through the beef chunks with a knife; mechado means 'larded', from the Spanish mecha (wick). Marinate the beef in the soy sauce and calamansi for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Lift out the beef, pat it dry and brown it in the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 4 to 5 minutes. Lift it out.
  3. Saute the garlic until golden and the onion until soft.
  4. Return the beef with the marinade, tomato sauce, stock, bay leaves and sugar. Cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, until tender.
  5. Add the potatoes and carrot and simmer for 15 to 20 minutes, uncovered, until the vegetables are tender and the sauce has thickened. Season with pepper and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial beef braise, since chuck needs real time to become properly tender before vegetables are added.
  • Overcooking the potatoes and carrot, which can make them fall apart by serving time.

Authentic tips

  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.
  • Traditional recipes lard the beef with strips of pork fat before browning — optional, but adds richness.

Substitutions

  • Chuck replaces the traditional lardon-studded cut.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Dessert: Leche flan
