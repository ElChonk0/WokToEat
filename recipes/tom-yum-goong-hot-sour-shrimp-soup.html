Tom Yum Goong (Hot and Sour Shrimp Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Tom yum goong, a hot and sour soup of shrimp, lemongrass, galangal and kaffir lime, is one of Thailand's most famous dishes; the clear and creamy versions both appear in Thai kitchens.

Method

  1. Peel and devein the shrimp, keeping the heads and shells. Fry the shells and heads in a dry pot for 2 minutes, then add the water and simmer for 10 minutes. Strain; this shrimp stock gives the soup its depth.
  2. Bring the stock to a boil with the lemongrass, galangal and lime leaves and simmer for 3 to 5 minutes until fragrant. The aromatics are for flavor, not for eating.
  3. Add the mushrooms and chilies and simmer for 2 minutes, then stir in the nam prik pao.
  4. Add the shrimp and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, just until pink, then turn off the heat.
  5. Season off the heat with fish sauce and lime juice; boiling lime juice turns bitter. Stir in the evaporated milk for the creamy version. Taste: sour, salty and hot in balance. Serve topped with cilantro.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling after adding the lime juice makes it bitter.
  • Overcooking the shrimp makes them rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • Add evaporated milk for the creamy nam khon version.
  • Do not eat the lemongrass, galangal or leaves.

Substitutions

  • Add evaporated milk for the creamy nam khon version.
  • Chicken replaces shrimp.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.
