Deulgireum Makguksu (Perilla Oil Buckwheat Noodles) 들기름 막국수

▾

Surprise me with

Cold buckwheat noodles dressed simply with nutty perilla oil, soy and seaweed are a Gangwon Province specialty that became a nationwide trend in the 2020s.

  • 400 g14 ozDried buckwheat noodles (memil guksu)
  • 60 ml1/4 cupPerilla oil
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 8 g2 tspSugar
  • 4 sheets4 sheetsRoasted seaweed (gim), crumbled
  • 20 g3 tbspToasted perilla or sesame seeds, ground
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, finely sliced

Method

  1. Toast the perilla seeds in a dry pan over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant, then grind them coarsely. Toast the seaweed sheets briefly over a burner or in the pan until crisp, and crumble them.
  2. Mix the soy sauce and sugar until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Boil the buckwheat noodles according to the packet, usually 4 to 5 minutes, stirring so they do not stick. Drain and rinse them under cold running water, rubbing them between your hands to wash off the starch, until cold. Drain well.
  4. Toss the noodles with the soy mixture and most of the perilla oil until every strand is glossy. Perilla oil's grassy, nutty flavor is the point of the dish, so use the real thing if you can.
  5. Pile the noodles in bowls and top generously with the crumbled seaweed, ground perilla seeds, green onion and a final drizzle of perilla oil. Mix at the table and eat right away.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Undrained noodles dilute the dressing.
  • Sesame oil is a different flavor; perilla oil is the point.

Authentic tips

  • Grind the seeds just before serving for the best aroma.
  • Add a little cold kelp broth for a slightly soupy version.

Substitutions

  • Soba noodles replace memil guksu.
  • Toasted sesame oil if perilla oil is unavailable.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.
