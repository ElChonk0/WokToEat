Nasi Kandar Ayam (Penang Chicken with Mixed Curries)

▾

Surprise me with

Nasi kandar, rice with an array of curries, is a Penang institution of Indian-Muslim origin; fried chicken ladled with mixed curry sauces is a favorite.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 45 min

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with the chili powder, turmeric, curry powder and salt and marinate for at least 1 hour. Toss with the rice flour just before frying.
  2. Heat the oil to 165 C (330 F) and fry the chicken in batches for 12 to 15 minutes, until deep red-brown, crisp and cooked through. Drain on a rack and keep 3 tablespoons of the oil.
  3. For the kuah (gravy), blend the shallots, garlic and ginger. Fry the whole spices and curry leaves in the reserved oil for 30 seconds, add the paste for 3 minutes, then the gravy curry powder mixed with a little water and fry for 5 to 8 minutes until dark and the oil separates.
  4. Add the tomato, water and tamarind and simmer for 15 minutes, then add the okra and simmer 5 minutes. Stir in the coconut milk, season with salt and sugar and keep it at a gentle bubble. The gravy should be loose and generous.
  5. Pile rice on each plate, add the fried chicken, okra and pickle, and flood it with the mixed curry gravy (banjir, or 'flooded', as Penang stalls say). Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Thin sauce makes soggy rice.
  • Overcooked vegetables go limp.

Authentic tips

  • Mixing several curries is the point; try fish, chicken and vegetable curries.
  • Banjir (flood) the plate with sauce, as in Penang.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought curry sauce saves time.
  • Several vegetable curries can join the plate.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat the chicken in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes the curry sauce for 2 months.

Serve with
