Ebi Furai (Breaded Fried Shrimp) エビフライ

▾

Surprise me with

Ebi furai is a yoshoku (Western-influenced Japanese) classic of panko-breaded shrimp, popular in school lunches and diners.

Method

  1. Devein the shrimp, trim the tip of each tail and scrape out the water inside so it does not spatter. Make 4 or 5 shallow cuts across the belly and press the shrimp flat with your fingers until you feel the sinews snap. This keeps them straight in the oil.
  2. Season with salt, pepper and sake and leave for 5 minutes, then pat dry.
  3. Coat each shrimp in flour, dip in the egg beaten with the water, and press into the panko, leaving the tail bare.
  4. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) in a Dutch oven and fry 4 or 5 at a time for 2 to 3 minutes, until golden and the shrimp are just opaque.
  5. Drain upright on a rack and serve hot with tartar sauce, shredded cabbage and lemon.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is too cool makes the breading greasy.
  • Crowding the pan causes soggy breading.

Authentic tips

  • Trim the tail tips and squeeze out the water so they don't spatter.
  • Tartar sauce is a popular alternative.

Substitutions

  • Squid or fish fillets take the same coating.
  • An air fryer at 200 C (400 F) for 8 minutes replaces frying.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freeze breaded raw shrimp for 1 month; fry from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and shredded cabbage
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
