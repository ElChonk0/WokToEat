Roti Canai with Dhal (Flaky Flatbread with Lentil Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Roti canai, a flaky flatbread of Indian-Muslim origin, is a Malaysian breakfast icon, served with dhal or curry at mamak stalls across the country.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, 20 g ghee, dough water and half the salt into a soft dough, knead 8 minutes, divide into 8 balls and coat in oil; rest at least 2 hours.
  2. Simmer the lentils with the turmeric in the dhal water for 20 minutes until soft and creamy.
  3. Fry the mustard seeds and curry leaves in the oil until they pop, add the onion, garlic and ginger for 5 minutes, then the tomato and curry powder for 5 minutes; stir into the lentils with the remaining salt and simmer 5 minutes.
  4. Stretch each dough ball very thin, fold into a square and cook on a hot griddle with ghee for 2 minutes per side until golden.
  5. Clap the roti to fluff it and serve with the dhal.

Common mistakes

  • Dough that isn't rested tears.
  • Insufficient oil makes it tough instead of flaky.

Authentic tips

  • Stretch rather than roll the dough; that is what makes the layers.
  • The dhal on its own, with rice and a vegetable, is a standard mamak and rice-shop dish.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought frozen roti canai saves time.
  • Chickpea curry also works.

Storage

Leftovers: The dhal keeps 5 days refrigerated; roti is best fresh but reheats on a dry griddle.

Freezing: The dhal freezes for 3 months; cooked roti freeze for 1 month.
