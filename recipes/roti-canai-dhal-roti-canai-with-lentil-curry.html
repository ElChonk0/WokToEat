Roti Canai with Dhal (Flaky Flatbread with Lentil Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Roti canai, a flaky flatbread of Indian-Muslim origin, is a Malaysian breakfast icon, served with dhal or curry at mamak stalls across the country.

Method

  1. Knead the flour, water, condensed milk, salt and 1 tablespoon of ghee for 8 to 10 minutes into a very soft, smooth, elastic dough. Divide into 8 balls, coat them generously with ghee and rest them, covered, for at least 4 hours or overnight. Long resting is what lets the dough stretch paper-thin.
  2. Simmer the lentils with the water and turmeric for 20 minutes until soft and broken down. Add the potato and curry powder and simmer 10 minutes more.
  3. Heat the dhal oil and fry the mustard and cumin seeds until they pop, then the curry leaves, onion, garlic and ginger for 4 minutes and the tomato for 2 minutes. Stir this tempering into the lentils and season with salt.
  4. On an oiled surface, flatten a dough ball and stretch it outward with oiled hands until it is translucent and very thin, about 40 cm (16 in) across. Torn holes are fine.
  5. Fold the edges in to make a square, trapping air and layers inside.
  6. Fry on a flat griddle or large skillet over medium heat with a little ghee for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until golden and crisp in spots. Then scrunch it between your hands to break up the layers and fluff it. Serve hot with the dhal.

Common mistakes

  • Dough that isn't rested tears.
  • Insufficient oil makes it tough instead of flaky.

Authentic tips

  • Stretch rather than roll the dough; that is what makes the layers.
  • The dhal on its own, with rice and a vegetable, is a standard mamak and rice-shop dish.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought frozen roti canai saves time.
  • Chickpea curry also works.

Storage

Leftovers: The dhal keeps 5 days refrigerated; roti is best fresh but reheats on a dry griddle.

Freezing: The dhal freezes for 3 months; cooked roti freeze for 1 month.
