Jiāng Cōng Lo Mein (Ginger-Scallion Noodles) 姜葱捞面

▾

Surprise me with

Thin egg noodles tossed in ginger-scallion oil and oyster sauce is a Cantonese noodle-shop classic, often served alongside wontons or brisket.

  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

  • 400 g14 ozFresh thin egg noodles (Hong Kong style)
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, finely minced
  • 6 stalks6 stalksGreen onion, finely chopped
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 60 ml1/4 cupNeutral oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspVegetarian oyster sauce (mushroom sauce)
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 200 g7 ozBean sprouts or choy sum, blanched

Method

  1. Put the ginger, green onion and salt in a heatproof bowl.
  2. Heat the oil until it just smokes and pour it over the aromatics; it will sizzle. Stir.
  3. Boil the noodles for 30 to 60 seconds, rinse briefly in cold water to remove starch, then dip back into the boiling water for 10 seconds and drain well.
  4. Toss the hot noodles with the vegetarian oyster sauce, soy sauce, sesame oil and most of the ginger-scallion oil.
  5. Serve with the blanched vegetables and the rest of the oil spooned on top.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is not hot enough leaves the aromatics raw and harsh.
  • Undrained noodles water down the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • The cold rinse then quick re-dip is the noodle-shop trick for springy noodles.
  • Make a jar of ginger-scallion oil; it keeps a week refrigerated.

Substitutions

  • Regular oyster sauce if it does not need to be vegetarian.
  • Spaghetti works in a pinch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Wontons or blanched greens
  • Soup: Seaweed and egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
