Jiāng Cōng Lo Mein (Ginger-Scallion Noodles) 姜葱捞面

▾

Surprise me with

Thin egg noodles tossed in ginger-scallion oil and oyster sauce is a Cantonese noodle-shop classic, often served alongside wontons or brisket.

Ingredients

  • 350 g12 ozFresh thin egg noodles (Hong Kong style)
  • 5 ml1 tspNeutral oil, for the noodles
  • 250 g9 ozBok choy, quartered
  • 50 g2 ozGinger, julienned
  • 6 stalks6 stalksGreen onion, cut into fine strips
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 240 ml1 cupVegetable stock
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 2 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspVegetarian oyster sauce (mushroom sauce)
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater, for the slurry
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil with a teaspoon of oil. Blanch the bok choy for 70 to 80 seconds until bright green, dip it in cold water and arrange it around a platter.
  2. In the same water cook the noodles in two batches for about 30 seconds each, stirring. Dip them in cold water for 5 seconds, drain well and toss with the noodle oil so they do not clump. Pile them in the center of the platter.
  3. Mix the stock, salt, sugar, mushroom sauce and soy sauce in a bowl, and the cornstarch with its water.
  4. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat with the oil, lower to medium and fry the ginger for 10 seconds. Raise the heat, add the green onion and fry for 50 to 60 seconds, until fragrant and just wilted.
  5. Pour in the stock mixture and taste. Lower the heat, stir in the slurry, then boil over high heat for about 30 seconds until the sauce is lightly thick and glossy. Turn off the heat and add the sesame oil.
  6. Pour the ginger-scallion sauce over the noodles at the table, just before eating, so they stay springy.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is not hot enough leaves the aromatics raw and harsh.
  • Undrained noodles water down the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • The cold rinse then quick re-dip is the noodle-shop trick for springy noodles.
  • Make a jar of ginger-scallion oil; it keeps a week refrigerated.

Substitutions

  • Regular oyster sauce if it does not need to be vegetarian.
  • Spaghetti works in a pinch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Wontons or blanched greens
  • Soup: Seaweed and egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
