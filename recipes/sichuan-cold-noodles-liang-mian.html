Sichuan Cold Noodles (Liang Mian) 凉面

▾

Surprise me with

A refreshing Sichuan street-food staple especially popular in summer, liang mian tosses cooked-and-cooled wheat noodles in a numbing, savory sesame-chili sauce — meatless by tradition, though shredded cucumber and bean sprouts add crunch.

  • Cook 10 min

Method

  1. Cook the noodles until just tender, drain, and rinse under cold water. Toss them with a little oil and spread them out, fanning them to cool quickly; this keeps them springy and stops them sticking.
  2. Thin the sesame paste with 2 to 3 tablespoons of water, stirring until smooth.
  3. Mix in the soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, garlic, chili oil and Sichuan pepper. The sauce should be salty, sour, sweet, spicy and numbing in balance.
  4. Blanch the bean sprouts for 30 seconds and cool them.
  5. Pile the noodles over the cucumber and bean sprouts, pour the sauce over and top with peanuts. Toss before eating.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the cold-water rinse, which leaves the noodles starchy and prone to clumping once sauced.
  • Serving the noodles while still warm, which is a distinctly different dish — this one depends on being properly chilled.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse well under cold water and let the noodles cool completely before saucing; that is what makes the dish refreshing.
  • Thin the sesame paste slowly with cold water or it seizes.

Substitutions

  • Shredded chicken or tofu can be added on top for a heartier, protein-forward version.
  • No sesame paste: well-stirred natural peanut butter works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; sauce may need loosening with a splash of water before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended.
