Gān Shāo Xiā Rén (Dry-Braised Prawns) 干烧虾仁

▾

Surprise me with

Dry-braised prawns apply Sichuan's gan shao method to shrimp, producing a spicy, sweet-sour glaze that clings to each piece; a common banquet and home dish.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbLarge prawns, shelled and deveined
  • 10 ml2 tspSalt and Shaoxing wine, for the prawns
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch, for the prawns
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 30 g2 tbspPixian doubanjiang
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, garlic and green onion whites, minced
  • 30 g2 tbspKetchup
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine, for the sauce
  • 100 ml1/3 cupStock
  • 5 ml1 tspChinkiang vinegar
  • 15 g1/2 ozGreen onion greens, sliced

Method

  1. Toss the prawns with salt, Shaoxing wine and cornstarch and leave for 10 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and sear the prawns for 1 minute, until they just turn pink. Lift them out.
  3. Add the remaining oil, lower the heat to medium and fry the doubanjiang for 1 minute until red, then the ginger, garlic and green onion whites for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the ketchup and fry for 30 seconds, then the sugar, sauce Shaoxing wine and stock, and simmer for 1 minute.
  5. Return the prawns and toss over high heat for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce reduces to a thick glaze (dry-braising means simmering until the sauce clings). Add the vinegar and green onion and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the prawns while reducing the sauce makes them rubbery; take them out first.
  • Using too little oil keeps the glaze from clinging.

Authentic tips

  • The ketchup is a classic Sichuan restaurant addition for sweetness and body.
  • Keep the shells on and peel at the table for more flavor, if you prefer.

Substitutions

  • Sriracha plus a little miso replaces doubanjiang.
  • Scallops make a richer dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day.

Freezing: Not recommended.
