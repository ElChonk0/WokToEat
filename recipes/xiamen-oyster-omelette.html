Oyster Omelette 海蛎煎

Xiamen’s oyster omelette binds small fresh oysters with sweet potato starch and egg into a crisp-edged, chewy pancake, a street food spread across Taiwan and Southeast Asia as o-a-jian or or-luak.

  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Rinse the oysters gently in salted water and drain.
  2. Whisk the starch with the water, fish sauce and half the green garlic into a thin batter; fold in the oysters.
  3. Heat oil in a wide pan until shimmering and pour in a quarter of the batter in a thin layer.
  4. When the bottom crisps, about 2 minutes, pour a beaten egg over the top and scatter more green garlic.
  5. Flip, cook 1–2 minutes until crisp and set, and repeat. Serve with cilantro and chili sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Making the batter thick; it should be thin so the starch turns chewy and the edges crisp.

Authentic tips

  • Hot oil and a wide pan matter more than technique: crowding makes it gummy.

Substitutions

  • Small shrimp or clams replace the oysters.
  • Potato starch replaces sweet potato starch.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheated omelettes turn rubbery.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Congee or rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
