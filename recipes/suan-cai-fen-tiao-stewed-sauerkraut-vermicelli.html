Suān Cài Fěn Tiáo (Stewed Sauerkraut with Vermicelli) 酸菜粉条

▾

Surprise me with

Suan cai, Chinese sauerkraut fermented in crocks, is the winter staple of Dongbei, and stewing it with sweet potato vermicelli and tofu makes a tangy, warming dish.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 400 g14 ozChinese sauerkraut (suan cai), rinsed, squeezed and finely shredded
  • 150 g5 ozSweet potato vermicelli, soaked
  • 200 g7 ozFirm or frozen tofu, cubed
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 30 g1 ozGarlic and ginger, minced
  • 55Dried chilies
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 700 ml3 cupsVegetable stock
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt

Method

  1. Shred the sauerkraut as finely as you can and squeeze out the brine.
  2. Heat the oil in a pot and fry the garlic, ginger and chilies for 30 seconds, then the sauerkraut for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant and slightly dry.
  3. Add the stock, soy sauce and tofu and simmer for 10 minutes. Frozen tofu, spongy from freezing, soaks up the sour broth.
  4. Add the vermicelli and simmer for 5 to 8 minutes, until clear and slippery.
  5. Taste before adding salt (the pickles are salty), season with white pepper and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Over-salting: the sauerkraut is salty already.
  • Overcooking makes the noodles mushy.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse the sauerkraut if it is very sour.
  • Add a few slices of pork belly for a traditional version.

Substitutions

  • Rinsed European sauerkraut replaces suan cai.
  • Glass noodles replace sweet potato vermicelli.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the noodles absorb the broth.

Freezing: Freeze without the noodles for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or buns
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
