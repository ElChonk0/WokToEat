Zhēng Qié Zi (Steamed Eggplant with Garlic Soy Dressing) 蒸茄子

▾

Surprise me with

Steaming is the light Cantonese way with eggplant: the strips turn creamy without oil and are dressed with garlic, soy and sizzling oil.

Method

  1. Cut the eggplants into batons about 8 cm (3 in) long, leaving some of the skin on so the pieces hold together. Soak them in cold water with the vinegar for 10 minutes, which keeps the flesh pale, then drain and squeeze gently.
  2. Arrange the eggplant on a heatproof plate. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer, bring the water to a full boil and steam the plate covered over high heat for 10 to 12 minutes, until a chopstick slides through the thickest piece with no resistance.
  3. Meanwhile mix the soy sauces, mushroom sauce, vinegar, sugar and water, and the cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  4. Heat the oil in a small pan over high heat until it shimmers, add the garlic, ginger, chili and green onion whites and stir for 1 minute until fragrant. Add the sauce, simmer 1 minute, then stir in the slurry until glossy. Turn off the heat and add the sesame oil.
  5. Pour off any liquid that has collected on the eggplant plate, spoon the garlic sauce over and scatter with the green onion greens.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Under-steamed eggplant is spongy; it should be silky.
  • Leaving the steaming water on the plate dilutes the dressing.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the skin side down so the eggplant keeps its purple color.
  • It is also good cold as a summer side.

Substitutions

  • Globe eggplant, peeled and cut into batons; steam 15 minutes.
  • Add minced pork on top before steaming for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; serve cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with a main dish
  • Soup: Spinach tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
