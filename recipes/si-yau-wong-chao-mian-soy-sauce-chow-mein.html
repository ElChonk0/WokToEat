Si Yau Wong Chao Mian (Supreme Soy Sauce Chow Mein) 豉油皇炒面

Soy sauce chow mein is a dim sum and breakfast staple in Hong Kong: thin egg noodles fried with only bean sprouts, onion and scallions so the soy-glazed noodles are the point.

Method

  1. Blanch the noodles in boiling water for 30 to 60 seconds, drain thoroughly and spread out to steam dry. Mix the soy sauces and sugar.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat, add the noodles in an even layer and fry for 2 to 3 minutes per side until lightly crisp. Move to a plate.
  3. Add the last tablespoon of oil and stir-fry the onion for 1 minute.
  4. Return the noodles, pour the sauce around the edge of the wok and toss with chopsticks or tongs for 1 minute until evenly dark and glossy.
  5. Add the bean sprouts and green onion and toss for 30 seconds until just wilted. Finish with sesame oil and sesame seeds.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too much sauce makes the noodles soggy; they should look shiny, not wet.
  • Cooking the sprouts too long loses their crunch.

Authentic tips

  • Dark soy is for color and light soy for salt; the balance gives the deep mahogany look.
  • In Hong Kong this is eaten with congee for breakfast.

Substitutions

  • Add strips of fried tofu or shiitake for more protein.
  • Dried thin egg noodles: boil 3 minutes and dry well.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain congee
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hot milk tea
