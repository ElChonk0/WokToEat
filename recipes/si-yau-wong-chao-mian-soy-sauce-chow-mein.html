Si Yau Wong Chao Mian (Supreme Soy Sauce Chow Mein) 豉油皇炒面

▾

Surprise me with

Soy sauce chow mein is a dim sum and breakfast staple in Hong Kong: thin egg noodles fried with only bean sprouts, onion and scallions so the soy-glazed noodles are the point.

Method

  1. Blanch the noodles in boiling water for 30 to 60 seconds, loosening them, then rinse in cold water and drain very thoroughly. Toss them with the tossing oil, using your hands and chopsticks, so every strand is coated; oiled noodles fry without clumping. Mix the soy sauces, mushroom sauce, sugar and water.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until a faint ring of smoke appears, lower to low and add the oil. Spread the noodles out and leave them for 30 to 60 seconds. Raise to medium, flip and leave another 30 to 60 seconds, then raise to high and sear and flip for 1 to 2 minutes more, until they are crisp and browned in places.
  3. Push the noodles aside, fry the onion in the space for 1 minute, then toss it through.
  4. Pour the sauce in gradually while tossing with chopsticks, stopping when the noodles are an even, glossy brown; you may not need it all.
  5. Add the bean sprouts and green onion and toss for 30 to 60 seconds until just wilted. Turn off the heat, add the sesame oil and seeds and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too much sauce makes the noodles soggy; they should look shiny, not wet.
  • Cooking the sprouts too long loses their crunch.

Authentic tips

  • Dark soy is for color and light soy for salt; the balance gives the deep mahogany look.
  • In Hong Kong this is eaten with congee for breakfast.

Substitutions

  • Add strips of fried tofu or shiitake for more protein.
  • Dried thin egg noodles: boil 3 minutes and dry well.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain congee
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hot milk tea
