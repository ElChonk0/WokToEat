Suān Dòu Jiǎo Chǎo Ròu Mò (Pickled Long Beans with Minced Pork) 酸豆角炒肉末

▾

Pickled long beans with minced pork are a Hunan staple, fried hot with chilies and eaten over rice or noodles.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 300 g10 ozPork, ground
  • 250 g9 ozPickled long beans, chopped
  • 8 g1/4 ozDried chilies
  • 30 g2 tbspGarlic and ginger
  • 5 ml1 tspLight soy sauce
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozFresh chilies

Method

  1. Rinse the pickled beans briefly, squeeze dry and chop into 5 mm pieces; chop the fresh chilies.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat and fry the pork 3 minutes until browned and crumbly; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, dried chilies, garlic and ginger and fry 20 seconds, then the fresh chilies.
  4. Add the beans and stir-fry 3 minutes until they smell tangy and look dry.
  5. Return the pork with the soy sauce and toss 1 minute.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the rinse makes it too salty to eat with rice.
  • Wet beans spatter and steam; squeeze them dry.

Authentic tips

  • Serve over rice or noodles; a spoonful goes a long way.
  • It keeps for several days and tastes better reheated.

Substitutions

  • Sauerkraut with a pinch of chili replaces pickled long beans.
  • Ground chicken thigh replaces the pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or congee
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
