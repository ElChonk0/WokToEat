Dry-Fried Beef 干煸牛肉丝

▾

Surprise me with

Gan bian niu rou si dry-fries shredded beef until chewy and concentrated, then tosses it with celery and chili in a technique that intentionally minimizes sauce, letting the beef's flavor dominate.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the Shaoxing wine and salt; no starch, because dry-frying needs the meat to lose its moisture.
  2. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and add the beef. Stir-fry for 8 to 10 minutes, as the beef first releases its juices and then fries in the oil, until the strips are dark, dry and slightly chewy. This slow dry-frying (gan bian) is the whole technique.
  3. Push the beef aside, lower the heat and fry the doubanjiang for 1 minute until the oil is red. Add the chilies, peppercorns, garlic and ginger for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the celery and carrot and stir-fry over high heat for 1 to 2 minutes until just tender.
  5. Season with soy sauce and sugar, toss until everything is coated and dry, then finish with sesame oil and ground Sichuan pepper.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial dry-frying step, which is what concentrates the beef's flavor and gives the dish its characteristic chewy texture.
  • Adding too much liquid at the end — this dish should end up dry, not saucy.

Authentic tips

  • The dry-frying technique concentrates flavor by design; resist the urge to add extra sauce or stock.
  • Cutting the beef into thin, even strips against the grain ensures the best texture after the long dry-fry.

Substitutions

  • Pork can be prepared the same way for an equally authentic dry-fried dish.
  • Reduce the Sichuan pepper for a milder, less numbing version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the texture stays chewy-crisp, so reheat in a dry pan.

Freezing: Not recommended; texture suffers.
