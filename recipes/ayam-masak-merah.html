Ayam Masak Merah

▾

Surprise me with

A Malay dish whose name means "chicken cooked red," ayam masak merah simmers chicken in a sweet-spicy tomato-chili sauce, commonly served at celebrations and Hari Raya gatherings.

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with the turmeric and salt. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry the chicken for 4 to 5 minutes per side until golden, without cooking it through. Lift it out and pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil.
  2. Blend the onion, garlic, soaked chilies, ginger, galangal and lemongrass whites into a paste. Fry it in the reserved oil over medium heat for 3 to 5 minutes until fragrant and darker.
  3. Add the bruised lemongrass greens, star anise and cinnamon for 1 minute, then the tomato paste and ketchup and stir for 30 seconds until glossy.
  4. Add the water, coconut milk, sugar and salt, return the chicken and bring to a boil. Cover and simmer over low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the red gravy is thick.
  5. Turn off the heat, stir in the tomato chunks so they soften without turning to mush, taste for salt and sugar and garnish with cilantro. Serve with nasi tomato or plain rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the tomato paste-frying step, which leaves a raw, sharp tomato taste instead of a deep, rounded one.
  • Using too little dried chili, which won't achieve the dish's characteristic deep red color.

Authentic tips

  • Soaking dried chilies in hot water for 15 minutes before blending softens them for a smoother paste.
  • This dish is traditionally served at festive occasions — it scales up easily for a crowd.

Substitutions

  • Tomato passata replaces fresh tomato.
  • Reduce the dried chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
