Malaysian Beef Rendang

▾

Surprise me with

Malaysia's version of rendang, closely related to the Indonesian original, is often slightly wetter and features a distinct blend of local spices — a festive dish especially popular during Hari Raya celebrations.

Method

  1. Make kerisik: toast the grated coconut in a dry pan over low heat, stirring constantly, for 10 to 15 minutes until deep golden, then pound or blend it into an oily paste.
  2. Blend the fresh and dried chilies, shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal, turmeric and one lemongrass stalk into a smooth paste.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy wok over medium heat and fry the paste with the star anise and bruised lemongrass for 8 to 10 minutes, until fragrant and the oil starts to separate.
  4. Add the beef, coconut milk and lime leaves, bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered over low heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, stirring every 15 minutes, until the beef is nearly tender and the liquid has reduced by half.
  5. Add the kerisik, tamarind, palm sugar, salt and turmeric leaf and keep cooking over low heat for 45 minutes to 1 hour, stirring more often as it thickens, until the sauce has almost disappeared, the coconut oil has separated and the beef is dark brown and fork-tender. This long reduction is what defines rendang; lower the heat if it starts to catch.
  6. Rest for 15 minutes before serving with rice or ketupat. Rendang keeps for days and tastes better the day after.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — patience is what develops the sauce's depth and color.
  • Skipping the kerisik, which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • This Malaysian version can be left slightly wetter than the drier Indonesian original — adjust the final reduction to taste.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can be prepared the same way with a shorter cooking time.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.
