Malaysian Beef Rendang

▾

Surprise me with

Malaysia's version of rendang, closely related to the Indonesian original, is often slightly wetter and features a distinct blend of local spices — a festive dish especially popular during Hari Raya celebrations.

Method

  1. Blend shallot, garlic, and soaked dried chilies into a smooth paste.
  2. Fry the paste with galangal and lemongrass in oil until deeply fragrant, 5–6 minutes.
  3. Add beef and stir to coat, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  4. Pour in coconut milk, add kaffir lime leaves and tamarind paste, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Cook uncovered over low heat, stirring occasionally, 1 to 1.25 hours until the sauce reduces and darkens.
  6. Stir in kerisik in the last 10 minutes; season with salt and sugar to taste.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — patience is what develops the sauce's depth and color.
  • Skipping the kerisik, which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • This Malaysian version can be left slightly wetter than the drier Indonesian original — adjust the final reduction to taste.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can be prepared the same way with a shorter cooking time.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.
