Panang Pork Curry

▾

Surprise me with

A pork adaptation of Thailand's thick, nutty panang curry, this version simmers pork in a rich curry paste and coconut cream reduction — heartier than the chicken version due to pork's fattier profile.

Method

  1. Simmer the thicker coconut cream with curry paste over medium heat until fragrant and the oil separates slightly, 3–4 minutes.
  2. Add pork and stir to coat in the paste, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  3. Add remaining coconut milk, fish sauce, palm sugar, and ground peanuts; stir to combine.
  4. Simmer gently, uncovered, 18–20 minutes until the pork is tender and the sauce has thickened.
  5. Stir in kaffir lime leaves in the last 2 minutes.
  6. Garnish with sliced chili and Thai basil before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the curry hard, which can cause the coconut milk to separate into an oily texture.
  • Skipping the initial paste-frying step, which is key to developing the dish's full aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Panang should be noticeably thicker than red or green curry — don't thin it out with extra liquid.
  • Ground peanuts (not whole) blend into the sauce for the characteristic panang texture and nuttiness.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly slices make a richer curry.
  • Chicken or beef take the same paste.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; thin with coconut milk.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
