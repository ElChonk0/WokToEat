Semur Daging (Indonesian Beef Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

A Dutch-influenced Indonesian stew, semur braises beef in kecap manis with warm spices like nutmeg and clove — reflecting Indonesia's colonial history while remaining thoroughly Indonesian in flavor.

Method

  1. Grind the shallots, garlic and candlenuts into a coarse paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the paste for 5 minutes until golden and fragrant. Add the nutmeg, pepper, cloves and cinnamon for 30 seconds. The warm spices show semur's Dutch-colonial roots (from smoor, to braise).
  3. Add the beef and stir for 5 minutes until browned on the outside.
  4. Add the kecap manis, soy sauce, tomato and water, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 hours, until the beef is tender.
  5. Add the potatoes and simmer uncovered for 20 to 25 minutes, until they are cooked and the sauce is thick, dark and glossy. Serve topped with fried shallots, with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Using regular soy sauce instead of kecap manis, which is thicker and considerably sweeter.
  • Rushing the initial beef braise, since chuck needs real time to become properly tender.

Authentic tips

  • The warm spices (nutmeg, clove) are what distinguish semur from other Indonesian braises — don't skip them.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Short rib replaces chuck.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the potatoes.

Serve with
