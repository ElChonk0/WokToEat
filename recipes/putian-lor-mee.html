Putian Braised Noodles 莆田卤面

▾

Surprise me with

Putian’s lu mian is a celebration noodle: thick wheat noodles simmered in a seafood and pork broth with clams, oysters and shrimp until the liquid thickens into a gravy. It is served at birthdays and weddings.

Method

  1. Fry the shallots in oil until golden; add the pork and shiitake and stir-fry 3 minutes.
  2. Add the cabbage and stir-fry until wilted, then the stock and soy; bring to a boil.
  3. Add the clams, cover and cook until they open, 3–4 minutes.
  4. Add the noodles and simmer, stirring, 5–6 minutes until they soften and the broth thickens from their starch.
  5. Add the shrimp and oysters for the last minute; fold in chives and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Pre-cooking the noodles separately; the noodles release starch into the pot, and that starch is what thickens the broth.

Authentic tips

  • Putian cooks add a spoonful of dried shrimp to the shallots for a deeper seafood base.

Substitutions

  • Udon replaces fresh thick noodles.
  • Squid and fish balls can join the seafood.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; loosen with water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is a full meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
