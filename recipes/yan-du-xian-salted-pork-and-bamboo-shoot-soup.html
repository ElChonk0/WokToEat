Yān Dǔ Xiān (Salted Pork and Bamboo Shoot Soup) 腌笃鲜

▾

Surprise me with

Yan du xian, salty cured pork and fresh pork simmered with spring bamboo shoots, is a Shanghai and Jiangnan springtime soup that celebrates the first bamboo of the year.

  • 150 g5 ozCured pork or ham
  • 250 g9 ozFresh pork belly
  • 400 g14 ozSpring bamboo shoots
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 2 L8 cupsWater
  • 20 g2 tbspScallion

Method

  1. Blanch the cured pork and fresh belly in boiling water for 3 minutes; drain and rinse. Boil the cut bamboo shoots for 5 minutes and drain.
  2. Put the meats in a pot with the water, ginger and wine, bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer and cook 90 minutes.
  3. Cut the pork into slices, return it to the pot and add the bamboo shoots.
  4. Simmer 30 minutes more until the broth is milky and the shoots are tender.
  5. Skim the surface, taste and add salt only if needed (the cured pork salts the soup); garnish with scallion.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard breaks the delicate bamboo and clouds the soup.
  • Salting too soon: the cured pork makes it salty already.

Authentic tips

  • The name means "salty, simmered, fresh": salted pork and fresh pork provide the two flavors.
  • Use fresh spring bamboo if you can; canned shoots will not give the same sweetness.

Substitutions

  • Smoked bacon or country ham replaces the cured pork.
  • Canned bamboo shoots work; rinse and blanch them first.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months, though bamboo shoots lose some crispness.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
