Fish Koliwada (Mumbai Fried Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Fish koliwada, spiced batter-fried fish, comes from the Koliwada neighborhood of Mumbai, home to the city's Koli fishing community, and is a pub and street-food favorite.

Method

  1. Toss the fish with the ginger-garlic paste, lemon juice, chili powder, turmeric, ajwain, garam masala and salt and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Add the yogurt, gram flour and rice flour and mix until each piece is coated in a thick, sticky batter, adding a splash of water only if needed. Koliwada fish comes from the Koli fishing villages of Mumbai.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 175 C (345 F) and fry the fish in batches for 3 to 4 minutes until deep red-gold and crisp.
  4. Drain on a rack. Fry the curry leaves in the hot oil for a few seconds until they crackle.
  5. Sprinkle the fish with chaat masala and the fried curry leaves and serve with lemon and green chutney.

Cook’s notes

Written for haddock fillet. Mumbai cooks use pomfret, surmai or bombil.

Common mistakes

  • Too thin a batter slides off; keep it thick enough to cling.
  • Crowded pans lower the oil temperature and make the fish greasy.

Authentic tips

  • For less oil, roast the coated pieces on a rack at 220°C (425°F) for 15 minutes, turning once.
  • Use a thermometer on electric and induction stoves.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin replaces the haddock.
  • Chicken or paneer take the same coating.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; re-crisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Green chutney and sliced onion
  • Soup: Tomato saar
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Masala chai
