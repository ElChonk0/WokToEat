Yuxiang Shrimp (Fish-Fragrant Shrimp) 鱼香虾仁

▾

Surprise me with

Yuxiang, or "fish-fragrant," is a Sichuan flavor profile of pickled chili, ginger, garlic and scallion in a sweet, sour and savory sauce, and it is applied to shrimp, pork and eggplant alike.

  • 500 g1 lbShrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 15 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 30 g2 tbspPickled chili paste (pao la jiao)
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger, garlic and scallion whites, minced
  • 30 ml2 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 100 g3.5 ozWater chestnuts or bamboo shoots, diced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Toss the shrimp with a pinch of salt and the cornstarch.
  2. Mix the vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and 3 tablespoons of water in a bowl.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the shrimp for 1 to 2 minutes until just pink. Lift them out.
  4. Add the remaining oil, lower the heat and fry the pickled chili paste for 30 seconds until red, then the ginger, garlic and scallion whites for 20 seconds. Add the water chestnuts.
  5. Return the shrimp, pour in the sauce and toss for 30 seconds until glossy and thick. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the shrimp; they finish cooking in the sauce.
  • Burning the chili paste, which turns it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Properly balanced, the sauce is sweet, sour and salty at once with a gentle chili heat.

Substitutions

  • Sliced chicken or firm tofu take the same sauce.
  • Fresh minced chilies plus a little extra vinegar replace the pickled chili paste.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day; reheated shrimp turn rubbery.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Mandarin orange segments
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
