Ipoh Prawn Mee

▾

Surprise me with

Ipoh-style prawn mee is a Malaysian noodle soup with a sweeter, deeper prawn-and-pork-bone broth than the Singapore version, served with yellow noodles, vermicelli, pork slices and prawns.

Method

  1. Peel the prawns, keeping the heads and shells, and set the meat aside. Blanch the pork bones in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Fry the shallots, garlic and dried shrimp in the oil for 3 minutes, add the prawn heads and shells and press them for 5 minutes until the oil turns orange and fragrant.
  3. Add the bones, rock sugar and water, bring to a boil, then simmer gently for 40 minutes. Strain and season with the salt and white pepper.
  4. Poach the pork slices in the simmering broth for 1 minute, lift out, then poach the prawn meat for 1 to 2 minutes until pink and curled.
  5. Blanch the noodles, vermicelli, kangkong and bean sprouts in boiling water for 1 minute each and divide into bowls.
  6. Top with the pork and prawns, ladle over the hot broth, and add the fried shallots and chili paste.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard clouds the broth and toughens the meat; keep a gentle simmer.
  • Over-poaching the prawns makes them rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • The sweetness comes from rock sugar and the pork bones, so do not skip them.
  • Serve the chili paste on the side.

Substitutions

  • Chicken bones replace pork bones.
  • Skip the pork slices for a prawn-only bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sliced red chilies in soy sauce
  • Soup: The dish is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced lime juice
