Gimbap (Korean Seaweed Rice Rolls) 김밥

Gimbap, rice and fillings rolled in roasted seaweed and seasoned with sesame oil, is the standard Korean picnic, school-trip and quick-breakfast food, sold at snack shops all over the country.

Method

  1. Mix the warm rice with the sesame oil, salt and sesame seeds and leave it uncovered to cool to body temperature.
  2. Beat the eggs and cook into a thick omelet in an oiled skillet over low heat, then cut into long strips. Fry the ham, carrot and crab sticks separately in a little oil for 1 to 2 minutes each.
  3. Blanch the spinach for 20 seconds, squeeze dry and season with a pinch of salt, the garlic and a few drops of sesame oil. Simmer the fish cake strips with the soy sauce and sugar for 2 minutes until glazed.
  4. Lay a seaweed sheet shiny side down on a bamboo mat. Spread a quarter of the rice thinly over the lower two-thirds, line up a quarter of each filling in the middle, then roll it up tightly, pressing as you go, and seal the edge with a few grains of rice.
  5. Brush the roll with sesame oil, slice it into 1.5 cm (1/2 in) rounds with a wet knife, and repeat with the rest.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Spreading the rice too thick makes rolls that split; keep it a thin, even layer.
  • Wet fillings soak the seaweed; squeeze the spinach thoroughly.

Authentic tips

  • Wipe the knife with a damp cloth between slices for clean cuts.
  • Leftover gimbap is traditionally dipped in egg and pan-fried the next day.

Substitutions

  • Bulgogi beef or canned tuna mixed with mayonnaise replace the ham.
  • Use fried tofu strips and skip the crab and fish cake for a vegetarian roll.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day; refrigerated rice hardens, so dip slices in beaten egg and pan-fry them.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi
  • Soup: Eomuk guk (fish cake soup)
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
