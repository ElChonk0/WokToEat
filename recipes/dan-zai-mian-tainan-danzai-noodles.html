Dàn Zǎi Miàn (Tainan Danzai Noodles) 擔仔麵

▾

Surprise me with

Danzai noodles originated in Tainan in 1895 as a fisherman's off-season street food: a small bowl of noodles with a shrimp-head broth and minced pork.

  • 3 cloves3 clovesGarlic, crushed to a paste, for topping

Method

  1. Peel the shrimp, keeping the heads and shells. Fry the shells and heads in half the oil over medium heat for 3 minutes, pressing the heads, then add the stock and simmer for 15 minutes. Strain; this shrimp stock is the base of the soup. Poach the shrimp in it for 1 minute and lift them out.
  2. Make the rou sao: fry the shallots in the remaining oil for 6 to 8 minutes until golden, add the pork and garlic and cook for 5 minutes until browned. Add the soy sauce, rock sugar, rice wine, five-spice and 200 ml (3/4 cup) of water and simmer for 30 minutes until thick.
  3. Blanch the noodles and bean sprouts in boiling water for 30 seconds; danzai bowls are small, so cook a small handful at a time.
  4. Put the noodles and sprouts in bowls, add a spoon of rou sao, a shrimp, a little garlic paste and the chives, and ladle over the hot shrimp stock.
  5. Finish with a drop of black vinegar and cilantro. The dish began with Tainan fishermen selling noodles from a shoulder pole (danzai) in the off-season.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles go soft.
  • Cooking the shrimp too long makes them rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • Traditional bowls are small, a snack, not a meal.
  • The shrimp-head broth is the heart.

Substitutions

  • Chicken stock plus shrimp shells replaces shrimp-head broth.
  • Cooked pork belly slices replace minced pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: A second small bowl is typical
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
