Singapore Fish Ball Noodles

▾

Surprise me with

Fish ball noodles, bouncy fish balls with egg noodles dressed in vinegar and chili, are a Singapore hawker breakfast of Teochew origin.

  • 400 g14 ozFish balls (and fish cake), store-bought or homemade
  • 300 g10 ozEgg noodles (mee pok or mee kia)
  • 1.5 L6 cupsChicken stock
  • 40 g1.5 ozDried anchovies (ikan bilis), rinsed
  • 4 g1 tspSugar
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 150 g5 ozChoy sum
  • 30 ml2 tbspBlack vinegar
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili sauce or sambal
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspLard or shallot oil
  • 15 g1/2 ozFried shallots
  • 20 g3/4 ozScallion, chopped

Method

  1. Bring the stock to a boil with the anchovies and simmer gently for 30 minutes, then strain and season with the sugar and salt. The anchovies give the broth its savory depth.
  2. Add the fish balls and fish cake and simmer for 2 to 3 minutes until hot through and floating; store-bought ones are already cooked and only need warming.
  3. For the dry version, mix the vinegar, chili sauce, soy sauce and lard in each serving bowl.
  4. Blanch the choy sum for 1 minute. Cook the noodles in a strainer basket in boiling water for about 45 seconds, dip in cold water and back in the hot water for a few seconds, then drain well and toss in the sauce.
  5. Top the noodles with the choy sum, fried shallots and scallion, and serve the fish balls in a bowl of the hot broth on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles loses their bounce.
  • Boiling the fish balls hard makes them rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • Fishcake slices are a classic addition.
  • Serve dry (with sauce) or in soup.

Substitutions

  • Spaghetti replaces mee pok.
  • Fish cake slices are a classic addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and soup separately; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili sauce
  • Soup: The soup is the side
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Kopi
