Hainanese Pork Chop

▾

Surprise me with

A Hainanese-Chinese fusion dish popular in Singapore and Malaysia's Western-style coffee shops (kopitiam), Hainanese pork chop breads and fries pork in a distinctly local style, served with a tangy tomato-Worcestershire sauce.

  • Cook 30 min

Method

  1. Pound the chops between plastic wrap to an even 1 cm (1/2 in) thickness; thick chops burn outside before the center cooks. Season with the oyster sauce, wine and pepper and marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Roast or air-fry the potato wedges at 200 C (400 F) for 20 to 25 minutes until golden, or deep-fry them.
  3. Dredge the chops in cornstarch, dip in egg and press into the cracker crumbs. Crushed cream crackers, not breadcrumbs, give the Hainanese chop its light, crackly crust.
  4. Shallow-fry in the oil over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until golden and cooked through. Keep the heat moderate so the crumbs do not darken before the pork cooks. Drain on a rack.
  5. For the sauce, fry the onion in a little oil for 3 minutes, add the peas for 1 minute, then the ketchup, Worcestershire, sugar, water and tomato and simmer for 3 minutes. Stir in the slurry and bring to a boil until thick and glossy.
  6. Slice the chops, lay them on a plate with the potatoes and pour the sweet-tangy sauce over just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan while frying, which lowers the oil temperature and produces a greasy crust.
  • Making the sauce too thin, which won't cling properly to the fried chops.

Authentic tips

  • This dish's distinctive tomato-Worcestershire sauce sets it apart from Japanese tonkatsu, despite similar breading technique.
  • Serve over rice or with a side of fries, in the style of a kopitiam Western set meal.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works well with the same breading and sauce.
  • No Worcestershire sauce: a mix of soy sauce and a splash of vinegar approximates the tang.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the coating softens on reheating.

Freezing: Freezes well, breaded and uncooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fries, peas and steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Ais kacang
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
