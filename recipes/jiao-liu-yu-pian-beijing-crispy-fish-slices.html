Jiāo Liū Yú Piàn (Crispy Fish Slices in Sweet-Sour Sauce) 焦熘鱼片

▾

Surprise me with

Jiao liu, deep-frying until crisp and then tossing in a glossy sauce, is a classic technique of northern Chinese banquet cooking; fish slices in sweet-sour sauce are a standard example.

Method

  1. Season the fish with the salt and egg white and coat each slice in the cornstarch, pressing it on.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok to 180°C (350°F) and fry the slices in two batches for 2 minutes until pale gold; remove and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Reheat the oil to 195°C (385°F) and fry again for 30 to 45 seconds until crisp and deep gold; drain well.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil, fry the garlic, ginger and scallions for 15 seconds, then add the vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and water and bring to a boil.
  5. Stir in the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water to thicken, add the sesame oil, and slide in the fish to coat for 10 seconds. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet.

Common mistakes

  • Crowding the wok lowers the oil temperature and makes the fish greasy.
  • Coating the fish early in sauce makes it soggy.

Authentic tips

  • A thermometer helps on electric and induction stoves, where the oil recovers slowly.
  • The sauce should taste sharply sweet and sour before thickening.

Substitutions

  • Haddock replaces the cod.
  • Tomato ketchup (2 tablespoons) added to the sauce gives a red, more Western version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and cucumber salad
  • Soup: Corn egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Hawthorn candies
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
