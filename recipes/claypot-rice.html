Claypot Rice

A Cantonese one-pot dish cooked directly in a clay vessel, prized for the crisp, faintly charred rice crust that forms against the hot clay walls — a textural contrast central to the dish's appeal.

  • Prep 20 min (+30 min soak)
  • Cook 30 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Soak rice in water 30 minutes, then drain.
  2. Place rice and the correct amount of fresh water in a cast-iron Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot (or a claypot); bring to a boil uncovered.
  3. Once boiling, arrange chicken, sausage, and mushrooms over the rice, reduce to low heat, and cover.
  4. Cook covered 15–18 minutes, resisting the urge to lift the lid, until the rice is tender.
  5. In the final 1–2 minutes, raise the heat briefly to encourage a crust at the bottom — listen for a faint crackling sound.
  6. Mix soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, and sesame oil; drizzle over once cooked, top with scallion, and serve from the pot.

Common mistakes

  • Lifting the lid while cooking: it releases steam needed to cook the rice evenly and can prevent the crust from forming.

Authentic tips

  • A well-seasoned clay or heavy cast-iron pot holds heat more evenly than a thin metal pot, which matters for the crust.

Substitutions

  • A cast-iron Dutch oven is the best electric or induction substitute for a claypot; on those stoves turn the heat up for the last 2 minutes to form the crust, and let the pot sit off the heat 5 minutes afterwards.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheat gently with a splash of water to revive the rice without drying it further.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the prized crust softens once frozen and reheated.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched choy sum on the side
  • Soup: Clear chicken and corn soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
