Claypot Rice with Chicken and Chinese Sausage 腊肠滑鸡煲仔饭

▾

Surprise me with

Bo zai fan is a Cantonese one-pot dish in which rice, marinated chicken and cured sausage cook together so the sausage fat seasons the rice and a crisp crust forms at the bottom of the pot.

Method

  1. Rinse the rice three times until the water runs almost clear, then cover it with the boiling soaking water and leave for 10 minutes; this gives the grains a head start so the rice cooks through before the crust burns. Soak the sausage in hot water for a few minutes too, then slice it on a slant.
  2. Squeeze the soaked mushrooms dry, trim the stems and halve them. Pat the chicken dry, then mix it with the marinade soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, pepper, wine, cornstarch and water until absorbed. Stir in the sesame oil, ginger and shallot and leave for 15 minutes.
  3. Drain the rice and put it in a heavy pot: a small cast-iron Dutch oven works on gas, electric and induction. Stir in 1 teaspoon of oil and the boiling cooking water, cover and set over medium heat.
  4. When the water is nearly boiling and has sunk to the level of the rice, with small steam holes appearing, spread the rice level. Arrange the chicken, sausage and mushrooms on top, cover again and turn the heat to low.
  5. Cook for 12 to 15 minutes without lifting the lid, until you hear a faint crackling at the edges and the chicken is cooked through. That sound means the water is gone and the bottom has started to toast.
  6. For the crust, drizzle the crust oil around the inside edge of the pot so it runs down the sides. Raise the heat to medium and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, turning the pot a quarter turn every 1 to 2 minutes so each side of the base gets direct heat. Lower the heat if it smells sharp rather than toasty.
  7. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 5 minutes, which loosens the crust. Stir the topping sauce ingredients together, drizzle over the rice, scatter the green onion and serve from the pot, scraping up the crisp rice from the bottom.

Common mistakes

  • Lifting the lid while it cooks lets out the steam the rice needs and stops the crust forming.
  • Too much water leaves the rice mushy instead of crisp at the bottom.

Authentic tips

  • A clay pot is the traditional vessel on a gas flame; a cast-iron Dutch oven holds heat as evenly and works on gas, electric and induction.
  • Add the sauce at the end, off the heat, so it stays bright rather than cooked out.

Substitutions

  • No Chinese sausage: thick-cut bacon lardons are a reasonable, less traditional substitute.
  • Dried shiitake, soaked and sliced, give a deeper flavor than fresh; use the soaking water as part of the measured water.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat covered with a splash of water to loosen the rice.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably for a month, though the crust softens.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Stir-fried gai lan with garlic
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
