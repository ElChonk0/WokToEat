Claypot Rice with Chicken and Chinese Sausage 腊肠滑鸡煲仔饭

▾

Surprise me with

Bo zai fan is a Cantonese one-pot dish in which rice, marinated chicken and cured sausage cook together so the sausage fat seasons the rice and a crisp crust forms at the bottom of the pot.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 2 stalks2 stalksScallion, sliced, for garnish

Method

  1. Soak the rice in water for 30 minutes, then drain. Meanwhile, marinate the chicken with half the oyster sauce, 1 tablespoon of the light soy sauce, the ginger and 1 teaspoon of sesame oil for 15 minutes.
  2. Put the rice and the measured water in a cast-iron Dutch oven or heavy pot and bring to a boil uncovered over medium-high heat, about 5 minutes, until the water has dropped to the level of the rice and small steam holes appear.
  3. Spread the chicken, sausage and mushrooms over the rice, cover, turn the heat to low and cook 15 to 18 minutes without lifting the lid, until the rice is tender and the chicken is cooked through.
  4. Raise the heat to medium for the last 2 minutes, turning the pot a quarter turn every 30 seconds, until you hear a faint crackle; then take it off the heat and leave it covered for 5 minutes.
  5. Mix the remaining oyster sauce, light soy, dark soy and sesame oil, drizzle over the rice, scatter the scallion and serve from the pot, scraping up the crust.

Common mistakes

  • Lifting the lid while it cooks lets out the steam the rice needs and stops the crust forming.
  • Too much water leaves the rice mushy instead of crisp at the bottom.

Authentic tips

  • A clay pot is the traditional vessel on a gas flame; a cast-iron Dutch oven holds heat as evenly and works on gas, electric and induction.
  • Add the sauce at the end, off the heat, so it stays bright rather than cooked out.

Substitutions

  • No Chinese sausage: thick-cut bacon lardons are a reasonable, less traditional substitute.
  • Dried shiitake, soaked and sliced, give a deeper flavor than fresh; use the soaking water as part of the measured water.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat covered with a splash of water to loosen the rice.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably for a month, though the crust softens.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Stir-fried gai lan with garlic
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
