Shoyu Ramen (Soy Sauce Ramen) 醤油ラーメン

▾

Surprise me with

Shoyu ramen, noodles in a clear chicken and soy broth, is the oldest style of Japanese ramen, served in Tokyo's Asakusa from 1910, and remains the Tokyo standard.

Method

  1. Make the chashu: sear the pork belly in a pot, then add 60 ml of the soy sauce, the mirin, sake, sugar and 250 ml water, cover and simmer 25 minutes, turning, until tender. Slice thinly and keep the liquid as the tare.
  2. Simmer the chicken stock and dashi with the ginger, garlic and green onion whites for 15 minutes, then strain.
  3. Put 2 tablespoons of the chashu liquid, 2 teaspoons of the remaining soy sauce and 2 teaspoons of chicken fat into each warmed bowl.
  4. Boil the noodles according to the packet, usually 1.5 to 2 minutes, and drain very well. Ladle the hot broth into the bowls, stir, and add the noodles.
  5. Top with chashu, a halved egg, menma, nori and green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the broth hard makes it cloudy; shoyu ramen should be clear.
  • Undrained noodles water down the soup.

Authentic tips

  • Warm the bowls with boiling water first; ramen cools fast.
  • Marinate the soft-boiled eggs in leftover chashu liquid overnight.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought roast pork or chicken thigh replace homemade chashu.
  • Dried noodles work; cook 1 minute less than the packet so they stay firm.

Storage

Leftovers: Store broth, chashu and eggs separately for 3 days; cook noodles fresh.

Freezing: The broth and chashu freeze for 3 months.
