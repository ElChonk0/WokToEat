Jiāng Cōng Chǎo Xiè (Ginger-Scallion Crab) 姜葱炒蟹

▾

Wok-fried crab with ginger and scallion is a Cantonese banquet dish whose generous ginger offsets the richness of the crab.

  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil
  • 45 g1.5 ozGinger, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozGreen onion, cut into 5 cm (2 in) lengths, whites and greens separated
  • 3 cloves3 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspOyster sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 ml1 tspDark soy sauce
  • 2 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspGround white pepper
  • 240 ml1 cupWater
  • 3 g1 tspCornstarch, for the slurry
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Put the live crab in the freezer for 30 minutes so it goes still, then scrub it, remove the top shell and gills and cut the body into pieces. Crack the claws and leg shells with the back of a cleaver so the sauce gets in. Use raw crab; precooked crab overcooks.
  2. Mix the oyster sauce, soy sauces, sugar, pepper, wine and water. Pat the crab dry and coat the cut, meaty edges of the body and claws in cornstarch, which seals in the juices.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat with the oil. Fry the body and claw pieces cut-side down for 1 minute, lower the heat, turn them and cook 1 minute more until the shells turn red. Drain, leaving about 1 tablespoon of oil in the wok.
  4. Fry the ginger, garlic and green onion whites over high heat for 20 seconds, then the legs for 30 to 40 seconds. Return the crab and the top shell and pour in the sauce.
  5. Cover and cook over high heat for 3 minutes, until the crab is cooked through and the sauce is bubbling.
  6. Lower the heat, stir in the slurry cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon of water until the sauce is glossy, add the green onion greens and turn off the heat. Finish with the sesame oil and serve with plenty of napkins.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking dries out the sweet meat; 4 minutes covered is enough for a 1 kg crab.
  • Skipping the starch lets the juices leak out and the sauce will not cling.

Authentic tips

  • Cut the ginger into thick coins; plenty of it is the point.
  • Crack the claws first so they absorb the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or lobster tails can replace the crab.
  • Toss cooked thick rice noodles in the leftover sauce for a second dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; leftover crab keeps 1 day and the meat can be picked into fried rice.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or e-fu noodles
  • Soup: Choy sum
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
