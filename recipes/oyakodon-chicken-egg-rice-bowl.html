Oyakodon (Chicken and Egg Rice Bowl) 親子丼

▾

Surprise me with

“Oyakodon” literally means “parent-and-child rice bowl,” referring to chicken and egg served together over rice — a fast, comforting Japanese home meal.

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the sake. Spread the onion in a wide skillet (cook two servings at a time) and pour in the dashi, soy sauce, mirin and sugar so the onion is just covered.
  2. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, add the chicken and simmer uncovered for about 5 minutes, turning once, until it is cooked through and the onion is soft.
  3. Crack the eggs into a bowl and cut through them lightly with chopsticks, leaving streaks of white and yolk; well-beaten eggs set flat and lose oyakodon's silky texture.
  4. With the broth gently simmering, pour two-thirds of the egg in a circle over the chicken. When the edges set, after about 30 seconds, pour over the rest with the mitsuba.
  5. Cover and cook for 30 seconds to 1 minute, until the egg is just set but still glossy and soft. Slide it over bowls of hot rice with the broth and sprinkle with shichimi.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the egg into a firm omelet; it should be soft and glossy and finishes setting off the heat.
  • Adding all the egg at once, which makes an even, silky set harder.

Authentic tips

  • Small single-portion pans make sliding each serving onto its rice much easier, as Japanese diners do.
  • Mitsuba (Japanese parsley) is the traditional garnish.

Substitutions

  • No dashi: chicken stock with a few dried shiitake steeped in it approximates the umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps a day refrigerated, though the egg firms on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended; the egg texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
