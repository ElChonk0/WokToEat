He Tao Xia (Honey Walnut Shrimp) 合桃虾

Crisp shrimp in a sweet, creamy mayonnaise sauce with candied walnuts is a Hong Kong banquet creation of the 1980s and 1990s that became a Chinese American restaurant favorite.

  • Prep 25 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 500 g1.1 lbLarge shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 11Egg white
  • 50 g1/2 cupCornstarch
  • 100 g1 cupWalnut halves
  • 50 g1/4 cupSugar, for the walnuts
  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater, for the walnuts
  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 80 ml1/3 cupMayonnaise
  • 30 ml2 tbspHoney
  • 15 ml1 tbspSweetened condensed milk
  • 5 ml1 tspLemon juice

Method

  1. Simmer the sugar and water in a small pan, add the walnuts and stir 2 to 3 minutes until coated and syrupy. Spread on parchment to cool.
  2. Season the shrimp with salt, mix with the egg white and toss in the cornstarch.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 175 C (350 F). Fry the walnuts for 1 minute until glassy, lift out, then fry the shrimp in batches for 2 to 3 minutes until crisp. Drain.
  4. Whisk the mayonnaise, honey, condensed milk and lemon juice in a large bowl.
  5. Toss the hot shrimp in the sauce, plate, and scatter the candied walnuts on top.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Saucing the shrimp in a hot pan makes the mayonnaise split; toss in a bowl.
  • Under-coated walnuts burn in the oil; keep them moving.

Authentic tips

  • Serve on a bed of blanched broccoli, as restaurants do.
  • Japanese mayonnaise gives a richer sauce.

Substitutions

  • Pecans replace walnuts.
  • Air fryer: spray the coated shrimp and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 8 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed broccoli and rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
