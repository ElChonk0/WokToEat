Gamja-jeon (Korean Potato Pancakes) 감자전

▾

Gamja-jeon, crisp pancakes of grated potato bound by their own starch, are a specialty of potato-growing Gangwon Province, served with soy dipping sauce and makgeolli.

Method

  1. Grate the potatoes finely into a bowl, catching all the liquid. Tip them into a sieve set over another bowl and press out the liquid, then leave that liquid to stand for about 10 minutes until the starch settles in a white layer.
  2. Carefully pour off the water, keeping the starch at the bottom. Mix the starch back into the grated potato with the salt, green onion and chili. That starch is what binds the pancake and gives it a chewy center with no flour at all.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a nonstick skillet over medium heat. Spoon in a quarter of the batter and spread it into a thin round about 1 cm (1/2 in) thick.
  4. Lower the heat to medium-low and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the bottom is golden and crisp, then flip, add a little more oil around the edge and cook 2 to 3 minutes more. Repeat with the rest.
  5. Mix the soy sauce, vinegar and gochugaru for dipping and serve the pancakes hot. Grated potato darkens quickly, so cook the batter soon after mixing.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Throwing away the settled starch makes the pancakes fall apart.
  • Too high a heat browns the outside before the potato cooks through.

Authentic tips

  • Waxy-starchy all-purpose potatoes give the best chewy-crisp texture.
  • Grate just before cooking; the potato discolors as it stands.

Substitutions

  • Add a little grated onion or zucchini for flavor.
  • A spoon of potato starch rescues a mixture that is too wet.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheat leftovers in a dry skillet.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month after cooking.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi
  • Soup: Kongnamul guk (bean sprout soup)
  • Dessert: Steamed sweet potatoes
  • Beverage: Makgeolli
