Hong Kong-Style Steamed Whole Fish

▾

Surprise me with

Steamed whole fish is a centerpiece of Cantonese banquets and Sunday family dinners alike, prized because steaming preserves the fish's natural sweetness better than almost any other method. Serving a whole fish also carries a symbolic wish for abundance, since "yu" (fish) sounds like the word for "abundance" in Chinese.

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry and score it lightly a couple of times on each side; place on a heatproof plate that fits inside your steamer.
  2. Tuck about a third of the ginger and green onion inside the fish's cavity and scatter the rest underneath where the fish will sit.
  3. Bring water to a rolling boil in a wok or large pot fitted with a steamer rack; the water should already be boiling before the fish goes in.
  4. Steam the fish, covered, about 8–10 minutes per inch of thickness at the thickest part, until the flesh turns opaque and flakes easily.
  5. While the fish steams, warm the soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, and white pepper in a small pot just until the sugar dissolves; keep warm.
  6. Remove the fish from the steamer and carefully pour off any liquid that has collected on the plate — it tastes overcooked and slightly bitter, so discard it.
  7. Scatter the remaining raw ginger and green onion over the hot fish.
  8. Heat the 2 tbsp oil in a small pan until just smoking, then pour it directly over the ginger and green onion — this is what releases their aroma in seconds.
  9. Spoon the warm soy sauce mixture over the fish, garnish with cilantro, and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Starting the fish in cold or lukewarm steamer water, which overcooks the outside before the inside is done.
  • Leaving the liquid that collects under the fish on the plate — it carries a strong, slightly bitter taste and should always be discarded.

Authentic tips

  • A fish that's truly fresh needs almost nothing else — resist the urge to add more sauce or spice than this recipe calls for.
  • Choose a fish no thicker than about 4 cm (1.5 in) at the thickest point so it cooks through evenly without drying out the tail end.

Substitutions

  • No whole fish available? Use two fish fillets (basa, tilapia) and reduce steaming time to 6–8 minutes.
  • Light soy sauce can be swapped for low-sodium soy sauce with no change to the method.

Storage

Leftovers: Flake any leftover fish and stir into the next day's congee or fried rice.

Freezing: Best eaten fresh; the texture suffers after freezing once cooked. Freeze the raw fish, not the finished dish, if needed.
