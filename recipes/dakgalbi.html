Dakgalbi 닭갈비

▾

Surprise me with

Originating in Chuncheon in the 1960s, dakgalbi is a communal stir-fry of gochujang-marinated chicken, cabbage, and rice cakes cooked together on a large hot pan at the table — a beloved late-night and group dining dish.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 30 min marinating)
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Combine gochujang, soy sauce, sugar, garlic, and ginger; coat chicken and marinate at least 30 minutes.
  2. Heat a wide pan or skillet and add the marinated chicken; cook over medium-high heat 5 minutes.
  3. Add sweet potato and cabbage, stirring occasionally, and cook 5 minutes more.
  4. Add rice cakes and green onion; continue cooking, stirring, until the chicken is cooked through and rice cakes are soft, 4–5 minutes.
  5. Drizzle with sesame oil just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams rather than sears the ingredients and dulls the char flavor.
  • Adding rice cakes too early, which can make them gummy before the rest of the dish is ready.

Authentic tips

  • A traditional flat, wide pan (or a large skillet) mimics the communal cooking style this dish is known for.
  • Toasting a little extra gochujang directly in the pan before adding vegetables deepens the flavor further.

Substitutions

  • No rice cakes: thick udon noodles make a good substitute for the chewy, starchy component.
  • Add shredded cheese on top at the end for a popular modern variation (dakgalbi with cheese).

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; excellent turned into fried rice the next day.

Freezing: Not recommended — the vegetables and rice cakes don't hold texture well.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, lettuce wraps and kimchi; finish with fried rice in the pan
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer or barley tea
