Dakgalbi 닭갈비

▾

Surprise me with

Originating in Chuncheon in the 1960s, dakgalbi is a communal stir-fry of gochujang-marinated chicken, cabbage, and rice cakes cooked together on a large hot pan at the table — a beloved late-night and group dining dish.

Method

  1. Soak the rice cakes in cold water for 20 minutes so they soften evenly. Mix the gochugaru, gochujang, soy sauce, rice wine, sugar, syrup, curry powder, garlic, ginger and sesame oil into a sauce, keeping back a spoonful if you plan to make fried rice afterwards.
  2. Toss the chicken in the sauce and leave it for 30 minutes while you cut the vegetables. Slice the sweet potato thin so it cooks in the same time as the chicken.
  3. Heat a large cast-iron skillet or flat pan over medium-high heat with the oil. Add the chicken first and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes, so it gets a head start and cooks through.
  4. Add the sweet potato, rice cakes and cabbage and keep stirring for about 5 minutes, scraping the bottom. Lower the heat if it sticks, or add a splash of water.
  5. Add the perilla leaves and green onion and cook for 3 to 4 minutes more, until the rice cakes and sweet potato are soft and the chicken is cooked through. Serve straight from the pan.
  6. For the traditional finish, add a bowl of cooked rice, the reserved sauce, a drizzle of sesame oil and torn seaweed to the leftover sauce in the pan, and fry over medium heat for about 5 minutes, scraping up the browned bits.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams rather than sears the ingredients and dulls the char flavor.
  • Adding rice cakes too early, which can make them gummy before the rest of the dish is ready.

Authentic tips

  • A traditional flat, wide pan (or a large skillet) mimics the communal cooking style this dish is known for.
  • Toasting a little extra gochujang directly in the pan before adding vegetables deepens the flavor further.

Substitutions

  • No rice cakes: thick udon noodles make a good substitute for the chewy, starchy component.
  • Add shredded cheese on top at the end for a popular modern variation (dakgalbi with cheese).

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; excellent turned into fried rice the next day.

Freezing: Not recommended — the vegetables and rice cakes don't hold texture well.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, lettuce wraps and kimchi; finish with fried rice in the pan
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer or barley tea
