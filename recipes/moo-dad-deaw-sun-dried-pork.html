Moo Dad Deaw (Thai Sun-Dried Pork) หมูแดดเดียว

▾

Surprise me with

Moo dad deaw, pork marinated and dried in the sun for a few hours before frying, is a northeastern Thai (Isaan) specialty.

Method

  1. Mix the pounded garlic and cilantro root with the pepper, soy sauce, oyster sauce, fish sauce, palm sugar and coriander. Coat the pork strips and marinate overnight in the refrigerator.
  2. Lay the strips on a rack and dry them in direct sun for 4 to 6 hours, turning once, until the surface is dry and tacky. Without strong sun, dry them in an oven at its lowest setting (about 60 C / 140 F) with the door ajar for 2 to 3 hours. Drying concentrates the flavor and gives the chewy texture.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) in a Dutch oven.
  4. Fry the pork in batches for 3 to 4 minutes, until dark brown and slightly crisp at the edges. The sugar darkens quickly, so watch it.
  5. Drain and serve hot with sticky rice and nam jim jaew. Moo dad deaw is a classic drinking snack.

Common mistakes

  • Over-drying makes it leathery.
  • Hot oil burns the sugar; fry in small batches.

Authentic tips

  • Pork shoulder with a little fat is best.
  • Serve with jaew dipping sauce.

Substitutions

  • An oven at 80 C (175 F) for 1 hour replaces sun-drying.
  • Pork neck gives the best texture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes raw marinated slices for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice and jaew dipping sauce
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
