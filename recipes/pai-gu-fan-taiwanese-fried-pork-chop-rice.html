Pái Gǔ Fàn (Taiwanese Fried Pork Chop Rice) 排骨飯

▾

Surprise me with

Pai gu fan, rice topped with a fried marinated pork chop, is a Taiwanese lunchbox and railway-station staple, popularized by the Taiwan Railways bento.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 12 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 45 ml3 tbspSoy sauce
  • 4 g1.5 tspFive-spice and white pepper
  • 90 g3/4 cupSweet potato starch
  • 500 g17 ozCooked rice
  • 100 g3.5 ozPickled cabbage
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil for frying

Method

  1. Pound the chops thin and marinate with the soy sauce, five-spice and white pepper for 1 hour.
  2. Coat in the starch and rest 5 minutes.
  3. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) and fry the chops 4-5 minutes per side until golden.
  4. Drain on a rack.
  5. Serve over rice with the pickled cabbage and a spoonful of braising sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking dries the pork.
  • Skipping the rest makes the coating fall off.

Authentic tips

  • Boneless chops are quicker to cook.
  • Add a braised egg and greens for a full bento.

Substitutions

  • Boneless chops are quicker to cook.
  • Air-fry at 200 C (400 F) for 12 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes cooked chops for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Braised egg and greens
  • Soup: Clear pork bone soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Winter melon tea
