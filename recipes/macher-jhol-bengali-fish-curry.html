Macher Jhol (Bengali Fish Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Macher jhol, a light turmeric and nigella fish stew, is the everyday Bengali fish curry, eaten with rice across West Bengal and Bangladesh.

Method

  1. Rub the fish with half the turmeric and half the salt and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Heat the mustard oil in a wide pan until it smokes, then lower the heat slightly; this removes its raw pungency. Fry the fish for 2 to 3 minutes per side until golden and firm. Lift it out. Fry the potatoes in the same oil for 5 minutes until golden.
  3. Add the nigella seeds, bay leaf and green chilies to the oil for 20 seconds, then the ginger paste, cumin, coriander, chili and remaining turmeric with a splash of water, and fry for 2 minutes. Add the tomato for 3 minutes.
  4. Add the water, remaining salt and sugar and the potatoes and simmer for 10 minutes until the potatoes are tender. Jhol is a light, thin curry, not a thick gravy.
  5. Slide in the fish and simmer for 4 to 5 minutes. Scatter with cilantro and serve with plenty of rice, as an everyday Bengali lunch.

Common mistakes

  • Overfrying hardens the fish.
  • Stirring breaks it.

Authentic tips

  • Mustard oil must be heated until it stops smelling raw.
  • Rohu is traditional.

Substitutions

  • Tilapia or catfish replace rohu.
  • Cauliflower is a classic addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.
