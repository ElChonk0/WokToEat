Gyu Tataki (Seared Beef Tataki) 牛たたき

▾

Surprise me with

Tataki, a technique of searing the outside and serving the inside rare, then slicing, is Japanese in origin and used for beef as well as bonito.

  • Prep 15 min (plus 1 hr chilling)
  • Cook 5 min

Method

  1. Bring the beef to room temperature for 30 minutes and pat it dry. Rub it all over with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat the oil in a cast-iron skillet over high heat until it smokes. Sear the beef for 1 to 2 minutes on each side, including the ends, until a deep brown crust forms but the inside stays rare. Tataki means 'pounded', from the old Tosa way of searing and then patting on the seasoning.
  3. Plunge the beef into ice water for 1 minute to stop the cooking, pat it dry, wrap it tightly and chill for at least 1 hour so it slices thinly and cleanly.
  4. Soak the sliced onion in cold water for 10 minutes to remove its sharpness, then drain well. Mix the ponzu with the soy sauce.
  5. Slice the beef against the grain as thinly as you can and fan it over the onion. Top with daikon, ginger, green onion and garlic chips and spoon the ponzu over just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • A cool pan cooks the meat gray all the way through.
  • Slicing with the grain leaves it chewy.

Authentic tips

  • Use very fresh, high-quality beef.
  • Chill briefly in the freezer to make slicing easy.

Substitutions

  • Sirloin or eye round work well.
  • Bottled ponzu replaces homemade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated, sliced and covered.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold sake
