Beef Sukiyaki すき焼き

▾

Surprise me with

A communal hot pot dish traditionally cooked tableside, sukiyaki simmers thinly sliced beef with vegetables and tofu in a sweet soy broth, classically dipped in raw beaten egg before eating.

Method

  1. Make the warishita: bring the sake and mirin to a boil for 30 seconds to cook off the alcohol, then add the soy sauce and sugar and stir until dissolved. Keep the dashi nearby to thin the pot as it reduces.
  2. Arrange the beef, vegetables, tofu and shirataki on a platter. Keep the shirataki away from the beef in the pot; its calcium makes meat tough.
  3. Heat a cast-iron skillet or shallow Dutch oven over medium heat on a tabletop burner or the stove and melt the beef fat. Add a few slices of beef with a sprinkle of sugar and a splash of warishita and cook for 30 to 60 seconds, just until browned. This first, Kansai-style bite is eaten right away.
  4. Pour in enough warishita to cover the bottom by 1 cm (1/2 in) and arrange the negi, cabbage, mushrooms, tofu and shirataki in sections. Simmer over medium heat for 5 to 7 minutes, until the vegetables soften.
  5. Add the beef a few slices at a time and cook for under a minute, until just pink, along with the shungiku. Overcooked beef turns tough.
  6. Dip each bite in beaten raw egg, which cools and softens the sweet, salty sauce. Top up with warishita and dashi as the pot reduces, and finish with udon or rice in the remaining broth.

Common mistakes

  • Adding all the beef at once, which overcrowds the pan and makes it hard to cook each slice properly.
  • Making the sauce too dilute, which loses the concentrated sweet-savory flavor sukiyaki is known for.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is traditionally cooked and eaten gradually at the table, adding ingredients in batches rather than all at once.
  • The raw egg dip is optional but traditional, cooling the hot beef slightly and adding a rich, silky coating.

Substitutions

  • No dashi: a light beef stock works as a substitute.
  • Shirataki noodles are a traditional addition if available.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the vegetables and tofu suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Udon in the last broth, raw egg for dipping
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Warm sake or sencha
