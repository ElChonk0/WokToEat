Beef Sukiyaki すき焼き

▾

Surprise me with

A communal hot pot dish traditionally cooked tableside, sukiyaki simmers thinly sliced beef with vegetables and tofu in a sweet soy broth, classically dipped in raw beaten egg before eating.

Method

  1. Combine soy sauce, mirin, sugar, and dashi to make the warishita sauce.
  2. Heat a wide, shallow pan or hot pot and add a little of the sauce; sear a portion of the beef slices briefly.
  3. Add napa cabbage, mushrooms, green onion, and tofu, along with more of the sauce, arranging in sections.
  4. Simmer, adding more sauce and beef gradually as ingredients are eaten, keeping the pot topped up.
  5. Serve with a small bowl of raw beaten egg for dipping, if desired.

Common mistakes

  • Adding all the beef at once, which overcrowds the pan and makes it hard to cook each slice properly.
  • Making the sauce too dilute, which loses the concentrated sweet-savory flavor sukiyaki is known for.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is traditionally cooked and eaten gradually at the table, adding ingredients in batches rather than all at once.
  • The raw egg dip is optional but traditional, cooling the hot beef slightly and adding a rich, silky coating.

Substitutions

  • No dashi: a light beef stock works as a substitute.
  • Shirataki noodles are a traditional addition if available.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the vegetables and tofu suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Udon in the last broth, raw egg for dipping
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Warm sake or sencha
