Qīng Tāng Niú Nǎn (Clear Broth Beef Brisket) 清汤牛腩

▾

Surprise me with

Beef brisket simmered in a clear, lightly spiced broth with daikon is a Hong Kong specialty, the paler sibling of soy-braised brisket, served with noodles or on its own.

Method

  1. Blanch the whole brisket for 10 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Wrap the spices and tangerine peel in a cloth bag. Put the brisket, ginger, spice bag and water in a large pot, bring to a boil and simmer gently, covered, for 2 hours.
  3. Lift out the brisket, let it cool 10 minutes, then cut into thick slices across the grain.
  4. Return the slices to the broth with the daikon and simmer 40 minutes until both are tender. Remove the spice bag and skim the fat.
  5. Season with salt and serve with green onion, a dish of chili sauce and, if you like, noodles.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard clouds the broth; it should barely bubble.
  • Slicing before simmering makes the meat dry; cook it whole first.

Authentic tips

  • Ask for brisket with some fat and tendon; that is what makes it silky.
  • Let it rest overnight; the fat sets and lifts off.

Substitutions

  • Beef shin or chuck work, though less silky.
  • Pressure cooker: 45 minutes on high, then add the daikon for 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Egg noodles or steamed rice
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot tea
