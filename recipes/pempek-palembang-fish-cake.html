Pempek (Palembang Fish Cake with Cuko Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

Pempek, chewy fish and tapioca cakes served in a dark sweet-sour vinegar sauce (cuko), is the signature dish of Palembang in South Sumatra.

Method

  1. Make the cuko first: simmer the palm sugar, water, tamarind, garlic, chilies and dried shrimp for 10 minutes until slightly syrupy, then add the vinegar and a pinch of salt. Strain and cool. The sweet, sour, spicy cuko is what pempek is eaten for.
  2. Mix the fish with the ice water, garlic, salt and sugar until sticky. Add the tapioca starch gradually, mixing lightly with your hands just until a soft, slightly sticky dough forms. Overworking makes pempek hard.
  3. Shape the dough into logs (lenjer) or into hollow cups, pour a beaten egg into each and pinch closed (kapal selam, the 'submarine').
  4. Boil the pempek in plenty of water for 10 to 15 minutes, until they float and are firm. Drain and cool.
  5. Slice the logs and fry them with the kapal selam in oil at 170 C (340 F) for 3 to 4 minutes until golden and crisp at the edges.
  6. Serve with cucumber and noodles, drowned in cuko.

Common mistakes

  • Too much starch makes them rubbery.
  • Over-boiling cracks them.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh wolf-herring is traditional; mackerel works.
  • Add a quail egg inside for kapal selam.

Substitutions

  • Mackerel replaces wolf herring.
  • Pan-fried or boiled both work.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-fry.

Freezing: Freezes boiled pempek for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cuko sauce and noodles
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
