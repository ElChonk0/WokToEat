Nán Rǔ Shāo Jī (Roast Chicken with Red Fermented Bean Curd) 南乳烧鸡

Red fermented bean curd (nam yu) is a classic Cantonese marinade for roast meats, giving a reddish skin and a savory, slightly floral depth.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 1 hr 10 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 1.6 kg3.5 lbWhole chicken
  • 3 cubes3 cubesFermented red bean curd (nam yu)
  • 15 ml1 tbspNam yu liquid from the jar
  • 4 cloves4 clovesGarlic, minced

  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 30 ml2 tbspHoney
  • 15 ml1 tbspHot water

Method

  1. Mash the bean curd with its liquid, garlic, ginger, wine, soy sauce, sugar and five-spice. Rub it all over and inside the chicken and refrigerate at least 4 hours or overnight.
  2. Heat the oven to 200 C (400 F). Set the chicken breast-side up on a rack over a foil-lined tray with a little water in it.
  3. Roast for 50 minutes, then brush with the honey thinned with the hot water.
  4. Roast 10 to 15 minutes more until the skin is deep mahogany and the thigh reaches 74 C (165 F). Cover loosely with foil if it darkens too fast.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, then chop into pieces and serve with the pan juices.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Brushing the honey on at the start burns it.
  • Skipping the water in the tray lets drippings smoke.

Authentic tips

  • Marinating overnight lets the bean curd flavor go right through the meat.
  • Spatchcocking the chicken cuts the roasting time to about 45 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thighs roast in 30 to 35 minutes.
  • No nam yu: use 2 tablespoons of hoisin plus a little extra soy.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; good cold or reheated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic broccoli
  • Soup: Bok choy and pork soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
