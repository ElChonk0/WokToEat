Gaa Lei Gai (Cantonese Curry Chicken with Potatoes) 咖喱鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Mild, coconut-enriched curry chicken with potatoes is a Hong Kong cha chaan teng and home staple, shaped by curry powders brought through British colonial and Southeast Asian trade.

  • Cook 40 min

Method

  1. Pat the chicken dry and mix it with the marinade oyster sauce, curry powder, salt and wine, then work in the cornstarch a little at a time until each piece is lightly coated. Keep the cut potatoes in cold water until needed so they do not brown, then drain and dry them well.
  2. Heat a Dutch oven over high heat with 1 tablespoon of oil. Fry the potato and carrot chunks for 2 to 3 minutes until the potato edges turn golden, then lift them out. They only need color now; they finish softening in the sauce.
  3. Add 1 tablespoon of oil and the chicken skin-side down. Sear over medium heat without moving for 2 to 3 minutes, add the last of the oil around the edge, turn and sear 2 minutes more. Add the garlic, ginger and onion chunks and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  4. Add the curry powder, wine, sugar and salt and stir for 30 seconds so the spices toast in the fat without burning. Return the potatoes and carrot and pour in the boiling water to just cover. Bring to a boil, scrape up anything stuck to the bottom and taste for salt.
  5. Cover and simmer over medium-low heat for 20 to 25 minutes, until a chopstick slides easily into a potato and the chicken is tender. Uncover, raise the heat and boil for 2 to 3 minutes to reduce the sauce a little.
  6. Add the onion strips and coconut milk and turn the heat to low. Mix the slurry cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water and stir it in gradually until the sauce coats a spoon; it should be thick enough to cling to rice. Finish with the sesame oil and green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding curry powder to a dry, very hot pot burns it; keep the heat at medium and stir constantly.
  • Boiling hard after the coconut milk goes in can make it split.

Authentic tips

  • Like many curries it tastes better the next day.
  • Hong Kong diners serve it over rice or with a crusty bun to mop up the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs: simmer 15 minutes.
  • Evaporated milk is a common Hong Kong substitute for coconut milk.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months, though the potatoes turn slightly grainy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Bok choy and pork soup
  • Dessert: Red bean ice
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
