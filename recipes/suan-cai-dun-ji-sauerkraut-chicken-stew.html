Suān Cài Dùn Jī (Sauerkraut Chicken Stew) 酸菜炖鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Suan cai, Chinese sauerkraut fermented in crocks, is the winter staple of Dongbei and is often stewed with meat for a tangy, warming dish.

  • 900 g2 lbChicken, bone-in, chopped
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and garlic, sliced
  • 300 g10 ozChinese sauerkraut (suan cai), rinsed, squeezed and shredded
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 1 L4 cupsHot water
  • 100 g3.5 ozGlass noodles, soaked
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper

Method

  1. Blanch the chicken for 3 minutes and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven and fry the ginger and garlic for 30 seconds, then the sauerkraut for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant and slightly dry. Frying the suan cai first brings out its sour depth.
  3. Add the chicken, Shaoxing wine and soy sauce and stir for 2 minutes.
  4. Pour in the hot water, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 30 to 35 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  5. Add the glass noodles for 5 minutes, season with salt and white pepper and serve. The tart, warming broth is classic Dongbei winter food.

Common mistakes

  • Over-salting: the sauerkraut is salty already.
  • Overcooking the noodles turns them mushy.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse the sauerkraut if it is very sour.
  • Serve in a pot at the table, with a dip of garlic and sesame sauce.

Substitutions

  • Rinsed European sauerkraut replaces suan cai; reduce the salt.
  • Pork belly can join the chicken for a richer stew.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the glass noodles.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or buns
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
