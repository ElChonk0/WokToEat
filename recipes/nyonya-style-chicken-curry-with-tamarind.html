Nyonya-Style Chicken Curry with Tamarind

▾

Surprise me with

Peranakan (Nyonya) cooking blends Chinese and Malay techniques; this tamarind-spiked chicken curry reflects that fusion, balancing coconut richness with a distinctive sour edge characteristic of Nyonya cuisine.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, candlenuts, soaked chilies, turmeric, galangal, lemongrass and belacan into a smooth paste with a little oil.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-low heat and fry the paste with the lime leaves for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring, until it darkens and the oil separates. Candlenuts thicken and enrich the sauce, a mark of Nyonya curries.
  3. Add the chicken and stir for 3 to 4 minutes until coated, then add the tamarind water. Bring to a boil and simmer, covered, for 15 minutes, adding the potatoes if using.
  4. Stir in the coconut milk, palm sugar and salt and simmer uncovered for 10 to 15 minutes, until the chicken and potatoes are tender and the gravy is thick.
  5. Taste: it should be tangy from the tamarind, rich and gently sweet. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the paste-frying step, which leaves the curry tasting flat and underdeveloped.
  • Using too little tamarind, which is essential to this curry's distinctive sour-savory Nyonya character.

Authentic tips

  • The balance of sour (tamarind) against rich (coconut milk) is the defining feature of Nyonya cooking — taste and adjust carefully.
  • This curry pairs particularly well with rice noodles as an alternative to plain rice.

Substitutions

  • Macadamia nuts replace candlenuts.
  • Boneless thighs shorten the cooking by 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
