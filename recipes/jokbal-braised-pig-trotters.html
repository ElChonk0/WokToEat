Jokbal (Braised Pig's Trotters) 족발

▾

Surprise me with

Jokbal, pig's trotters braised in soy sauce and rice wine and sliced, is a Korean drinking food that became popular in Seoul in the 1960s.

  • Cook 2.5 hr

Method

  1. Soak the trotters in cold water for 2 hours, changing the water once, to draw out blood. Scrape the skin with a knife and singe off any stray hairs over a burner or with a kitchen torch.
  2. Put the trotters in a pot of cold water with the doenjang, bring to a boil and boil for 10 minutes. Drain and rinse them well and clean the pot. This parboil removes scum and the strong porky smell.
  3. Return the trotters to the pot with the measured water, soy sauce, rice wine, sugar, syrup, coffee, onion, garlic, ginger, green onion, cinnamon, star anise and peppercorns. Bring to a boil over high heat.
  4. Lower the heat, cover and simmer for 1.5 to 2 hours, turning the trotters every 30 minutes so they color evenly, until the skin is glossy brown and a chopstick slides easily into the meat.
  5. Uncover and simmer for 15 to 20 minutes more, spooning the liquid over, until it has reduced and the skin is sticky and lacquered.
  6. Lift out the trotters and let them rest for 20 minutes; the gelatin firms up as they cool, which makes them easier to slice. Cut the meat and skin off the bone into slices 6 mm (1/4 in) thick and serve with salted shrimp, lettuce and kimchi.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanch leaves a muddy smell.
  • Boiling hard toughens the skin.

Authentic tips

  • Serve cold or warm with soju.
  • The liquid can be reused for the next batch.

Substitutions

  • Pork hock replaces trotters.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lettuce wraps with ssamjang and salted shrimp
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Soju
