Chicken Afritada

▾

Surprise me with

A Spanish-influenced Filipino stew, afritada braises chicken in tomato sauce with potatoes, carrots, and bell pepper — one of many Filipino dishes reflecting over three centuries of Spanish colonial influence on the cuisine.

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken in the soy sauce and calamansi for 20 minutes, then pat dry.
  2. Fry the potatoes and carrot in the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 4 to 5 minutes until lightly browned, then lift them out. Pre-frying keeps them from falling apart. Brown the chicken in the same oil for 5 minutes.
  3. Push the chicken aside and saute the garlic and onion for 3 minutes until soft.
  4. Add the tomato sauce, stock, bay leaf and fish sauce, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 20 minutes.
  5. Return the potatoes and carrot and simmer for 10 minutes, then add the bell peppers and peas for 5 minutes. Season with sugar and salt; it should be savory with a gentle tomato sweetness.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the bell pepper too early, which lets it turn mushy and lose its bright color.
  • Using plain water without fish sauce, which leaves the dish underseasoned compared to the traditional version.

Authentic tips

  • This stew is a great make-ahead dish, since the flavor deepens as it sits.
  • Fried potato and carrot pieces (browned in oil before adding to the stew) add extra depth if you have the time.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks add flavor; simmer 10 minutes longer.
  • Canned tomatoes plus a spoonful of ketchup replace tomato sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Leche flan
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
