Běijīng Jiàng Niú Ròu (Beijing Sauced Beef Shank) 北京酱牛肉

▾

Jiang niu rou, beef shank simmered in a soy-and-spice master sauce, is a Beijing deli staple, sliced and eaten cold with steamed buns.

Method

  1. Blanch the beef whole in boiling water for 10 minutes and rinse. Some Beijing cooks rub it with salt and leave it overnight first, which firms the texture.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry the sweet bean sauce for 2 minutes, stirring, until it darkens and smells toasty. Frying the jiang (sauce) is what gives jiang rou its name and deep flavor.
  3. Add the rock sugar, soy sauces, Shaoxing wine, spices, ginger, green onion and water, bring to a boil, and add the beef.
  4. Simmer gently, covered, for 1.5 to 2 hours, turning occasionally, until a chopstick goes in easily. Turn off the heat.
  5. Let the beef cool in the liquid for at least 4 hours, or overnight, so the sauce soaks into the center.
  6. Slice thinly against the grain and serve cold with crushed garlic, a little braising liquid and sesame oil. Keep the strained liquid (lao tang) in the freezer for the next batch.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the shank tough.
  • Slicing warm tears the meat.

Authentic tips

  • Leave the beef in the liquid overnight for the best flavor.
  • The liquid is a master stock to reuse.

Substitutions

  • Brisket replaces the shank.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 55 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Thin pancakes or steamed buns
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
