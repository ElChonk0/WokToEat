Dakdoritang (Spicy Braised Chicken) 닭도리탕

▾

Dakdoritang, chicken and potatoes braised in a gochugaru-and-gochujang sauce, is a Korean home classic.

Method

  1. Trim the excess fat and loose skin from the chicken. For a cleaner sauce, blanch the pieces in boiling water for 2 minutes, then drain and rinse. Bone-in thighs and drumsticks give the richest stew.
  2. Mix the gochugaru, gochujang, soy sauce, rice wine, sugar and pepper. Put the chicken, sauce and water in a Dutch oven and bring to a boil over high heat.
  3. Lower to medium, cover and simmer for 15 minutes so the chicken starts to absorb the sauce.
  4. Add the potatoes, carrot, onion, garlic and ginger, pushing them into the liquid. Cover and cook for 10 minutes, until the potatoes are nearly tender.
  5. Uncover and simmer for about 5 minutes more, spooning the sauce over, until the chicken is tender, the potatoes are soft and the sauce has thickened slightly and coats the pieces.
  6. Turn off the heat and stir in the green onion, chili, sesame oil and sesame seeds. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the potatoes too small makes them dissolve.
  • Skipping the blanching leaves scum and a greasy broth.

Authentic tips

  • The original name is dak-bokkeum-tang; avoid the older term.
  • Add sweet potato for sweetness.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thigh shortens the cooking by 10 minutes.
  • Sweet potato adds sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and kimchi
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
