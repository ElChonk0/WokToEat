Wantan Mee (Malaysian Wonton Noodles)

▾

Surprise me with

Wantan mee, springy egg noodles with char siu and wontons dressed in dark soy sauce, is a Malaysian Chinese breakfast and hawker staple, especially in Kuala Lumpur.

Method

  1. Mix the pork, shrimp, soy sauce, sesame oil and a pinch of pepper, chill for 30 minutes, then fold the wontons with a teaspoon of filling each, sealing the edges with water.
  2. Make garlic oil: cook the garlic in the oil over medium heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring, until pale gold, then lower the heat for 1 minute and take it off. Garlic browns fast at the end and turns bitter if it goes too far.
  3. In each serving bowl, mix 1 tablespoon of garlic oil with the oyster sauce, soy sauces and white pepper.
  4. Boil the wontons for 3 to 4 minutes until they float and lift them out. Blanch the choy sum for 30 to 40 seconds.
  5. Cook the noodles for about 40 seconds, loosening them, dip briefly in cold water and back into the hot water, then drain well. Toss them in the sauce until glossy.
  6. Top with the char siu, choy sum and wontons (or serve the wontons in a small bowl of soup), with pickled green chilies and fried shallots.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles lose their spring.
  • Not draining leaves the sauce watery.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse the noodles in cold water for extra springiness.
  • Serve with pickled green chilies.

Substitutions

  • Shop-bought wontons save time.
  • Chicken char siu is a lighter alternative.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and toppings separately; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked wontons for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled green chilies
  • Soup: Clear wonton broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Chinese tea
