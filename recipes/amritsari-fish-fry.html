Amritsari Fish Fry

▾

Surprise me with

Amritsari fish fry, fish in a spiced chickpea-flour batter, is a street-food classic of Amritsar in Punjab.

  • 700 g1.5 lbFirm white fish (sole, basa or cod), cut into fingers
  • 30 ml2 tbspLemon juice
  • 6 g1 tspSalt
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger-garlic paste
  • 6 g2 tspKashmiri chili powder

  • 3 g1 tspAjwain (carom seeds), crushed
  • 2 g1 tspGaram masala
  • 80 g2/3 cupGram flour (besan)
  • 20 g2 tbspRice flour
  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater
  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 5 g2 tspChaat masala
  • to tasteto tasteLemon wedges and mint chutney, for serving

Method

  1. Toss the fish with the lemon juice and half the salt and leave for 15 minutes, then drain and pat dry.
  2. Mix the ginger-garlic paste, chili, turmeric, ajwain, garam masala, gram flour, rice flour and remaining salt with the water into a thick batter that coats the back of a spoon. Ajwain is the signature flavor of Amritsari fish, and aids digestion of the fried batter.
  3. Coat the fish in the batter and leave for 20 minutes so it clings.
  4. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 170 C (340 F) and fry the fish in batches for 3 to 4 minutes until golden and crisp. For extra crunch, fry again at 190 C (375 F) for 30 seconds.
  5. Drain on a rack, sprinkle with chaat masala and serve hot with lemon and mint chutney. A Punjabi winter favorite from Amritsar.

Common mistakes

  • Thin batter doesn't stick.
  • Oil that's too cool makes it greasy.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with mint chutney.
  • Use a firm white fish.

Substitutions

  • Cod or tilapia replace the white fish.
  • Air-fry at 200 C (400 F) for a lighter version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Mint chutney and onion rings
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Lassi
  • Beverage: Cold beer
