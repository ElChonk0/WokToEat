Hēi Hú Jiāo Tiě Bǎn Niú (Iron-Plate Black Pepper Beef) 黑胡椒鐵板牛

▾

Sizzling iron-plate dishes with black pepper sauce are a Taiwanese restaurant staple, served with noodles and a fried egg.

  • Cook 12 min

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the soy sauce and baking soda and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Make the black pepper sauce: toast the crushed pepper in a dry saucepan over low heat for 1 minute until fragrant, then add half the butter, the garlic and a third of the onion and cook for 3 minutes. Add the stock, soy sauce, Worcestershire, ketchup and sugar, simmer for 5 minutes and thicken with the cornstarch slurry. It should be peppery, glossy and spoonable.
  3. Heat a cast-iron skillet or plate until very hot. Melt the remaining butter and sear the beef in one layer for 1 to 2 minutes per side until browned but still pink. Lift it out.
  4. Cook the remaining onion and the bell pepper in the same pan for 2 minutes until just softened.
  5. Serve the Taiwanese night-market way: on the sizzling skillet, crack an egg at the edge, pile on the noodles or rice, vegetables and beef, and pour the black pepper sauce over at the table so it hisses.

Common mistakes

  • A cool pan steams the beef.
  • Overcooking makes the beef tough.

Authentic tips

  • Black pepper should be coarsely cracked.
  • Serve with noodles and a fried egg.

Substitutions

  • Ribeye or strip steak replaces sirloin.
  • Serve on a cast-iron skillet if no iron plate.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Noodles with a fried egg
  • Soup: Corn soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Black tea
