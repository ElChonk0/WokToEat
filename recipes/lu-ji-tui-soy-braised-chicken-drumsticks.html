Lǔ Jī Tuǐ (Taiwanese Braised Chicken Drumsticks) 滷雞腿

▾

Surprise me with

Lu wei, braising in a soy-based spiced stock, is a defining Taiwanese method; braised chicken drumsticks are a staple of lunchboxes and bento shops.

  • Cook 45 min

  • 44Hard-boiled eggs (optional)

Method

  1. Blanch the drumsticks in boiling water for 3 minutes, then drain and rinse off the scum. This keeps the braising liquid clean.
  2. Heat a little oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the garlic, ginger and green onion for 2 minutes until fragrant. Add the rock sugar and stir until it melts and turns amber; caramelizing the sugar gives the deep color and gloss.
  3. Add the drumsticks and toss to coat, then pour in the rice wine and soy sauce and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Add the water and spices, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 30 to 35 minutes, turning occasionally, until the meat is tender and pulling from the bone.
  5. Add the eggs for the last 20 minutes. Turn off the heat and let everything soak in the liquid for 30 minutes so the color and flavor deepen.
  6. Serve with rice, or reduce some liquid to a glaze and spoon it over.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the skin split.
  • Skipping the steep leaves the meat less flavorful.

Authentic tips

  • Add boiled eggs and tofu to the braise.
  • Strain and refrigerate the liquid as a master stock.

Substitutions

  • Whole bone-in thighs work with the same timing.
  • Hard-boiled eggs and tofu cooked in the braise join the plate.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickled greens
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
