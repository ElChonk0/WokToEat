Larb Moo (Spicy Pork Salad) ลาบหมู

▾

Surprise me with

A signature dish of Thailand's Isaan region, larb tosses minced meat with fish sauce, lime, chili, and toasted rice powder for a dish that's simultaneously spicy, sour, and herbaceous — often served with sticky rice.

Method

  1. Toast the sticky rice in a dry skillet over medium-low heat for 10 to 15 minutes, shaking often, until deep golden, then grind it coarsely.
  2. Cook the pork with the water in a skillet over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes, breaking it up finely, until just cooked through. Cooking it in water rather than oil keeps it tender and stops it browning.
  3. Take it off the heat and season with fish sauce, lime juice, chili flakes and sugar. Taste and adjust: it should be sour, salty and spicy.
  4. Just before serving, toss in the shallots, green onion, mint, cilantro and most of the rice powder; the herbs wilt if added too early.
  5. Sprinkle with the remaining rice powder and serve warm or at room temperature with raw vegetables and sticky rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the herbs too early, which wilts them and dulls their bright, fresh flavor.
  • Skipping the toasted rice powder, which provides essential texture and a distinctive nutty flavor central to larb.

Authentic tips

  • Toasted rice powder is simple to make at home: toast raw sticky rice in a dry pan until deep golden, then grind fine.
  • This dish should taste assertively balanced between sour, salty, and spicy — taste and adjust before serving.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken or beef both work well with the same method.
  • No sticky rice for the powder: any raw rice, toasted and ground, works similarly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the herbs lose some vibrancy.

Freezing: Not recommended — fresh herbs don't hold up.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice and raw cabbage
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Singha beer
