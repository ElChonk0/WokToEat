Chicken Bicol Express–Style

▾

Surprise me with

Named after the Bicol Express train, this dish captures the fiery cuisine of the Bicol region, known for its heavy use of coconut milk and chili — traditionally made with pork, adapted here for chicken.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat and saute the garlic, onion and ginger for 3 minutes.
  2. Add the chicken and cook for 5 minutes until lightly browned, then stir in the shrimp paste and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Pour in the coconut milk, bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 15 minutes, stirring often, until the chicken is tender and the sauce reduces.
  4. Add the chilies and coconut cream and simmer for 5 to 10 minutes more, until the sauce is thick and the oil starts to separate. Bicol Express is named after the train to the Bicol region, famed for its chilies and coconut.
  5. Season with fish sauce and serve with plenty of rice.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little shrimp paste out of caution — it's the dish's defining savory backbone, not just a minor seasoning.
  • Boiling the coconut milk too hard, which can cause it to separate.

Authentic tips

  • Adding chilies in two stages (early and late) builds layered heat rather than a single flat spiciness.
  • This dish is traditionally quite spicy; adjust chili quantity to taste, but a little heat is central to its character.

Substitutions

  • Dried shrimp plus a little fish sauce replace bagoong.
  • Pork is the traditional meat.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the coconut milk may separate when reheated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh mango
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
