Bei Jing Kao Ya (Beijing Roast Duck) 北京烤鸭

Beijing roast duck is a dish of air-dried, honey-glazed duck roasted until the skin shatters, carved at the table and eaten in thin pancakes with scallion, cucumber and sweet bean sauce.

  • Cook 1 hr 20 min

Method

  1. Pat the duck dry, rub the cavity with the salt and five-spice, and stuff it with the ginger and the 40 g green onion.
  2. Scald the skin with the boiling water until it tightens. Dissolve the maltose and vinegar in the hot water and brush it all over the skin.
  3. Dry the duck uncovered on a rack in the refrigerator for 24 hours, until the skin feels like paper.
  4. Roast the duck breast-side up on a rack at 200 C (400 F) for 30 minutes, turn it breast-side down for 20 minutes, then breast-side up again for 20 to 30 minutes until the skin is a deep golden brown and the thigh reaches 74 C (165 F).
  5. Rest for 15 minutes, then carve off the crisp skin and slice the meat thinly.
  6. Steam the pancakes for 3 minutes until soft, and serve with the duck, sweet bean sauce, scallion strips and cucumber for people to wrap.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the 24-hour drying leaves soft skin; the drying is the most important step.
  • Roasting the duck in a pan without a rack leaves the skin greasy.

Authentic tips

  • Pierce the skin with a skewer in a few places without cutting the meat to let the fat drain.
  • Save the duck fat for roasting vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Flour tortillas replace Mandarin pancakes.
  • Hoisin plus a pinch of sugar replaces sweet bean sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; crisp the skin in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the pancakes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: The pancakes and vegetables are the side
  • Soup: Duck bone soup with napa cabbage
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
