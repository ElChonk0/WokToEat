Běi Jīng Kǎo Yā (Beijing Roast Duck) 北京烤鸭

▾

Surprise me with

Beijing roast duck is a dish of air-dried, honey-glazed duck roasted until the skin shatters, carved at the table and eaten in thin pancakes with scallion, cucumber and sweet bean sauce.

  • Prep 1 hr (plus 24 to 48 hr drying)
  • Cook 1.5 hr

Method

  1. Trim excess fat from the cavity opening and cut off the wing tips. If you have a pump or a straw, loosen the skin from the meat by blowing air between them at the neck; this separation is what lets the skin crisp like glass. Otherwise, gently work your fingers under the breast skin.
  2. Rub the cavity with the salt and five-spice and stuff it with the ginger, green onion and star anise. Close the opening with a skewer.
  3. Set the duck on a rack in the sink and pour the boiling water slowly over every part of the skin, turning it, until the skin tightens and turns pale. Scalding shrinks the pores so the skin can dry and crisp.
  4. Dissolve the maltose in the hot water with the vinegar and brush it all over the skin in an even coat. Let it dry for 30 minutes, then brush a second coat.
  5. Hang the duck by the neck, or set it upright on a can or rack, in front of a fan or in the refrigerator, uncovered, for 24 to 48 hours, until the skin feels dry and papery like parchment.
  6. Heat the oven to 200 C (400 F). Put the duck breast-side up on a rack over a roasting pan half-filled with water, which catches the fat and keeps it from smoking.
  7. Roast for 30 minutes, then lower to 180 C (350 F) and roast for 45 to 50 minutes more, until the skin is deep mahogany and the thigh reaches 75 C (165 F). Turn the duck once if one side darkens faster, and tent with foil if it colors too quickly.
  8. Meanwhile, fry the sweet bean sauce with the sugar, sesame oil and a splash of water over low heat for 2 to 3 minutes until glossy. Steam the pancakes for 5 minutes until soft and warm.
  9. Rest the duck for 10 minutes, then carve the crisp skin first into small pieces, as Beijing chefs do, and slice the meat with some skin attached.
  10. To eat, spread a pancake with sauce, add a few slices of duck and skin, green onion and cucumber, and roll it up. Simmer the carcass into a soup to finish the meal, as at Quanjude.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the 24-hour drying leaves soft skin; the drying is the most important step.
  • Roasting the duck in a pan without a rack leaves the skin greasy.

Authentic tips

  • Pierce the skin with a skewer in a few places without cutting the meat to let the fat drain.
  • Save the duck fat for roasting vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Flour tortillas replace Mandarin pancakes.
  • Hoisin plus a pinch of sugar replaces sweet bean sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; crisp the skin in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the pancakes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: The pancakes and vegetables are the side
  • Soup: Duck bone soup with napa cabbage
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
