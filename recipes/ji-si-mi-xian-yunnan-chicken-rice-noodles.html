Jī Sī Mǐ Xiàn (Yunnan Chicken Rice Noodles) 鸡丝米线

▾

Surprise me with

Rice noodles, mi xian, are Yunnan's staple, eaten in countless forms; a bowl in clear chicken broth is a classic breakfast.

  • Cook 30 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozRice noodles, fresh
  • 400 g14 ozChicken thighs, skinless
  • 1.2 L5 cupsChicken stock
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 60 g2 ozPickled greens
  • 15 ml1 tbspChili oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt

Method

  1. Poach the chicken in the stock with the ginger for 18 minutes until just cooked; lift out and shred when cool enough to handle.
  2. Bring the stock back to a simmer and season with the soy sauce and salt.
  3. Blanch the noodles in boiling water for 30 seconds if fresh (2-3 minutes if dried) and divide among bowls.
  4. Top with the chicken, pickled greens and scallion.
  5. Ladle the boiling stock over and finish with chili oil.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked rice noodles turn to mush; blanch just until loosened.
  • Lukewarm stock gives a flat bowl; it should be piping hot.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh rice noodles are sold in Chinese markets; dried work with longer cooking.
  • Add a spoonful of the local pickled chili at the table.

Substitutions

  • Fresh rice vermicelli replaces mi xian.
  • Chicken breast poached gently works.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
