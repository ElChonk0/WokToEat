Pepes Ikan (Fish Steamed in Banana Leaf)

▾

Surprise me with

Pepes, spiced fish wrapped in banana leaf and steamed or grilled, is a Sundanese (West Java) specialty.

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the lime juice and a pinch of salt and leave for 10 minutes, then pat dry. This freshens it and firms the flesh.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, turmeric, galangal, ginger and candlenuts into a paste and stir in the salt, sugar and oil. Raw paste is traditional for pepes; it cooks with the fish.
  3. Coat the fish thoroughly in the paste.
  4. Lay each piece on a softened banana leaf with a bay leaf, lemongrass, tomato and basil, fold the leaf into a parcel and pin the ends with toothpicks.
  5. Steam over boiling water for 25 to 30 minutes, until the fish is cooked and the paste is set.
  6. For a smoky finish, grill the parcels over charcoal or in a dry skillet for 3 to 4 minutes per side until the leaves char. Unwrap at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Unsoftened leaves tear.
  • Overcooking dries the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Foil works if you can't find leaves.
  • Serve with rice and raw vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Foil replaces banana leaves, with less aroma.
  • Tilapia or mackerel both work.
  • No grill: after steaming, broil the packets 2 to 3 minutes per side, or bake at 200°C (400°F) for 10 minutes, for char and smoke.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and raw vegetables
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
