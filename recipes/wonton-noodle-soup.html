Wonton Noodle Soup 云吞面

▾

Surprise me with

A Hong Kong institution, wonton noodle soup pairs delicate shrimp-and-pork wontons with springy thin egg noodles in a light broth, traditionally seasoned simply enough to let the wontons and noodles carry the dish.

Method

  1. Rinse the chopped pork, massage it briefly, drain and press it firmly against a sieve to squeeze out the water. Toss the shrimp with their cornstarch and a pinch of pepper. Chop the pork into small bits rather than a paste, so the filling has some bite.
  2. Mix the filling cornstarch, salt, sugar, pepper, oyster sauce and water into a smooth paste, add the pork and stir for 30 to 45 seconds, then add the sesame oil. Lay the shrimp and green onion on top without mixing, cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes. Stir with a wet spoon just before folding.
  3. Fold the wontons with about 1 teaspoon of filling each: dab water on two edges, fold into a triangle and press out the air, then bring the two long corners together and pinch them. Seal well, or they leak in the pot. Keep them covered so the wrappers do not dry.
  4. Bring the stock, broth water, oil and salt to a boil, add the bok choy, cover and cook for 1 to 2 minutes until just tender, then lift it out. Season the broth with the fish sauce, sesame oil and white pepper and keep it hot.
  5. Boil the noodles in a large pot of water for about 1 minute, spreading them with chopsticks. Lift them into a bowl of cold water for a few seconds, then back into the boiling water for 15 to 20 seconds. That quick cold dip is what gives wonton noodles their springy bite. Drain, toss with the noodle oil and divide among bowls.
  6. Boil the wontons in the same pot over high heat, stirring so they do not stick, then lower to medium once the boil returns. Cook for 4 to 5 minutes in all, until they float and the filling is firm.
  7. Top the noodles with the bok choy and wontons, pour the hot broth over and scatter with chives. Traditionally the noodles sit on top of the wontons so they do not soften in the broth too quickly.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling the wontons, which makes them difficult to seal and prone to bursting during boiling.
  • Overcooking the thin egg noodles, which turns them mushy — they need only a minute or two.

Authentic tips

  • Boiling the wontons separately from the noodles, then combining in the serving bowl, keeps the broth clear rather than starchy.
  • A light hand with seasoning in the broth is traditional — the wontons themselves carry most of the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken can replace pork in the filling for a lighter version.
  • No fresh wonton wrappers: thin dumpling wrappers work as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store noodles, wontons, and broth separately.

Freezing: Uncooked wontons freeze excellently for up to 2 months; cook directly from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched choy sum with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot tea
