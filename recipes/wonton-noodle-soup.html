Wonton Noodle Soup 云吞面

A Hong Kong institution, wonton noodle soup pairs delicate shrimp-and-pork wontons with springy thin egg noodles in a light broth, traditionally seasoned simply enough to let the wontons and noodles carry the dish.

  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Combine ground pork, shrimp, green onion, ginger, soy sauce, and sesame oil; mix until sticky.
  2. Place a small spoonful of filling in the center of each wonton wrapper, wet the edges, and fold into a pouch, sealing well.
  3. Bring stock to a simmer in a pot.
  4. Boil wontons in a separate pot of water until they float and are cooked through, 4–5 minutes; drain.
  5. Cook egg noodles briefly in boiling water, just until tender, 1–2 minutes; drain.
  6. Divide noodles and wontons among bowls, ladle hot stock over top, and garnish with chives.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling the wontons, which makes them difficult to seal and prone to bursting during boiling.
  • Overcooking the thin egg noodles, which turns them mushy — they need only a minute or two.

Authentic tips

  • Boiling the wontons separately from the noodles, then combining in the serving bowl, keeps the broth clear rather than starchy.
  • A light hand with seasoning in the broth is traditional — the wontons themselves carry most of the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken can replace pork in the filling for a lighter version.
  • No fresh wonton wrappers: thin dumpling wrappers work as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store noodles, wontons, and broth separately.

Freezing: Uncooked wontons freeze excellently for up to 2 months; cook directly from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched choy sum with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hot tea
