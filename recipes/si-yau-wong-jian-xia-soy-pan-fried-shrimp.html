Si Yau Wong Jian Xia (Soy-Glazed Pan-Fried Shrimp) 豉油皇煎虾

▾

Surprise me with

Shell-on shrimp pan-fried until crisp and glazed with a sweet soy and garlic sauce is a Cantonese home and banquet classic; the shells are part of the pleasure.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 10 min

Method

  1. Snip off the legs and feelers, cut along the back of the shell with scissors and pull out the vein, leaving the shell on. Pat the shrimp dry and toss with the garlic salt, then the cornstarch, so the shells crisp. Mix the soy sauces, sugar and water in a cup.
  2. Heat a large skillet or flat-bottom wok over high heat, then lower to medium and add 2 tablespoons of oil. Lay the shrimp in a single layer and leave them alone, adding the last tablespoon of oil around the edge. Fry about 2 minutes until the undersides are red and crisp, then turn each one and fry about 2 minutes more.
  3. Push the shrimp aside and fry the garlic, ginger and shallot in the space over low heat for 20 to 30 seconds until golden; take care, as garlic turns bitter if it browns too far.
  4. Splash the wine around the edge, pour in the sauce and raise the heat. Toss for 30 to 60 seconds until the sauce bubbles down to a sticky glaze on the shells.
  5. Scatter with green onion and serve hot. The shells are crisp enough to eat, or peel them at the table and suck the sauce off.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet shrimp steam instead of crisping.
  • Overcooking makes the meat stick to the shell.

Authentic tips

  • Cutting the shell lets the sauce reach the meat.
  • Eat the shells if they are crisp; that is how it is enjoyed in Hong Kong.

Substitutions

  • Peeled shrimp: cook 1 minute per side.
  • Add a little chili for heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic choy sum
  • Soup: Tomato tofu soup
  • Dessert: Almond jelly
  • Beverage: Cold beer
