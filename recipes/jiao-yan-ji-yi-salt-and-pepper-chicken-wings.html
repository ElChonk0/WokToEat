Jiāo Yán Jī Yì (Salt-and-Pepper Chicken Wings) 椒盐鸡翼

▾

Surprise me with

Salt-and-pepper (jiu yim) frying is a Cantonese restaurant technique: the food is fried crisp, then tossed in a hot wok with garlic, chili and spiced salt; wings are one of its most popular forms.

Method

  1. Pat the wings very dry. Cut two shallow slashes across each flat and prick the thick part of each drumette so the marinade gets in and the meat cooks evenly.
  2. Mix the soy sauces, oyster sauce, wine, marinade salt, sugar and white pepper, massage it into the wings and leave for 20 to 30 minutes while you prepare the aromatics. Mix the garlic salt, seasoning salt and white pepper in a small bowl.
  3. Mix the cornstarch and flour. Coat the wings a few at a time, pressing the coating on with one hand and shaking off the excess with the other (one wet hand, one dry hand), so every piece has a thin, even layer.
  4. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 150 C (300 F). Fry the drumettes for about 5 minutes, until pale golden and floating, then the flats for 3 to 4 minutes. Keep the heat at medium so the oil stays near 150 C; hotter oil browns the outside before the meat is cooked. Drain on a rack.
  5. Raise the oil to 175 C (350 F). Return the drumettes, add the flats 30 seconds later, and fry for about 1 minute more until deep golden and crisp. The second fry dries out the crust. Drain on the rack.
  6. Pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil. Over medium heat fry the garlic and shallots for 10 to 15 seconds, add half the green onion and the chilies for 30 seconds, then the bell pepper for 30 seconds, until fragrant but not browned. Drain the aromatics of excess oil if they look greasy.
  7. Return the wings and toss for 20 to 30 seconds, lifting from the bottom of the wok, sprinkling in the seasoning salt as you go so it does not settle on the bottom. Scatter with the remaining green onion and serve at once, while the crust is crisp.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet wings spatter and never crisp; dry them well before marinating.
  • Burning the garlic in the final toss makes it bitter; keep it to 30 seconds.

Authentic tips

  • The second fry at higher heat is what gives the restaurant-style shatter.
  • Make extra spiced salt; it keeps for months in a jar.

Substitutions

  • Air fryer: spray the coated wings with oil and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 22 to 25 minutes, turning once, then do the garlic toss in a pan.
  • Drumettes alone, or boneless thigh pieces fried for 5 minutes, work the same way.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a 220 C (425 F) oven or air fryer for 8 minutes to re-crisp.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months after frying; reheat from frozen in a hot oven.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed jasmine rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Cold beer or jasmine tea
