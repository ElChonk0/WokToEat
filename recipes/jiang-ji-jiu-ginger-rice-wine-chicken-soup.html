Jiāng Jī Jiǔ (Ginger and Rice Wine Chicken Soup) 姜鸡酒

Chicken simmered with lots of ginger and sweet rice wine is a traditional Toisanese and Cantonese confinement dish, given to new mothers in the month after childbirth and shared with family to celebrate a birth.

  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt

Method

  1. Dry-fry the ginger slices in a Dutch oven over medium heat for 5 minutes until they shrink and smell spicy, then add the sesame oil and fry for 2 minutes more.
  2. Add the chicken and fry for 5 minutes, turning, until the skin is lightly golden.
  3. Add the mushrooms, rice wine, water and sugar. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 30 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  4. Add the eggs for the last 10 minutes so they take on the color of the broth.
  5. Season with salt and serve in bowls with plenty of broth.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry-frying leaves the ginger harsh; it should be toasted and fragrant.
  • Boiling hard drives off the wine aroma; keep it at a gentle simmer.

Authentic tips

  • Old, mature ginger is preferred for its heat.
  • Families often add pork trotters and black vinegar for a related confinement dish, zhu jiao jiang.

Substitutions

  • Dry sherry mixed half and half with water replaces Chinese rice wine.
  • Black sesame oil is traditional; toasted sesame oil works.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; the flavor deepens.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the eggs.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Sweet ginger soup with tangyuan
  • Beverage: Warm water or tea
