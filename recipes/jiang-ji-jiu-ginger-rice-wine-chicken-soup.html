Jiāng Jī Jiǔ (Ginger and Rice Wine Chicken Soup) 姜鸡酒

▾

Surprise me with

Chicken simmered with lots of ginger and sweet rice wine is a traditional Toisanese and Cantonese confinement dish, given to new mothers in the month after childbirth and shared with family to celebrate a birth.

  • 120 g4 ozGinger, peeled and thinly sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspSesame oil
  • 10 g1/3 ozDried wood ear mushrooms, soaked
  • 66Dried shiitake mushrooms, soaked
  • 88Dried red dates (jujubes), pitted and halved
  • 80 g1/2 cupRaw peanuts, soaked (optional)
  • 400 ml1 2/3 cupsChinese drinking rice wine (mi jiu)
  • 1 L4 cupsWater
  • 10 g2 tspRock sugar or brown sugar
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 44Hard-boiled eggs, peeled

Method

  1. Soak the wood ear and shiitake in warm water for 20 minutes and the peanuts in hot water for 1 hour if using. Drain, trim the mushroom stems and tear the wood ear into pieces. Rinse the red dates.
  2. Put the chicken in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil and cook for 2 minutes until the scum rises. Drain and rinse the pieces; this keeps the finished broth clean and clear.
  3. In a dry Dutch oven over medium heat, cook the ginger slices for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring, until they shrink, curl and smell sharp. Add the sesame oil and fry for 1 to 2 minutes more until the edges turn golden. Do not rush this step: well-fried ginger gives the soup its warming bite.
  4. Add the chicken and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until lightly golden. Add the peanuts, mushrooms, water and sugar and bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 20 minutes.
  5. Add the red dates, eggs and rice wine and simmer, covered, for 15 minutes more, until the chicken is tender and the eggs have taken on the color of the broth. Adding the wine later keeps its sweet, heady flavor rather than boiling it all away.
  6. Season with salt, dissolving it in a ladle of broth before stirring it back in, and serve in deep bowls with plenty of broth.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry-frying leaves the ginger harsh; it should be toasted and fragrant.
  • Boiling hard drives off the wine aroma; keep it at a gentle simmer.

Authentic tips

  • Old, mature ginger is preferred for its heat.
  • Families often add pork trotters and black vinegar for a related confinement dish, zhu jiao jiang.

Substitutions

  • Dry sherry mixed half and half with water replaces Chinese rice wine.
  • Black sesame oil is traditional; toasted sesame oil works.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; the flavor deepens.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the eggs.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Sweet ginger soup with tangyuan
  • Beverage: Warm water or tea
