Mandu Jeongol (Dumpling Hot Pot) 만두전골

▾

Surprise me with

Mandu jeongol, dumplings, beef and vegetables arranged in a shallow pot and simmered at the table in a light broth, is a Korean winter sharing dish.

Method

  1. Make the broth first if you have time: simmer dried anchovies and a square of kelp in the water for 15 minutes, then strain. Season it with the soup soy sauce, garlic and salt; it should taste lightly seasoned, as the dumplings and beef add salt.
  2. Arrange the cabbage, tofu, onion and mushrooms in clusters in a wide, shallow pot or Dutch oven, leaving the center for the beef and dumplings. Arranging rather than mixing keeps each ingredient intact and looks good at the table.
  3. Pour in enough broth to come halfway up the ingredients and bring it to a boil over medium-high heat. Simmer for 5 minutes until the cabbage starts to soften.
  4. Add the dumplings straight from the freezer and the beef, pushing them into the broth, and sprinkle the gochugaru over. Simmer for 6 to 8 minutes, until the dumplings float and their filling is hot through and the beef is cooked.
  5. Add the green onion and enoki for the last minute. Bring the pot to the table on a burner and ladle into bowls, topping up with broth as it reduces.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard splits the dumplings.
  • Adding the beef too early makes it tough.

Authentic tips

  • Finish with noodles or rice in the leftover broth.
  • A portable burner at the table keeps it simmering.

Substitutions

  • Kimchi dumplings for a spicy version.
  • Use pork slices instead of beef.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi and rice
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Soju or barley tea
