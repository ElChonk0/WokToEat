Yī Pǐn Guō (Hui Layered Chicken Pot) 一品锅

▾

Surprise me with

Yi pin guo, a layered pot of chicken, pork, egg dumplings and tofu simmered together, is a celebration dish of Huizhou, traditionally served at New Year and weddings.

Method

  1. Line the bottom of a large, heavy Dutch oven or clay pot with the bamboo shoots, which can take long cooking without burning.
  2. Build the layers from the bottom up: pork belly, then chicken, then the fried tofu, shiitake and meatballs, then the cabbage, and finally the egg dumplings arranged on top. Yi pin guo ('first-rank pot') was a Ming dynasty official's dish; each layer was a course.
  3. Mix the soy sauces, rock sugar and Shaoxing wine with the stock and pour it over, tucking the ginger and green onion around the sides.
  4. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over the lowest heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, without stirring, so the flavors drip down through the layers.
  5. Bring the pot to the table and serve layer by layer.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring destroys the layers that define the dish.
  • Cooking too hard breaks the egg dumplings.

Authentic tips

  • The layered presentation is traditional; scoop vertically so every portion has all the layers.
  • Egg dumplings are sold in Chinese groceries or made by frying thin omelets around meat.

Substitutions

  • Omit the egg dumplings if you can't find them; extra tofu fills the layer.
  • Pork shoulder replaces the belly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently without stirring.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
