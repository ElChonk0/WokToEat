Gū Lū Yú Kuài (Sweet and Sour Fish) 咕噜鱼块

▾

Surprise me with

Gu lu (gu lao) is the Cantonese sweet and sour style best known with pork; the same sauce is used on fried fish pieces, with peppers, onion and pineapple.

Method

  1. Sprinkle the fish with the fish salt and leave for 5 minutes, then pat it very dry with paper towels. Mix it gently with the marinade cornstarch, pepper and egg yolk. Mix the vinegar, ketchup, sugar and sauce water, and the slurry with 1 tablespoon of water.
  2. Whisk the flour, batter cornstarch, baking powder and salt with the ice water until smooth, then stir in the batter oil. Ice-cold batter fries up lighter and crisper.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 165 C (330 F); a drop of batter should rise to the top at once. Dip the fish in the batter and lower the pieces in one at a time over medium heat so they do not stick together. Fry for 3 to 4 minutes until they float and turn pale gold, then drain on a rack.
  4. Raise the oil to 200 C (390 F) and fry the fish again for 30 to 40 seconds, until deep golden and crisp. Drain. Fry the onion and pepper in the hot oil for 25 seconds to brighten them, and drain.
  5. Pour the oil out of the wok. Bring the sauce to a simmer over medium-low heat and stir in half the slurry over low heat until glossy. Add the vegetables and pineapple over high heat and thicken with more slurry if needed.
  6. Turn off the heat, add the fish and toss quickly until coated. Serve at once, before the crust softens.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet. The firm, thick flakes hold up to double frying.

Common mistakes

  • Sauce poured over the fish early makes the crust soggy; toss at the last moment.
  • Skipping the second fry leaves the coating soft.

Authentic tips

  • A thermometer takes the guesswork out of the oil temperature on electric and induction stoves.
  • Taste the sauce before adding the cornstarch and balance sweet and sour.

Substitutions

  • Haddock or other firm white fish replaces the cod.
  • Canned pineapple juice replaces part of the water for extra sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; the coating softens in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
