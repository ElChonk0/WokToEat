Gū Lū Yú Kuài (Sweet and Sour Fish) 咕噜鱼块

Gu lu (gu lao) is the Cantonese sweet and sour style best known with pork; the same sauce is used on fried fish pieces, with peppers, onion and pineapple.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozIcelandic cod fillet, in 3 cm pieces
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper

  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch, for the sauce

Method

  1. Toss the cod with the salt, pepper and egg, then coat each piece in the cornstarch and shake off the excess.
  2. Heat the frying oil in a wok or pot to 180°C (350°F) and fry the fish in two batches for 3 minutes until pale gold; lift out and rest 5 minutes.
  3. Raise the oil to 190°C (375°F) and fry the fish again for 1 minute until crisp and deep gold; drain on a rack.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Stir-fry the garlic, onion and pepper over high heat for 1 minute, then add the ketchup, vinegar, sugar, water and pineapple and bring to a boil.
  5. Stir in the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water, cook 20 seconds until glossy, toss in the fish for 10 seconds and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet. The firm, thick flakes hold up to double frying.

Common mistakes

  • Sauce poured over the fish early makes the crust soggy; toss at the last moment.
  • Skipping the second fry leaves the coating soft.

Authentic tips

  • A thermometer takes the guesswork out of the oil temperature on electric and induction stoves.
  • Taste the sauce before adding the cornstarch and balance sweet and sour.

Substitutions

  • Haddock or other firm white fish replaces the cod.
  • Canned pineapple juice replaces part of the water for extra sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; the coating softens in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
