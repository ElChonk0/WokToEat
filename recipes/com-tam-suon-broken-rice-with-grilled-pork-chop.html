Cơm Tấm Sườn Nướng (Broken Rice with Grilled Lemongrass Pork Chop)

▾

Com tam suon, broken rice with a grilled lemongrass pork chop, is a Saigon street breakfast and lunch icon.

Method

  1. Pound the lemongrass, garlic and shallot to a paste and mix with the marinade fish sauce, honey and oyster sauce. Pound the chops to an even thickness, coat them and marinate at least 1 hour, or overnight.
  2. Rinse the broken rice and cook it with a little less water than usual, about 20 minutes, until tender but separate.
  3. Grill the chops over medium heat for 5 to 6 minutes per side, turning when caramelized, until charred at the edges and cooked through; rest 3 minutes.
  4. Stir the dipping fish sauce, sugar and lime juice into 60 ml warm water until the sugar dissolves.
  5. Serve the chops on the rice with the cucumber, tomato, pickles and sauce.

Common mistakes

  • High heat burns the honey before the pork is cooked; use medium heat.
  • Mushy rice; broken rice needs less water than whole jasmine.

Authentic tips

  • A fried egg and a slice of steamed egg meatloaf (chả trứng) are the classic Saigon additions.
  • Brush the chops with scallion oil as they come off the grill, as stalls do.

Substitutions

  • Jasmine rice replaces broken rice; chicken thighs take the same marinade.
  • No grill: broil on a rack about 10 cm (4 in) from the element for 5 to 6 minutes per side, or roast at 220°C (425°F) for 15 to 18 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat the pork in a pan.

Freezing: Freezes the marinated raw pork for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried egg and fish sauce
  • Soup: Canh
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
