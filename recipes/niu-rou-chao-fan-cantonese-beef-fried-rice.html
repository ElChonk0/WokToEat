Niú Ròu Chǎo Fàn (Cantonese Beef Fried Rice) 牛肉炒饭

▾

Surprise me with

Fried rice with velveted beef and lettuce is a Hong Kong restaurant and diner standard, quick enough for a weeknight.

  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 300 g10 ozBeef flank steak, thinly sliced then diced
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce, for the beef
  • 1 g1/4 tspBaking soda
  • 5 g2 tspCornstarch
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 800 g5 cupsCooked jasmine rice, cold
  • 33Eggs, beaten
  • 11Onion, diced
  • 150 g5 ozLettuce, shredded
  • 3 stalks3 stalksGreen onion, chopped
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce, for the rice
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the soy sauce, baking soda, cornstarch and a teaspoon of oil and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and sear the beef for 1 minute until just browned; it should still be pink inside. Move to a plate.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the onion and cook 1 minute, then the eggs; stir once and add the rice before they set. Toss for 3 minutes until hot.
  4. Add the soy sauce, oyster sauce and salt and toss for 1 minute, then return the beef.
  5. Toss in the lettuce and green onion for 30 seconds and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the beef with the rice from the start makes it tough; sear it briefly and add it back at the end.
  • Using freshly cooked rice makes it gummy.

Authentic tips

  • The pinch of baking soda is the restaurant trick for tender beef.
  • Shred the lettuce finely so it wilts in seconds.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh replaces beef.
  • Add a handful of bean sprouts for crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.
