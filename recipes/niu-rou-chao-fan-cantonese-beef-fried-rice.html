Niú Ròu Chǎo Fàn (Cantonese Beef Fried Rice) 牛肉炒饭

▾

Surprise me with

Fried rice with velveted beef and lettuce is a Hong Kong restaurant and diner standard, quick enough for a weeknight.

Method

  1. Rinse the beef in cold water, squeeze it dry and mix with the oyster sauce, soy sauce, wine, cornstarch, water and pepper. Break up the rice with wet hands so no clumps remain; if it is very cold, microwave it for 60 to 90 seconds first so it separates easily.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat with 1 tablespoon of oil. Pour in the eggs and stir for about 20 seconds until nearly set but still moist, then lift them out.
  3. Add another tablespoon of oil and sear the beef for about 20 seconds per side, until about 80% cooked. Lift it out.
  4. Add the ginger and onion and fry for 15 to 20 seconds, then add the rice and drizzle the last of the oil around the edge. Lower the heat slightly so the aromatics do not burn, leave the rice for 30 seconds to toast, then stir-fry for about 2 minutes, pressing out clumps.
  5. Season with the salt and light soy, then the dark soy for color, and stir-fry for 1 minute until every grain is evenly tinted and you hear it crackle.
  6. Return the egg and beef, raise the heat and toss for 30 seconds. Add the lettuce, green onion and sesame oil, toss just until the lettuce wilts, and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the beef with the rice from the start makes it tough; sear it briefly and add it back at the end.
  • Using freshly cooked rice makes it gummy.

Authentic tips

  • The pinch of baking soda is the restaurant trick for tender beef.
  • Shred the lettuce finely so it wilts in seconds.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh replaces beef.
  • Add a handful of bean sprouts for crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.
