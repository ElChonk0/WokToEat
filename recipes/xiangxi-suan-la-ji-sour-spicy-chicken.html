Xiāngxī Suān Là Jī (Western Hunan Sour-Spicy Chicken) 湘西酸辣鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Western Hunan's Miao and Tujia peoples love sour and spicy flavors from pickled chilies and fermented vegetables, which they use to cook chicken.

  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, bone-in, chopped
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGarlic and ginger, sliced
  • 10 g1/3 ozDried chilies
  • 60 g2 ozPickled red chilies, chopped
  • 100 g3.5 ozSour pickled vegetables (pao cai or suan cai), chopped
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 250 ml1 cupWater
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt
  • 20 g3/4 ozGreen onion and cilantro

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and stir-fry the chicken for 6 to 8 minutes until golden.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger and dried chilies and fry for 1 minute, then the pickled chilies and pickled vegetables for 2 minutes until the oil turns orange and the pickles smell sour and fragrant.
  3. Add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauce and water, cover and simmer for 15 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  4. Uncover and reduce for 3 to 5 minutes until the sauce clings. Western Hunan (Xiangxi) cooking, from the Miao and Tujia peoples, relies on sour pickles for flavor.
  5. Taste before adding salt, finish with green onion and cilantro and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • The pickles are salty, so taste before adding anything else.
  • Skipping the browning gives pale, flabby skin.

Authentic tips

  • Use the pickling brine to adjust the sourness.
  • Serve with plain rice to soften the heat.

Substitutions

  • Sauerkraut with a splash of vinegar replaces the sour pickles.
  • Boneless thigh cooks faster; shorten by 8 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the sourness mellows.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Tomato egg soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
