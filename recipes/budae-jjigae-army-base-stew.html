Budae Jjigae (Army Base Stew) 부대찌개

▾

Surprise me with

Budae jjigae, a stew of sausage, Spam, kimchi and instant noodles, arose in the 1950s after the Korean War when cooks used surplus U.S. army rations.

Method

  1. Cut the meats, tofu and vegetables into bite-size pieces. Mix the gochugaru, gochujang, soup soy sauce, garlic and pepper with 2 tablespoons of the broth into a seasoning paste.
  2. Arrange the kimchi, Spam, sausages, bacon, tofu, onion, mushrooms and beans in separate clusters in a wide, shallow pot or Dutch oven, with the seasoning paste in the center. Arranging them in groups looks good at the table and lets everyone pick what they like.
  3. Pour in most of the broth, keeping some back, and bring to a boil over medium-high heat. Boil for about 10 minutes, until the bacon is cooked through, the kimchi has softened and the broth is red and rich.
  4. Meanwhile, cook the ramen in a separate pot for 1 minute less than the packet says and drain. Cooking it separately stops the noodles soaking up all the stew.
  5. Add the noodles, green onion and cheese to the pot, topping up with the reserved broth if it has reduced. Bring the pot to the table on a burner, keep it bubbling and eat the noodles first while they are chewy.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles go soft.
  • Too much stock makes the stew thin.

Authentic tips

  • A slice of American cheese is a traditional add-on.
  • Use the ramen seasoning sparingly; the dish is salty already.

Substitutions

  • Hot dogs replace sausages.
  • Leave out the instant ramen seasoning to control the salt.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the noodles soften.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
