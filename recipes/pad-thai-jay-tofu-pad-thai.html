Pad Thai Jay (Vegetarian Pad Thai)

▾

Surprise me with

Pad thai was promoted as a national dish by Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram in the 1930s; the vegetarian "jay" version uses tofu and soy sauce in place of dried shrimp and fish sauce.

Method

  1. Soak the noodles in room-temperature water for 1 to 2 hours until pliable, then drain. Simmer the tamarind, palm sugar, soy sauce and salt for 2 minutes to make the sauce.
  2. Heat half the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry the tofu for 3 to 4 minutes until golden. Add the shallots, garlic and radish and stir for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the noodles and most of the sauce and toss for 2 to 3 minutes, adding splashes of water, until the noodles are soft and chewy. Cook two servings at a time so they do not clump.
  4. Push the noodles aside, add the remaining oil and the eggs, let them set for 30 seconds, then scramble and fold them in.
  5. Toss in the bean sprouts, chives and half the peanuts for 30 seconds, keeping them crunchy. Serve with the rest of the peanuts, lime, chili flakes and sugar.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaked noodles turn to mush.
  • Crowding the wok steams the noodles.

Authentic tips

  • Cook in two batches.
  • Add pickled radish for authentic flavor.

Substitutions

  • Rice noodles can be soaked in warm water to avoid mushiness.
  • Omit egg for a vegan dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheat in a hot wok with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lime wedges and extra peanuts
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
