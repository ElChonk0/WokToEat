Bò Hé Niú Ròu (Yunnan Mint Beef) 薄荷牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Mint is eaten fresh and cooked in Yunnan, where it pairs with beef in stir-fries and salads.

  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

4servings (recipe serves 4)

  • 450 g1 lbBeef sirloin, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozFresh mint
  • 30 g1 ozChilies
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspCornstarch
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the beef 4 mm thick across the grain and marinate with the soy sauce and cornstarch for 10 minutes.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat until smoking and sear the beef 1 minute; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, garlic and chilies for 15 seconds.
  4. Return the beef and toss 30 seconds.
  5. Fold in the mint for 10 seconds, just to wilt, and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough.
  • Cooking the mint too long makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Use fresh, tender mint leaves.
  • Serve at once with rice.

Substitutions

  • Pork takes the same method.
  • Thai basil replaces mint.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the mint darkens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
