Teochew Fish Noodle Soup

▾

Surprise me with

Teochew fish noodle soup pairs slices of fresh fish and a clear, sweet broth made from dried flounder and bones with noodles; Singapore stalls serve it with fish dumplings and fried shallots.

Method

  1. Toast the dried flounder in a dry pan over medium heat for 3 minutes until fragrant and light brown.
  2. Rinse the fish bones, then simmer them with the flounder, ginger, wine and water, uncovered, for 40 minutes, skimming off any foam.
  3. Strain the broth, season with the salt, white pepper and fish sauce, and keep it at a bare simmer.
  4. Poach the sliced fish in the broth for 1 minute until just opaque, then lift out.
  5. Blanch the noodles for 1 minute and the choy sum for 30 seconds, drain, and divide into bowls.
  6. Top with the fish, salted radish, shallots and green onion, and ladle over the hot broth.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the bones hard clouds the broth; keep it at a gentle simmer.
  • Overcooking the fish slices makes them break up.

Authentic tips

  • Toasting the dried flounder gives the broth its depth.
  • Slice the fish against the grain, about 5 mm thick.

Substitutions

  • Fish balls or fish dumplings add more texture.
  • Dried anchovies replace the flounder.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; cook the fish fresh.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili-soy dipping sauce
  • Soup: The dish is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
