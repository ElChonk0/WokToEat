Cháng Shā Sù Mǐ Fěn (Changsha Vegetarian Rice Noodles) 长沙素米粉

▾

Surprise me with

Rice noodles (mi fen) in a spicy, savory broth are a Changsha breakfast institution, eaten at noodle shops from early morning.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbFresh round rice noodles (mi fen)
  • 88Dried shiitake mushrooms
  • 10 g1/3 ozKombu (dried kelp)
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater
  • 15 g1/2 ozGinger, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic, minced
  • 80 g3 ozPickled long beans, chopped
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozScallion and cilantro

Method

  1. Simmer the shiitake, kombu and ginger in the water for 30 minutes, removing the kombu after 10 minutes, to make a savory vegan stock. Season with soy sauce and salt and slice the mushrooms.
  2. Fry the garlic in the oil for 30 seconds, add the shiitake and pickled long beans and stir-fry for 2 minutes for the topping.
  3. Blanch the rice noodles in boiling water for 30 seconds to 1 minute until hot and soft, and divide among bowls.
  4. Ladle over the hot stock and pile on the mushroom and bean topping.
  5. Finish with chili oil, scallion and cilantro. Hunan rice noodles are a breakfast staple.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles makes them mushy.
  • A weak stock gives a flat bowl; simmer dried shiitake with the stock.

Authentic tips

  • Dried shiitake and kelp give a savory vegetarian stock.
  • Chili oil is essential.

Substitutions

  • Fried tofu cubes add protein.
  • Crushed peanuts add crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 4 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled long beans
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
