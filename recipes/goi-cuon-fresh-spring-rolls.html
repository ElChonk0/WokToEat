Gỏi Cuốn (Fresh Spring Rolls)

▾

Surprise me with

Gỏi cuởn are translucent rice-paper rolls filled with herbs, vermicelli, and shrimp or pork, served cold with a dipping sauce — a refreshing counterpoint to the fried spring rolls more familiar outside Vietnam.

Method

  1. Set up an assembly station: a shallow dish of warm water for dipping wrappers, and all fillings within reach.
  2. Dip one rice paper wrapper in warm water 5–10 seconds until just pliable, not fully soft, and lay flat on a clean surface.
  3. Layer a lettuce leaf, a small portion of vermicelli, herbs, a strip of pork, and 2 shrimp halves near the bottom third of the wrapper.
  4. Fold the bottom edge over the filling, tuck in the sides, and roll tightly, tucking a chive stalk to stick out the end partway through.
  5. Repeat with remaining wrappers, keeping finished rolls covered with a damp towel to prevent drying out.
  6. Serve with peanut hoisin dipping sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the rice paper: it becomes too soft to handle and tears — a brief dip is enough since it keeps softening as it sits.

Authentic tips

  • Not overfilling each roll makes it far easier to roll tightly without tearing.

Substitutions

  • No pork: extra shrimp or thin strips of cooked chicken work fine.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten the same day; rice paper toughens and can stick together after a day in the fridge.

Freezing: Not recommended — rice paper texture does not hold up to freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Peanut-hoisin dipping sauce
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
