Cài Fàn (Shanghai Vegetable Rice) 菜饭

▾

Surprise me with

Cai fan, rice cooked with bok choy and often cured pork, is a Shanghai home staple; this version uses mushrooms and keeps it meatless.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 300 g1.5 cupsShort-grain rice, rinsed
  • 400 g14 ozShanghai bok choy, chopped (stems and leaves separate)
  • 66Dried shiitake, soaked and diced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 5 g1 tspGinger, minced
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsWater or mushroom soaking water

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger and shiitake for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  2. Add the bok choy stems and stir-fry for 2 minutes, then the leaves until just wilted. Season with Shaoxing wine, soy sauce, salt and sugar. (Shanghai cai fan often includes salted pork; this is the vegan version.)
  3. Add the drained rice and stir for 1 minute to coat it in the oil.
  4. Pour in the water, bring to a boil, then cover and cook over the lowest heat for 15 minutes without lifting the lid. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 10 minutes.
  5. Fluff the rice, mixing in the greens, and scrape up the golden crust from the bottom (guo ba) to serve with it.

Common mistakes

  • Too much water makes it mushy; use 450 ml for 300 g of rice.
  • Lifting the lid during cooking lets the steam escape.

Authentic tips

  • Fry the rice in oil first for a more fragrant result.
  • Day-old cai fan is good fried.

Substitutions

  • Napa cabbage replaces the bok choy.
  • Add diced fried tofu for protein.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat with a splash of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
