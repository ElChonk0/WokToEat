Niú Ròu Wán Tāng (Fujian Beef Ball Soup) 牛肉丸汤

▾

Surprise me with

Hand-beaten beef balls, springy and juicy, are a specialty of Fujian and neighboring Chaoshan, usually served in a clear soup with celery and fried garlic.

Method

  1. Place the beef in a bowl with the salt and pepper and beat or slap it against the bowl for 5 minutes until very sticky; add the starch and ice water gradually while beating.
  2. Chill the mixture 20 minutes.
  3. Bring a pot of water to a gentle simmer (not boiling), then shape the mixture into walnut-sized balls and drop them in.
  4. Simmer 8 minutes until the balls float and are cooked through.
  5. Heat the stock, add the balls and serve with celery, cilantro and fried garlic.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard roughens the surface and toughens the balls.
  • Not beating long enough gives crumbly, flat balls.

Authentic tips

  • Fatty beef in the mix gives juiciness; add about 20 percent fat.
  • Slapping the mixture against the bowl is what gives the characteristic bounce.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork mixed in softens the texture.
  • Store-bought beef balls save time.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked balls for 2 months; poach from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
