Haemul Pajeon (Seafood Scallion Pancake) 해물파전

▾

Surprise me with

Pajeon, a scallion pancake, is a traditional Korean food, especially on rainy days when it is paired with makgeolli; haemul pajeon adds shrimp and squid.

  • 200 g7 ozScallions, long, halved crosswise

  • 200 ml3/4 cupIce water

  • 15 ml1 tbspSesame oil

  • 11Egg, beaten

  • 10 ml2 tspRice vinegar, for dipping
  • 15 ml1 tbspWater, for dipping
  • 1 g1/2 tspGochugaru, for dipping

Method

  1. Drain the seafood well and toss it with the sesame oil. Pat the scallions dry; wet seafood or scallions make a soggy pancake. Mix the dipping soy sauce, vinegar, water and gochugaru.
  2. Whisk the pancake mix and rice flour with the ice water into a thin, smooth batter, and fold in half the seafood. Ice-cold water keeps the pancake crisp.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a large nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Spread half the batter thinly, then lay half the scallions over it in a single layer, pressing them in. Top with half the remaining seafood, the chili and drizzle half the egg over.
  4. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the bottom is deep golden, lowering the heat a little if it browns too fast.
  5. Flip it once, add another tablespoon of oil around the edge and press down with a spatula. Cook 3 to 4 minutes more until crisp. Repeat with the rest.
  6. Cut into squares and serve hot with the dipping sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Thick batter makes a doughy pancake.
  • Flipping too early tears it.

Authentic tips

  • Add more oil to crisp the edges.
  • Serve with makgeolli.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp alone replaces mixed seafood.
  • Chives replace some scallions.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day, reheated in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Soy-vinegar dip
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Makgeolli
