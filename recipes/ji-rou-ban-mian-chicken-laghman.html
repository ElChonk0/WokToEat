Ji Rou Ban Mian (Chicken Laghman) 鸡肉拌面

Laghman, hand-pulled noodles with a stir-fried topping, is the staple of Uyghur cooking; chicken versions are a lighter alternative to lamb.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium
  • Draft Xinjiang Chicken LunchDinnerStir-fryNoodles

  • 350 g12 ozHand-pulled or wheat noodles, fresh
  • 450 g1 lbChicken thighs, sliced
  • 400 g14 ozTomatoes and peppers
  • 100 g3.5 ozOnion and garlic
  • 3 g1 tspCumin
  • 15 ml1 tbspSoy sauce
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat and fry the chicken 4 minutes until golden.
  2. Add the onion, garlic and cumin and stir-fry 2 minutes.
  3. Add the tomatoes, peppers, soy sauce and salt and simmer 10 minutes until saucy.
  4. Boil the noodles 3-4 minutes and drain.
  5. Pile the noodles into bowls and spoon the sauce on top; mix at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles turn soft.
  • A watery sauce does not cling to the noodles; reduce it.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh pulled noodles give the best chew.
  • Add a splash of black vinegar at the table.

Substitutions

  • Lamb is the traditional meat.
  • Fresh egg noodles or udon replace hand-pulled noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Store sauce and noodles separately; the sauce keeps 4 days.

Freezing: The sauce freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Dessert: Hami melon
  • Beverage: Black tea
