Saba Shioyaki (Salt-Grilled Mackerel) 鯖の塩焼き

▾

Surprise me with

Salt-grilled mackerel is a beloved Japanese home and izakaya dish, valued for its richness and the way its skin crisps over a flame.

Method

  1. Pat the fillets dry, brush them with sake and score the skin with a shallow X.
  2. Sprinkle salt evenly over both sides and leave for 20 to 30 minutes. The salt draws out moisture and the fishy smell; pat it dry with a paper towel afterwards.
  3. Heat the broiler to high, or a fish grill or oiled cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat, and line the tray with foil.
  4. Broil the fillets skin-side up about 10 cm (4 in) from the heat for 6 to 8 minutes, until the skin blisters and browns in spots and the flesh is just opaque. There is no need to turn them.
  5. Serve with a mound of grated daikon dressed with a few drops of soy sauce, and a lemon wedge. The daikon cuts the richness of the fish.

Common mistakes

  • Not drying leaves the skin steamed.
  • Overcooking dries the flesh.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh mackerel needs no extra oil.
  • Rinse excess salt if it forms a crust.

Substitutions

  • Horse mackerel or sardines take the same method.
  • Canned mackerel is a poor stand-in; avoid.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; flake into rice.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
