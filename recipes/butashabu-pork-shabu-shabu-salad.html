Butashabu (Pork Shabu-Shabu Salad) 豚しゃぶ

▾

Surprise me with

Shabu-shabu, thin slices swished in hot broth, is a Japanese hot pot; pork is a lighter, cheaper alternative to beef, often served cold on a salad with sesame sauce.

  • 400 g14 ozPork loin or belly, very thinly sliced (shabu-shabu cut)
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater
  • 5 g1/6 ozKombu

  • 30 g2 tbspJapanese sesame paste or tahini

  • 15 ml1 tbspRice vinegar
  • 8 g2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspMayonnaise (optional)
  • 45 ml3 tbspPonzu

Method

  1. Soak the kombu in the water for 20 minutes, then bring it slowly to just below a boil, remove the kombu and add the sake.
  2. Whisk the sesame paste, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and mayonnaise into a creamy dressing, thinning with a spoon of water.
  3. Turn the heat down until the water is barely simmering, around 80 C (175 F). Swish a few pork slices at a time through the water for 20 to 40 seconds, just until they turn pale with no pink. Water that is too hot makes the pork tough and dry.
  4. Lift the slices into a bowl of cool (not icy) water for a few seconds, then drain them on a paper towel. Cool, not iced, water keeps the fat soft.
  5. Arrange the lettuce, cucumber and tomato on a platter, pile the pork on top and scatter with green onion and shiso. Serve with the sesame dressing and ponzu.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the pork tough and dry.
  • Not cooling quickly leaves the pork warm and soft.

Authentic tips

  • Loin is lean; use half belly for richer slices.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Belly slices are richer than loin.
  • Chicken breast also works, cooked 2 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day if the dressing is kept separate.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
