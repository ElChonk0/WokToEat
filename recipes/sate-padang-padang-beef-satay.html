Sate Padang (Padang Beef Satay)

▾

Surprise me with

Sate Padang, beef and offal skewers with a spicy turmeric rice-flour sauce, is a specialty of West Sumatra with a thick yellow-brown sauce.

Method

  1. Simmer the beef in the water for 1.5 hours, until tender. Lift it out and keep 600 ml (2.5 cups) of the broth. Cut the beef into bite-size pieces.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, ginger, galangal, turmeric, coriander and cumin into a paste. Then heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and fry the spice paste (bumbu) with the lemongrass, turmeric leaf and lime leaves, stirring often, for 8 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried bumbu tastes sharp and grassy; this step is the base of Indonesian cooking.
  3. Toss half the beef pieces in a spoonful of the fried paste. Add the rest of the beef and the broth to the pot with the salt and simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Whisk in the rice flour slurry and simmer, stirring constantly, for 5 minutes, until the sauce is thick, smooth and golden. Rice flour is what gives sate Padang its thick, yellow gravy.
  5. Thread the coated beef onto skewers and grill over hot charcoal or a grill pan for 1 to 2 minutes per side, just to char.
  6. Serve the skewers over sliced ketupat with the thick sauce poured over and fried shallots on top.

Common mistakes

  • Lumpy sauce: whisk the flour slurry in gradually.
  • Overgrilling makes the beef dry.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with crackers.
  • Traditionally includes beef tongue and offal.

Substitutions

  • Beef tongue and offal are traditional.
  • Chicken replaces beef.
  • No grill: lay the skewers on a wire rack over a foil-lined sheet pan and broil on high about 10 cm (4 in) from the element for 3 to 4 minutes per side, or roast at 230°C (450°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until charred at the edges and cooked through.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat with extra broth.

Freezing: Freeze raw skewers for 2 months.
