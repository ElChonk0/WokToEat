Ginisang Munggo (Sautéed Mung Bean Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

Ginisang munggo, a mung bean stew with greens, is traditionally eaten on Fridays in the Philippines, a meatless day for many Catholic households.

Method

  1. Simmer the mung beans in the water, partly covered, for 35 to 45 minutes, until they burst and soften. Add more water if it gets too thick.
  2. In a separate pan, heat the oil and saute the garlic until golden, then the onion and tomatoes for 4 to 5 minutes until the tomatoes collapse.
  3. Scrape the sauteed mixture into the beans with the fried tofu and simmer for 10 minutes, stirring, until thick and creamy.
  4. Season with soy sauce, salt and pepper.
  5. Stir in the greens until wilted and serve with rice. Traditionally eaten on Fridays.

Common mistakes

  • Under-cooked beans stay gritty.
  • Overcooking the greens loses color.

Authentic tips

  • Traditionally flavored with pork; this version is meatless.
  • Eaten on Fridays.

Substitutions

  • Pork and shrimp are the traditional additions.
  • Canned mung beans shorten the cooking.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; thin with water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
