Jian Ji Pa (Hong Kong Pan-Fried Chicken Steak) 煎鸡扒

▾

Surprise me with

Boneless pan-fried chicken thigh "steak" is a staple of Hong Kong cha chaan teng diners, served over rice or spaghetti with an onion and soy gravy or a black-pepper sauce.

Method

  1. Pat the thighs dry and, if any part is much thicker than the rest, butterfly it open so each one lies an even thickness. Score the flesh side in a shallow crosshatch so the meat stays flat in the pan.
  2. Mix the oyster sauce, soy sauce, wine, salt, sugar, white pepper, ginger and marinade cornstarch into a paste. Coat the thighs, then rest them meat-side down in the paste for 10 to 30 minutes.
  3. Dredge each thigh lightly in the dredging cornstarch and shake off the excess; the thin dry layer is what turns the skin crackly.
  4. Heat a heavy skillet or flat-bottom wok over high heat, add the oil and, once it shimmers, turn the heat down to medium-low. Lay the thighs in skin-side down and press them flat with a spatula. Cook without moving for 5 to 7 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and releases on its own.
  5. Turn and cook the meat side for 5 to 6 minutes, then turn back skin-side down for 1 to 2 minutes to re-crisp. The thickest part should read 74 C (165 F), or the juices should run clear. Move the chicken to a board.
  6. Pour off all but a spoonful of fat. Fry the garlic for 10 seconds and the onion for 30 seconds, add the finishing wine and cover at once over high heat for 30 to 40 seconds, so the onion steams in the wine and pan juices. Uncover, sprinkle with the garlic salt and toss.
  7. Slice the chicken, set it on the onions and scatter with green onion. Keep the onions under the chicken rather than on top, so the skin stays crisp.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the thighs too early tears the skin; leave them until they release on their own.
  • Pouring the sauce over the skin softens it; serve it underneath.

Authentic tips

  • Diners often finish the plate with a fried egg on the rice.
  • A black-pepper sauce (onion, butter, coarse pepper, soy and stock) is the other classic gravy.

Substitutions

  • Boneless skinless thighs work; cook 5 minutes per side.
  • Serve over spaghetti instead of rice, as many diners do.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat skin-side down in a dry pan.

Freezing: Freezes well raw in its marinade for 2 months; thaw overnight before cooking.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Hong Kong borscht (tomato and cabbage soup)
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
