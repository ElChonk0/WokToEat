Laing (Bicol Taro Leaves in Coconut Milk)

▾

Surprise me with

Laing, dried taro leaves simmered in coconut milk and chilies, is a classic of Bicol, the Philippines' region of fiery coconut dishes.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium heat and saute the garlic, onion and ginger for 3 minutes. Add the coconut milk and lemongrass and bring to a gentle simmer.
  2. Add the dried taro leaves on top and do not stir for the first 15 minutes. Stirring raw taro leaves before they are cooked releases oxalates that make the mouth itch.
  3. Once the leaves have softened and sunk, stir gently and simmer for 30 to 40 minutes, stirring now and then, until the coconut milk reduces and the leaves are very soft.
  4. Add the coconut cream, chilies, tofu, soy sauce and salt and simmer for 10 minutes more, until thick and the coconut oil separates.
  5. Serve with rice. Laing usually includes shrimp paste and pork; this is the vegan version.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring early or undercooking the taro leaves leaves an itchy throat; cook fully.
  • Boiling hard splits the coconut milk.

Authentic tips

  • Dried taro leaves must be cooked long.
  • Spicy by tradition; add more chilies.

Substitutions

  • Spinach replaces taro leaves, but the texture changes.
  • Add pork for the traditional version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
