Gua Bao (Taiwanese Pork Belly Buns) 割包

▾

Surprise me with

Gua bao, braised pork belly tucked into a steamed lotus-leaf bun with pickled greens, peanut powder and cilantro, is a Taiwanese street food that is sometimes called the Taiwanese hamburger.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork belly whole for 5 minutes, rinse and cut into 1 cm (1/2 in) thick slices about the width of a bun.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat, fry the ginger, green onion and garlic for 1 minute, then the rock sugar until it melts and caramelizes. Add the pork and turn to coat.
  3. Add the rice wine, soy sauces, five-spice, star anise and water. Cover and simmer gently for 1 to 1.25 hours, until the pork is meltingly tender and the sauce is reduced and sticky.
  4. Stir-fry the pickled mustard greens in a dry pan for 2 to 3 minutes with the sugar until fragrant and dry.
  5. Steam the buns for 5 to 8 minutes until soft and hot.
  6. Fill each bun with a slice of pork, a spoon of mustard greens, a generous sprinkle of sweet peanut powder and cilantro. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Under-braised belly stays chewy.
  • Sauce that is too salty overpowers the peanut sweetness.

Authentic tips

  • Brush a little braising sauce on the pork.
  • Make the braise a day ahead for easier slicing.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner filling; braise 30 minutes longer.
  • Store-bought mantou buns, folded, replace lotus-leaf buns.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat the pork gently and steam fresh buns.

Freezing: Freezes the braised pork for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
