Gua Bao (Taiwanese Pork Belly Buns) 割包

Gua bao, braised pork belly tucked into a steamed lotus-leaf bun with pickled greens, peanut powder and cilantro, is a Taiwanese street food that is sometimes called the Taiwanese hamburger.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork belly in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Simmer in the soy sauce, rock sugar, five-spice and water, covered, for 90 minutes until the pork is meltingly tender.
  3. Steam the buns for 8 minutes.
  4. Slice the pork thickly.
  5. Open each bun and fill with the pork, pickled greens, peanut-sugar powder and cilantro.

Common mistakes

  • Under-braised belly stays chewy.
  • Sauce that is too salty overpowers the peanut sweetness.

Authentic tips

  • Brush a little braising sauce on the pork.
  • Make the braise a day ahead for easier slicing.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner filling; braise 30 minutes longer.
  • Store-bought mantou buns, folded, replace lotus-leaf buns.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat the pork gently and steam fresh buns.

Freezing: Freezes the braised pork for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
