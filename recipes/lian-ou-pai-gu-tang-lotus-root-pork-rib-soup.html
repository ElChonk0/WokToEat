Lián Ǒu Pái Gǔ Tāng (Lotus Root and Pork Rib Soup) 莲藕排骨汤

▾

Surprise me with

Lotus root and pork ribs, simmered gently for a milky soup, are a staple of Hunan and Hubei home cooking and winter tonics.

Method

  1. Blanch the ribs for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Cut the lotus root with a rolling cut into irregular chunks; smash some with the flat of a cleaver so the starch releases into the soup.
  3. Put the ribs, lotus root, ginger, Shaoxing wine, red dates and water in a clay pot or Dutch oven, bring to a boil and skim.
  4. Simmer, covered, over low heat for 2 hours, until the ribs are tender and the lotus root is soft and starchy with pink-tinged threads. Starchy lotus root turns the broth slightly creamy; crisp varieties stay crunchy.
  5. Add the goji berries for the last 10 minutes and season with salt and white pepper. Garnish with green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard clouds the soup; keep it at a gentle simmer.
  • Adding the salt early toughens the meat.

Authentic tips

  • Choose starchy lotus root (the kind with seven holes) for a creamy broth.
  • Soak the cut lotus root in water with a splash of vinegar to stop it browning.

Substitutions

  • Pork neck bones replace ribs; skim a little more.
  • Corn on the cob can replace the lotus root.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the broth improves.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months, though lotus root softens.

Serve with
