Loh Bak (Five-Spice Pork Rolls) 卤肉

▾

Surprise me with

A Teochew-Chinese dish popular in both Malaysia and Singapore, loh bak wraps five-spice seasoned ground pork in bean curd skin before frying until crisp — a popular hawker-stall side dish or snack.

Method

  1. Combine ground pork, water chestnuts, five-spice, soy sauce, egg, and cornstarch; mix until sticky.
  2. Cut bean curd skin sheets into rectangles and dampen slightly to make them pliable.
  3. Place a portion of filling along one edge of each sheet and roll tightly into a log, sealing the edge with a little water.
  4. Steam the rolls 15 minutes to cook the filling through, then let cool slightly.
  5. Heat oil to 175°C (350°F) and fry the rolls until golden and crisp, 4–5 minutes.
  6. Slice and serve with a sweet chili or plum dipping sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the steaming step and frying directly, which risks an undercooked interior with a burnt exterior.
  • Rolling too loosely, which lets the rolls fall apart during frying.

Authentic tips

  • Bean curd skin is fragile when dry — dampening it slightly with a wet cloth makes it much easier to roll without tearing.
  • The two-stage cooking (steam, then fry) ensures the filling is fully cooked while achieving a crisp exterior.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken can replace pork for a lighter filling.
  • No bean curd skin: spring roll wrappers work as a substitute, though texture differs.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in an oven to restore crispness.

Freezing: Freezes well, steamed and uncooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sweet chili sauce and chili dip
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Chinese tea
