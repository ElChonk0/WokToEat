Fish Head Bee Hoon Soup

Fish head bee hoon is a Singapore zi char noodle soup of fried fish head and bones simmered into a milky broth with evaporated milk, served with thin rice vermicelli.

Method

  1. Soak the vermicelli in warm water for 10 minutes, then drain.
  2. Pat the fish head dry and fry it in the oil in a hot pot for 4 minutes per side until golden. Add the ginger and garlic for 1 minute.
  3. Splash in the wine, add the water, tomato and mustard greens, and bring to a rolling boil. Cook for 15 minutes until the broth turns milky white.
  4. Add the vermicelli and cook for 3 minutes, then add the sliced fillet and cook for 1 minute until just opaque.
  5. Stir in the evaporated milk, pepper and salt, and heat for 1 minute without boiling.
  6. Ladle into bowls and top with the green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Not frying the head first leaves the broth thin and fishy; browning it makes the broth milky.
  • Boiling after adding the milk can split the soup.

Authentic tips

  • A rolling boil, unlike most soups, is what turns the broth milky.
  • Ask the fishmonger to split the head and remove the gills.

Substitutions

  • Fish bones with extra fillet replace the head.
  • Coconut milk replaces evaporated milk.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten fresh; keep the broth and noodles separate.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sliced chili in soy sauce
  • Soup: The dish is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
