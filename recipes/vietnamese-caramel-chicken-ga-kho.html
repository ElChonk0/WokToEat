Vietnamese Caramel Chicken (Gà Kho)

The chicken counterpart to the famous thit kho (caramel-braised pork), this dish uses the same deep, dark caramel-fish sauce base, simmered until the chicken is sticky, glossy, and deeply savory.

Method

  1. Melt sugar in a pot over medium heat until deep amber, watching closely.
  2. Add chicken carefully and turn to coat in the caramel.
  3. Add fish sauce, garlic, shallot, and black pepper.
  4. Pour in coconut water, bring to a simmer, add hard-boiled eggs if using, cover, and braise 20 minutes.
  5. Uncover and continue simmering 5–10 minutes, spooning sauce over the chicken, until glossy and reduced.
  6. Garnish with green onion and chili; serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the chicken too vigorously during braising, which can break it apart — spoon the sauce over instead.
  • Letting the caramel burn, which makes the whole dish bitter rather than pleasantly deep and sweet.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is traditionally made in a clay pot; a heavy Dutch oven distributes heat just as gently and works on gas, electric and induction.
  • The hard-boiled eggs, simmered in the sauce, are a very traditional and popular addition.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly can be substituted or added alongside chicken for a version closer to classic thit kho.
  • Regular water with a slightly higher sugar amount works if coconut water isn't available.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably overnight.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and boiled greens

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
