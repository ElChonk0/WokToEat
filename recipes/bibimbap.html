Bibimbap

▾

Surprise me with

“Bibimbap” means “mixed rice” — a composed bowl of rice topped with seasoned vegetables, meat, and a fried egg, mixed together with gochujang just before eating; the dish is often traced to practical ways of using up leftover banchan.

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the soy sauce, sugar, rice wine, a third of the garlic and a teaspoon of sesame oil and leave for 20 minutes. Stir the gochujang, sauce sugar, sauce sesame oil and water into a smooth sauce.
  2. Bring a pot of salted water to a boil. Blanch the bean sprouts for about 1 minute and the spinach for about 40 seconds, cooling each in cold water and squeezing it dry. Season each separately with a pinch of salt, a little garlic and sesame oil. Brief blanching keeps them crisp and bright.
  3. Heat a skillet over medium-high heat with a little oil. Stir-fry the carrot for 1 minute, the zucchini for 1 minute and the mushrooms for 1 to 2 minutes, each with a pinch of salt, keeping them separate. Then sear the beef over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes until just cooked.
  4. Fry the eggs sunny-side up in a little oil so the yolks stay runny.
  5. Divide the rice among wide bowls and arrange the vegetables and beef in separate sections on top, with an egg in the center. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve with the gochujang sauce; everyone mixes in as much as they like.
  6. For the dolsot (stone bowl) style, brush a heavy bowl or cast-iron skillet with sesame oil, add the rice and heat over medium until it sizzles and forms a golden crust underneath, about 5 minutes, before adding the toppings.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing the bowl before serving; each eater mixes their own so the toppings stay distinct until the last moment.
  • Crowding the pan with the beef, which steams it instead of searing.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking each vegetable separately keeps the colors and flavors distinct.
  • A heated stone bowl (dolsot) crisps the rice at the bottom; a cast-iron skillet does the same at home.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef, seasoned the same way, is a quicker topping.
  • Thinly sliced chicken or extra mushrooms make a lighter bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps components separated in the fridge 2–3 days; reassemble and reheat the rice and protein, using a fresh egg.

Freezing: Rice and beef freeze well separately; vegetables are best made fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Gochujang and a bowl of broth
  • Soup: Doenjang soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
