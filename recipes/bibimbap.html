Bibimbap

“Bibimbap” means “mixed rice” — a composed bowl of rice topped with seasoned vegetables, meat, and a fried egg, mixed together with gochujang just before eating; the dish is often traced to practical ways of using up leftover banchan.

Method

  1. Blanch the spinach and bean sprouts separately for 30 seconds each, squeeze dry, and season each with a little sesame oil, garlic and salt.
  2. Sauté the carrot and zucchini separately in a little oil for 1 to 2 minutes until just tender, and the mushrooms with 1 teaspoon of soy sauce until soft.
  3. Toss the beef with the remaining soy sauce, the sugar, a little sesame oil and garlic for 15 minutes, then stir-fry in a very hot pan for 2 to 3 minutes until just cooked.
  4. Fry the eggs sunny-side up, keeping the yolks runny.
  5. Divide the rice among bowls, arrange each vegetable and the beef in its own section, top with an egg and a spoonful of gochujang, and mix at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing the bowl before serving; each eater mixes their own so the toppings stay distinct until the last moment.
  • Crowding the pan with the beef, which steams it instead of searing.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking each vegetable separately keeps the colors and flavors distinct.
  • A heated stone bowl (dolsot) crisps the rice at the bottom; a cast-iron skillet does the same at home.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef, seasoned the same way, is a quicker topping.
  • Thinly sliced chicken or extra mushrooms make a lighter bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps components separated in the fridge 2–3 days; reassemble and reheat the rice and protein, using a fresh egg.

Freezing: Rice and beef freeze well separately; vegetables are best made fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Gochujang and a bowl of broth
  • Soup: Doenjang soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
