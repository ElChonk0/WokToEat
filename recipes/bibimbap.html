Bibimbap

“Bibimbap” means “mixed rice” — a composed bowl of rice topped with seasoned vegetables, meat, and a fried egg, mixed together with gochujang just before eating; the dish is often traced to practical ways of using up leftover banchan.

Method

  1. Blanch spinach and bean sprouts separately for 30 seconds each; squeeze dry and season each with a little sesame oil, garlic, and salt.
  2. Sauté carrot and zucchini separately in a little oil until just tender; season lightly with salt.
  3. Sauté mushrooms with soy sauce and a little garlic until softened.
  4. Marinate beef briefly in soy sauce, sesame oil, and garlic, then stir-fry until just cooked.
  5. Fry eggs sunny-side up, keeping the yolks runny.
  6. Divide rice among bowls; arrange each vegetable and the beef in separate sections on top.
  7. Top each bowl with a fried egg and a spoonful of gochujang; mix thoroughly at the table before eating.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing the bowl before serving: bibimbap is meant to be mixed by the eater, which keeps each topping's texture distinct until the last moment.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking each vegetable separately, even briefly, keeps the dish visually striking and keeps each flavor distinct rather than muddled.

Substitutions

  • No beef: thinly sliced chicken or extra mushrooms make a lighter or vegetarian bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps components separated in the fridge 2–3 days; reassemble and reheat the rice and protein, using a fresh egg.

Freezing: Rice and beef freeze well separately; vegetables are best made fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Gochujang and a bowl of broth
  • Soup: Doenjang soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
