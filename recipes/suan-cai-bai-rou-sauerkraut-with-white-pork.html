Suān Cài Bái Ròu (Dongbei Sauerkraut and White Pork Stew) 酸菜白肉

▾

Surprise me with

Suan cai bai rou, sauerkraut stewed with sliced boiled pork belly, is a Dongbei winter hot pot staple, eaten with garlic and sesame dips.

  • Cook 1.5 hr

  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt

  • 90 ml1/3 cupGarlic, crushed, with soy sauce and chili oil, for dipping

Method

  1. Simmer the pork belly in the water with the ginger and green onion for 40 minutes until just cooked. Lift it out, cool, then slice it very thinly; keep the broth. 'Bai rou' means white meat, for the plain poaching.
  2. Shred the sauerkraut as finely as possible; fine shreds release more flavor.
  3. Fry the sauerkraut in the oil for 3 minutes, then add it to the broth and simmer for 20 minutes.
  4. Add the tofu, glass noodles and sliced pork and simmer for 10 minutes. Season with salt and white pepper.
  5. Serve with the garlic dip for the pork.

Common mistakes

  • Undercooked sauerkraut is harsh; it needs the long simmer to mellow.
  • Over-salting: both the sauerkraut and the dip carry salt.

Authentic tips

  • Mix the garlic dip with sesame paste, fermented bean curd and chili oil.
  • Blood sausage is a traditional addition.

Substitutions

  • Rinsed European sauerkraut replaces suan cai.
  • Thin-sliced pork shoulder works if belly is too rich.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the noodles.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or buns, with garlic dipping sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
