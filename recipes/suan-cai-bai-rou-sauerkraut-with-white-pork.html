Suān Cài Bái Ròu (Dongbei Sauerkraut and White Pork Stew) 酸菜白肉

▾

Surprise me with

Suan cai bai rou, sauerkraut stewed with sliced boiled pork belly, is a Dongbei winter hot pot staple, eaten with garlic and sesame dips.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 90 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozPork belly
  • 500 g1.1 lbChinese sauerkraut
  • 100 g3.5 ozGlass noodles
  • 50 g4 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater
  • 90 ml6 tbspGarlic dip
  • 200 g7 ozTofu

Method

  1. Put the pork belly in a pot with the ginger and scallion and cover with cold water; bring to a boil, skim, and simmer 40 minutes. Lift out and slice thinly.
  2. Soak the glass noodles for 15 minutes. Rinse the sauerkraut and slice finely.
  3. Add the sauerkraut to the broth, bring to a boil and simmer 15 minutes.
  4. Add the pork slices, tofu and noodles and simmer 8 minutes.
  5. Serve in the pot with the garlic dip.

Common mistakes

  • Undercooked sauerkraut is harsh; it needs the long simmer to mellow.
  • Over-salting: both the sauerkraut and the dip carry salt.

Authentic tips

  • Mix the garlic dip with sesame paste, fermented bean curd and chili oil.
  • Blood sausage is a traditional addition.

Substitutions

  • Rinsed European sauerkraut replaces suan cai.
  • Thin-sliced pork shoulder works if belly is too rich.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the noodles.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or buns, with garlic dipping sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
