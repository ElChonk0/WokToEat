Gaji Namul (Seasoned Steamed Eggplant) 가지나물

▾

Gaji namul, steamed eggplant torn into strips and dressed with soy, garlic and sesame, is a classic summer banchan in Korean homes.

Method

  1. Quarter the eggplants lengthwise, then cut them into 5 cm (2 in) pieces. Cutting before steaming lets them cook evenly and stops them tearing apart afterwards.
  2. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer, bring the water to a boil and steam the eggplant covered over high heat for 3 to 5 minutes, until soft but still holding its shape. Overcooked eggplant turns mushy.
  3. Spread the eggplant on a plate to cool for a few minutes, then tear any large pieces into strips with your fingers if you like.
  4. Mix the soy sauce, garlic, green onion, gochugaru, sesame oil and sugar, add the eggplant and toss very gently with your fingertips.
  5. Taste and adjust with a little more soy sauce or gochugaru, sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve at room temperature as a banchan.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Over-steamed eggplant turns into paste.
  • Not squeezing leaves the dressing watery.

Authentic tips

  • Tearing rather than cutting gives more surface for the dressing.
  • It keeps well, making it ideal banchan.

Substitutions

  • Microwave the eggplant, covered, for 4 to 5 minutes instead of steaming.
  • Leave out the chili for a mild version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
