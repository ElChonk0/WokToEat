Luó Bo Niú Nǎn Bāo (Daikon and Beef Brisket Claypot) 萝卜牛腩煲

▾

Surprise me with

Beef brisket braised with daikon in a clay pot is a Cantonese winter staple and dim sum favorite, with a rich sauce flavored with tangerine peel and fermented bean curd.

  • Cook 2.5 hr

Method

  1. Put the beef in a pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil for 1 to 2 minutes, skimming, then drain and rinse. This removes the scum that would cloud and muddy the sauce.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, garlic, star anise, bay leaves and tangerine peel for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant. Raise the heat to high, add the beef and sear for 6 to 8 minutes until the edges are lightly browned.
  3. Add the chu hou paste and mashed bean curd and stir for 1 minute, until they coat the beef and smell rich. Add the wine, oyster sauce and soy sauces and bring to a boil, then the water and sugar.
  4. Cover, return to a boil, then lower to medium-low and simmer at a slow bubble for 1.5 to 2 hours, stirring now and then, until the beef is very tender. Rough flank or chuck, with its seams of connective tissue, turns silkier than lean brisket.
  5. Add the daikon, pushing it down into the sauce, and cook covered for 30 to 45 minutes until a chopstick slides through it and it has turned amber. Taste for salt.
  6. For a glossy sauce, stir in the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons of water while it bubbles. Scatter with green onion and serve from the pot with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the daikon at the start breaks it down to mush before the brisket is tender.
  • Boiling instead of simmering makes the brisket stringy and the sauce cloudy.

Authentic tips

  • Choose brisket with some fat and tendon for the classic gelatinous sauce.
  • Fermented red bean curd (nam yu) gives the signature flavor; do not skip it.

Substitutions

  • Short rib or beef shank replaces the brisket; shank needs 30 minutes longer.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Better the next day; keeps 4 days refrigerated and reheats gently on the stove.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months without the daikon, which turns spongy; add fresh daikon when reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched choy sum
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
