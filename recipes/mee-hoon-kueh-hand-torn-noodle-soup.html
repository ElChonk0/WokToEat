Mee Hoon Kueh (Hand-Torn Noodle Soup)

Mee hoon kueh is a Hakka noodle soup of hand-torn dough pieces cooked in an anchovy broth, popular across Malaysia and Singapore.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, 150 ml water, salt and 15 ml oil into a soft dough, knead for 5 minutes, cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Toast the anchovies in a dry pot over medium heat for 2 minutes, add the 1.5 L water and simmer for 20 minutes, then strain.
  3. Mix the pork with 15 ml of the soy sauce and the cornstarch. Fry the garlic and mushrooms in the 30 ml oil for 2 minutes, add the pork and stir for 4 minutes until browned.
  4. Pour the broth over the pork mixture, bring to a boil and season with the remaining soy sauce, white pepper and salt.
  5. Flatten the dough into a thin sheet on your palm, tear off thumbnail-size pieces and drop them into the boiling broth. Cook for 3 minutes until they float and turn chewy.
  6. Add the greens for 1 minute and serve in bowls.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Pieces torn too thick stay doughy; stretch them thin, nearly translucent.
  • Skipping the dough rest makes it spring back and hard to tear.

Authentic tips

  • Tear straight into the pot for the most irregular, chewy shapes.
  • Add a spoonful of fried shallots at the end.

Substitutions

  • Fish balls or sliced fish replace the pork.
  • Pre-cut dough sheets replace hand-torn dough.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; the dough absorbs broth, so add water on reheating.

Freezing: Freeze the dough and broth separately for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sambal chili
  • Soup: The broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
