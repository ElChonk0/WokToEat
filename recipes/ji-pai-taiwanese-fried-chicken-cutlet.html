Jī Pái (Taiwanese Fried Chicken Cutlet) 雞排

▾

Surprise me with

Ji pai, a giant fried chicken cutlet the size of a face, is a Taiwanese night-market icon, sold with an assortment of spice dusts.

Method

  1. Butterfly the breast and pound it to an even 1 cm (1/2 in), until it is plate-sized. Marinate with the soy sauce, rice wine, garlic, five-spice, white pepper, sugar and egg for at least 1 hour.
  2. Press the chicken firmly into the coarse sweet potato starch on both sides, then rest for 5 minutes until the starch looks damp.
  3. Heat the oil to 160 C (325 F) in a Dutch oven and fry the cutlets one at a time for 4 to 5 minutes until cooked through.
  4. Raise the heat to 190 C (375 F) and fry for 30 to 60 seconds more until golden and very crisp. The second, hotter fry drives out the oil.
  5. Fry the basil for a few seconds until crisp. Drain the cutlet and dust it generously with pepper salt and chili powder. Serve in a paper bag, night-market style.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the rest makes the coating slide off.
  • Overcrowding drops the oil temperature and makes the crust greasy.

Authentic tips

  • Slash thicker parts so the cutlet is an even thickness.
  • Serve in a paper bag for the authentic night-market feel.

Substitutions

  • Thigh stays juicier than breast; pound it to 1 cm.
  • Bake on a rack at 230 C (450 F) for 20 minutes for a lighter version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; recrisp in a hot oven for 10 minutes the next day.

Freezing: Freeze cooked cutlets for 1 month; reheat from frozen in a hot oven.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or fries
  • Soup: Corn soup
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
  • Beverage: Bubble milk tea
