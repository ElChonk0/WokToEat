Pla Tod Lat Prik (Crispy Fried Fish with Chili Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

Crispy fried whole fish topped with a sweet, sour and spicy chili sauce is a staple of Thai restaurants and homes, typically using sea bass or snapper.

  • 900 g2 lbWhole fish (sea bass, snapper or tilapia), cleaned and deeply scored
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 45 g1/3 cupCornstarch or rice flour
  • 1 L4 cupsNeutral oil for frying
  • 8 cloves8 clovesGarlic
  • 30 g1 ozRed chilies (mix of long and bird's eye)
  • 33Shallots
  • 30 ml2 tbspTamarind paste
  • 30 ml2 tbspFish sauce
  • 40 g3 tbspPalm sugar
  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater
  • 15 g1/2 cupThai basil, fried crisp (optional)

Method

  1. Score the fish deeply on both sides, about every 2.5 cm (1 in), so it cooks through and crisps. Pat it very dry, rub with salt and dust inside and out with cornstarch.
  2. Heat the oil in a large flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 180 C (350 F). Slide the fish in and fry for 6 to 8 minutes per side, without moving it until the first side is crisp, until deep golden and crunchy. Drain on a rack.
  3. Pound the garlic, chilies and shallots into a coarse paste.
  4. In a small pan with 2 tablespoons of the frying oil, fry the paste over medium heat for 2 minutes until fragrant. Add the tamarind, fish sauce, palm sugar and water and simmer for 3 to 4 minutes until thick, glossy and sticky.
  5. Fry the basil leaves in the hot oil for a few seconds until translucent and crisp.
  6. Put the fish on a platter, pour the hot sauce over and scatter with the crisp basil.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is too cool makes the fish greasy.
  • Overcooking dries it.

Authentic tips

  • Use a wide, deep pan or wok.
  • Serve with rice and herbs.

Substitutions

  • Fillets fry in 5 minutes.
  • Bake at 220 C (425 F) for a lighter dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the sauce keeps 4 days separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
