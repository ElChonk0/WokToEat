Shorshe Maach (Bengali Mustard Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Shorshe maach, fish in a pungent mustard seed sauce, is a Bengali classic traditionally made with hilsa (ilish) and eaten with plain rice.

  • 800 g1.75 lbFish steaks (hilsa, rohu or salmon)
  • 4 g1.5 tspGround turmeric
  • 7 g1.25 tspSalt
  • 20 g2 tbspBlack mustard seeds
  • 25 g2.5 tbspYellow mustard seeds
  • 44Green chilies
  • 10 g1 tbspPoppy seeds (optional)
  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater, for the paste
  • 60 ml1/4 cupMustard oil
  • 1 g1/2 tspNigella seeds
  • 33Green chilies, slit, for the sauce
  • 200 ml3/4 cupWater, for the sauce

Method

  1. Rub the fish with half the turmeric and half the salt and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Soak the mustard seeds and poppy seeds in warm water for 15 minutes, then grind them with the green chilies, a pinch of salt and the paste water into a smooth paste. Grind with salt and chilies, and do not over-grind, or the mustard turns bitter.
  3. Heat the mustard oil until smoking, lower the heat and fry the fish for 1 to 2 minutes per side until lightly golden. Lift it out.
  4. Add the nigella seeds and slit chilies to the oil, then the mustard paste, remaining turmeric and salt, and the sauce water. Simmer for 3 to 4 minutes.
  5. Return the fish and simmer gently for 5 minutes, spooning the sauce over. Drizzle with a little raw mustard oil for its sharp kick. Serve with steamed rice; with hilsa it is Bengal's most celebrated dish.

Common mistakes

  • Over-grinding makes the mustard bitter.
  • Boiling hard for long turns it bitter too.

Authentic tips

  • Grind with cold water.
  • Hilsa is the classic fish.

Substitutions

  • Salmon or catfish replace hilsa.
  • Mustard paste should be cold-ground.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.
