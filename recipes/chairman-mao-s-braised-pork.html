Chairman Mao's Braised Pork 毛氏红烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

Said to be Mao Zedong's favorite dish and named in his honor, hong shao rou braises pork belly in soy sauce and rock sugar until deeply glossy and tender — a Hunanese classic now found across China.

  • Cook 45 min

Method

  1. Blanch pork belly chunks in boiling water 3 minutes to remove impurities; drain.
  2. Melt rock sugar in a dry pot over medium heat until amber, watching closely.
  3. Add pork and turn to coat in the caramel.
  4. Add both soy sauces, wine, ginger, star anise, and dried chili, stirring to coat.
  5. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 30 minutes.
  6. Uncover and reduce the sauce over medium heat 10 minutes until thick and glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the caramel burn before adding the pork, which makes the whole dish bitter.
  • Skipping the initial blanch, which leaves the sauce cloudier and slightly muddier in flavor.

Authentic tips

  • This dish tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have fully melded.
  • Choose pork belly with a good ratio of fat to lean — the fat renders down and becomes meltingly tender.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner, less traditionally rich version.
  • Brown sugar replaces rock sugar, with a slightly less glossy sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor deepens.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched greens
  • Soup: Simple egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
