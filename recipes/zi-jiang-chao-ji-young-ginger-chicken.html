Zǐ Jiāng Chǎo Jī (Young Ginger Chicken) 子姜炒鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Young ginger, tender and mildly spicy, is a spring ingredient in Hunan, where it is stir-fried in quantity with chicken.

  • Cook 12 min

  • 500 g1.1 lbChicken thighs, sliced
  • 150 g5 ozYoung ginger, sliced
  • 40 g1.5 ozFresh chilies
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the chicken thin and toss with half the soy sauce and a spoonful of water. Slice the young ginger thinly and cut the chilies into strips.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat and stir-fry the chicken 2 minutes until golden; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, then the ginger and garlic, and stir-fry 1-2 minutes until the ginger looks translucent at the edges.
  4. Add the chilies and toss 30 seconds.
  5. Return the chicken with the remaining soy sauce and the wine and cook 1 minute until glossy and cooked through.

Common mistakes

  • Using mature ginger makes the dish harshly fibrous; young ginger is pink-tipped and tender.
  • Crowding the wok steams the chicken instead of searing it.

Authentic tips

  • If you cannot find young ginger, slice regular ginger very thin and blanch it 30 seconds first.
  • The ginger is eaten as a vegetable here, so be generous.

Substitutions

  • Regular ginger sliced very thin replaces young ginger; blanch it 1 minute to soften the bite.
  • Thinly sliced beef takes the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Pear
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
