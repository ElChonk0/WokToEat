Ji Rou Sa Mu Sa (Chicken Samsa) 鸡肉萨木萨

Samsa, baked pastries filled with spiced meat and onion, are a Silk Road food of Xinjiang; chicken versions are a lighter alternative to the traditional lamb.

  • Prep 45 min
  • Cook 30 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 300 g10.5 ozFlour

  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil, for dough and brushing
  • 400 g14 ozChicken thighs, minced
  • 150 g5 ozOnion
  • 5 g2 tspCumin and black pepper
  • 6 g1 tspSalt
  • 15 g2 tbspSesame seeds
  • 11Egg

Method

  1. Mix the flour, water and 15 ml oil into a dough, knead 5 minutes and rest, covered, 30 minutes; roll out thin and cut into 12 cm squares.
  2. Mix the chicken, finely diced onion, cumin, pepper and salt.
  3. Place a spoonful in each square and fold into triangles, sealing the edges with water.
  4. Brush with the egg and oil and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  5. Bake at 200 C (400 F) for 25 minutes until golden.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling bursts the seams.
  • Raw onion makes the filling watery; dice finely and do not salt in advance.

Authentic tips

  • Store-bought puff pastry makes a flakier shortcut.
  • Lamb with fat is the classic filling.

Substitutions

  • Lamb with fat is the traditional filling.
  • Store-bought puff pastry makes a flakier shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked samsa for 2 months; bake from frozen for 35 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black tea and dried fruit
  • Soup: Clear lamb or chicken broth
  • Dessert: Hami melon
  • Beverage: Black tea
