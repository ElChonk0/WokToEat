Jī Ròu Sà Mù Sà (Chicken Samsa) 鸡肉萨木萨

▾

Surprise me with

Samsa, baked pastries filled with spiced meat and onion, are a Silk Road food of Xinjiang; chicken versions are a lighter alternative to the traditional lamb.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt, water and a tablespoon of the oil into a smooth dough and rest it, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the chicken with the onion, cumin, pepper and salt. Uyghur samsa traditionally uses lamb with plenty of lamb fat and onion, which keeps the filling juicy.
  3. Divide the dough into 12 pieces, roll each into a thin square or round, brush with oil and put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center. Fold the corners in to make a square parcel and pinch to seal.
  4. Place seam-side down on a lined tray, brush with egg and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  5. Bake at 220 C (425 F) for 20 to 25 minutes until deep golden; in Xinjiang they are stuck to the walls of a tonur (clay oven). Serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling bursts the seams.
  • Raw onion makes the filling watery; dice finely and do not salt in advance.

Authentic tips

  • Store-bought puff pastry makes a flakier shortcut.
  • Lamb with fat is the classic filling.

Substitutions

  • Lamb with fat is the traditional filling.
  • Store-bought puff pastry makes a flakier shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked samsa for 2 months; bake from frozen for 35 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black tea and dried fruit
  • Soup: Clear lamb or chicken broth
  • Dessert: Hami melon
  • Beverage: Black tea
