Jiāo Yán Pái Gǔ (Salt-and-Pepper Pork Chops) 椒盐排骨

Salt-and-pepper pork chops are a Cantonese restaurant and dai pai dong classic, in which fried pork is tossed with toasted pepper salt, chilies and garlic.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 12 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 700 g1.5 lbPork chops or ribs, cut
  • 60 g1/2 cupCornstarch
  • 11Egg
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and chilies
  • 8 g1.5 tspPepper salt
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil for frying

Method

  1. Cut the pork into 3 cm pieces and marinate with the soy sauce and beaten egg for 20 minutes.
  2. Toss the pork in the cornstarch, pressing so each piece is thickly coated, and shake off the excess.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry in two batches for 5-6 minutes until golden and cooked through; drain.
  4. Raise the heat to 190 C (375 F) and refry all the pork for 1 minute until deeply crisp; drain on a rack.
  5. In a clean wok over medium-high heat, fry the garlic and chilies in 1 tablespoon of oil for 30 seconds, then toss in the pork with the pepper salt and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is not hot enough makes the coating greasy so that it slides off; test with a pinch of cornstarch, which should sizzle at once.
  • Tossing the pork with the seasoning long before serving softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Toast the pepper salt in a dry pan for 1 minute to deepen the flavor.
  • The second fry is what makes the crust shatter.

Substitutions

  • Chicken wings or thighs take the same coating and cook in about the same time.
  • Extra-firm tofu cubes make a vegetarian version; fry until deeply golden.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; recrisp in a 200 C (400 F) oven or air fryer for 6 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze fried pieces for 1 month; reheat from frozen in a hot oven, not the microwave.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh orange slices
  • Beverage: Cold lager or jasmine tea
