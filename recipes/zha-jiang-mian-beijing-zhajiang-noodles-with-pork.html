Beijing Zha Jiang Mian with Pork 北京炸酱面

▾

Surprise me with

Zha jiang mian is the defining noodle of Beijing: wheat noodles topped with a pork and fermented soybean sauce and a heap of fresh shredded vegetables.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 90 g6 tbspYellow soybean paste
  • 30 g2 tbspSweet bean sauce
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 300 g10 ozCucumber, bean sprouts, radish
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Fry the pork in the oil in a wok over medium-high heat for 4 minutes until browned and crumbly.
  2. Add the ginger, scallion and both pastes and stir 1 minute, then pour in 100 ml water.
  3. Simmer over low heat for 10 minutes, stirring often, until the sauce is thick and glossy and the oil rises to the top.
  4. Cook the noodles in boiling water until tender, drain and divide among bowls.
  5. Spoon over the sauce and top with the cucumber, bean sprouts and radish; mix everything well at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the paste makes the whole sauce bitter; add water if it sticks.
  • Too much paste makes it very salty; taste before serving.

Authentic tips

  • Stir the sauce often so it does not catch.
  • Traditional toppings include blanched bean sprouts, shredded cucumber and radish, and edamame.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef or chicken replaces the pork.
  • Hoisin mixed with miso replaces yellow soybean paste.

Storage

Leftovers: Store sauce and noodles separately; the sauce keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: The sauce freezes for 3 months; cook fresh noodles.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Shredded cucumber, radish and bean sprouts
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
