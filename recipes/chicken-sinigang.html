Chicken Sinigang

▾

Surprise me with

A beloved Filipino sour soup traditionally made with tamarind, sinigang is often called Filipino comfort food at its most essential — a warm, tangy broth loaded with vegetables that varies by region and season.

Method

  1. If using fresh tamarind, boil it in 250 ml (1 cup) of the water for 10 minutes until soft, then mash and strain the pulp, keeping the sour liquid.
  2. Heat the oil in a pot and saute the onion, tomatoes and ginger for 3 minutes until the tomatoes soften. Add the chicken and fish sauce and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the water and taro, bring to a boil, skim, and simmer for 20 minutes until the chicken is tender and the taro has softened and thickened the broth.
  4. Add the tamarind liquid or sinigang mix, then the radish and simmer for 5 minutes. Sinigang should be distinctly sour; adjust with more tamarind.
  5. Add the long beans and chilies for 3 minutes, then the kangkong for 1 minute. Season with fish sauce and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the leafy greens too early, which overcooks them and dulls their color.
  • Under-seasoning the sourness — sinigang is meant to have a pronounced tang; taste and add more tamarind base as needed.

Authentic tips

  • Packaged sinigang mix is a reliable, widely used shortcut even in many Filipino households — there's no shame in using it.
  • This soup welcomes almost any vegetable on hand — eggplant, okra, and taro are all traditional additions.

Substitutions

  • No kangkong: spinach or even bok choy work as substitutes.
  • Shrimp or pork ribs can replace or supplement chicken for variety.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the vegetables soften further.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably without the leafy greens; add fresh greens when reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and fish sauce with chilies
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
