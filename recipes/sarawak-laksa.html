Sarawak Laksa

▾

Surprise me with

Sarawak laksa is a Malaysian noodle soup from Borneo with a spice-paste broth thinner and less coconut-heavy than curry laksa, served with rice vermicelli, shredded chicken, prawns and omelette strips.

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in the water for 25 minutes until cooked, lift it out and shred, and keep the stock. Poach the prawns in the stock for 2 minutes, lift out and peel, returning the shells to the stock to simmer for 10 minutes before straining.
  2. Blend the chilies, shallots, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, belacan, coriander, cumin and tamarind into a smooth paste.
  3. Fry the paste in the oil over medium heat for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring, until the oil separates and the paste smells fragrant and toasty.
  4. Pour in the strained stock, bring to a boil, then simmer for 15 minutes. Stir in the coconut milk, salt and sugar and heat gently without boiling.
  5. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt, cook them as a thin omelette in a lightly oiled pan, roll up and slice into thin strips.
  6. Soak the vermicelli in hot water for 5 minutes, drain, and divide into bowls with the bean sprouts. Top with the chicken, prawns and omelette, ladle over the hot broth, and finish with cilantro and a squeeze of lime.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the coconut milk hard splits it and makes the soup greasy.
  • Rushing the spice paste leaves a raw, sharp taste; fry until the oil separates.

Authentic tips

  • The prawn-shell stock gives the broth its depth, so do not skip it.
  • The spice paste keeps 5 days in the refrigerator, so double it to save time.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought Sarawak laksa paste replaces the homemade paste.
  • Thick rice noodles replace vermicelli.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; keep noodles and toppings separate.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.
