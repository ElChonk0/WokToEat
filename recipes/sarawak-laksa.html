Sarawak Laksa

▾

Surprise me with

Sarawak laksa is a Malaysian noodle soup from Borneo with a spice-paste broth thinner and less coconut-heavy than curry laksa, served with rice vermicelli, shredded chicken, prawns and omelette strips.

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in the water for 20 minutes until cooked, add the prawns for the last 3 minutes, then lift both out. Peel the prawns, return the shells to the stock and simmer 15 minutes more; shred the chicken. Strain and keep the stock.
  2. Blend the soaked chilies, shallots, garlic, lemongrass, galangal and belacan into a paste, then mix in the coriander and cumin.
  3. Fry the paste in the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring often, until very dark and fragrant and the oil separates. Sarawak laksa paste is cooked longer than most, which gives it its deep, slightly smoky, sour-spicy character.
  4. Add the stock and tamarind, bring to a boil and simmer for 15 minutes. Strain the broth through a sieve for the classic smooth soup, pressing on the paste. Stir in the coconut milk and season with salt and sugar; do not let it boil hard.
  5. Make a thin omelet from the eggs and cut it into strips. Blanch the vermicelli and bean sprouts and divide among bowls.
  6. Top with the chicken, prawns and omelet, ladle the hot broth over, and serve with cilantro, lime and sambal belacan.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the coconut milk hard splits it and makes the soup greasy.
  • Rushing the spice paste leaves a raw, sharp taste; fry until the oil separates.

Authentic tips

  • The prawn-shell stock gives the broth its depth, so do not skip it.
  • The spice paste keeps 5 days in the refrigerator, so double it to save time.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought Sarawak laksa paste replaces the homemade paste.
  • Thick rice noodles replace vermicelli.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; keep noodles and toppings separate.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.
