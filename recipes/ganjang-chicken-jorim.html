Ganjang Chicken Jorim 간장 닭조림

▾

Surprise me with

Jorim describes Korea's soy-braising technique, most famously applied to potatoes (gamja jorim); this chicken version braises thighs with potatoes and carrots in the same savory-sweet soy sauce base.

Method

  1. Trim the excess fat from the chicken. Blanch the pieces in boiling water for 2 minutes, then drain and rinse; this removes scum and gives a cleaner, glossier sauce.
  2. Mix the soy sauce, rice wine, sugar, syrup, garlic, ginger, pepper and water. Put the chicken and the sauce in a Dutch oven, bring to a boil over high heat, then cover and simmer over medium heat for 15 minutes.
  3. Add the potatoes, carrot, onion and chilies, pushing them into the liquid. Cover and cook for 10 to 12 minutes, until the potatoes are nearly tender.
  4. Uncover, raise the heat to medium-high and cook for 5 to 8 minutes, spooning the sauce over and turning the pieces gently, until the liquid has reduced to a glossy glaze that coats the chicken. Jorim means braised down, so let it reduce, but watch the bottom so the sugar does not burn.
  5. Turn off the heat, stir in the sesame oil and garnish with green onion and sesame seeds. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the potatoes too small, which causes them to fall apart before the chicken finishes braising.
  • Adding sesame oil too early, which cooks off its aroma over the long braise — add it at the very end.

Authentic tips

  • Cutting all the vegetables to a similar size ensures they finish cooking at roughly the same time as the chicken.
  • This dish is a great make-ahead option, since the flavor deepens overnight in the fridge.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks add flavor; braise 10 minutes longer.
  • Add hard-boiled eggs or quail eggs for the last 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; good in lunch boxes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and kimchi
  • Soup: Kongnamul guk
  • Dessert: Sliced fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
