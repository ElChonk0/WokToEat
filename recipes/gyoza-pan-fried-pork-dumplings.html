Gyoza (Pan-Fried Pork Dumplings) 餃子

▾

Surprise me with

Gyoza arrived in Japan from northern China after the Second World War, and Japanese cooks adapted them into garlic-scented, crisp-bottomed dumplings that became a ramen shop staple.

Method

  1. Mix the minced cabbage with a pinch of salt, rest 10 minutes and squeeze dry.
  2. Mix the pork with the garlic, ginger, soy sauce and sesame oil until sticky; fold in the cabbage.
  3. Place a teaspoon of filling in each wrapper and pleat on one side.
  4. Heat the oil in a nonstick pan over medium-high heat and arrange the gyoza flat side down; fry 2 minutes until golden.
  5. Add 100 ml water, cover and steam 5 minutes; uncover until the water evaporates and the bottoms crisp. Serve with soy, vinegar and chili oil.

Common mistakes

  • Too much filling makes sealing hard.
  • Skipping the squeeze makes the dumplings watery.

Authentic tips

  • Garlic chives are traditional in the filling.
  • Freeze uncooked on a tray and cook from frozen.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken thigh replaces some pork.
  • Garlic chives replace cabbage.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-fry in a nonstick pan.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; cook from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Wakame soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
