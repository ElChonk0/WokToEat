Dakgangjeong (Sweet Crispy Glazed Chicken) 닭강정

▾

Surprise me with

Dakgangjeong, boneless fried chicken pieces in a sticky sweet glaze, is a Korean market and street food favorite, famously sold in Sokcho's Jungang Market.

Method

  1. Trim the fat from the chicken and cut it into bite-size pieces. For extra-tender meat, soak it in the milk in the refrigerator for 30 minutes, then drain. Toss with the salt, pepper, grated garlic and ginger and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Make the sauce: bring the soy sauce, mirin, vinegar, gochujang, syrup, sugar, sauce garlic and ginger and the chilies to a boil, then simmer over medium-low for 3 to 4 minutes until slightly thick. Stir in the sesame oil and turn off the heat. For a mild version, leave out the gochujang and add 1 more tablespoon of soy sauce.
  3. Coat the chicken evenly in potato starch, pressing it on, and shake off the excess. Potato starch fries up lighter and crunchier than flour.
  4. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok to 165 C (330 F). Fry the chicken in two batches, without crowding, for about 3 minutes until set and lightly golden. Drain on a rack and let the oil come back to temperature.
  5. Fry all the chicken again at 165 C (330 F) for 1 to 2 minutes, until golden and very crisp. The second fry drives out moisture so the crust stays crunchy under the glaze.
  6. Warm the sauce in a wide pan over medium-low heat, add the chicken and toss until every piece is glazed. Scatter with peanuts and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Glazing over heat burns the sugar; take the pan off before adding the chicken.
  • Skipping the second fry leaves a crust that softens quickly.

Authentic tips

  • Unlike yangnyeom chicken, dakgangjeong is meant to stay crunchy when cold, so it travels well.
  • Leave out the gochujang for a soy-garlic version kids like.

Substitutions

  • Air fryer: spray the coated chicken and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 18 minutes, shaking twice.
  • Cornstarch replaces potato starch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; good cold or warmed in an oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled radish cubes (chikin-mu)
  • Soup: Eomuk-tang (fish cake soup)
  • Dessert: Hotteok
