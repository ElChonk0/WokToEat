Mapo Tofu with Pork 麻婆豆腐

Originating from a Chengdu restaurant run by a pockmarked woman (the dish's namesake), mapo tofu combines silky tofu with ground pork in a numbing, fiery doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorn sauce.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 250 g9 ozPork, ground

  • 3 cloves3 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 10 g2 tspGinger, minced
  • 5 ml1 tspSichuan peppercorn, ground
  • 200 ml3/4 cupChicken stock
  • 20 ml4 tspCornstarch slurry
  • to tasteto tasteGreen onion, sliced, for garnish

Method

  1. Stir-fry ground pork in oil over medium-high heat until browned and cooked through.
  2. Add doubanjiang, black beans, garlic, and ginger; fry until fragrant and the oil turns red.
  3. Add stock and bring to a simmer.
  4. Gently slide in tofu cubes, being careful not to break them; simmer 5 minutes.
  5. Stir in the cornstarch slurry gradually to thicken the sauce.
  6. Sprinkle with ground Sichuan peppercorn and green onion just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the tofu too vigorously, which breaks the cubes apart before serving.
  • Adding the Sichuan peppercorn too early, which cooks off some of its numbing aroma — add it at the end.

Authentic tips

  • Sliding the tofu in gently, and shaking the pan rather than stirring with a spoon, keeps the cubes intact.
  • Freshly ground Sichuan peppercorn has noticeably more aroma than pre-ground.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef can replace pork for a slightly different, still authentic-adjacent flavor.
  • Silken tofu can be used for an even softer texture, though it's more delicate to handle.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently to avoid breaking the tofu.

Freezing: Not recommended — tofu texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, essential
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea or cold beer
