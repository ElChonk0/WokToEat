Sup Ekor (Malay Oxtail Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Sup ekor, oxtail soup spiced with star anise, cinnamon and cloves, is a Malay and Singaporean favorite served with rice and bread.

Method

  1. Blanch the oxtail in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse. Pound or blend the shallots, garlic and ginger into a paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat and fry the paste for 3 minutes, then the star anise, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom, coriander and pepper for 1 minute until fragrant. These warm spices are what make sup ekor taste Malay rather than European.
  3. Add the oxtail and water, bring to a boil and skim. Simmer gently, partly covered, for 3 hours, until the meat falls away from the bone, topping up with hot water as needed.
  4. Skim off as much fat as you can (or chill the soup and lift it off). Add the potatoes, carrots and celery and simmer for 20 minutes, then the tomatoes for 5 minutes, until the vegetables are soft but whole.
  5. Season with the salt and serve in bowls topped with fried shallots, celery leaves and green onion, with lime and chili-soy dip on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanch gives a cloudy broth.
  • Adding vegetables too early makes them mushy.

Authentic tips

  • Skim the fat for a clearer soup.
  • Serve with rice or bread.

Substitutions

  • Beef short ribs replace oxtail.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the simmer to 90 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; skim the fat.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or bread
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the main
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
