Málà Féi Niú (Sichuan Hot Pot Fatty Beef) 麻辣肥牛

▾

Surprise me with

Hot pot is Chongqing's signature meal; thin-sliced fatty beef is the classic item cooked in the mala broth, here adapted into a pan dish.

Method

  1. Melt the tallow in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry the doubanjiang for 3 to 5 minutes, stirring, until the oil is deep red and fragrant.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger, black beans, star anise, cinnamon, dried chilies and peppercorns and fry for 2 minutes more without burning the chilies. Stir in the hot pot base if using.
  3. Add the stock and sugar and simmer for 15 minutes to let the broth mature.
  4. Bring the pot to the table on a portable burner. Cook the potatoes and cabbage first, then the tofu and enoki.
  5. Swish the beef slices in the boiling broth for 10 to 20 seconds, just until they change color.
  6. Dip each piece in sesame oil mixed with garlic, which cools and softens the heat.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the beef in more than a minute makes the thin slices tough.
  • Using a thick cut gives a chewy, ungainly result; the slices should be paper-thin.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with extra dipping oil of sesame oil and garlic.
  • Choose well-marbled beef such as ribeye or short plate for the most flavor.

Substitutions

  • Thinly sliced lamb takes the same sauce.
  • Pork belly slices make a richer version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheated fatty beef turns greasy.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold plum juice
