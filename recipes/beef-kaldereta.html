Beef Kaldereta

▾

Surprise me with

A Spanish-influenced Filipino stew, kaldereta braises beef in tomato sauce with liver spread and vegetables until rich and hearty — a celebration dish reflecting the Philippines' Iberian culinary inheritance.

Method

  1. Marinate the beef in the soy sauce and calamansi for 30 minutes. Lift it out and pat dry, keeping the marinade.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat and brown the beef in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until well colored. Lift it out.
  3. Lower the heat, saute the garlic until golden and the onion until soft, then stir in the tomato paste for 1 minute.
  4. Return the beef with the marinade, tomato sauce, stock, bay leaves and chili. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, until the beef is fork-tender.
  5. Add the potatoes and carrot and simmer for 15 to 20 minutes until tender. Stir in the liver spread, which thickens the sauce and gives kaldereta its rich, earthy flavor.
  6. Add the bell pepper, olives and cheese and simmer for 5 minutes until the cheese melts. Season with salt and pepper and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial beef braise, since chuck needs real time to become properly tender before vegetables are added.
  • Skipping the liver spread, which gives kaldereta its distinctive richness and slight tang.

Authentic tips

  • Liver spread is what distinguishes kaldereta from similar tomato-based stews like afritada — don't skip it if aiming for authenticity.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Goat is the traditional meat.
  • Omit the liver spread for a lighter flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the potatoes.

Serve with
