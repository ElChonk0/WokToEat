Lóngjǐng Xiā Rén (Dragon Well Tea Shrimp) 龙井虾仁

▾

Longjing xia ren pairs freshwater shrimp with fresh Dragon Well tea leaves, a signature dish of Hangzhou; its delicate flavor makes it a showpiece of Zhejiang cooking.

  • 500 g1.1 lbFreshwater shrimp, peeled
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 11Egg white
  • 10 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 4 g2 tspLongjing (Dragon Well) green tea leaves
  • 60 ml1/4 cupHot water (80 C / 175 F), for the tea
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 250 ml1 cupNeutral oil, for passing through oil

Method

  1. Rinse the shrimp repeatedly in cold water until it runs clear, then dry them thoroughly in a towel. Toss with the salt and egg white until sticky, then the cornstarch, and refrigerate for 1 hour. The chill and coating give the shrimp their crisp, white, jade-like look.
  2. Brew the tea leaves in the hot water for 1 minute. Boiling water would cook the leaves and make them bitter.
  3. Heat the oil to about 130 C (265 F), quite cool, and slide in the shrimp, separating them with chopsticks. 'Pass' them through the oil for 30 to 45 seconds, just until they turn white. Drain. This gentle oil-poaching keeps them tender.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Return the shrimp over high heat with the tea leaves and 2 tablespoons of the tea, and drizzle the Shaoxing wine around the hot edge.
  5. Toss for 15 to 20 seconds and serve at once. A Hangzhou classic made with spring tea from the nearby hills.

Common mistakes

  • Using hot oil, which toughens the shrimp.
  • Brewing the tea too long, which makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Use new-season (pre-Qingming) leaves if possible.

Substitutions

  • Brewed green tea leaves plus a little tea liquid replace fresh Longjing.
  • Small freshwater shrimp are traditional; large shrimp cut in half work.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; eat within the hour.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Longjing tea
