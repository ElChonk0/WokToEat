Lychee Pork 荔枝肉

▾

Surprise me with

Fuzhou’s most famous sweet-and-sour dish contains no lychee: crosshatched pork curls into small, bumpy, red-glazed balls that look like the fruit. It has been on Fuzhou banquet menus for centuries.

Method

  1. Score each pork slice in a tight crosshatch about two-thirds deep, then cut into 4 cm squares.
  2. Season with a pinch of salt and soy, then dredge thoroughly in potato starch, working it into the cuts.
  3. Fry at 180°C (350°F) until the pieces curl into balls and turn golden, about 3 minutes; drain. Fry the water chestnuts 30 seconds.
  4. In a clean wok, fry the garlic, add red lees or ketchup, vinegar, sugar, soy and water, and simmer until syrupy.
  5. Toss the pork and water chestnuts through the sauce for 20 seconds and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the crosshatch too shallow, so the pieces stay flat instead of curling into "lychees".

Authentic tips

  • Traditional Fuzhou versions colour the sauce with red wine lees; ketchup is the common modern shortcut.
  • Fry twice (once to cook, once hotter for 30 seconds) for a crust that survives the sauce longer.

Substitutions

  • Tomato ketchup plus a little rice vinegar replaces red wine lees.
  • Chicken breast strips work too.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the coating softens quickly.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh lychee
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
