Singapore Beef Hor Fun

▾

Surprise me with

A wok-fried noodle dish combining the smoky char of dry-fried noodles with a glossy, egg-thickened gravy poured over top, beef hor fun is a hawker-stall favorite in Singapore and Malaysia.

Method

  1. Mix the beef with its soy sauce, oyster sauce, wine, cornstarch and baking soda and leave for 30 minutes. Peel the noodles apart.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it smokes, add 2 tablespoons of oil and spread the noodles out. Fry them undisturbed for about 1 minute per side until charred in spots, toss with the dark soy and spread on a platter. This wok char is the breath of the wok (wok hei) that defines hor fun.
  3. Add 1 tablespoon of oil and sear the beef in a single layer for about 1 minute without moving it, then turn it once; it should still be pink. Lay it over the noodles.
  4. Lower the heat to medium, add the remaining oil and fry the garlic and black beans for 30 seconds. Add the stock, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar and bring to a simmer, then add the choy sum and bean sprouts for 1 minute.
  5. Stir in the slurry until the gravy is thick and glossy, then turn the heat down and drizzle in the egg while stirring slowly so it sets into silky ribbons. Turn off the heat.
  6. Pour the egg gravy over the beef and noodles and serve at once, with sliced green chilies in soy sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef before adding it back to the gravy, which toughens it further as the gravy simmers.
  • Not achieving enough char on the noodles first, which misses the smoky wok-hei contrast that defines this dish.

Authentic tips

  • The contrast between smoky, charred noodles and silky egg gravy is the whole point of this dish — don't skip the high-heat noodle stir-fry.
  • Stir the egg in slowly, in a thin stream, for delicate ribbons rather than large clumps.

Substitutions

  • Chicken replaces beef.
  • Dried rice noodles, soaked, replace fresh.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.
