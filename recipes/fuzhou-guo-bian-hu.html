Fuzhou Guo Bian Hu 锅边糊

Fuzhou’s breakfast staple is made by drizzling rice batter around the hot rim of a wok of simmering seafood broth; the thin sheets set, then get scraped into the soup. It is eaten with oyster cakes.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 4 hr soaking)
  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Blend the soaked rice with the water into a smooth, thin batter (or whisk 200 g rice flour with the water).
  2. Fry the dried shrimp and shiitake in oil, add the stock and clams and simmer until the clams open; remove the clams.
  3. Keep the broth at a strong simmer, with the level about 5 cm below the rim of a wide wok or pan. Brush the dry upper sides with oil.
  4. Ladle a thin ring of batter around the hot sides; cover 1 minute until it sets, then scrape the sheets into the broth. Repeat.
  5. Return the clams, add the shrimp and celery, season with fish sauce, and simmer 2 minutes.
  6. Serve in bowls topped with fried shallots.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the wok sides cool; the batter only sets into sheets on a hot surface.

Authentic tips

  • A non-stick wok makes the scraping easier. If the sheets stick, a thinner batter helps.

Substitutions

  • Rice flour mixed with water (about 1:3) replaces soaked rice.
  • Fish balls can be added.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; the batter thickens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried dough sticks
  • Soup: Not needed; this is a thick soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
