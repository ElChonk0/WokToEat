Huá Dàn Xiā Rén (Silky Scrambled Eggs with Shrimp) 滑蛋虾仁

▾

Surprise me with

Hua dan, "slippery egg", is the Cantonese technique of cooking eggs just to the point of setting so they stay soft and glossy; shrimp is its classic partner.

  • Cook 5 min

  • 200 g7 ozShrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the shrimp
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspGround white pepper
  • 3 g1 tspCornstarch, for the shrimp
  • 66Eggs
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the eggs
  • 2 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 5 ml1 tspNeutral oil, for the eggs
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, finely chopped
  • 3 ml1/2 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Rinse the shrimp, then dry them very well on paper towels; wet shrimp make watery eggs. Halve any large ones and toss them with the salt and pepper, then the cornstarch.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat for 1 minute, add 1 tablespoon of oil and the shrimp and cook for about 30 seconds, until they are just turning pink but not cooked through. Tip them onto a plate and turn off the heat.
  3. Beat the eggs with the salt, sugar and oil just before cooking, until large bubbles form; beaten too early, they lose the air that makes them fluffy. Fold in the green onion and the shrimp.
  4. Reheat the wok over high heat, add the remaining oil and pour in the eggs. Leave for 10 seconds until the edges set, then push the cooked layer from the edge to the center with a spatula, letting the raw egg run underneath. Fold and stack the layers for about 30 seconds in all.
  5. Take the wok off the heat while the eggs are still glossy and slightly runny on top; the residual heat finishes them. Slide onto a plate, drizzle with sesame oil and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the eggs until dry loses the whole point; pull them while they still look wet.
  • Wet shrimp water down the eggs; dry them well.

Authentic tips

  • The cornstarch slurry in the eggs is what keeps them silky and stops them weeping.
  • Restaurants serve this over plain rice as a one-plate lunch.

Substitutions

  • Char siu or crab meat in place of shrimp.
  • Add a few chopped garlic chives for color.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed jasmine rice and garlic pea shoots
  • Soup: Bok choy and pork soup
  • Dessert: Ginger milk curd
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
