Buta no Shogayaki (Ginger Pork) 豚の生姜焼き

A hugely popular Japanese home-cooking staple, shogayaki quickly pan-fries thin pork slices in a ginger-soy glaze — fast, cheap, and satisfying enough to be a teishoku (set meal) fixture.

Method

  1. Whisk grated ginger, soy sauce, mirin, sake, and sugar for the marinade.
  2. Coat pork slices and marinate 10 minutes.
  3. Heat a little oil in a pan and stir-fry onion until softened, 2–3 minutes; remove.
  4. Sear pork slices in the same pan over medium-high heat, 2–3 minutes per side.
  5. Return onion to the pan with remaining marinade, tossing until the sauce thickens and coats everything.
  6. Serve over shredded cabbage.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams the pork instead of searing it properly.
  • Using bottled ginger paste instead of fresh grated ginger, which lacks the same bright, sharp aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Freshly grated ginger, including its juice, is what gives this dish its bright, distinct flavor — don't skip grating it yourself.
  • This is a very fast dish; have everything prepped before you start cooking.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder slices are richer than loin.
  • Chicken thigh works with the same sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; good in bento.

Freezing: Freezes raw marinated slices for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and shredded cabbage
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
