Buta no Shogayaki (Ginger Pork) 豚の生姜焼き

▾

Surprise me with

A hugely popular Japanese home-cooking staple, shogayaki quickly pan-fries thin pork slices in a ginger-soy glaze — fast, cheap, and satisfying enough to be a teishoku (set meal) fixture.

  • 600 g1.3 lbPork loin, sliced 3 mm (1/8 in) thin
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger, grated with its juice
  • 15 ml1 tbspSake, for the pork
  • 10 g1 tbspAll-purpose flour
  • 45 ml3 tbspSoy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspMirin
  • 30 ml2 tbspSake, for the sauce
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 15 g1 tbspOnion, grated, for the sauce
  • 11Onion, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 200 g7 ozCabbage, finely shredded, for serving

Method

  1. Snip through the strip of connective tissue between the fat and meat in a few places so the slices stay flat in the pan. Rub the pork with half the ginger juice and the sake, leave for 5 minutes, then dust lightly with flour.
  2. Mix the soy sauce, mirin, sake, sugar, grated onion and the rest of the ginger into a sauce.
  3. Heat half the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and sear the pork in a single layer, in batches, for about 1 minute per side until browned but still slightly pink. It finishes in the sauce.
  4. Add the remaining oil and cook the sliced onion over medium heat for 5 to 6 minutes until soft and translucent.
  5. Return the pork, pour in the sauce and toss for 1 to 2 minutes until it thickens and coats the meat. The flour coating helps it cling.
  6. Serve with a mound of finely shredded cabbage and rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams the pork instead of searing it properly.
  • Using bottled ginger paste instead of fresh grated ginger, which lacks the same bright, sharp aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Freshly grated ginger, including its juice, is what gives this dish its bright, distinct flavor — don't skip grating it yourself.
  • This is a very fast dish; have everything prepped before you start cooking.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder slices are richer than loin.
  • Chicken thigh works with the same sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; good in bento.

Freezing: Freezes raw marinated slices for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and shredded cabbage
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
