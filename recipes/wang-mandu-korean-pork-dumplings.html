Wang Mandu (Korean Pork and Kimchi Dumplings) 왕만두

▾

Mandu, Korean dumplings, probably arrived from China and Mongolia and are eaten year-round, especially at New Year in a soup.

Method

  1. Soak the noodles in warm water for 30 minutes, then chop them finely. Blanch the bean sprouts for 1 minute, cool them, chop and squeeze out as much water as you can. Squeeze the kimchi and tofu dry in a cloth as well. Wet filling makes soggy wrappers that tear.
  2. Mix the pork, kimchi, tofu, sprouts, noodles, onion, green onion, garlic, ginger, soy sauce, sesame oil, gochugaru, salt and pepper by hand until evenly combined. Fry a small spoonful to taste and adjust the seasoning.
  3. Put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center of a wrapper. Wet the edge with water, fold it into a half-moon and press out the air as you seal it firmly. For the traditional round shape, bring the two corners together and pinch them.
  4. Line a steamer basket with wet cheesecloth or parchment with holes. Bring the water to a full boil and steam the dumplings in a single layer, covered, for 10 to 12 minutes (12 to 15 from frozen), until the wrappers are translucent and the filling is cooked through.
  5. Serve hot with soy sauce mixed with a little vinegar. To freeze, lay the raw dumplings on a tray in one layer until firm, then bag them.

Common mistakes

  • Unsqueezed kimchi and tofu make a watery filling.
  • Overfilling splits the wrappers.

Authentic tips

  • Mandu soup (manduguk) is the New Year version.
  • Fry leftovers in a little oil.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef replaces some pork.
  • Squeeze the kimchi very dry.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam or pan-fry.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; steam from frozen for 15 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Soy-vinegar dip
  • Soup: Mandu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
