Wang Mandu (Korean Pork and Kimchi Dumplings) 왕만두

▾

Surprise me with

Mandu, Korean dumplings, probably arrived from China and Mongolia and are eaten year-round, especially at New Year in a soup.

  • 4040Dumpling wrappers
  • 300 g10 ozPork, ground
  • 150 g5 ozKimchi, chopped and squeezed
  • 150 g5 ozTofu, crumbled
  • 60 g2 ozGlass noodles
  • 30 g1 ozGarlic and sesame oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce

Method

  1. Squeeze the chopped kimchi dry; soak the glass noodles 20 minutes, boil 5 minutes and chop. Squeeze the crumbled tofu in a cloth.
  2. Mix the pork with the kimchi, tofu, noodles, garlic, sesame oil and soy sauce until sticky.
  3. Place a spoonful in each wrapper, fold and pleat.
  4. Steam over high heat for 12 minutes (or boil 6-8 minutes).
  5. Serve with a dip of soy sauce and vinegar.

Common mistakes

  • Unsqueezed kimchi and tofu make a watery filling.
  • Overfilling splits the wrappers.

Authentic tips

  • Mandu soup (manduguk) is the New Year version.
  • Fry leftovers in a little oil.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef replaces some pork.
  • Squeeze the kimchi very dry.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam or pan-fry.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; steam from frozen for 15 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Soy-vinegar dip
  • Soup: Mandu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
