Dà Pán Jī (Xinjiang Big Plate Chicken) 大盘鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Da pan ji, chicken stewed with potatoes, peppers and chilies and served over hand-pulled belt noodles on a huge plate, is a Xinjiang dish of the late 20th century that became its most famous Han-Uyghur fusion.

Method

  1. Heat the oil and sugar in a large wok or Dutch oven over medium heat until the sugar melts and turns amber, then add the chicken and turn it until glossy and lightly browned.
  2. Add the dried chilies, peppercorns, spices, ginger, garlic and green onion whites and stir-fry for 1 minute, then the doubanjiang for 1 minute until the oil turns red.
  3. Add the soy sauces and beer, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 15 minutes. Da pan ji ('big plate chicken') was created by Sichuanese migrants in Xinjiang in the 1980s.
  4. Add the potatoes and simmer for 15 minutes until tender, then the peppers for 2 minutes.
  5. Meanwhile cook the belt noodles and drain. Pile the noodles on a large platter, pour the chicken, potatoes and plenty of sauce over, and toss at the table so the noodles soak up the sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the potatoes too early makes them dissolve into the sauce.
  • Overcooked noodles go soft and cannot hold the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • The sauce should be thick enough to coat the noodles.
  • Serve family style on one huge plate.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs cook faster; shorten the simmer by 10 minutes.
  • Wide fresh pasta (pappardelle) replaces belt noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat the chicken and cook fresh noodles.

Freezing: Freezes the chicken without the potatoes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: The belt noodles are included
  • Soup: Cold cucumber and tomato salad
  • Dessert: Watermelon or hami melon
  • Beverage: Strong black tea
