Eomuk-tang (Korean Fish Cake Soup) 어묵탕

▾

Eomuk-tang, skewered fish cakes simmered in an anchovy and radish broth, is a Korean winter street food sold from carts, where the broth is ladled into paper cups.

Method

  1. Bring the water, anchovies, kelp and onion to a boil, then simmer over medium heat for 10 to 15 minutes. Remove the kelp after 10 minutes and strain out the rest. Add a dried chili for gentle heat if you like.
  2. Blanch the fish cakes in boiling water for 30 seconds to remove the excess oil, then drain. Thread the sheets onto skewers in folds, street-stall style, and cut the round ones in half.
  3. Add the radish to the broth and boil for about 5 minutes, until translucent and soft. Season with the garlic, soup soy sauce and fish sauce.
  4. Add the skewered fish cakes and simmer for 5 minutes, so they soften and flavor the broth.
  5. Season with pepper, add the green onion and chili and turn off the heat. Serve with the skewers in the broth and a little soy sauce for dipping.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the kelp too long makes the broth slimy and bitter.
  • Overcooking the fish cakes makes them swell and go soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Add a little Korean chili flakes or cheongyang chili for a spicy version.
  • A dip of soy, vinegar and chopped chili is the classic accompaniment.

Substitutions

  • Dashi packets replace homemade anchovy broth.
  • Add boiled eggs or rice cakes to make it more filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; the fish cakes soften further.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Gimbap or tteokbokki
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Hotteok
  • Beverage: Hot barley tea
