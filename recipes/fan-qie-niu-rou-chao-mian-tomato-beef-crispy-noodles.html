Fān Qié Niú Ròu Chǎo Miàn (Tomato Beef over Crispy Noodles) 番茄牛肉炒面

▾

Surprise me with

Velveted beef in a sweet-sour tomato gravy poured over a crisp cake of pan-fried egg noodles is a Hong Kong diner and Toisanese restaurant classic.

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the baking soda, oyster sauce, pepper, cornstarch and water until the liquid is absorbed. Steam the noodles over boiling water for about 10 minutes, then let them cool and dry while you cut the vegetables.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok or large skillet over high heat with 2 tablespoons of oil, then lower to medium-low. Spread the noodles in a loose, even layer and leave them alone. After 1 minute add 1 tablespoon of oil around the edge; after another 30 seconds check that the underside is golden and crisp.
  3. Flip the noodle cake, add another tablespoon of oil around the edge, raise the heat to medium and fry for 2 to 3 minutes until the second side is crisp. Slide it onto a platter.
  4. Toss the beef with 1 teaspoon of oil. Heat the remaining oil over high heat, add the beef and leave it for 20 to 30 seconds, then lower the heat and stir for about 30 seconds more, until only about 70% cooked. Lift it out.
  5. In the oil left in the wok, fry the ginger and onion over low heat for 15 to 20 seconds, then the tomatoes for 20 to 30 seconds until they start to soften. Add the ketchup, then the sauce water, raise the heat to high and add the sugar and salt. Taste: it should be bright, sweet and tangy.
  6. Lower the heat, return the beef and add the oyster sauce, then stir in the slurry gradually until the sauce is thick and glossy. Add the green onion and turn off the heat.
  7. Ladle the tomato beef over the noodles at the table and serve at once, so the noodles are still crunchy underneath.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet noodles never crisp; drain and air-dry them first.
  • Overcooking the beef in the sauce makes it tough; add it back at the end.

Authentic tips

  • The contrast of crunchy and gravy-soaked noodles is the point, so pour the sauce just before eating.
  • A little Worcestershire is the Hong Kong diner signature.

Substitutions

  • Serve the tomato beef over rice instead.
  • Chicken thigh replaces beef.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the sauce separately for 3 days; re-crisp noodles in a pan.

Freezing: The tomato beef sauce freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Hong Kong borscht
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
