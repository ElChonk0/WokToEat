Panang Curry Chicken Thigh

▾

Surprise me with

Panang curry is thicker and nuttier than the more common red curry, built on a rich curry paste with ground peanuts and simmered with less coconut milk for a more concentrated, clinging sauce.

Method

  1. Simmer the thicker coconut cream (top of an unshaken can) with the curry paste over medium heat until fragrant and the oil separates slightly, 3–4 minutes.
  2. Add chicken and stir to coat in the paste, cooking 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add the remaining coconut milk, fish sauce, palm sugar, and ground peanuts; stir to combine.
  4. Simmer gently, uncovered, 12–15 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce has thickened.
  5. Stir in kaffir lime leaves in the last 2 minutes.
  6. Garnish with sliced chili and Thai basil before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the curry hard, which can cause the coconut milk to separate into an oily, less appealing texture.
  • Skipping the initial paste-frying step ('cracking' the curry), which is key to developing the dish's full aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Panang should be noticeably thicker than red or green curry — don't thin it out with extra liquid.
  • Ground peanuts (not whole) blend into the sauce for the characteristic panang texture and nuttiness.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works if added in the last 8 minutes.
  • Store-bought panang paste saves effort; fry it well in the cream.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the curry thickens, so thin with coconut milk.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Mango sticky rice
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
