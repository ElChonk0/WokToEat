Buldak (Korean Fire Chicken) 불닭

Buldak ("fire chicken") emerged from a small Korean restaurant in the early 2000s as a dish so spicy it was meant to burn away stress — it has since become a defining symbol of Korea's extreme-heat food trend, spawning instant noodle spin-offs sold worldwide.

Method

  1. Whisk gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, brown sugar, garlic, ginger, rice syrup, and sesame oil into a thick, fiery sauce.
  2. Coat chicken thoroughly in the sauce and marinate at least 30 minutes, or up to overnight for deeper heat.
  3. Heat a large pan or skillet over medium-high heat with a little oil.
  4. Add the marinated chicken and cook, stirring occasionally, 12–15 minutes until deeply caramelized at the edges and cooked through.
  5. If using cheese, reduce heat to low, scatter mozzarella over the top, cover, and let it melt 2–3 minutes.
  6. Serve immediately, directly from the pan.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking over too high heat without stirring, which lets the sugary sauce scorch before the chicken cooks through.
  • Skipping the marinating time entirely, which leaves the chicken tasting more like a sauced dish than one truly seasoned through.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is genuinely very spicy as written; start with half the gochugaru if you want to gauge your heat tolerance before committing.
  • The cheese-topped version (chizu buldak) is extremely popular for taming the heat with rich, melted mozzarella — don't skip it if serving to a mixed crowd.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast can replace thigh, though it dries out more easily given the high-heat cooking.
  • Reduce gochugaru and gochujang by a third for a considerably milder version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently in a covered pan.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and cheese or egg roll to cool the heat
  • Soup: Kongnamul guk
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
