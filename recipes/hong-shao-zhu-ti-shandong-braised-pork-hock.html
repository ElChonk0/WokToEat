Hóng Shāo Zhū Tí (Shandong Braised Pork Hock) 红烧猪蹄

▾

Braised pork hock is a northern Chinese winter dish, valued for its gelatin and rich flavor.

Method

  1. Cut the hock into 5 cm pieces, cover with cold water, boil 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Melt the rock sugar in a dry pot over low heat until amber, add the hock and turn to coat.
  3. Add the ginger, scallion, garlic, star anise, cinnamon, soy sauce and wine, then pour in the water.
  4. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer on low for 90 minutes until the skin is soft and sticky.
  5. Uncover and boil 8 minutes to reduce the sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the sugar makes the sauce bitter; take it off the heat when amber.
  • Skipping the blanching leaves a scummy sauce.

Authentic tips

  • Cook until the skin yields to a spoon.
  • A pressure cooker does the braise in 35 minutes.

Substitutions

  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.
  • Pork trotters replace the hock.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the sauce sets to a jelly.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
