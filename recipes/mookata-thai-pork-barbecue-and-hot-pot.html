Mookata (Thai Pork Barbecue and Hot Pot) หมูกระทะ

▾

Surprise me with

Mookata is a Thai grill-and-hot-pot meal in which pork and seafood are grilled on the dome of a pan while a peppery broth simmers in the moat around it.

Method

  1. Marinate both cuts of pork with the soy sauce, oyster sauce, garlic, white pepper and sugar for 20 minutes.
  2. Pound the dip garlic, chilies and cilantro roots, then stir in the lime juice, fish sauce, sugar and 30 ml of water.
  3. Heat a tabletop grill pan or a ridged cast-iron pan with a deep moat and bring the stock to a simmer around it, or use a separate pot.
  4. Rub the dome with the pork fat, then grill the pork slices for 1 to 2 minutes per side so the fat drips into the stock.
  5. Add the cabbage, noodles and mushrooms to the simmering stock and cook for 5 minutes, then crack in the eggs.
  6. Eat the grilled pork dipped in the sauce, and sip the broth between rounds.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the pork too thick leaves it undercooked; slice it very thin.
  • A pan that is not hot enough steams the pork instead of searing it.

Authentic tips

  • Let the dripping pork fat flavor the broth, then top up with stock as it reduces.
  • A stovetop griddle and a pot work if you lack a mookata pan.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh and shrimp can replace some of the pork.
  • Thai chili-lime seafood sauce works as a ready-made dip.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the broth keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freeze the marinated raw pork for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed jasmine rice and fresh lettuce
  • Soup: The broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer or iced Thai tea
