Gān Guō Xiā (Dry Pot Shrimp) 干锅虾

▾

Surprise me with

Gan guo, or dry pot, is a Hunan and Sichuan style where ingredients are stir-fried with dried chilies and spices and served in a pot over a small flame without much sauce.

  • 700 g1.5 lbShrimp, shell on
  • 20 g2/3 ozDried chilies
  • 5 g2 tspSichuan peppercorns
  • 250 g9 ozLotus root and celery, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozOnion, sliced
  • 30 g2 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and ginger
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Trim the shrimp's whiskers and slit the backs to devein, keeping the shells on; pat dry. Slice the lotus root, celery and onion.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat and fry the shrimp for 1-2 minutes until pink and lightly crisp; lift out.
  3. In the same oil, fry the dried chilies and peppercorns for 20 seconds, then the doubanjiang, garlic and ginger for 1 minute.
  4. Add the lotus root and stir-fry 3 minutes, then the celery and onion for 1 minute.
  5. Return the shrimp with the wine, toss 30 seconds and serve in a hot pot.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the shrimp in the first fry makes them tough.
  • Letting the vegetables steam instead of fry leaves them limp.

Authentic tips

  • Shell-on shrimp give much more flavor; eat them with your hands.
  • Serve in a pre-warmed cast-iron skillet or heavy pot to keep it hot.

Substitutions

  • Fish chunks or squid replace the shrimp.
  • Use fewer chilies for less heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
