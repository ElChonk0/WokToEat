Naga-Style Pork with Bamboo Shoot

▾

Surprise me with

From India's Naga hill tribes in the northeast, this dish reflects a distinctly different Indian culinary tradition, pairing pork with fermented bamboo shoot and minimal spicing — smoky, pungent, and utterly unlike mainstream Indian curries.

Method

  1. Put the pork and salt in a heavy pot with no oil and cook over medium heat, covered, for 10 minutes, until it releases its fat and juices.
  2. Pound the ginger, garlic and chilies coarsely in a mortar and add them to the pork with the bamboo shoot.
  3. Add the water, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer for 45 to 60 minutes, until the pork is tender and the fat has rendered into the broth.
  4. Uncover and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, until the gravy is thick and the pork is glossy. The fermented bamboo gives a sharp, sour tang that cuts the fat.
  5. Serve with plain rice and a raja mircha chutney.

Common mistakes

  • Adding too much extra seasoning — this dish is intentionally minimalist, letting the fermented bamboo shoot's pungency shine through.
  • Using fresh (non-fermented) bamboo shoot, which lacks the distinctive sour, funky flavor central to this dish.

Authentic tips

  • Fermented bamboo shoot has a genuinely strong, distinctive aroma — it's available at stores specializing in Northeast Indian or Southeast Asian ingredients.
  • This dish is traditionally very simply seasoned, letting the smoky pork and pungent bamboo shoot carry the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Sauerkraut replaces fermented bamboo shoot, rinsed.
  • Bhut jolokia is traditional; use fewer chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
