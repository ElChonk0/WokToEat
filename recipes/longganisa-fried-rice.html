Longganisa Fried Rice

▾

Surprise me with

A breakfast staple built around longganisa, a sweet Filipino pork sausage, fried alongside garlic rice and often topped with a fried egg — a classic “silog” combination.

Method

  1. Put the longganisa in a skillet with the water, cover and simmer for 8 to 10 minutes, turning, until the water has evaporated.
  2. Keep cooking in the fat that renders out for 4 to 5 minutes, turning, until the sausages are caramelized and sticky. Lift them out and slice half of them; keep the rest whole.
  3. Fry the garlic in the sausage fat and oil over medium-low heat for 1 to 2 minutes until golden, then add the rice and salt and toss over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes, pressing out clumps.
  4. Toss in the sliced longganisa and cook for 1 minute so the rice picks up its sweetness.
  5. Fry the eggs sunny-side up and serve each plate of rice with a whole sausage, an egg, green onion and spiced vinegar. Long-si-log is a classic Filipino breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Using freshly cooked rice: it turns gummy when fried — day-old, refrigerated rice fries far better.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking the garlic in the rendered longganisa fat instead of plain oil adds significant flavor to the rice.

Substitutions

  • No longganisa: any sweet breakfast sausage works reasonably, though the flavor profile shifts.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2–3 days; reheats well in a pan.

Freezing: Garlic rice freezes acceptably; cook fresh eggs when reheating.

Serve with
