Longganisa Fried Rice

A breakfast staple built around longganisa, a sweet Filipino pork sausage, fried alongside garlic rice and often topped with a fried egg — a classic “silog” combination.

Method

  1. Cook longganisa per package directions (usually pan-fried with a little water, then browned); slice and set aside, reserving rendered fat in the pan.
  2. Add a little oil if needed, then fry the garlic until golden and fragrant.
  3. Add the day-old rice, breaking up clumps, and fry 4–5 minutes until heated through and lightly crisp in spots.
  4. Fold the sliced longganisa back into the rice.
  5. In a separate pan, fry the eggs to preference (sunny-side up is traditional).
  6. Serve the garlic rice topped with a fried egg and scallion, with the longganisa alongside or mixed in.

Common mistakes

  • Using freshly cooked rice: it turns gummy when fried — day-old, refrigerated rice fries far better.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking the garlic in the rendered longganisa fat instead of plain oil adds significant flavor to the rice.

Substitutions

  • No longganisa: any sweet breakfast sausage works reasonably, though the flavor profile shifts.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2–3 days; reheats well in a pan.

Freezing: Garlic rice freezes acceptably; cook fresh eggs when reheating.

Serve with
