Hóng Shāo Zhū Jiǎo (Hunan Braised Pig Trotters) 红烧猪脚

▾

Surprise me with

Braised pig trotters, rich in collagen, are a Hunan winter dish simmered with chilies and star anise.

  • Cook 2 hr

  • 10 g1/3 ozDried chilies
  • 60 g4 tbspGinger and garlic
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 8 g3 tspStar anise and cinnamon
  • 800 ml3.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Cover the trotters with cold water, bring to a boil for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse well.
  2. Melt the rock sugar in a dry pot over low heat until amber, add the trotters and stir to coat in caramel.
  3. Add the ginger, garlic, dried chilies, star anise and cinnamon and fry 2 minutes.
  4. Pour in the soy sauces and 800 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and simmer 90 minutes until the skin is soft and sticky.
  5. Uncover and reduce the sauce over high heat for 5 minutes until glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanching leaves a scummy, off-tasting sauce.
  • Burning the sugar makes the whole pot bitter; pull it off the heat when amber.

Authentic tips

  • Cook until the skin yields to a spoon.
  • A pressure cooker does this in 35 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Pork hock replaces the trotters with more meat and less skin.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the sauce sets to a jelly.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
