Shēng Jiān Bāo (Pan-Fried Pork Buns) 生煎包

▾

Surprise me with

Sheng jian bao, pan-fried pork buns with a crisp base and juicy filling, are a Shanghai breakfast staple, sold at shops across the city.

  • 300 g2.5 cupsFlour

  • 100 g3.5 ozPork skin aspic, chopped (jellied stock)
  • 40 g3 tbspScallion and ginger
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspSesame seeds
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Mix the flour and yeast with 170 ml warm water and knead 8 minutes into a smooth dough; cover and prove 1 hour until doubled.
  2. Mix the pork, aspic, half the scallion and ginger and soy sauce, stirring in one direction until sticky; chill.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 balls, roll each into a 9 cm circle, fill with 2 tablespoons of filling and pleat closed.
  4. Heat the oil in a wide nonstick pan over medium heat and place the buns pleats up; fry 2 minutes until the bases are golden.
  5. Add 100 ml water, cover and steam over medium-high heat for 6-8 minutes, until the water is gone and the bases crackle.
  6. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and the remaining scallion and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Too much water leaves the bases soggy; add only 100 ml.
  • Leaving the lid on too long after the water cooks off burns the bottoms.

Authentic tips

  • Pleats-up buns keep the soup inside.
  • Dip in black vinegar and a few strips of ginger.

Substitutions

  • Unflavored gelatin dissolved in hot stock replaces pork skin aspic.
  • Shrimp and pork make a classic variation.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; leftovers keep 1 day and recrisp in a covered nonstick pan.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked buns for 2 months; fry from frozen with extra water and a longer steam.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sweet soy milk or clear soup
  • Soup: Pickles
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
