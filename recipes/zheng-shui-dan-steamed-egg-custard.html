Zheng Shui Dan (Steamed Egg Custard) 蒸水蛋

Zheng shui dan is a silky Cantonese steamed egg, a staple of home cooking, congee shops and children's meals.

  • Prep 5 min
  • Cook 12 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 44Eggs

  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 20 g2 tbspScallion
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt

Method

  1. Beat the eggs gently with chopsticks, trying not to create foam, and stir in the warm water and salt.
  2. Strain the mixture through a fine sieve into a shallow heatproof dish and skim any bubbles from the surface.
  3. Cover the dish with a plate or foil and set it over simmering water in a steamer, leaving the lid slightly ajar.
  4. Steam over low heat for 10-12 minutes, until the center is just set and wobbles as a whole.
  5. Drizzle with the soy sauce and sesame oil and top with scallion.

Common mistakes

  • Steaming over high heat boils the custard and gives a honeycomb of holes instead of a silky surface.
  • Beating the eggs vigorously creates bubbles that pit the top.

Authentic tips

  • Cover the dish so condensation does not drip onto the custard, and prop the steamer lid open with a chopstick.
  • Use warm rather than cold water for a more even set.

Substitutions

  • Vegetable stock gives a richer flavor than water.
  • Add soaked, chopped shiitake for a heartier custard.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day and can be eaten cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or congee
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
