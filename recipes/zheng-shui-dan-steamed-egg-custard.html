Zhēng Shuǐ Dàn (Steamed Egg Custard) 蒸水蛋

▾

Zheng shui dan is a silky Cantonese steamed egg, a staple of home cooking, congee shops and children's meals.

  • 44Eggs
  • 450 ml2 cupsWarm water or vegetable stock (35 to 45 C / 95 to 113 F)
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 10 ml2 tspNeutral oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 3 ml1/2 tspSesame oil
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 20 g2 tbspScallion, chopped

Method

  1. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer and add enough water to come a third of the way up a shallow heatproof bowl. Take the bowl out, cover the wok and bring the water to a boil over high heat.
  2. Beat the eggs with the salt and oil, then stir in a quarter of the warm water until foamy, and the rest until combined. Warm water, not hot, gives the silkiest custard; for a firmer set use about 350 ml (1.5 cups). Pour through a sieve into the bowl and skim off the surface bubbles, which leave holes.
  3. Set the bowl on the rack, cover the wok and keep the heat high until the water boils again, 1 to 4 minutes, then turn it down to a gentle simmer for about 8 minutes. A fierce boil makes the custard pitted and spongy, and lifting the lid lets the heat escape.
  4. Shake the wok gently: the custard is done when it wobbles like set gelatin all over, with no liquid ripples in the center. If it is still liquid, give it another minute.
  5. Drizzle with the soy sauce and sesame oil, dust with white pepper and scatter with scallion. Serve hot with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Steaming over high heat boils the custard and gives a honeycomb of holes instead of a silky surface.
  • Beating the eggs vigorously creates bubbles that pit the top.

Authentic tips

  • Cover the dish so condensation does not drip onto the custard, and prop the steamer lid open with a chopstick.
  • Use warm rather than cold water for a more even set.

Substitutions

  • Vegetable stock gives a richer flavor than water.
  • Add soaked, chopped shiitake for a heartier custard.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day and can be eaten cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or congee
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
