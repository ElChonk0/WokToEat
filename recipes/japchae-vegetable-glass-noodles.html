Japchae (Glass Noodle Stir-Fry) 잡채

▾

Surprise me with

Japchae, sweet potato glass noodles stir-fried with vegetables, was created for a royal feast in the 17th century and is now a Korean celebration staple.

Method

  1. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic, sesame seeds and pepper until the sugar dissolves. Cut all the vegetables into thin strips about the length of the noodles.
  2. Boil the noodles for 6 to 8 minutes, until fully soft and translucent; they should be tender, not al dente. Rinse in cold water, drain, snip them shorter and toss with 3 tablespoons of the sauce.
  3. Blanch the spinach until just wilted, cool it, squeeze it dry, cut it into lengths and season with a pinch of salt.
  4. Heat a little oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and stir-fry the noodles for about 4 minutes, until glossy and slightly sticky. Move them to a large bowl.
  5. Stir-fry the carrot with a pinch of salt for 1 minute, then the onion and pepper until just translucent, then the green onion for 30 seconds, adding each to the bowl. Cook the mushrooms with 2 tablespoons of sauce for 2 minutes. Keeping the vegetables separate keeps their colors bright and their texture crisp.
  6. Add the spinach and remaining sauce and toss everything together with tongs or your hands. Taste and adjust with a little soy sauce or sugar. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Common mistakes

  • Undercooked noodles are hard; cook until springy.
  • Stir-frying everything together makes the vegetables mushy.

Authentic tips

  • Cook each vegetable separately for color.
  • Serve warm or at room temperature.

Substitutions

  • Add thin beef strips for a traditional version.
  • Cook each vegetable separately for color.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; microwave with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
