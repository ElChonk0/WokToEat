Adobong Pusit (Squid Adobo)

▾

Surprise me with

Adobo, the Philippines' national braise, is made here with squid cooked in vinegar, soy sauce and garlic, with its ink creating a dark sauce.

  • Cook 20 min

  • 3 g1 tspBlack pepper

  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Fry the garlic and onion in the oil until soft, 3 minutes.
  2. Add the squid and ink, vinegar, soy sauce, pepper and bay leaves.
  3. Do not stir for the first 5 minutes so the vinegar cooks off.
  4. Simmer 15 minutes, then uncover and reduce the sauce.
  5. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring right after adding vinegar leaves a raw sour edge.
  • Overcooking makes the squid rubbery; cook 20 minutes or less.

Authentic tips

  • The ink makes the sauce black and rich.
  • Clean the squid carefully to keep the ink sacs intact.

Substitutions

  • Cuttlefish replaces squid.
  • Coconut milk makes a creamy version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
