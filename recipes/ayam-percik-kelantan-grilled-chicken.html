Ayam Percik (Kelantan Grilled Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Ayam percik, chicken grilled and basted with a spiced coconut sauce, is a specialty of Kelantan on Malaysia's east coast.

Method

  1. Score the chicken to the bone and rub it with the grated ginger, turmeric and salt. Marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Steam the chicken over boiling water for 15 minutes, until partly cooked. Steaming first keeps the meat juicy and shortens the time on the grill, where the sugary sauce would otherwise burn before the chicken is done.
  3. Blend the shallots, garlic, lemongrass and belacan into a paste. Fry the chili paste in the oil over medium-low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, add the spice paste and cook for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring, until it darkens and the oil separates.
  4. Add the coconut milk, bruised lemongrass, palm sugar, tamarind and salt and simmer over low heat for 15 minutes, stirring often, until it thickens to a rich, pourable sauce.
  5. Add the chicken to the sauce, coat it and simmer for 5 minutes. Lift it onto a foil-lined tray and keep simmering the sauce for 5 minutes more until thick.
  6. Grill or roast the chicken at 200 C (400 F) for 25 to 30 minutes, basting with the sauce every 10 minutes, until charred in spots and cooked through (74 C / 165 F). Percik means 'splashed', for the repeated basting. Serve with rice and the remaining sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the sauce hard splits the coconut milk.
  • Basting too early burns the sugar.

Authentic tips

  • Charcoal grilling gives the best smoky flavor.
  • Serve with rice or ketupat.

Substitutions

  • A broiler or barbecue replaces charcoal.
  • Light coconut milk makes a lighter sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in an oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Nasi kerabu or steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Cendol
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
