Xiǎo Chǎo Jī (Hunan Small-Wok Chicken) 小炒鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Xiao chao, "small stir-fry," is Hunan's fast, fiery home-cooking style, usually built on green chilies and fermented black beans.

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Put the green chilies in a dry flat-bottom wok over medium heat with a pinch of salt and press them against the wok with a spatula for 3 to 4 minutes, until blistered and softened. Lift them out. Dry-frying the chilies first (gan bian) is the Hunan way; it tames their rawness and deepens their flavor.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in the wok over high heat until smoking and stir-fry the chicken for 3 to 4 minutes, until just cooked and browned at the edges. Push it aside.
  4. Add the remaining oil, the garlic, ginger and fermented black beans and fry for 20 seconds until fragrant.
  5. Return the chilies, add the sauce soy sauce and a pinch of sugar and toss everything over the highest heat for 30 to 60 seconds, until the chicken is coated and smoky. Hunan home cooking (xiao chao, 'small-wok stir-fry') is all about this fierce, quick heat. Serve with plenty of rice.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking in a crowded or cool pan steams the chicken; use a very hot wok.
  • Burning the black beans turns the dish bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Char the chilies until they blister; the smokiness is the signature.
  • Rinse the black beans briefly if they taste very salty.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder takes the same method.
  • Bell peppers replace long chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day and reheats in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
