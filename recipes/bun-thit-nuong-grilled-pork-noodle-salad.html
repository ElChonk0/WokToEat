Bún Thịt Nướng (Grilled Pork Noodle Salad)

▾

Surprise me with

A Vietnamese noodle salad built around char-grilled lemongrass pork served over rice vermicelli with fresh herbs, pickled vegetables, and a sweet-tart nuớc chấm dressing — a cornerstone of Vietnamese home and street food.

  • Cook 10 min

Method

  1. Pound or blend the lemongrass, garlic and shallots into a fine paste, then mix with the fish sauce, sugar and oil. Massage it into the pork and marinate for at least 1 hour, or overnight. The sugar caramelizes over the heat into the smoky, sticky crust.
  2. Make the nuoc cham: stir the sugar into the warm water until dissolved, then add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic and chili. Taste; it should be sweet, sour and salty in balance, and light enough to pour over noodles.
  3. Grill the pork over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until caramelized with charred edges.
  4. Fill bowls with lettuce, cucumber and herbs, then the vermicelli, and top with the pork and pickles.
  5. Drizzle with scallion oil, scatter with peanuts and pour over the nuoc cham. Add a fried spring roll for bun thit nuong cha gio.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the char on the pork: that caramelized edge is central to the dish — a hot grill or pan matters more than marinade time.

Authentic tips

  • Make a double batch of nuớc chấm; it keeps a week refrigerated and is useful well beyond this dish.

Substitutions

  • No lemongrass: lemon zest plus a little extra garlic and ginger gets partway there.
  • No grill: spread the meat in a single layer on a foil-lined sheet pan and broil on the top rack for 3 to 4 minutes, flip, and cook 2 to 3 minutes more until charred at the edges; work in two batches so it browns instead of steaming.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps components separate in the fridge 2–3 days; assemble fresh when serving.

Freezing: Marinated raw pork freezes well; the assembled salad does not freeze.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Nuoc cham and crushed peanuts are included
