Chicken Lo Mein 鸡丝捞面

▾

Surprise me with

Lo mein ("tossed noodles") boils egg noodles and tosses them with a light soy-based sauce rather than stir-frying them in the wok like chow mein — a defining distinction between the two techniques in Cantonese cooking.

Method

  1. Boil noodles according to package directions until just tender; drain and toss with a little sesame oil to prevent sticking.
  2. Marinate chicken in a little soy sauce for 10 minutes.
  3. Stir-fry chicken in hot oil until just cooked through; remove and set aside.
  4. Fry garlic briefly, then add cabbage and carrot, stir-frying 2–3 minutes until just tender-crisp.
  5. Add noodles, oyster sauce, soy sauce, and dark soy sauce, tossing everything together over high heat until evenly coated.
  6. Return chicken to the wok with green onion, tossing to combine before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles in the boiling step, since they'll continue cooking briefly once tossed in the wok.
  • Using too much dark soy sauce, which can make the dish taste overly salty rather than just adding color.

Authentic tips

  • Tossing the boiled noodles with a little oil right after draining prevents them from clumping while you prepare the rest of the dish.
  • Fresh lo mein noodles (not chow mein or ramen noodles) give the most authentic chewy texture.

Substitutions

  • Pork, beef, or shrimp all work well with the same method.
  • No fresh egg noodles: dried lo mein or even spaghetti work in a pinch, though texture differs.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fortune-cookie-style almond cookies
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
