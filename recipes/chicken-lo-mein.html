Chicken Lo Mein 鸡丝捞面

▾

Surprise me with

Lo mein ("tossed noodles") boils egg noodles and tosses them with a light soy-based sauce rather than stir-frying them in the wok like chow mein — a defining distinction between the two techniques in Cantonese cooking.

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the marinade oyster sauce, white pepper, cornstarch and water, squeezing it with your hand for 30 seconds until the liquid is absorbed. Stir the sauce ingredients (oyster sauce, both soy sauces, sugar, cornstarch and stock) together in a cup.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to a boil, add the noodles and loosen them with chopsticks. Cook until tender but still with a firm bite at the center, usually 3 to 4 minutes for fresh noodles. Drain, rinse under cold water to stop the cooking and toss with 1 teaspoon of the oil; they will soften further in the wok.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it just smokes, add 1 tablespoon of oil and spread the chicken out. Leave it 30 seconds, then stir-fry for 1 to 2 minutes until it turns white and is just cooked. Lift it out.
  4. Add the remaining oil and stir-fry the shallots, garlic and mushrooms for 1 minute until fragrant. Add the onion and carrot for 1 minute, then the cabbage for 1 minute, until just wilting. Splash the wine around the edge of the wok.
  5. Stir the sauce and pour it in; it will bubble and thicken within about 30 seconds. Add the noodles and chicken and toss with tongs or chopsticks for 1 to 2 minutes, lifting from the bottom, until the noodles are hot and evenly glossy.
  6. Add the bean sprouts and green onion and toss for 30 seconds more, so the sprouts stay crunchy. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve straight away.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles in the boiling step, since they'll continue cooking briefly once tossed in the wok.
  • Using too much dark soy sauce, which can make the dish taste overly salty rather than just adding color.

Authentic tips

  • Tossing the boiled noodles with a little oil right after draining prevents them from clumping while you prepare the rest of the dish.
  • Fresh lo mein noodles (not chow mein or ramen noodles) give the most authentic chewy texture.

Substitutions

  • Pork, beef, or shrimp all work well with the same method.
  • No fresh egg noodles: dried lo mein or even spaghetti work in a pinch, though texture differs.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fortune-cookie-style almond cookies
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
