Yaki Udon (Stir-Fried Udon) 焼きうどん

▾

Surprise me with

Yaki udon developed in postwar Kokura as a heartier alternative to yakisoba, using thick udon noodles instead of thin wheat noodles — stir-fried quickly with pork and vegetables in a savory soy-based sauce.

Method

  1. Blanch the frozen udon in boiling water for 30 to 40 seconds, just until the strands loosen, then drain well. It is already cooked; longer makes it mushy. Mix the soy sauce, sake, mirin, dashi powder and Worcestershire.
  2. Heat the oil in a large skillet or flat-bottom wok over medium heat and cook the pork with salt and pepper for 2 to 3 minutes until browned.
  3. Add the onion, then the carrot, cabbage and shiitake and stir-fry for 2 minutes. Cover and let them steam over medium-low heat for 2 to 3 minutes until just wilted.
  4. Raise the heat to medium-high, add the udon and green onion and toss with tongs. Pour the sauce around the hot edge of the pan so it sizzles and caramelizes, then toss for 1 to 2 minutes until the noodles are evenly coated and any liquid has cooked off.
  5. Pile onto plates and top with bonito flakes and pickled red ginger.

Common mistakes

  • Not loosening frozen udon before cooking, which leaves clumped, unevenly heated noodles.
  • Using too little heat, which steams rather than sears the noodles and misses the desired slight char.

Authentic tips

  • Udon's thick, chewy texture holds up well to high-heat stir-frying — don't be shy with the heat.
  • The Worcestershire sauce is a distinctly Japanese pantry touch in this dish, giving a tangy depth different from soy sauce alone.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or beef work well with the same method.
  • No udon: thick fresh ramen noodles are a reasonable substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture suffers.
