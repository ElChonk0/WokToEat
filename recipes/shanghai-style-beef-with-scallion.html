Shanghai-Style Beef with Scallion 葱烧牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

A Jiangnan take on the northern Chinese scallion-braising technique, this dish uses generous charred scallion as an aromatic base for tender braised beef, milder and sweeter than its Beijing counterpart.

  • Cook 2 hr

  • 8 stalks8 stalksGreen onions, cut into 6 cm (2.5 in) segments
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced

  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Blanch the beef for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry half the green onion for 8 to 10 minutes, until dark brown and crisp. This scallion oil (cong you) is the heart of the dish. Lift the onions out and keep them.
  3. Fry the ginger in the scallion oil for 1 minute, then add the beef and brown it for 3 minutes over medium heat.
  4. Add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces, sugar, water and the fried green onion. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 hours, until the beef is tender.
  5. Add the remaining green onion and reduce over medium-high heat for 5 to 10 minutes, until the sauce is glossy and the fresh onion is soft. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little scallion — this dish is meant to be scallion-forward.
  • Skipping the char step, which is what gives the scallion its distinctive smoky-sweet flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The charred scallion is as much a vegetable component of this dish as an aromatic — don't skimp on quantity.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Brisket replaces the chuck.
  • Add a little rice vinegar at the end to cut richness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
