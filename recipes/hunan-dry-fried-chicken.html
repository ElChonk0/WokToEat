Hunan Dry-Fried Chicken 干煸鸡

▾

Surprise me with

A twice-cooked technique common across Hunan and Sichuan cuisine, dry-frying (gan bian) first blanches or poaches the protein, then fries it until the edges crisp, concentrating flavor before a final aromatic toss.

Method

  1. Blanch chicken strips in boiling water 2 minutes; drain and pat very dry.
  2. Heat oil in a wok and fry the chicken strips until the edges are golden and slightly crisp, 4–5 minutes; remove.
  3. In the same wok, toast dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns until fragrant, careful not to burn.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, and celery; stir-fry 1–2 minutes.
  5. Return chicken to the wok with soy sauce, wine, and sugar; toss over high heat until everything is well combined and mostly dry (not saucy).
  6. Serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Not patting the chicken dry after blanching, which causes oil to spit and prevents proper crisping.
  • Adding too much liquid at the end — this dish should end up dry and concentrated, not saucy.

Authentic tips

  • The dry-frying technique concentrates flavor by design; resist the urge to add extra sauce or stock.
  • Blanching first removes excess moisture and makes the frying step faster and cleaner.

Substitutions

  • Green beans are a traditional pairing alongside or instead of celery.
  • Reduce the Sichuan peppercorn for a milder, less numbing dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a dry hot pan to bring back the chew.

Freezing: Not recommended; the crisp texture is lost.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Light tofu soup
  • Dessert: Sliced apple
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
