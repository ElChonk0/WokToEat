Niú Ròu Zhuā Fàn (Beef Polo) 牛肉抓饭

▾

Surprise me with

Polo, rice cooked with carrots and meat, is the festive Uyghur dish; beef versions are common outside lamb country.

  • Cook 1.5 hr

  • 100 g1/2 cupChickpeas, cooked (optional)

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high heat and brown the beef for 5 to 6 minutes. Add the onion and fry for 5 minutes until golden.
  2. Add the carrots and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until they soften and color the oil orange. Season with cumin and salt.
  3. Pour in the hot water and simmer, covered, for 40 minutes. This broth, the zirvak, is the base the rice cooks in.
  4. Spread the drained rice evenly over the top without stirring, scatter with raisins and chickpeas, and push the garlic head into the center. Add just enough boiling water to cover the rice by 1 cm (1/2 in).
  5. Cook uncovered over medium-high heat until the water has been absorbed into the rice, then poke a few holes to the bottom with a chopstick, cover tightly and cook over the lowest heat for 25 to 30 minutes.
  6. Gently mix the rice with the carrots and beef, and serve heaped on a platter. Polo, or zhua fan ('grab rice'), is eaten by hand at Uyghur gatherings.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the rice makes it gummy.
  • Under-cooked beef stays chewy; braise first.

Authentic tips

  • Soak the rice 30 minutes for fluffier grains.
  • Lamb is traditional.

Substitutions

  • Lamb is the traditional meat.
  • Brown basmati replaces white; add 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat with a splash of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cucumber and tomato salad
  • Soup: Clear broth
  • Dessert: Dried apricots and nuts
  • Beverage: Black tea
