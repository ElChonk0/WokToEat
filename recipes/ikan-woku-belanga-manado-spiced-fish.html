Ikan Woku Belanga (Manado Spiced Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Woku is a Manado (North Sulawesi) spice base of lemongrass, basil, lime leaf and chilies, used with fish.

  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the lime juice and a pinch of salt and leave for 10 minutes, then pat dry. This freshens it and firms the flesh.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, turmeric, candlenuts and chilies into a paste.
  3. Heat the oil in a wide pot (belanga) over medium heat and fry the spice paste with the lemongrass, leaves and pandan, stirring often, for 6 to 8 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried paste tastes sharp and grassy.
  4. Add the tomato and cook for 2 minutes, then the water and salt, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Add the fish and simmer gently for 8 to 10 minutes, spooning the sauce over, until it flakes and the sauce has thickened. Turn off the heat and stir in the green onion and basil; woku is defined by its heap of fresh herbs.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring breaks the fish.
  • Overcooking dries it.

Authentic tips

  • Basil is used in large quantities.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Fish fillets replace steaks.
  • Thai basil replaces kemangi.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.
