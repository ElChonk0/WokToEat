Sambal Daging (Malay Beef in Sambal)

▾

Surprise me with

Sambal daging, beef cooked in a rich chili paste, is a Malay rice-table staple, often served at weddings and feasts.

Method

  1. Simmer the beef in the water with a pinch of salt, covered, for 30 to 40 minutes until tender, then drain, keeping the liquid. Pre-cooking makes the beef tender before it meets the sambal.
  2. Blend the dried and fresh chilies, shallots, garlic and belacan into a paste.
  3. Heat the oil in a wok or Dutch oven over medium-low heat and fry the paste with the lemongrass for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring often, until it is dark red and the oil separates (pecah minyak). Patient frying is what makes sambal rich rather than raw and harsh.
  4. Add the tomato, tamarind, palm sugar and salt and cook for 2 minutes, then add the beef and a splash of its cooking liquid.
  5. Cook for 5 to 8 minutes, stirring, until the sambal coats the beef and the sauce is thick and glossy. Add the onion rings for the last 2 minutes so they stay slightly crisp. Serve with nasi lemak or rice.

Common mistakes

  • Under-frying the paste leaves it raw and sharp.
  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.

Authentic tips

  • Cook until the sauce clings.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or lamb take the same sambal.
  • Reduce the chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Nasi lemak or steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Kuih
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
