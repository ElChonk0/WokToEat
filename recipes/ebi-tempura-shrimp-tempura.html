Ebi Tempura (Shrimp Tempura) 海老天ぷら

▾

Surprise me with

Tempura reached Japan through Portuguese traders in the 16th century and was refined into a delicate fried dish in Edo; shrimp is the most iconic ingredient.

Method

  1. Simmer the dashi, soy sauce and mirin for 1 minute to make the tentsuyu dipping sauce.
  2. Devein the shrimp, trim the tails and scrape out their water, then score the belly 4 or 5 times and press until the sinews snap so they fry straight. Pat very dry.
  3. Heat the oil (with the sesame oil, for aroma) to 175 C (345 F) in a Dutch oven.
  4. Only now make the batter: beat the egg with the ice water, add the cake flour all at once and stir just a few strokes with chopsticks, leaving lumps. Cold, barely mixed batter develops little gluten, which is what makes tempura light and lacy.
  5. Dust each shrimp in flour, hold it by the tail, dip it in the batter and lower it into the oil. Fry 3 or 4 at a time for 1.5 to 2 minutes, until pale gold and crisp, and drop a few extra drips of batter onto each for a lacier coat.
  6. Drain on a rack and serve at once, with the tentsuyu and grated daikon and ginger.

Common mistakes

  • Overmixing the batter develops gluten and makes it heavy.
  • Warm batter gives a greasy coating; keep it ice cold.

Authentic tips

  • Fry in small batches to keep the oil temperature.
  • Cake flour gives the lightest crust.

Substitutions

  • Squid and vegetables take the same batter.
  • Sparkling water in the batter keeps it light.

Storage

Leftovers: Best within minutes; leftovers recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Hot soba or rice
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer or sencha
