Bái Cài Zhū Ròu Tāng (Bok Choy and Pork Soup) 白菜猪肉汤

▾

Surprise me with

A quick, clear soup of greens and sliced pork is everyday Cantonese home cooking, served alongside rice and other dishes rather than as a meal on its own.

Method

  1. Trim the fat from the pork and slice it across the grain about 2 mm thick; partly freezing it for 20 minutes makes thin slicing easier. Massage it with the oyster sauce, cornstarch and water for 30 seconds until the liquid is absorbed. The coating keeps the slices silky in the broth.
  2. Separate the bok choy stalks and rinse them well, as grit hides at the base. Halve any large stalks and keep the stems and leaves apart.
  3. Bring the stock, water, oil, ginger and mushrooms to a boil in a covered pot over high heat, then simmer 5 minutes so the mushrooms flavor the broth. The oil keeps the bok choy glossy and green.
  4. Add the bok choy stems and boil for 1 minute, then the leaves; cover and wait for the pot to come back to a gentle boil.
  5. Drop in the pork a few slices at a time, stirring so they do not clump. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, just until the soup returns to a boil and the pork has turned pale; longer makes it tough.
  6. Season with salt and white pepper, scatter with green onion and serve at once, before the bok choy loses its color in the hot broth.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the pork hard makes it tough; keep the soup at a gentle simmer.
  • Adding the leaves with the stems overcooks them.

Authentic tips

  • Cantonese families often add a few goji berries or a slice of dried tangerine peel.
  • Soaking water from the mushrooms, strained, adds depth.

Substitutions

  • Napa cabbage replaces bok choy; simmer 4 minutes.
  • Minced pork balls instead of slices.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; the greens soften.

Freezing: Not recommended; the greens go limp.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with steamed fish
  • Soup: This is the soup course
  • Dessert: Fresh orange segments
  • Beverage: Hot tea
