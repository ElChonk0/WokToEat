Char Kway Teow (Stir-Fried Flat Noodles)

▾

Surprise me with

A hawker-stall icon of Malaysia and Singapore, char kway teow stir-fries flat rice noodles in dark soy and chili paste with Chinese sausage, shrimp, and cockles over ferocious wok heat — prized for the smoky "wok hei" only a blazing-hot wok can deliver.

Method

  1. Loosen the noodles into single strands; if cold and stiff, microwave them briefly. Mix the soy sauces, oyster sauce, chili paste, sugar and pepper. Cook in two batches of two servings each so the wok stays hot; a crowded wok steams the noodles.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat until it smokes, add half the lard and fry half the garlic for 10 seconds. Add half the sausage, fish cake and shrimp and toss for about 1 minute, until the shrimp just turn pink.
  3. Push everything to the side, crack in an egg and scramble it lightly for 20 seconds.
  4. Add half the noodles and half the sauce and toss with a lifting motion for 1 to 2 minutes, letting them sit against the hot wok between tosses so the edges char. That smoky char (wok hei) is the soul of the dish.
  5. Turn off the heat, add half the bean sprouts, chives and cockles and toss for 10 to 15 seconds so the sprouts stay crunchy and the cockles just warm through. Serve at once and repeat with the second batch.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking over insufficient heat, which fails to achieve the smoky "wok hei" flavor that defines this dish.
  • Overcrowding the wok, which steams rather than sears the noodles — cook in batches if needed for a home stove.

Authentic tips

  • The wok needs to be genuinely very hot: a flat-bottom carbon-steel or cast-iron wok on the highest setting of a gas, electric or induction stove, cooked in small batches so it does not cool down.
  • Traditional versions include cockles; they can be added along with the shrimp if available, though they're optional.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can replace or supplement shrimp for those avoiding shellfish.
  • No fresh kway teow: soak dried flat rice noodles until pliable, though texture differs slightly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1–2 days refrigerated; noodle texture softens on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture doesn't survive freezing.
