Xiā Mǐ Chǎo Fěn Sī (Stir-Fried Glass Noodles with Dried Shrimp) 虾米炒粉丝

Mung bean vermicelli stir-fried with dried shrimp, egg and cabbage is a thrifty Cantonese home dish; the shrimp season the noodles as they absorb the sauce.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 200 g7 ozMung bean glass noodles, soaked
  • 40 g1.5 ozDried shrimp, soaked and chopped
  • 33Eggs, beaten
  • 250 g9 ozNapa cabbage, shredded
  • 11Carrot, julienned
  • 44Fresh shiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 120 ml1/2 cupChicken stock or shrimp soaking water
  • 3 stalks3 stalksGreen onion, cut into lengths
  • 100 g3.5 ozBean sprouts

Method

  1. Drain the noodles and cut into shorter lengths with scissors.
  2. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a flat-bottom wok and scramble the eggs into soft pieces. Lift out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the dried shrimp over medium heat for 1 minute until fragrant, then stir-fry the cabbage, carrot and mushrooms for 2 minutes.
  4. Add the noodles, soy sauces, oyster sauce and stock and toss with tongs for 2 to 3 minutes until the noodles absorb the liquid.
  5. Add the eggs, bean sprouts and green onion and toss 30 seconds.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Long uncut noodles clump into a tangle; snip them.
  • Too little liquid leaves the noodles stiff and sticking to the wok.

Authentic tips

  • The shrimp soaking water is full of flavor; use it as the stock.
  • This is a good use for leftover cabbage.

Substitutions

  • Rice vermicelli replaces glass noodles.
  • Add shredded pork for a heartier dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; add a splash of water to reheat.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed fish
  • Soup: Tomato tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
