Suàn Pán Zi with Mushrooms (Hakka Vegetarian Abacus Seeds) 算盘子

Abacus seeds are Hakka beads made from a dough of yam or taro and tapioca starch; the meat-free version is built around mushrooms and pressed tofu.

  • Cook 30 min

Method

  1. Steam the taro for 25 minutes until very soft, mash it while hot, and knead in the tapioca starch and 15 ml of the oil until you have a smooth, slightly sticky dough.
  2. Roll the dough into 1.5 cm ropes, cut into 1.5 cm pieces and press each with your thumb into a flat bead with a dent in the middle.
  3. Boil the beads in a large pot of water for 4 to 5 minutes until they float, then drain.
  4. Heat the remaining oil in a wok, fry the garlic, mushrooms, pressed tofu and carrot for 3 minutes until the mushrooms are fragrant and the tofu is lightly golden.
  5. Add the soy sauce and the water, then the beads and choy sum, and cook for 3 minutes, stirring, until the sauce clings and the greens are tender. Season with salt and white pepper.

Common mistakes

  • Too much starch makes the beads tough; add it gradually until the dough just stops sticking.
  • Skipping the boil leaves the beads raw in the middle.

Authentic tips

  • Use the mushroom soaking liquid for the sauce, since it replaces the savoriness of dried shrimp.
  • Keep the beads even in size so they cook through together.

Substitutions

  • Yam or sweet potato replaces taro.
  • Fried tofu puffs replace pressed tofu.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: The uncooked beads freeze for 1 month on a tray before bagging.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
