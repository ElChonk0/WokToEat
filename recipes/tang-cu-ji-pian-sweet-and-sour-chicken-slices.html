Táng Cù Jī Piàn (Shandong Sweet-and-Sour Chicken) 糖醋鸡片

▾

Surprise me with

Sweet-and-sour sauces come from Shandong's coastal cooking; sweet-and-sour chicken slices follow the pattern of the province's sweet-and-sour carp.

Method

  1. Cut the chicken into thin slices and toss with a pinch of salt.
  2. Beat the egg with the cornstarch into a thick batter and coat the chicken.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry in batches for 3 minutes until golden; drain. Reheat the oil to 190 C (375 F) and refry 30 seconds.
  4. In a small pan simmer the sugar, vinegar, ketchup, ginger, garlic and 60 ml water for 3 minutes until syrupy.
  5. Toss the chicken in the sauce and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Saucing early makes the coating soggy.
  • Oil that is too cool makes the slices greasy.

Authentic tips

  • Double frying is key for crispness.
  • Bake at 220 C (425 F) for a lighter version.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works if sliced thin and fried quickly.
  • Rice vinegar replaces Chinkiang for a lighter flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the batter softens within 20 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
