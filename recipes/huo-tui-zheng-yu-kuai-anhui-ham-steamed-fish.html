Huo Tui Zheng Yu Kuai (Ham-Steamed Fish) 火腿蒸鱼块

Ham is a prized flavoring in Anhui and Jiangnan cooking, and steaming fish with a few slices of cured ham is a common way to season it.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 12 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozChilean sea bass, in 5 cm pieces
  • 50 g1.75 ozCured ham (Jinhua or Serrano), thinly sliced
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced
  • 30 g1 ozScallions, cut in lengths
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 1 g1/4 tspSalt
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 10 g1/3 ozCilantro

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry and rub with the wine, salt and white pepper.
  2. Arrange the fish on a heatproof plate, tuck the ham slices and ginger between the pieces, and scatter with half the scallions.
  3. Steam over high heat for 8 to 10 minutes until the fish flakes at the thickest part.
  4. Pour away the liquid, discard the cooked ginger and scallions, and drizzle the fish with the soy sauce.
  5. Top with the fresh scallions and cilantro, heat the oil until smoking, and pour it over.

Cook’s notes

Written for Chilean sea bass.

Common mistakes

  • Ham varies in salt, so skip the extra salt if using a very salty ham.
  • Over-steaming dries the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Bacon-free ham tastes better; a small amount is enough.
  • Pour the hot oil just before serving.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin replaces the sea bass.
  • Shiitake mushrooms replace the ham.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and greens
  • Soup: Clear chicken soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
