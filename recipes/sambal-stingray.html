Sambal Stingray (Grilled Stingray with Sambal)

▾

Surprise me with

Sambal stingray, grilled over charcoal and topped with spicy sambal, is a Singapore hawker favorite popularized at the Newton and Lau Pa Sat food centers.

Method

  1. Rub the stingray with the salt and turmeric. Soften the banana leaves by passing them briefly over a flame or dipping them in hot water so they fold without cracking.
  2. Blend the chilies, shallots, garlic, candlenuts, lemongrass and belacan into a paste. Fry it in the oil over medium-low heat for 15 minutes, stirring, until dark red and the oil separates, then add the tamarind, sugar and salt and cook 2 minutes. This sambal is what the fish is all about.
  3. Lay each piece of stingray on a banana leaf and spread a thick layer of sambal over the top.
  4. Grill on a griddle, cast-iron skillet or grill over medium heat, leaf-side down, for 10 to 12 minutes, until the leaf chars and the fish is cooked through and flakes from the cartilage. Cover with a lid or foil to help it cook evenly; finish under a broiler for a charred top if you like.
  5. Serve on the leaf, squeezed with calamansi and scattered with red onion, with extra sambal and rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the ray rubbery.
  • Burning the sambal makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Skate wing is a good substitute.
  • Serve with lime and rice.

Substitutions

  • Skate wing replaces stingray.
  • Foil replaces banana leaf.
