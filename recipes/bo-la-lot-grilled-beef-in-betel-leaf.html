Bò Lá Lốt (Grilled Beef in Betel Leaves)

▾

Surprise me with

Bo la lot, beef seasoned with lemongrass and wrapped in betel leaves, is a southern Vietnamese grilled specialty eaten with rice noodles and herbs.

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the lemongrass, garlic, shallots, fish sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, curry powder and pepper and leave for 30 minutes.
  2. Make the nuoc cham: stir the sugar into the warm water until dissolved, then add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic and chili. Taste; it should be sweet, sour and salty in balance, and light enough to pour over noodles.
  3. Lay a betel leaf shiny-side down, put a tablespoon of filling near the stem end, and roll it up into a finger-thick cigar, tucking in the sides. Thread 5 or 6 rolls side by side onto two parallel skewers.
  4. Brush with oil and grill over medium charcoal or in a grill pan for 3 to 4 minutes per side, until the leaves are charred and fragrant and the beef cooked. Betel leaves release a peppery, smoky aroma as they char.
  5. Brush with scallion oil, scatter with peanuts and serve with vermicelli, lettuce, herbs and nuoc cham.

Common mistakes

  • Loose rolls fall apart on the grill.
  • Charcoal that is too hot burns the leaves.

Authentic tips

  • Soak the skewers first.
  • Grape leaves are a substitute for betel leaves.

Substitutions

  • Grape leaves replace betel leaves.
  • Ground pork also works.
  • No grill: lay the skewers on a wire rack over a foil-lined sheet pan and broil on high about 10 cm (4 in) from the element for 4 to 5 minutes per side, or roast at 230°C (450°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until charred at the edges and cooked through.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked rolls for 2 months; grill from frozen.

Serve with
