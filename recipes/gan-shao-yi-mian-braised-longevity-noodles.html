Gān Shāo Yī Miàn (Braised Yi Mein Longevity Noodles) 干烧伊面

▾

Surprise me with

Yi mein, fried egg noodles braised with mushrooms and chives, are served at Cantonese birthday and wedding banquets because their length symbolizes long life.

  • 2 cakes (300 g total)2 cakes (10 oz total)Dried yi mein (e-fu) noodles
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, sliced
  • 250 g9 ozFresh shiitake or straw mushrooms, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozEnoki mushrooms
  • 100 g3.5 ozYellow chives or garlic chives, cut into lengths
  • 300 ml1 1/4 cupsVegetable stock
  • 30 ml2 tbspVegetarian oyster sauce (mushroom sauce)
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 4 g1 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Blanch the noodle cakes in boiling water for about 1.5 minutes, loosening them with chopsticks, until just pliable but still slightly firm. Drain them very well; waterlogged noodles turn mushy and dilute the sauce. Mix the stock, mushroom sauce, soy sauces and sugar.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it just smokes, add the oil and lower the heat. Fry the ginger for 20 seconds, then add the shiitake and sear for 1 to 2 minutes over high heat until browned. Splash the wine around the edge.
  3. Pour in the sauce and bring it to a boil, then add the noodles. Toss with chopsticks over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes, lifting and turning, until the noodles have soaked up nearly all the liquid.
  4. Add the enoki and chives and toss for 30 to 60 seconds, just until the chives wilt.
  5. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and pile onto a platter. Long, unbroken noodles are a wish for long life, so avoid cutting them.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the noodles is considered bad luck at a celebration, and blanching too long makes them break.
  • Too much liquid makes them soggy; braise until nearly dry.

Authentic tips

  • Yellow chives are the traditional banquet choice.
  • Lobster yi mein is the luxury banquet version.

Substitutions

  • Regular oyster sauce if it does not need to be vegetarian.
  • Fresh egg noodles, fried crisp first, can stand in for yi mein.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Braised shiitake with bok choy
  • Soup: Winter melon seafood soup
  • Dessert: Longevity peach buns
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
