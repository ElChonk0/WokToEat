Nikujaga (Beef and Potato Stew) 肉じゃが

▾

Surprise me with

A staple of Japanese home cooking often called "mother's taste," nikujaga simmers beef, potato, and onion in a sweet soy dashi broth — a dish many Japanese people associate with childhood and comfort.

Method

  1. Sauté beef in a little oil in a pot until lightly browned.
  2. Add onion and carrot, stir-frying 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add potatoes and dashi stock; bring to a boil, skimming any foam.
  4. Add soy sauce, mirin, and sugar; reduce to a simmer, cover partially, and cook 20–25 minutes until the potatoes are tender.
  5. Garnish with blanched snow peas before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring too often once the potatoes are added, which can break them apart before they're fully tender.
  • Skipping the foam-skimming step, which leaves the broth cloudier than it should be.

Authentic tips

  • This dish tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have fully melded into the potatoes.
  • A cartouche (parchment paper lid) helps the potatoes cook evenly while keeping the broth from reducing too much.

Substitutions

  • Pork can replace beef for a butajaga variation.
  • No dashi: a light beef or vegetable stock works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; flavor deepens.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the potato texture softens further.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
