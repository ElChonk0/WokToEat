Beef Teriyaki

▾

Surprise me with

Applying Japan's glossy teriyaki glaze technique to beef instead of the more common chicken, this dish sears steak and reduces a mirin-soy sauce directly in the pan for a quick, satisfying dinner.

Method

  1. Take the steaks out of the refrigerator 30 minutes before cooking and pat them dry, so they sear rather than steam. Season with salt and pepper. Mix the soy sauce, mirin, sake and sugar.
  2. Heat the oil in a cast-iron skillet over low heat and fry the garlic for 2 to 3 minutes until golden, then lift it out; burnt garlic is bitter.
  3. Raise the heat to high and sear the steaks for 2 to 3 minutes per side for medium-rare (54 C / 130 F), turning once. Rest them on a board for 5 minutes.
  4. Pour off most of the fat, lower the heat to medium and add the sauce. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring, until it reduces to a glossy glaze that coats a spoon.
  5. Slice the steak against the grain, return it to the pan to coat in the glaze, and scatter with the garlic chips, sesame seeds and green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the steak before it's rested, which lets the juices run out rather than redistribute.
  • Overcooking the steak, since a leaner cut like flank or sirloin turns tough well past medium.

Authentic tips

  • Slicing the beef thinly against the grain after resting is what keeps it tender despite being a leaner cut.
  • The sauce should reduce until it coats the back of a spoon — too thin and it won't cling properly.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same sauce and method.
  • No mirin: a mix of sake and a touch more sugar approximates it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently to avoid overcooking further.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of cooked steak suffers when frozen and reheated.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched broccoli
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
