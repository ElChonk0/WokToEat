Nyonya Asam Fish (Tamarind Fish Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Asam fish is a Nyonya dish of fish stewed in a sour, spicy tamarind gravy with lemongrass, galangal and okra, served with rice.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, both chilies, galangal and belacan into a smooth paste. Mix the tamarind paste with the water and strain out the seeds.
  2. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-low heat and fry the paste with the lemongrass for 8 to 10 minutes, until dark and the oil separates.
  3. Pour in the tamarind water, bring to a boil and add the okra; simmer for 3 to 4 minutes.
  4. Add the fish and tomatoes and simmer gently for 5 to 7 minutes, spooning the gravy over, until the fish is just cooked through. Shake the pan rather than stirring so the fish stays whole.
  5. Season with the sugar and salt; asam fish should be distinctly sour, spicy and a little sweet. Tear in the laksa leaves and serve with rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the fish hard breaks it up; move it with the pot, not the spoon.
  • Overcooking the okra makes it slimy; add it late.

Authentic tips

  • Use a firm fish that holds its shape.
  • The gravy tastes better after resting for 10 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Lime juice replaces some tamarind in a pinch.
  • Prawns replace fish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and fried egg
  • Soup: Not needed; the gravy is thin and sour
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Iced lemongrass tea
