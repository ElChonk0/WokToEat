Mazesoba (Brothless Ramen) まぜそば

▾

Surprise me with

Mazesoba, thick ramen noodles mixed with a spicy minced pork sauce, raw egg yolk and garlic, began as Taiwan mazesoba in Nagoya in 2008 and became a nationwide ramen-shop style.

Method

  1. Heat a little oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and cook the pork with the garlic for 4 to 5 minutes, breaking it up, until browned. Add the doubanjiang, chili, sake, half the soy sauce and the sugar and cook for 2 minutes until the liquid is gone and the pork is glossy and spicy.
  2. In each bowl, mix the remaining soy sauce, the sesame oil, vinegar and a spoonful of the pork fat from the pan. This is the tare that coats the noodles.
  3. Cook the noodles according to the packet, usually 2 to 3 minutes, drain well and toss them straight into the tare in each bowl.
  4. Top with the spicy pork, garlic chives, green onion and nori, and drop an egg yolk in the center.
  5. Mix everything together thoroughly before eating so the yolk and tare coat every strand. Finish the last of the sauce with a small scoop of rice (oimeshi), the classic way.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet noodles dilute the sauce.
  • Too little mixing leaves the flavors separate.

Authentic tips

  • Shops serve a spoon of rice (oimeshi) to mop up the sauce at the end.
  • A little fish powder (dashi powder) over the top adds depth.

Substitutions

  • Use very fresh eggs or a soft-boiled egg if you prefer.
  • Ground chicken replaces pork.

Storage

Leftovers: The pork topping keeps 3 days.

Freezing: The pork topping freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Small bowl of rice
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
