Chuàn Chuàn Xiāng (Sichuan Skewer Hot Pot) 串串香

▾

Surprise me with

Chuan chuan xiang is a Sichuan street food of ingredients threaded onto bamboo skewers and cooked in a bubbling, numbing-spicy broth, then dipped in sesame oil or a dry chili mix.

Method

  1. Thread the pork, lotus root, tofu, tofu skin, mushrooms, potato and pepper onto the soaked skewers, one or two pieces each.
  2. Melt the tallow in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry the doubanjiang for 3 to 5 minutes until the oil is deep red. Add the garlic, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, dried chilies and peppercorns and fry for 2 minutes without burning.
  3. Add the stock, soy sauce, rock sugar and salt and simmer for 15 minutes so the broth matures.
  4. Mix the sesame oil, minced garlic, peanuts and cilantro into small dipping bowls.
  5. Set the pot over a portable burner at the table and cook the skewers in the simmering broth: 1 to 2 minutes for the pork and greens, 3 to 4 for the potato and lotus root.
  6. Dip each skewer in the sesame oil before eating. Chengdu stalls charge by the skewer, so people count the empty sticks.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pot drops the temperature and toughens the pork; cook a few skewers at a time.
  • Using too much Sichuan pepper numbs the mouth and masks the flavor; keep to 1 tablespoon.

Authentic tips

  • Dry-fry the dried chilies lightly to release the aroma.
  • The broth gets better as it simmers, so top it up with stock.

Substitutions

  • Beef slices, shrimp and enoki mushrooms are common skewer choices.
  • A ready-made hot pot base replaces the broth.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and cold cucumber salad
  • Soup: The broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Iced watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold soy milk or suan mei tang
