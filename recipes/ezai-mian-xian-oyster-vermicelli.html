O-a-mi-sua (Oyster Vermicelli) 蚵仔麵線

▾

Oyster vermicelli, a thick bonito-scented soup of wheat vermicelli with oysters, is a Taiwanese street food often eaten at breakfast.

  • 200 g7 ozRed (brown) wheat vermicelli (mian xian)
  • 250 g9 ozSmall oysters, shucked
  • 20 g2 tbspSweet potato starch, for coating the oysters
  • 150 g5 ozPork intestine, braised (optional)
  • 1.2 L5 cupsPork or chicken stock
  • 15 g1/2 ozBonito flakes

  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 30 g1/4 cupSweet potato starch, mixed with 60 ml water, for thickening
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic, crushed to a paste
  • 15 ml1 tbspBlack vinegar
  • 15 g1/2 cupCilantro
  • to tasteto tasteWhite pepper and chili sauce

Method

  1. Simmer the stock with the bonito flakes for 2 minutes, then strain. Add the shiitake, soy sauce, salt and sugar.
  2. Rinse the vermicelli and add it to the broth; the red, steamed type simmers for 5 to 10 minutes until soft and slightly broken, which is how it is eaten. Thicken with the starch slurry until glossy.
  3. Toss the oysters in the coating starch and drop them into a separate pot of boiling water for 30 to 60 seconds, just until they firm and plump. The coating keeps them silky.
  4. Add the oysters and the braised intestine to the thick soup.
  5. Ladle into bowls and top with garlic paste, black vinegar, cilantro, white pepper and chili sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked oysters turn rubbery.
  • Adding the slurry too fast makes lumps.

Authentic tips

  • Thick, starchy soup is the goal.
  • Add pork intestines for the traditional version.

Substitutions

  • Cooked pork intestine is traditional.
  • Dried bonito stock can be replaced with chicken stock.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the vermicelli thickens as it sits, so add water when reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried dough sticks
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Winter melon tea
