Bánh Cuốn (Steamed Rice Rolls with Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

Banh cuon, silky steamed rice-batter rolls filled with pork and wood ear, are a northern Vietnamese breakfast, a specialty of Thanh Tri in Hanoi.

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, salt, oil and water into a thin, smooth batter and rest it for 1 hour. The tapioca makes the sheets supple and slightly translucent.
  2. Stir-fry the shallots in a little oil for 1 minute, then the pork for 4 minutes, breaking it up, then the wood ear, fish sauce, sugar and pepper for 2 minutes. Let it cool.
  3. Make the nuoc cham: stir the sugar into the warm water until dissolved, then add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic and chili. Taste; it should be sweet, sour and salty in balance, and light enough to pour over noodles.
  4. Heat a lightly oiled nonstick pan over medium heat. Stir the batter, pour in a thin layer and swirl to cover the pan, then cover with a lid for 30 to 45 seconds, until the sheet turns translucent and the edges lift.
  5. Turn the sheet out onto an oiled tray, add a line of filling and roll it up. Repeat, stirring the batter each time, since it settles.
  6. Top the rolls with fried shallots and serve with cha lua, bean sprouts, herbs and nuoc cham. A Hanoi breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Thick batter makes thick, rubbery rolls.
  • Overfilling splits the sheets.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with cha lua (Vietnamese sausage) and herbs.
  • Hanoi cooks steam the sheets on a cloth.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought banh cuon sheets save time.
  • Shrimp replaces pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day, re-steamed.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cha lua (pork roll) and herbs
  • Soup: Canh
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
