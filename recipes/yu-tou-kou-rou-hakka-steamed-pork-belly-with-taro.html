Yu Tou Kou Rou (Hakka Steamed Pork Belly with Taro) 芋头扣肉

Kou rou is a Hakka banquet dish of pork belly that is fried, sliced and steamed in layers; the taro version alternates the pork with fried taro, which soaks up the rendered fat.

Method

  1. Cover the pork belly with cold water, add the ginger and wine, simmer 30 minutes until a skewer slides in, then dry the slab and prick the skin all over.
  2. Rub the skin with the dark soy sauce, then fry skin-side down in 180 C (350 F) oil under a splatter screen for 4 to 5 minutes until blistered and deep brown.
  3. Soak the pork in cold water for 10 minutes so the skin wrinkles, then slice it 5 mm thick. Cut the taro into slices of the same size and fry them in the same oil for 3 to 4 minutes until golden at the edges.
  4. Mash the fermented bean curd with the light soy sauce, sugar and garlic, and toss it with the pork and taro slices.
  5. Layer the pork skin-side down in a deep heatproof bowl, alternating with the taro, tuck in the star anise and pour in the water.
  6. Steam over high heat for 1 hour 30 minutes, topping up the steamer with boiling water as needed, then invert the bowl onto a plate.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the cold-water soak leaves the skin smooth instead of wrinkled and chewy.
  • Frying without a screen is dangerous: wet skin spits violently, so dry it well first.

Authentic tips

  • Press the layers firmly into the bowl so the unmolded dish holds its dome.
  • The steaming liquid becomes a rich sauce; spoon it over the rice.

Substitutions

  • Sweet potato or yam replaces taro.
  • White fermented bean curd is milder than red.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; resteam for 15 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; thaw overnight and resteam.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched greens
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh orange wedges
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
