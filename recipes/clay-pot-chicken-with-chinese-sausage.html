Clay Pot Chicken with Chinese Sausage 腊肠滑鸡煲

A one-pot Cantonese comfort dish where rice, chicken, and cured sausage cook together in a clay pot so the fat from the sausage renders directly into the rice, forming a prized crispy crust (bao zai fan) at the bottom.

  • Cook 30 min

Method

  1. Marinate chicken in half the oyster sauce, half the soy sauce, sesame oil, and ginger for 15 minutes.
  2. Rinse rice and add to a cast-iron Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot (or a clay pot) with the water; bring to a boil.
  3. Once boiling, arrange the marinated chicken and sausage slices over the rice, cover, and reduce to low heat.
  4. Cook covered 18–20 minutes, resisting the urge to lift the lid, until the rice is tender and a crust has formed on the bottom.
  5. Mix remaining oyster sauce and soy sauce; drizzle over the top once cooked and let sit covered 5 minutes.
  6. Garnish with green onion and serve directly from the pot.

Common mistakes

  • Lifting the lid during cooking, which lets steam escape and prevents the bottom crust from forming.
  • Using too much water, which leaves the rice mushy instead of forming a proper crust.

Authentic tips

  • A cast-iron Dutch oven holds heat as evenly as clay and gives the crispy bottom layer on gas, electric or induction; a clay pot works on gas but can crack on electric or induction unless it is rated for them.
  • Drizzling the sauce in at the very end, off heat, keeps its flavor bright rather than cooked out.

Substitutions

  • Boneless chicken breast works but turns drier; thigh is preferred here.
  • No Chinese sausage: thick-cut bacon lardons are a reasonable, if less traditional, substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat covered with a splash of water to loosen the rice.

Freezing: Not recommended — the crispy crust texture doesn't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Stir-fried gai lan with garlic
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
