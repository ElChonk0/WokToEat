Háo Yóu Niú Ròu (Beef in Oyster Sauce) 蚝油牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Beef stir-fried with oyster sauce is a Cantonese restaurant standard; velveted slices and a glossy sauce are the hallmarks of the style.

Method

  1. Soak the beef slices in cold water for a few minutes, drain and squeeze dry, then mix with the marinade oyster sauce, soy sauce, cornstarch, water, pepper and baking soda. Stir in 1 tablespoon of the oil. Mix the sauce oyster sauce, half the dark soy and the sugar, and the slurry cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it smokes, lower to medium and add the remaining oil. Add a few ginger slices, then the beef. Leave it for 30 seconds, turn and stir-fry for about 50 seconds until 80% cooked. Lift out the beef and ginger, leaving the oil.
  3. Over medium heat fry the rest of the ginger, the garlic and the green onion whites for 30 seconds. Add the mushrooms for 30 seconds, the carrot and celery for 20 seconds, then the snow peas for 20 seconds.
  4. Splash the wine around the edge of the wok, raise the heat to high and stir-fry for 40 seconds. Return the beef, add the sauce and toss, then taste and add the rest of the dark soy for color.
  5. Stir in the slurry and toss until the sauce turns glossy. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking all the beef at once steams it gray and tough; work in batches.
  • Too much baking soda leaves a soapy taste; 1 teaspoon per 500 g is enough.

Authentic tips

  • Freeze the beef for 20 minutes before slicing for even, thin slices.
  • Slice against the grain, perpendicular to the visible muscle lines.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works with the same sauce.
  • Serve over blanched broccoli or choy sum to stretch it into a full dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok for 1 minute only.

Freezing: Not recommended; reheated beef turns grey and chewy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
