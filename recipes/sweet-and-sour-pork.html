Sweet and Sour Pork 咕噜肉

▾

Surprise me with

One of the most internationally recognized Cantonese dishes, sweet and sour pork batters and fries pork before tossing it in a bright, tangy sauce built on vinegar, ketchup, and pineapple — a dim sum and takeout staple.

  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 11Red and green bell pepper, cut into triangles
  • 11Onion, cut into triangles
  • 150 g1 cupPineapple chunks
  • 60 ml1/4 cupKetchup
  • 50 g1/4 cupBrown sugar
  • 45 ml3 tbspRice vinegar
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 3 g1 tspCornstarch, for the sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the garlic salt and soy sauce for 1 minute, then mix in the egg and leave for 15 minutes. Use a fatty cut such as shoulder; lean pork turns dry under the crust. Stir the ketchup, sugar, vinegar, salt, sauce cornstarch and water together.
  2. Massage the flour into the pork for 1 to 2 minutes, adding a dash of water if it looks dry, until each piece has a thin, sticky layer. Then roll each piece in the dredging cornstarch and shake off the excess. The double layer is what builds a thick, crunchy crust.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 160 to 175 C (320 to 350 F). Fry the pork in batches for 6 to 7 minutes, leaving it alone for the first minute so the coating sets, until golden and cooked through. Drain on a rack.
  4. Raise the oil to 200 C (400 F) and fry all the pork again for 1 to 2 minutes, until deep golden and crisp. The second, hotter fry is what keeps the pork crunchy once it meets the sauce. Drain.
  5. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Stir-fry the onion and peppers over high heat for 30 seconds, then the pineapple for 30 seconds, and lift them out.
  6. Pour the sauce into the wok and boil for 1 to 2 minutes until thick and glossy. Return the pork, vegetables and pineapple and toss quickly for about 1 minute until coated. Serve at once, as the crust softens the longer it sits.

Common mistakes

  • Tossing the pork in sauce too far ahead of serving, which softens the crispy coating.
  • Using canned pineapple syrup as a sauce base without adjusting sugar, which can make the dish overly sweet.

Authentic tips

  • Frying twice (rest, then re-fry briefly) gives an extra-crisp coating that holds up better once sauced.
  • Fresh pineapple gives a brighter, less syrupy flavor than canned, if available.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same method for a lighter version.
  • No rice vinegar: white vinegar works, though flavor is slightly sharper.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the coating softens on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended — fried texture doesn't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
