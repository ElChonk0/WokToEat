Braised Tofu & Mushrooms

Braised tofu with mushrooms is a homestyle Cantonese dish built on the idea that tofu, left to soak in a savory braising liquid, takes on far more flavor than it has on its own — a technique used across Chinese home cooking to make plant proteins satisfying.

Method

  1. Pan-fry the tofu slabs in oil over medium-high heat until golden on both sides, about 3 minutes per side; remove and set aside.
  2. In the same pan, sauté garlic, ginger, and mushrooms until they soften and release their liquid, about 4 minutes.
  3. Add soy sauce, mushroom oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, and stock; bring to a simmer.
  4. Return the tofu to the pan, spooning sauce over top, and simmer gently 5 minutes so the tofu absorbs the braising liquid.
  5. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer briefly until the sauce thickens to a light glaze.
  6. Garnish with green onion and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the initial pan-fry of the tofu, which leaves it soft and prone to falling apart during braising.
  • Using too much liquid relative to the pan, turning a braise into a soup — keep the sauce just enough to coat, not submerge.

Authentic tips

  • Pressing the tofu under a weighted plate for 15–20 minutes before cooking removes excess water and helps it pan-fry to a better golden crust.
  • Dried shiitake, rehydrated in warm water, gives a deeper, more concentrated mushroom flavor than fresh — save the soaking liquid as part of the stock.

Substitutions

  • King oyster or button mushrooms both work in place of shiitake.
  • Check that the mushroom oyster sauce is vegan; some brands contain trace animal products.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheats well; the tofu continues to absorb flavor, making it arguably better the next day.

Freezing: Not recommended — tofu's texture turns spongy when frozen and thawed.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
