Chicken and Chestnut Braise, Jiangsu-Style 栗子烧鸡

▾

Surprise me with

A lighter, more wine-forward cousin of the Cantonese chestnut braise, this Jiangsu version uses less soy sauce and more Shaoxing wine, reflecting the region's generally more delicate, less salty flavor profile.

  • Cook 35 min

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, bone-in, cut into pieces
  • 250 g9 ozPeeled cooked chestnuts
  • 20 ml4 tspLight soy sauce
  • 60 ml1/4 cupShaoxing wine
  • 15 g1 tbspRock sugar
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion
  • 300 ml1.25 cupsChicken stock or water

Method

  1. Brown chicken pieces lightly in oil, then add ginger and green onion, stirring briefly.
  2. Add soy sauce, wine, and rock sugar, tossing to coat the chicken.
  3. Add stock, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 20 minutes.
  4. Add chestnuts and continue simmering uncovered 10 minutes until the sauce reduces slightly and the chestnuts warm through.
  5. Serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Using too much soy sauce, which pushes the flavor toward Cantonese-style rather than the intended lighter Jiangsu profile.
  • Adding the chestnuts too early and letting them break down into mush.

Authentic tips

  • This braise relies more on wine than soy sauce for its character — don't be tempted to add extra soy for color.
  • A little extra rock sugar toward the end can round out the sauce if it tastes too sharp from the wine.

Substitutions

  • Peeled vacuum-packed chestnuts save the soaking time.
  • Add shiitake mushrooms for extra depth.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the chestnuts soften but taste better.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months, though the chestnuts become mealy.

  • Dessert: Fresh pear
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
