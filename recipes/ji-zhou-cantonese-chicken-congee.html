Jī Zhōu (Cantonese Chicken Congee) 鸡粥

▾

Surprise me with

Congee (jook) is the everyday rice porridge of southern China, eaten for breakfast and when unwell; Cantonese cooks break the rice down until it is silky and cook chicken in it.

Method

  1. Rinse the rice three times, drain well and mix with the oil and salt; leave for 15 minutes. The oil and salt help the grains break apart into a smooth, creamy congee. Use raw rice, not cooked, which turns lumpy.
  2. Take the chicken off the bone and cut it into thin, bite-size slices, keeping the bones. Bring the water to a boil in a Dutch oven or large pot, add the rice and the bones, stir once and return to a boil.
  3. Lower to a steady simmer, partly cover and cook for 1 hour, stirring every 10 minutes and scraping the bottom, until the grains have burst. For an extra-silky texture, whisk the pot hard for 2 to 3 minutes at the end. Thin with boiling water whenever it gets thicker than heavy cream.
  4. While it cooks, mix the chicken with the oyster sauce, wine, cornstarch and water, massaging for 30 seconds until absorbed, then stir in the oil, which keeps the slices from clumping in the pot.
  5. Lift out the bones. Turn the heat up so the congee bubbles and add the chicken a little at a time over 30 to 60 seconds while stirring, then simmer for 2 to 3 minutes until it is cooked through and the congee returns to a boil. Add half the ginger.
  6. Season with salt and white pepper and serve in bowls topped with the remaining ginger, green onion, cilantro and a few drops of sesame oil.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Not stirring lets the rice catch and scorch on the bottom, especially on electric and induction hobs.
  • Adding the chicken at the start overcooks it; it needs only 10 minutes.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with youtiao (fried dough sticks) to dip, the classic pairing.
  • A clay pot is the traditional vessel; a heavy Dutch oven holds the simmer just as evenly.

Substitutions

  • Leftover cooked rice makes congee in 30 minutes: use 400 g with 1.5 L water.
  • Add sliced shiitake or a century egg with the chicken.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; it sets firm, so reheat with a splash of water, stirring.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; the texture loosens slightly but is fine reheated with stirring.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Youtiao (Chinese fried dough)
  • Soup: Not needed; the congee is the soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding (dou fu fa)
  • Beverage: Soy milk or hot tea
