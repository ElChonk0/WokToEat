Bun Bo Nam Bo (Southern Beef Vermicelli Bowl)

▾

Surprise me with

A southern Vietnamese noodle salad topping vermicelli with lemongrass-marinated beef, fresh herbs, and pickled vegetables, dressed with nuoc cham — lighter and served at room temperature, unlike its soup-based northern cousin.

Method

  1. Marinate the beef with the lemongrass, garlic, fish sauce, sugar and 1 teaspoon of oil for 20 minutes.
  2. Make the nuoc cham: stir the sugar into the warm water until dissolved, then add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic and chili. Taste; it should be sweet, sour and salty in balance, and light enough to pour over noodles.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the beef in two batches for 1 to 2 minutes until browned at the edges. Add the onion with the last batch for 1 minute.
  4. Divide the vermicelli among bowls over a bed of lettuce, herbs and bean sprouts, and top with the hot beef and its juices.
  5. Add the pickles, peanuts and fried shallots, pour over plenty of nuoc cham and toss before eating. Despite the name ('southern'), this dish comes from Hanoi.

Common mistakes

  • Not rinsing the noodles after cooking, which leaves them starchy and prone to clumping.
  • Skimping on the nuoc cham — this dish depends on it for most of its seasoning.

Authentic tips

  • Make the nuoc cham ahead; it keeps well refrigerated for over a week.
  • A mix of fresh herbs, not just one, gives this bowl its characteristic bright, complex flavor.

Substitutions

  • Grilled pork or shrimp can replace or supplement beef for variety.
  • No rice vermicelli: any thin rice noodle works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store noodles, beef, and sauce separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Nuoc cham and fried shallots are included
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Che
  • Beverage: Iced tea
