Beef Nihari (Slow-Cooked Mughlai Beef Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

Nihari, a slow-cooked beef shank stew with a rich gravy, originated in Old Delhi's Mughal kitchens and is traditionally eaten for breakfast with naan.

Method

  1. Heat the ghee in a large Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the onion for 12 to 15 minutes until deep brown. Lift out half for garnish.
  2. Add the ginger-garlic paste for 1 minute, then the beef and bones and fry for 5 minutes until browned. Add the nihari masala, chili, turmeric, fennel and ground ginger with a splash of water and fry for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Add the water and salt, bring to a boil, then cover tightly and cook over the lowest heat for 5 to 6 hours (or overnight in a 120 C / 250 F oven), until the meat falls apart. Nihari, from the Arabic nahar (morning), was cooked overnight and eaten at dawn.
  4. Whisk the atta with 250 ml (1 cup) of water until smooth, then stir it into the stew. Simmer for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until the gravy is thick and silky.
  5. Skim off some of the spiced fat (the tari) and keep it to spoon over each bowl.
  6. Serve topped with the tari, fried onions, ginger, green chilies, cilantro and lemon, with naan.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the meat tough.
  • Lumpy flour paste: mix it to a smooth slurry.

Authentic tips

  • Traditionally cooked overnight.
  • Eaten for breakfast with naan.

Substitutions

  • Lamb shank is traditional.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the cooking to 75 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Naan
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is the main
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Masala chai
