Hokkien Mee

Hokkien mee reflects the Hokkien (Fujianese) immigrant community's influence on Singaporean and Malaysian street food, combining yellow noodles and rice noodles stir-fried in a rich seafood-and-pork stock until the noodles nearly absorb it entirely.

Method

  1. If you have shrimp shells, simmer them in the stock 10 minutes to deepen the flavor, then strain; skip this with ready-made stock.
  2. Parboil both noodle types briefly, just to loosen them, and drain.
  3. Heat oil in a wok and stir-fry the pork until lightly browned; add shrimp and cook until just pink.
  4. Push the protein aside, crack in the eggs, and scramble briefly.
  5. Add garlic, then both noodles, tossing to combine.
  6. Pour in the stock gradually, letting the noodles absorb it as they cook — the defining technique of this dish, rather than a quick toss in sauce.
  7. Add oyster sauce and soy sauce, continuing to toss and let the liquid reduce until the noodles are tender, richly flavored, but not soupy.
  8. Stir in bean sprouts in the last minute; serve with lime and chili sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Treating this like a standard noodle stir-fry and adding stock all at once — letting the noodles slowly absorb it over several minutes gives Hokkien mee its signature texture.
  • Skipping the shrimp-shell stock step when shells are available — a simple step that meaningfully deepens the seafood flavor.

Authentic tips

  • A mix of two noodle textures — springy yellow noodles and soft rice vermicelli — is traditional and adds more textural interest than either noodle alone.
  • This dish rewards patience — letting the noodles cook a few extra minutes in the stock separates good Hokkien mee from average.

Substitutions

  • Squid can be added alongside or instead of shrimp.
  • Chicken can replace pork for a lighter, though less traditional, version.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheats reasonably well with a splash of water or stock to loosen the noodles.

Freezing: Not recommended — the noodle texture suffers significantly after freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sambal belacan and lime
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Chinese tea
