Liang Mian (Sichuan Cold Sesame Noodles) 凉面

Cold noodles dressed with sesame paste, chili oil and vinegar are a Sichuan street food and summer staple, sold from vendors across Chengdu.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozWheat noodles
  • 60 g4 tbspSesame paste
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic, grated
  • 150 g5 ozCucumber, julienned
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Cook the noodles in boiling water until just tender, drain and rinse under cold water until cool.
  2. Toss with the oil so they do not stick, spread out and fan or chill.
  3. Whisk the sesame paste with the soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, garlic and chili oil, thinning with 3 tablespoons of cold water until pourable.
  4. Toss the noodles with the sauce and top with the cucumber.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the noodles to clump gives a lumpy dish; oil and fan them as soon as they are rinsed.
  • A sauce that is too thick will not coat; thin it with water.

Authentic tips

  • Thin the sesame paste slowly or it seizes.
  • Add a pinch of ground Sichuan pepper for the classic numbing note.

Substitutions

  • Soba or rice noodles replace the wheat noodles.
  • Crushed peanuts add crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; loosen with a little water before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Smashed cucumber
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Iced jasmine tea
