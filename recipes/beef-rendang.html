Beef Rendang

▾

Surprise me with

Originating from the Minangkabau people of West Sumatra, rendang is a slow-braised beef dish cooked in coconut milk and spice paste until nearly dry and deeply caramelized — often cited as one of the world’s most delicious dishes.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, dried and fresh chilies, ginger, galangal and turmeric into a smooth paste with a splash of water.
  2. Make the kerisik if you do not have it: toast unsweetened grated coconut in a dry skillet over medium-low heat for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring constantly, until deep golden brown, then pound it until oily.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or wide wok over medium heat and fry the spice paste (bumbu) with the lemongrass, turmeric leaf and lime leaves, stirring often, for 8 to 10 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried bumbu tastes sharp and grassy; this step is the base of Indonesian cooking.
  4. Add the beef and stir for 5 minutes until coated, then pour in the coconut milk and bring to a boil. Lower the heat and simmer uncovered for 2 hours, stirring every 15 minutes, as the coconut milk reduces and the beef becomes tender.
  5. Stir in the kerisik, tamarind, palm sugar and salt. Keep cooking over low heat for 45 to 60 minutes more, stirring often now that it is thick, until the liquid has gone and the coconut oil separates.
  6. Continue to fry the beef in that oil, stirring and scraping constantly, for 10 to 15 minutes, until the paste turns dark brown and clings to the meat. This last frying stage is what makes a true dry rendang and preserves it.
  7. Rest for at least 30 minutes; rendang tastes best the next day. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — rendang is defined by patience; the sauce needs real time to darken and concentrate.
  • Skipping the kerisik, which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • True rendang should end up almost dry, with the sauce clinging to the meat rather than pooling.
  • Slow-cooked beef chuck is the most traditional protein for rendang and becomes meltingly tender by the end.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or pork can be prepared the same way with a shorter cooking time.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably, a hallmark of good rendang.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or lontong
