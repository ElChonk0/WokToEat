Korean BBQ Pork Belly (Samgyeopsal) 삼겹살

▾

Surprise me with

Samgyeopsal ("three-layer flesh," referring to pork belly's alternating fat and meat) is a Korean barbecue institution, grilled tableside and eaten wrapped in lettuce with garlic, ssamjang, and grilled kimchi.

Method

  1. Mix the doenjang, gochujang, sesame oil and minced garlic into ssamjang, adding a splash of water to loosen it. Mix the dipping sesame oil with the salt and pepper in small dishes. Wash and dry the lettuce and perilla leaves.
  2. Heat a cast-iron grill pan, tabletop grill or heavy skillet over medium heat. A pan with a drain, or a slight tilt, lets the fat run away so the pork crisps instead of frying in its own fat; otherwise blot the pan with a paper towel now and then.
  3. Lay the pork slices on the grill without crowding. No marinade is needed; good pork belly needs only salt. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until golden and crisp underneath, then turn and cook 2 to 3 minutes more.
  4. Cut the strips into bite-size pieces with kitchen scissors as they finish, and push them to the cooler edge. Grill the garlic, chilies, mushrooms and kimchi in the rendered fat until softened and caramelized.
  5. To eat, dip a piece of pork in the sesame-oil salt and wrap it in lettuce with garlic, kimchi, green onion salad and a dab of ssamjang.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the pork too thin for grilling, which can dry it out quickly on high heat — thicker slices hold up better.
  • Not rendering enough fat before serving, which can leave the dish greasy rather than crisp at the edges.

Authentic tips

  • Using kitchen scissors to cut the pork into bite-size pieces at the table is a genuine and practical Korean BBQ tradition.
  • Grilling the kimchi alongside the pork mellows its sourness and adds a smoky depth.

Substitutions

  • No grill: a very hot cast-iron pan works well as an indoor substitute.
  • Pork jowl (moksal) is another popular cut for this style of grilling.
  • Oven: roast the slices on a rack over a foil-lined sheet pan at 230°C (450°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, flipping once and adding the garlic and kimchi for the last 5 minutes, then broil 2 minutes to crisp.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan to re-crisp.

Freezing: Freezes well, raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lettuce, perilla leaves, ssamjang and garlic
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Soju or cold beer
