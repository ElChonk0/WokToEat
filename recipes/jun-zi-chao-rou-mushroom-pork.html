Yě Shēng Jūn Chǎo Ròu (Wild Mushroom Pork Stir-Fry) 野生菌炒肉

▾

Surprise me with

When Yunnan's wild mushrooms arrive in the summer rains, cooks stir-fry them with pork and chilies for a savory seasonal dish.

  • 400 g14 ozPork shoulder, sliced
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce, for the marinade
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 300 g10 ozMixed mushrooms (wild or cultivated), sliced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

  • 60 g2 ozGreen chilies, sliced
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce, for the sauce
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the marinade soy sauce, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water.
  2. Wild mushrooms must be cooked through: Yunnan cooks stir-fry them for at least 8 to 10 minutes with plenty of garlic, and only buy from trusted sources. Cultivated mushrooms such as king oyster, shiitake and oyster are a safe substitute.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil and stir-fry the mushrooms with the garlic over medium-high heat for 8 to 10 minutes until cooked through and browned.
  4. Add the remaining oil and the pork and stir-fry for 2 minutes until cooked, then the green chilies for 1 minute.
  5. Season with the Shaoxing wine, soy sauce and salt and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Crowding the pan steams the mushrooms.
  • Wild mushrooms must be cooked thoroughly.

Authentic tips

  • Cultivated mixed mushrooms work if wild ones are not available.
  • Do not rinse mushrooms long; wipe them clean.

Substitutions

  • Cultivated mixed mushrooms replace wild ones.
  • Beef takes the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
