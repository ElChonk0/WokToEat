Xiāng Xī Jiàng Niú Ròu (Western Hunan Sauced Beef) 湘西酱牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Beef braised in a soy and spice sauce and sliced cold is a staple of Xiangxi's Tujia and Miao communities, where cattle are raised in the hills.

  • Cook 2.5 hr

  • 700 g1.5 lbBeef shank, whole
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 60 g2 ozGinger and green onion
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 60 ml1/4 cupLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 g1/2 ozStar anise, cinnamon, bay leaves and Sichuan pepper
  • 10 g1/3 ozDried chilies
  • 1.2 L5 cupsWater
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil and cilantro, for serving

Method

  1. Blanch the shank whole for 10 minutes and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven and fry the ginger and green onion for 1 minute, then melt the rock sugar until amber. Turn the shank in the caramel until glossy.
  3. Add the soy sauces, Shaoxing wine, spices, chilies and water, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, until a chopstick slides in.
  4. Leave the beef to cool in the liquid for at least 2 hours, or overnight, so it absorbs the dark, spiced sauce (jiang) right through.
  5. Slice thinly against the grain and serve cold, drizzled with a little braising liquid and chili oil and scattered with cilantro.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the shank tough and stringy.
  • Slicing warm tears the beef; chill first.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the braising liquid for another batch; it improves each time.
  • Serve cold with chili dip for a typical Xiangxi snack.

Substitutions

  • Brisket replaces shank.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the braising liquid; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or steamed buns
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
