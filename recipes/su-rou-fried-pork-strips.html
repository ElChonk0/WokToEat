Sū Ròu (Crispy Fried Pork Strips) 酥肉

▾

Surprise me with

Su rou, pork strips coated in batter, fried crisp and often simmered in soup, is a northern Chinese New Year dish, found in Beijing and across the north.

Method

  1. Marinate the pork with the salt, five-spice, Sichuan pepper, ginger and Shaoxing wine for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the egg, cornstarch, flour and water into a thick batter and coat the pork strips.
  3. Heat the oil to 160 C (325 F) in a Dutch oven and fry the pork in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until pale gold and cooked through. Drain.
  4. Raise the oil to 190 C (375 F) and fry everything again for 1 minute until crisp and golden.
  5. Serve hot with pepper salt. Su rou is a northern Lunar New Year snack; leftovers are simmered in hot pot or stews, where the batter turns silky.

Common mistakes

  • A thin batter slides off; it should coat the back of a spoon.
  • Skipping the second fry leaves the coating soft.

Authentic tips

  • Su rou is often made ahead for New Year and reheated in soup.
  • Reheat in a 200 C (400 F) oven to recrisp.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh strips take the same batter.
  • Bake at 220 C (425 F) for a lighter version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; recrisp in a hot oven, or add to soup.

Freezing: Freeze fried strips for 2 months; add straight to soup.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear broth with greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
