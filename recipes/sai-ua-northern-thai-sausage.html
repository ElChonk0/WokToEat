Sai Ua (Northern Thai Herb Sausage) ไส้อั่ว

▾

Surprise me with

Sai ua, a coarse pork sausage packed with lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime and red curry paste, is a specialty of Chiang Mai and Lanna cooking.

Method

  1. Pound the lemongrass, galangal, shallots, garlic and turmeric into a paste with the red curry paste. Pounding gives sai ua its fragrant, slightly coarse texture.
  2. Mix the paste into the cold pork with the lime leaves, fish sauce, salt, sugar and herbs, kneading for 2 minutes until sticky. Fry a small patty to taste and adjust the seasoning.
  3. Slide the casing onto a sausage stuffer or funnel, tie the end and fill it, pressing out air pockets, into one long coil. Prick any bubbles with a needle. Without casings, shape the mixture into patties.
  4. Rest the coil in the refrigerator for 1 hour, or overnight, so the flavors meld.
  5. Grill the coil over medium-low charcoal or bake on a rack at 190 C (375 F), turning once, for 20 to 30 minutes, until browned and the center reaches 71 C (160 F). Low heat cooks it through before the casing splits.
  6. Rest for 5 minutes, slice and serve with sticky rice, fresh ginger, peanuts and chilies.

Common mistakes

  • Overstuffing bursts the casing.
  • High heat burns the outside before the inside cooks.

Authentic tips

  • Do not skip the prick; steam needs an escape.
  • Chiang Mai sai ua is traditionally served with sticky rice.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork wrapped in foil and grilled replaces casings.
  • Reduce the curry paste for a milder sausage.
  • In the oven: bake at 180°C (350°F) for 20 to 25 minutes, turning once, then broil 2 minutes to brown.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months, raw or cooked.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice, raw cabbage and chilies
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
