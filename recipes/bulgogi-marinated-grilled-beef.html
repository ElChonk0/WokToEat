Bulgogi (Marinated Grilled Beef) 불고기

Korea's most internationally recognized dish, bulgogi ("fire meat") marinates thin beef slices in a pear-soy marinade before grilling — a dish with roots tracing back centuries, now a global Korean food ambassador.

  • Cook 10 min

Method

  1. Combine soy sauce, grated pear, garlic, sesame oil, and sugar for the marinade.
  2. Coat beef thoroughly and marinate at least 1 hour, or overnight for best flavor.
  3. Heat a pan or grill over high heat and cook beef and onion together, stirring occasionally, 5–7 minutes until charred and cooked through.
  4. Garnish with sesame seeds and green onion before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Over-marinating past 24 hours, since the pear's enzymes can start to break down the meat's texture too much.
  • Cooking over too gentle heat, which misses the char that gives bulgogi its signature smoky edge.

Authentic tips

  • Grated Asian pear is both a sweetener and natural tenderizer, a genuinely distinctive part of the marinade.
  • High, quick heat is essential; this dish should sear and char, not stew in its own liquid.

Substitutions

  • Pork or chicken thigh both work well with the same marinade and method.
  • No Asian pear: a regular pear or a small amount of grated apple works reasonably well.
  • No grill: spread the meat in a single layer on a foil-lined sheet pan and broil on the top rack for 3 to 4 minutes, flip, and cook 2 to 3 minutes more until charred at the edges; work in two batches so it browns instead of steaming. Broil the onion on the same pan.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; great in a rice bowl.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with lettuce wraps and ssamjang
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Barley tea
