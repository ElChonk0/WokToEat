Chicken Chettinad

▾

Surprise me with

From the Chettinad region of Tamil Nadu, this fiery, deeply aromatic curry is built on a distinctive roasted spice blend of black pepper, star anise, and curry leaf, reflecting the region's reputation for bold, complex spicing.

Method

  1. Dry-toast dried chilies, peppercorns, coriander seeds, fennel seeds, and star anise until fragrant; grind into a spice powder.
  2. Sauté onion and curry leaves in oil until the onion softens and curry leaves crisp slightly.
  3. Add ginger-garlic paste and cook 1 minute, then add the ground spice powder, stirring briefly.
  4. Add tomato and cook until it breaks down into the spices, 3–4 minutes.
  5. Add chicken and stir to coat, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  6. Pour in coconut milk (or water for a drier version), cover, and simmer 20 minutes until the chicken is tender.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry-toasting of whole spices, which is essential to Chettinad cooking's distinctive roasted, aromatic character.
  • Grinding the spices too far in advance — freshly ground spice powder has noticeably more aroma.

Authentic tips

  • This curry can be made drier (without coconut milk, just chicken's own juices) for a more traditional, intensely spiced version.
  • Fresh curry leaves make a real difference here; dried ones lose most of their aroma.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs shorten the cooking by 10 minutes.
  • Dry-roast the whole spices; ground Chettinad masala is a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
