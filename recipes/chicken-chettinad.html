Chicken Chettinad

▾

Surprise me with

From the Chettinad region of Tamil Nadu, this fiery, deeply aromatic curry is built on a distinctive roasted spice blend of black pepper, star anise, and curry leaf, reflecting the region's reputation for bold, complex spicing.

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with turmeric and half the salt.
  2. Dry-roast the chilies, peppercorns, coriander, fennel, cumin, poppy seeds, whole spices and kalpasi in a skillet over medium-low heat for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant and a shade darker. Add the coconut and roast for 2 to 3 minutes more until golden. Cool and grind with a little water to a paste. Freshly roasted, black-pepper-heavy masala is the Chettinad signature.
  3. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and fry the onion and curry leaves for 8 to 10 minutes until golden. Add the ginger-garlic paste for 1 minute, then the tomatoes for 5 minutes until soft.
  4. Add the chicken and cook for 5 minutes, then the ground masala and fry for 3 minutes, stirring, until it coats the chicken and the oil separates.
  5. Add the water and remaining salt, cover and simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the gravy thick. Serve with rice or parotta.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry-toasting of whole spices, which is essential to Chettinad cooking's distinctive roasted, aromatic character.
  • Grinding the spices too far in advance — freshly ground spice powder has noticeably more aroma.

Authentic tips

  • This curry can be made drier (without coconut milk, just chicken's own juices) for a more traditional, intensely spiced version.
  • Fresh curry leaves make a real difference here; dried ones lose most of their aroma.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs shorten the cooking by 10 minutes.
  • Dry-roast the whole spices; ground Chettinad masala is a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
