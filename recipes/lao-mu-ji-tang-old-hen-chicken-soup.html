Lǎo Mǔ Jī Tāng (Old Hen Soup) 老母鸡汤

▾

Surprise me with

Slow-simmered old hen soup is a Jiangnan home staple, valued as a restorative for new mothers and the unwell, and used as the base for countless local dishes.

  • Cook 3 hr

  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, sliced
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, knotted
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 66Dried shiitake mushrooms, soaked

  • 10 g1 tbspGoji berries
  • 8 g1.5 tspSalt
  • 150 g5 ozBok choy (optional)

Method

  1. Put the chicken in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil and boil for 3 minutes. Drain and rinse in warm water.
  2. Put the chicken in a clay pot or Dutch oven with the ginger, green onion and the measured cold water. Bring slowly to a boil, skimming carefully.
  3. Add the Shaoxing wine and shiitake, then cover and simmer over the lowest heat for 2.5 to 3 hours. An old hen has tougher meat but far more flavor than a young bird, and needs this long, gentle simmer. Do not let it boil, or the broth turns cloudy.
  4. Add the goji berries for the last 10 minutes and the bok choy for the last 2 minutes.
  5. Season with salt only at the end and serve the clear, golden broth with the chicken and mushrooms.

Common mistakes

  • A hard boil clouds the soup; it should barely tremble.
  • Salting early firms the meat, so season at the end.

Authentic tips

  • Older hens give a richer soup than young chicken; use a stewing hen if you can find one.
  • The broth is the base for countless Jiangnan dishes and freezes well.

Substitutions

  • A regular whole chicken replaces the old hen; simmer 1 hour less.
  • Add dried scallops or Jinhua ham for more depth.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; skim the fat after chilling.

Freezing: Freezes very well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
