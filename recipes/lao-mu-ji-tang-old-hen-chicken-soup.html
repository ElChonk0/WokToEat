Lǎo Mǔ Jī Tāng (Old Hen Soup) 老母鸡汤

▾

Surprise me with

Slow-simmered old hen soup is a Jiangnan home staple, valued as a restorative for new mothers and the unwell, and used as the base for countless local dishes.

  • Cook 2 hr

  • 1.2 kg2.6 lbChicken, cut into pieces, bone-in
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger

  • 30 g1 ozShiitake mushrooms
  • 10 g1 tbspGoji berries
  • 2.5 L10 cupsWater
  • 10 g2 tspSalt

Method

  1. Put the chicken in a pot, cover with cold water, bring to a boil for 3 minutes, then drain and rinse off the scum.
  2. Return the chicken to a clean pot with the 2.5 L fresh water, ginger, wine and mushrooms and bring to a boil.
  3. Reduce to the lowest simmer, partly cover and cook 2 hours, skimming occasionally, until the broth is golden and the meat is very tender.
  4. Add the goji berries for the last 10 minutes and season with the salt just before serving.
  5. Skim off the fat, or chill and lift it from the top.

Common mistakes

  • A hard boil clouds the soup; it should barely tremble.
  • Salting early firms the meat, so season at the end.

Authentic tips

  • Older hens give a richer soup than young chicken; use a stewing hen if you can find one.
  • The broth is the base for countless Jiangnan dishes and freezes well.

Substitutions

  • A regular whole chicken replaces the old hen; simmer 1 hour less.
  • Add dried scallops or Jinhua ham for more depth.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; skim the fat after chilling.

Freezing: Freezes very well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
