Steamed Beef Meatballs 牛肉丸

▾

Surprise me with

A dim sum classic, these springy steamed beef balls get their distinctive bouncy texture from vigorous hand-mixing with baking soda — traditionally topped with a dried tangerine peel for aroma.

  • Prep 30 min (plus overnight resting)
  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. The day before, beat the beef with the salt for 2 minutes in a stand mixer with the paddle on medium-low, or by hand in one direction. Dissolve the baking soda in the cold water and add it in three parts, letting each be absorbed, then beat for 5 minutes more. Cover and refrigerate for 8 to 12 hours. This rest with baking soda is what gives the classic soft, springy bounce.
  2. Soak the tangerine peel in hot water for 20 minutes, scrape off the white pith and mince it. Mince the water chestnuts, green onion and cilantro.
  3. Beat the beef again for 5 to 10 minutes, adding the pork fat, tangerine peel, soy sauce, sugar, pepper, sesame oil and neutral oil. By hand, slap the mixture against the bowl repeatedly; it should turn pale, sticky and smooth.
  4. Mix the starch with its water and beat it in in three additions, then fold in the water chestnuts, green onion and cilantro. Chill for at least 1 hour so the mixture firms up and is easier to shape.
  5. Line a heatproof plate with the soaked bean curd sheet, which keeps the meatballs out of the juices as they steam. With wet hands, shape the mixture into balls about the size of a golf ball and set them on the sheet with space between them.
  6. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer and bring the water to a full boil. Steam covered over high heat for 12 to 14 minutes, until firm and no pink remains in the center. Serve hot with Worcestershire sauce for dipping.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the vigorous mixing and slapping step, which is essential for the dish's signature bouncy, springy texture.
  • Using lean beef without any fat, which produces drier, less tender meatballs.

Authentic tips

  • The slapping technique (repeatedly throwing the mixture against the bowl) develops the protein structure for a genuinely springy bite — don't skip it.
  • Baking soda helps tenderize the beef and contributes to the characteristic bouncy texture of dim sum-style beef balls.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork can replace beef, though the classic dim sum version specifically uses beef.
  • No dried tangerine peel: a little fresh orange zest is a reasonable, if less traditional, substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming.

Freezing: Freezes well, uncooked, for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Rice congee or steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
