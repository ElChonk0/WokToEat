Ròu Xiàn Bǐng (Beijing Meat Pies) 肉馅饼

▾

Surprise me with

Xian bing, pan-fried meat-filled pies, are a classic Beijing breakfast and street food of Hui and Han origin.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 300 g2.5 cupsFlour
  • 180 ml3/4 cupHot water
  • 400 g14 ozPork, ground
  • 100 g3.5 ozScallions
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Mix the flour and hot water into a shaggy dough, knead 5 minutes and rest, covered, 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork with the scallions, soy sauce and sesame oil, stirring in one direction until sticky.
  3. Divide the dough into 4 pieces, roll each into a 14 cm circle, put a quarter of the filling in the center and gather the edges to seal; flatten gently.
  4. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat and fry the pies 4-5 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.
  5. Serve hot, cut into wedges.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling tears the dough.
  • High heat browns the outside before the filling cooks.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the pies thin for a crisp, even bake.
  • Add a spoonful of water to the filling for juice.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef or lamb replaces the pork.
  • Napa cabbage can be added to the filling for a lighter pie.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot dry pan.

Freezing: Freeze cooked pies for 2 months; reheat from frozen over low heat.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Millet porridge
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
