Téng Jiāo Yú Piàn (Green Sichuan Pepper Fish Slices) 藤椒鱼片

▾

Surprise me with

Teng jiao is green Sichuan pepper, prized for a citrusy, tingling numbness; it flavors a modern style of poached fish slices that is milder than the red-chili boiled fish.

Method

  1. Mix the fish slices with the egg white, cornstarch, salt for the fish and wine and rest 15 minutes.
  2. Blanch the bean sprouts and enoki 30 seconds in boiling water and spread them in a large bowl.
  3. Heat half the oil in a wide pan over medium heat, fry the garlic, ginger, pickled chilies and half the green peppercorns for 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Add the stock, salt and white pepper and bring to a simmer; slide in the fish one slice at a time, and poach for 1 to 2 minutes until opaque.
  5. Pour the fish and broth over the vegetables, scatter the remaining peppercorns on top, heat the remaining oil until it smokes and pour it over to bloom the peppercorns.

Cook’s notes

Written for cod loin. Sichuan cooks use grass carp or catfish.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the poaching fish breaks it; slide the slices in and let them set.
  • Fish slices cut thick overcook on the outside before the middle is done.

Authentic tips

  • Green Sichuan peppercorns are gentler and more floral than red; buy them in brine or frozen.
  • Chill the fish for 20 minutes to slice thinly.

Substitutions

  • Haddock or sea bass replaces the cod.
  • Dried green Sichuan peppercorns, toasted and ground, replace fresh ones at 1 tablespoon.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; the fish firms up in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and cucumber salad
  • Soup: Clear tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh melon
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
