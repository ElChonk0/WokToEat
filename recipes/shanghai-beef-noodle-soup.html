Shanghai Beef Noodle Soup 上海牛肉面

▾

Surprise me with

Milder and sweeter than its Sichuan or Taiwanese cousins, Shanghai-style beef noodle soup relies on soy sauce and rock sugar for a gentler, more rounded broth typical of Jiangnan's less spicy cuisine.

Method

  1. Blanch the beef for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and spices for 1 minute, then melt the rock sugar until amber and turn the beef in it until glossy.
  3. Add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces and water, bring to a boil and simmer, covered, for 2 hours, until the beef is tender. Unlike the spicy Sichuan and Taiwanese versions, Shanghai's broth is clear-ish, savory and lightly sweet, with no chili.
  4. Taste the broth and adjust with salt; it should be well seasoned, since the noodles dilute it.
  5. Cook the noodles for 2 to 3 minutes, drain and divide among bowls with the bok choy.
  6. Ladle over the beef and broth and finish with green onion and white pepper.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the simmer, which doesn't give the beef enough time to become properly tender.
  • Using only dark soy sauce, which makes the broth too intensely colored and salty rather than gently sweet.

Authentic tips

  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half.
  • The rock sugar is what distinguishes this milder Shanghai style from spicier regional versions — don't skip it.

Substitutions

  • Beef short rib can replace shank for a richer broth.
  • No rock sugar: brown sugar works, in a slightly smaller quantity.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth and beef improve overnight; store noodles separately.

Freezing: The braised beef and broth freeze excellently for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
