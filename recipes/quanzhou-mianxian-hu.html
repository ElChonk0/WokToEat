Quánzhōu Miànxiàn Hú 面线糊

▾

Surprise me with

Quanzhou’s breakfast porridge is made not from rice but from fine wheat mee sua simmered in seafood broth until it thickens; diners add toppings such as pork, liver, shrimp and crisp fried dough.

Method

  1. Simmer the stock with the soaked dried shrimp 10 minutes.
  2. Break the mee sua into short lengths and add to the pot, stirring so it doesn’t clump; simmer 8 minutes until it softens into a thick soup.
  3. Toss the pork with a pinch of the starch, then slide it in with the shrimp and simmer 2 minutes.
  4. Stir the remaining starch with a little water and add to thicken to a loose porridge; season with fish sauce.
  5. Ladle into bowls; top with fried shallots, celery leaves, white pepper, a dash of black vinegar and sliced youtiao.

Common mistakes

  • Using salted mee sua without tasting first; many brands are heavily salted, so season at the end.

Authentic tips

  • The fried shallots and black vinegar are not optional garnishes in Quanzhou; they define the bowl.

Substitutions

  • Add sliced pork liver or fish balls with the pork for a more traditional mix.

Storage

Leftovers: Thickens as it sits; thin with hot stock to reheat. Best within a day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried dough sticks
  • Soup: Not needed; this is a thick soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
