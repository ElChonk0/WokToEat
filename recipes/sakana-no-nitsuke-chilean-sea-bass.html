Sakana no Nitsuke (Soy-Simmered Sea Bass) 魚の煮付け

Nitsuke is a classic Japanese method of simmering fish in a sweet soy broth with ginger; rich, fatty fish suit it well, and it is a common home and restaurant dish.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozChilean sea bass fillets, skin-on, about 150 g each
  • 120 ml1/2 cupSake
  • 60 ml1/4 cupMirin
  • 60 ml1/4 cupSoy sauce
  • 20 g5 tspSugar
  • 200 ml3/4 cupWater
  • 30 g1 ozGinger, sliced thin
  • 150 g5 ozKomatsuna or spinach
  • 40 g1.5 ozScallion, cut in 4 cm lengths

Method

  1. Place the fish in a bowl and pour boiling water over it, then drain and rinse off any scum to remove the fishy smell.
  2. Combine the sake, mirin, soy sauce, sugar, water and ginger in a wide pan and bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
  3. Lay the fish skin-side up in the pan, cover with a drop lid or a circle of parchment, and simmer over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, spooning the liquid over the fish twice.
  4. Lift the fish onto plates, add the greens and scallion to the pan and cook 1 minute.
  5. Boil the sauce uncovered for 2 to 3 minutes until slightly syrupy, then spoon it over the fish and greens.

Cook’s notes

Written for Chilean sea bass (Patagonian toothfish), a rich modern stand-in for the fatty fish traditionally simmered this way.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the boiling-water rinse leaves a fishy, cloudy sauce.
  • Simmering too hard breaks the fillets; keep a steady, gentle bubble.

Authentic tips

  • A drop lid keeps the sauce circulating over the fish so the top cooks evenly.
  • The sauce should coat the fish, not drown it.

Substitutions

  • Black cod, cod or snapper replaces the sea bass.
  • Add a few shiitake mushrooms or sliced burdock to the pan.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator and is good cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and spinach ohitashi
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Sliced orange
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
