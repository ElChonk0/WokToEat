Sate Lilit Babi (Balinese Minced Pork Satay)

▾

Surprise me with

Sate lilit, minced meat wound around lemongrass sticks, is a Balinese satay, with pork as the common festival version.

  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, turmeric, galangal, kencur, candlenuts, coriander and shrimp paste into base genep and fry it in the oil for 5 to 6 minutes until fragrant. Cool completely.
  2. Mix the pork, coconut, cooled paste, lime leaves, palm sugar and salt and knead for 2 minutes until sticky. The coconut keeps the satay moist and slightly sweet.
  3. Wet your hands and wrap a golf-ball-size portion around the top half of each lemongrass stalk, pressing it into an even, tight sausage. Lilit means 'to wrap'.
  4. Grill over medium charcoal or a grill pan for 8 to 10 minutes, turning, until browned and cooked through. The lemongrass perfumes the meat from inside.
  5. Serve hot with rice and sambal matah.

Common mistakes

  • A loose mixture falls off the stick; knead until tacky.
  • Cooking on high heat burns the outside.

Authentic tips

  • The lemongrass stick perfumes the satay as it grills.
  • Use bamboo skewers if you can't find lemongrass.

Substitutions

  • Minced chicken or fish replace pork.
  • Bamboo skewers replace lemongrass sticks.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat under a broiler.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked skewers for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and sambal matah
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
