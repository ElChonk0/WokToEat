Guang Shi Ju Xue Yu (Cantonese Baked Cod with Ginger and Scallion) 广式焗鳕鱼

Cantonese home cooks apply the flavors of steamed fish, ginger, scallion, soy and sizzling oil, to fillets cooked in the oven, a modern convenience version of a classic.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine

  • 10 g1/4 cupFresh cilantro
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 4 g1 tspSugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 C (400 F). Pat the cod dry, season with salt and pepper and lay it in a baking dish. Sprinkle with the wine and half the ginger.
  2. Cover loosely with foil and bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until the thickest part flakes and reaches 60 C (140 F).
  3. Meanwhile warm the soy sauce, sugar and water in a small pan until the sugar dissolves.
  4. Pour off any liquid in the dish, top the fish with the remaining ginger, green onion and cilantro, and spoon the soy mixture around it.
  5. Heat the oil in a small pan until it just smokes and pour it over the aromatics so they sizzle. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the fishy cooking liquid in the dish muddies the sauce; pour it off.
  • Oil that is not hot enough will not release the aroma of the ginger and scallion.

Authentic tips

  • This is the same finish as Cantonese steamed fish; steaming the fillets for 8 to 10 minutes works too.
  • Use the thickest loin pieces you can find so they stay moist.

Substitutions

  • Haddock, halibut or black cod work the same way.
  • No Shaoxing wine: use dry sherry.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat gently, covered.

Freezing: Not recommended after cooking.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic gai lan
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
