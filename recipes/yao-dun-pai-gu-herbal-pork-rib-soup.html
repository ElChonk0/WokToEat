Yào Dùn Pái Gǔ (Taiwanese Herbal Pork Rib Soup) 藥燉排骨

▾

Surprise me with

Yao dun pai gu, pork ribs in a Chinese-herb broth with angelica, licorice and wolfberry, is a Taiwanese night-market and winter restorative soup.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 800 g1.75 lbPork ribs, chopped into pieces
  • 30 g1 ozHerbal soup packet (dang gui, chuan xiong, shu di huang, licorice, dang shen)
  • 66Dried red dates
  • 10 g1 tbspGoji berries
  • 30 g1 ozGinger, sliced
  • 60 ml1/4 cupRice wine
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater
  • 6 g1 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper

Method

  1. Blanch the ribs in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Rinse the herbs briefly to remove dust.
  3. Put the ribs, herbs, red dates, ginger and water in a Dutch oven, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1 to 1.25 hours, until the meat is tender and the broth is dark and fragrant.
  4. Add the goji berries and rice wine for the last 10 minutes.
  5. Season with salt and white pepper and serve. A night-market staple, often with a bowl of rice and pickled vegetables.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the broth cloudy.
  • Too much herb packet makes the soup bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with a dipping sauce of soy sauce and chili.
  • Herbal packets are sold at Chinese markets.

Substitutions

  • Pork neck bones replace ribs.
  • Pre-mixed herbal packets are sold at Chinese markets.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the broth improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Warm ginger tea
