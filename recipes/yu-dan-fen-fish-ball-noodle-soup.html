Yú Dàn Fěn (Hong Kong Fish Ball Noodle Soup) 鱼蛋粉

▾

Surprise me with

Fish ball noodles are a Hong Kong everyday meal, found in cha chaan teng and noodle shops from breakfast onward, with bouncy fish balls and thin rice noodles in a clear broth.

  • Prep 30 min (plus 30 min chilling)
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Method

  1. Pat the fish and shrimp dry, then mince each with a cleaver and flatten it with the side of the blade, repeating until you have a fine, sticky paste. A food processor works, but pulse in short bursts so it does not warm up.
  2. Whisk the salt, sugar, pepper, egg, cornstarch and ice water, add the paste with the tangerine peel and herbs and mix hard for 2 to 3 minutes, then add the sesame oil. Pick up the paste and slap it back into the bowl 20 to 30 times; this develops the springy bounce of good fish balls. Chill for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Bring a pot of water to a bare simmer. With wet hands, squeeze the paste up through your fist and scoop off balls with a wet spoon, dropping them into the water. Poach for 3 to 4 minutes until they float and turn white and firm, then lift them out.
  4. Bring the stock to a boil with the ginger and season with the soy sauce, fish sauce and pepper. Add the fish balls to warm through, and blanch the choy sum in the soup for 1 minute.
  5. Soak or boil the rice noodles according to the packet until tender, drain and divide among bowls.
  6. Top the noodles with the fish balls and greens, ladle the hot soup over and scatter with fried shallots.

Common mistakes

  • Soaking the noodles too long or cooking them in the soup makes them mushy and clouds the broth.
  • A hard boil toughens the fish balls.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with chili oil and a dish of dark vinegar.
  • A good stock matters most; add a few dried flounder or pork bones when simmering for authenticity.

Substitutions

  • Rice vermicelli replaces the thin rice noodles.
  • Beef balls or fish cake slices make a good addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the noodles and broth separately; keeps 2 days and the noodles are best blanched fresh.

Freezing: Freeze the broth only, for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched lettuce with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
