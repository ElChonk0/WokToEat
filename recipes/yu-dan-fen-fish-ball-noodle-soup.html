Yu Dan Fen (Hong Kong Fish Ball Noodle Soup) 鱼蛋粉

Fish ball noodles are a Hong Kong everyday meal, found in cha chaan teng and noodle shops from breakfast onward, with bouncy fish balls and thin rice noodles in a clear broth.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozFish balls
  • 300 g10 ozThin rice noodles
  • 1.2 L5 cupsChicken or pork stock
  • 150 g5 ozChoy sum
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 15 g1 tbspFried shallots
  • 20 g2 tbspScallion

Method

  1. Soak the rice noodles in warm water for 10 minutes.
  2. Bring the stock to a gentle simmer with the soy sauce and white pepper, add the fish balls and cook 3-4 minutes until they float and are hot through.
  3. Blanch the choy sum in boiling water for 1 minute; drain.
  4. Dip the noodles in boiling water for 20-30 seconds until just tender and divide them among bowls.
  5. Ladle the stock and fish balls over, arrange the greens on top and finish with the fried shallots and scallion.

Common mistakes

  • Soaking the noodles too long or cooking them in the soup makes them mushy and clouds the broth.
  • A hard boil toughens the fish balls.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with chili oil and a dish of dark vinegar.
  • A good stock matters most; add a few dried flounder or pork bones when simmering for authenticity.

Substitutions

  • Rice vermicelli replaces the thin rice noodles.
  • Beef balls or fish cake slices make a good addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the noodles and broth separately; keeps 2 days and the noodles are best blanched fresh.

Freezing: Freeze the broth only, for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched lettuce with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
