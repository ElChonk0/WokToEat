Hunan Chicken 剁椒鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Hunan cooking favors fresh and pickled chilies over the numbing peppercorns of neighboring Sichuan, and this stir-fry built on fermented chopped chili (duo jiao) is drier and more intensely spicy than its Sichuan cousins.

Method

  1. Marinate chicken in soy sauce, half the wine, and cornstarch for 15 minutes.
  2. Stir-fry chicken in hot oil over high heat until just cooked through; remove and set aside.
  3. In the same wok, fry duo jiao, garlic, and ginger until fragrant and the oil turns red.
  4. Add bell pepper and stir-fry 1–2 minutes until just tender-crisp.
  5. Return chicken to the wok with remaining wine and sugar; toss everything together over high heat until combined.
  6. Serve immediately while hot.

Common mistakes

  • Using Sichuan-style doubanjiang as a substitute for duo jiao — the flavor profile is noticeably different and less bright.
  • Overcooking the chicken in the first stir-fry, since it will cook further when returned to the wok.

Authentic tips

  • Duo jiao is sold in most Chinese grocery stores refrigerated; it keeps for months once opened.
  • Hunan dishes tend to be drier than Sichuan ones — resist adding extra liquid to the pan.

Substitutions

  • No duo jiao: a mix of fresh chopped red chilies and a splash of rice vinegar approximates the tang.
  • Bell pepper can be swapped for celery for a different crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the peppers suffers.
