Hunan Chicken 剁椒鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Hunan cooking favors fresh and pickled chilies over the numbing peppercorns of neighboring Sichuan, and this stir-fry built on fermented chopped chili (duo jiao) is drier and more intensely spicy than its Sichuan cousins.

  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Put the peppers and chilies in a dry flat-bottom wok over medium heat with a pinch of salt and press them against the wok with a spatula for 3 to 4 minutes, until blistered and softened. Lift them out. Dry-frying the chilies first (gan bian) is the Hunan way; it tames their rawness and deepens their flavor.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil over high heat and stir-fry the chicken for 3 to 4 minutes until cooked through. Push it aside.
  4. Add the remaining oil, garlic, ginger and duo jiao and fry for 30 seconds until the oil turns red.
  5. Return the peppers, add the sauce soy sauce, sugar and vinegar and toss for 30 seconds. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Using Sichuan-style doubanjiang as a substitute for duo jiao — the flavor profile is noticeably different and less bright.
  • Overcooking the chicken in the first stir-fry, since it will cook further when returned to the wok.

Authentic tips

  • Duo jiao is sold in most Chinese grocery stores refrigerated; it keeps for months once opened.
  • Hunan dishes tend to be drier than Sichuan ones — resist adding extra liquid to the pan.

Substitutions

  • No duo jiao: a mix of fresh chopped red chilies and a splash of rice vinegar approximates the tang.
  • Bell pepper can be swapped for celery for a different crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the peppers suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Light tomato egg soup
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
