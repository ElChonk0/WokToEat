Naengmyeon (Cold Buckwheat Noodles) 냉면

▾

Surprise me with

Naengmyeon originated in northern Korea, particularly Pyongyang, as a way to enjoy noodles even in the depths of winter using an ice-cold beef and radish broth — now a beloved warm-weather dish across all of Korea.

Method

  1. Simmer the beef in the water with the onion, radish chunk, garlic, ginger, green onion and peppercorns, skimming off the foam, for about 1 hour until tender. Add the soup soy sauce for the last 10 minutes.
  2. Lift out the beef, strain the broth and skim off all the fat. Season about 1.2 L (5 cups) of the broth with the sugar, salt and vinegar; it should taste tangy and slightly sweet, and a splash of dongchimi or kimchi juice adds depth. Chill, then put it in the freezer for 1 to 2 hours until slushy with ice crystals.
  3. Toss the thin radish slices with the vinegar, sugar and salt until dissolved and leave to pickle. Slice the beef thinly once cold.
  4. Boil the noodles according to the packet, usually 1 minute or less, then drain and plunge them into ice water, rubbing them under cold running water until very cold and no longer slippery with starch.
  5. Coil the noodles in chilled bowls, top with the beef, radish, cucumber, pear and egg, and pour the icy broth around them. Serve with mustard and vinegar to add at the table, and scissors to cut the long noodles.

Common mistakes

  • Serving the broth before it's fully chilled, which misses the point of this refreshing cold dish entirely.
  • Not rinsing the noodles under cold water immediately after cooking, which leaves them starchy and prone to clumping.

Authentic tips

  • The broth genuinely needs several hours to chill properly — plan ahead, or make it a day in advance.
  • Diners traditionally adjust the balance with mustard and vinegar at the table to their own taste.

Substitutions

  • No buckwheat noodles: sweet potato glass noodles work as a chewier alternative.
  • Chicken stock can replace beef stock for a lighter version, though beef is traditional.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store broth and noodles separately.

Freezing: The broth freezes well for up to a month; cook fresh noodles when serving.
