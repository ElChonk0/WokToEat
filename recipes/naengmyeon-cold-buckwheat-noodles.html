Naengmyeon (Cold Buckwheat Noodles) 냉면

Naengmyeon originated in northern Korea, particularly Pyongyang, as a way to enjoy noodles even in the depths of winter using an ice-cold beef and radish broth — now a beloved warm-weather dish across all of Korea.

Method

  1. Simmer beef in water 50–60 minutes until tender; remove beef, slice thin, and strain the broth.
  2. Season the broth with rice vinegar and sugar, then chill thoroughly in the refrigerator, at least 3 hours or overnight.
  3. Quick-pickle the sliced daikon in a little extra vinegar and sugar while the broth chills.
  4. Cook buckwheat noodles according to package directions; rinse immediately under cold water and drain well.
  5. Divide noodles among bowls and ladle the ice-cold broth over top.
  6. Top with sliced beef, pickled daikon, cucumber, pear, and half a hard-boiled egg; serve with mustard and extra vinegar on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Serving the broth before it's fully chilled, which misses the point of this refreshing cold dish entirely.
  • Not rinsing the noodles under cold water immediately after cooking, which leaves them starchy and prone to clumping.

Authentic tips

  • The broth genuinely needs several hours to chill properly — plan ahead, or make it a day in advance.
  • Diners traditionally adjust the balance with mustard and vinegar at the table to their own taste.

Substitutions

  • No buckwheat noodles: sweet potato glass noodles work as a chewier alternative.
  • Chicken stock can replace beef stock for a lighter version, though beef is traditional.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store broth and noodles separately.

Freezing: The broth freezes well for up to a month; cook fresh noodles when serving.

Serve with
