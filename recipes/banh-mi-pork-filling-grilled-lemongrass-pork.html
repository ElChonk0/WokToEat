Banh Mi Pork Filling (Grilled Lemongrass Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

Banh mi reflects Vietnam's French colonial history, marrying a baguette with distinctly Vietnamese fillings; this grilled lemongrass pork is one of the most popular fillings, alongside pate and fresh herbs.

Method

  1. Combine lemongrass, garlic, shallot, fish sauce, and sugar for the marinade.
  2. Coat pork thoroughly and marinate at least 2 hours, or overnight for best flavor.
  3. Grill or pan-sear over medium-high heat, 3–4 minutes per side, until charred and cooked through.
  4. Split baguettes and spread with mayo; layer in the grilled pork, pickled carrot and daikon, cucumber, and cilantro.
  5. Serve immediately while the bread is crisp and the pork is warm.

Common mistakes

  • Mincing lemongrass too coarsely, which leaves tough, fibrous pieces in the finished filling.
  • Overfilling the sandwich, which makes it difficult to eat and unbalances the flavors.

Authentic tips

  • Use only the tender inner portion of the lemongrass stalk, near the base, and mince it very finely for the best texture.
  • A quick pass under the broiler crisps the baguette and warms it through before assembling.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same marinade for a chicken banh mi.
  • No baguette: a soft roll works, though the classic airy-crisp texture is part of the dish's appeal.

Storage

Leftovers: Store grilled pork separately, up to 3 days refrigerated; assemble sandwiches fresh.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.
