Ham Sui Gok (Fried Glutinous Rice Dumplings) 咸水角

▾

Surprise me with

Ham sui gok, crisp-chewy fried glutinous rice dumplings filled with pork, dried shrimp and mushroom, are a classic of the Cantonese dim sum fried trolley.

  • Cook 35 min

Method

  1. Stir the hot water into the wheat starch and knead for 30 seconds into a smooth paste. In another bowl rub the lard and sugar into the glutinous rice flour, add the cold water and knead for 1 minute. Combine the two doughs and knead for 1 to 2 minutes until smooth and soft, like play dough. Wrap and refrigerate for 1 hour so the moisture evens out; unevenly hydrated dough bursts in the oil.
  2. Mix the pork with the salt, cornstarch and water. Soak the dried shrimp and mushrooms in warm water for 10 minutes, then dice them with the turnip and celery to the same small size.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat, add the filling oil and fry the garlic and shrimp for 15 seconds, the pork for 30 to 40 seconds, then the mushrooms, turnip and celery for 45 seconds.
  4. Add the oyster sauce, soy sauce, pepper and five-spice, then stir in the slurry cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons of water gradually and cook for 30 seconds until the filling is moist and bound. Stir in the sesame oil and herbs, spread on a plate and cool completely.
  5. Divide the dough into 14 pieces. Flatten each into a disc about 6 mm (1/4 in) thick, put a tablespoon of filling in the center, fold into a half-moon and pinch the edge firmly shut, then roll it gently into a football shape about the size of a large egg. Keep the rest of the dough covered so it does not dry and crack.
  6. Heat the frying oil in a Dutch oven to 160 C (320 F), then turn off the heat and slip in half the dumplings. Stir gently so they do not stick to the bottom; they will float after about 3 minutes.
  7. Turn the heat back to medium to bring the oil back to 160 C and fry for 8 to 10 minutes, turning, then lower the heat for the last 3 minutes. Floating means they are only partly done: the long, gentle fry puffs the shells and turns them crisp and golden without bursting.
  8. Drain on a rack and fry the second batch the same way. Serve warm.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is too hot browns the outside before the dough puffs; they can also burst.
  • Warm filling softens the dough and makes it tear.

Authentic tips

  • The wheat starch gives the crisp shell; without it the dumplings are just chewy.
  • Fry from frozen at 150 C (300 F) for a few minutes longer.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken replaces pork.
  • Neutral oil can replace lard in the dough.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; re-crisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freeze shaped, uncooked dumplings for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and cheung fun
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
