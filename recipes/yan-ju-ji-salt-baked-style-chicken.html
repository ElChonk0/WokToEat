Salt-Baked Style Chicken 盐焗鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Yan ju ji, chicken traditionally baked buried in heated salt, comes from the Hakka communities of Guangdong and became a Cantonese restaurant classic; this home version uses the oven.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 1 hr 10 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 1 kg2.2 lbWhole chicken legs, bone-in

  • 3 g1 tspSand ginger powder
  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt, for the chicken
  • 20 g2/3 ozGinger, sliced
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, cut into lengths
  • 1 kg2.2 lbCoarse salt, for baking
  • 10 g2 tspSalt, for the dip
  • 2 g1 tspSand ginger powder, for the dip
  • 15 ml1 tbspSesame oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil or melted lard

Method

  1. Pat the legs dry. Rub them all over with the wine, sand ginger powder, five-spice and the chicken salt, tuck the ginger and green onion under the skin and against the meat, and refrigerate uncovered for at least 1 hour, or up to overnight, so the seasoning reaches the meat and the skin dries.
  2. Wrap each leg tightly in two layers of parchment, folding the ends underneath, then in a layer of foil, so no salt can get in and the juices stay inside. This is the home version of the paper wrap used for salt-baked chicken.
  3. Heat the oven to 220 C (425 F). Spread the baking salt in a Dutch oven or deep baking dish and heat it, uncovered, in the oven for 20 minutes until very hot; alternatively, stir it in a dry wok over medium-high heat for 8 to 10 minutes until it is too hot to touch and faintly yellow.
  4. Scoop out half the salt, lay the parcels on the remaining layer and pour the rest over so they are buried. Cover and bake for 40 to 45 minutes. The hot salt surrounds the chicken with dry, even heat, cooking it gently in its own juices.
  5. Dig out one parcel and check: the thickest part should read 74 C (165 F) and the juices should run clear. If not, bury it again for 5 to 10 minutes. Rest the parcels for 10 minutes. The salt can be cooled, sieved and kept for the next time.
  6. Make the dip: toast the dip salt in a dry pan over low heat for about 1 minute until hot, take it off the heat and stir in the sand ginger powder, sesame oil and neutral oil. Adding the ginger off the heat keeps its perfume from scorching.
  7. Unwrap the chicken over a bowl to catch the juices. Pull the meat into large pieces by hand, which keeps it soft and juicy, or chop through the bone. Toss with a little of the juices and serve the dip on the side; it is salty, so dip lightly.

Common mistakes

  • Gaps in the parchment let coarse salt reach the meat and make it harshly salty, so fold the ends tightly.
  • Chopping straight out of the oven pushes the juices out; the 10-minute rest keeps the meat moist.

Authentic tips

  • Sand ginger (sha jiang) is the Hakka perfume of this dish; if you cannot find it, use extra ground ginger and a pinch of white pepper.
  • The salt can be reused for the next batch once cooled.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works but dries easily; roast 10 minutes less and rest it well.
  • A spatchcocked small whole chicken gives the classic presentation; add 15 minutes to the roasting time.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; serve cold or shred into noodle soup.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; thaw overnight and reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.
