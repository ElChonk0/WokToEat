Salt-Baked Style Chicken 盐焗鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Yan ju ji, chicken traditionally baked buried in heated salt, comes from the Hakka communities of Guangdong and became a Cantonese restaurant classic; this home version uses the oven.

  • Cook 50 min

  • 1 kg2.2 lbWhole chicken legs, bone-in
  • 600 g21 ozCoarse salt
  • 50 g3 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 2 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 3 g1 tspSand ginger powder
  • 15 ml1 tbspSesame oil

Method

  1. Pat the chicken legs dry. Rub them all over with the five-spice, sand ginger powder, a pinch of salt and the wine, tuck the ginger and scallion under the skin, and refrigerate for 1 hour so the seasoning reaches the meat.
  2. Wrap each leg tightly in two layers of parchment, folding the ends under so no salt can get inside.
  3. Spread the coarse salt about 2 cm deep in a baking dish and heat it in a 200 C (400 F) oven for 15 minutes.
  4. Nestle the wrapped chicken in the hot salt and bake for 40 minutes, until the thickest part reads 75 C (165 F).
  5. Rest the parcels for 10 minutes, then unwrap, chop through the bone and drizzle with the sesame oil and the juices.

Common mistakes

  • Gaps in the parchment let coarse salt reach the meat and make it harshly salty, so fold the ends tightly.
  • Chopping straight out of the oven pushes the juices out; the 10-minute rest keeps the meat moist.

Authentic tips

  • Sand ginger (sha jiang) is the Hakka perfume of this dish; if you cannot find it, use extra ground ginger and a pinch of white pepper.
  • The salt can be reused for the next batch once cooled.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works but dries easily; roast 10 minutes less and rest it well.
  • A spatchcocked small whole chicken gives the classic presentation; add 15 minutes to the roasting time.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; serve cold or shred into noodle soup.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; thaw overnight and reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with ginger-scallion dip
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
