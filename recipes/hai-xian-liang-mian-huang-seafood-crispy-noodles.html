Hǎi Xiān Liǎng Miàn Huáng (Seafood over Crispy Pan-Fried Noodles) 海鲜两面黄

Hong Kong-style chow mein is a cake of noodles fried golden on both sides (liang mian huang) and topped with a seafood and vegetable gravy, a banquet and restaurant classic.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Blanch the noodles 30 seconds and drain very dry. Heat 3 tablespoons of oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat, press in the noodles as a flat cake and fry 5 to 6 minutes per side until golden and crisp. Slide onto a platter.
  2. Toss the seafood with the cornstarch. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry it for 2 minutes until just cooked. Lift out.
  3. Add the last of the oil and stir-fry the garlic, ginger, mushrooms, carrot and greens for 2 minutes.
  4. Add the stock, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar and bring to a boil, then thicken with the cornstarch mixed with a little water.
  5. Return the seafood, add the sesame oil and pour over the noodle cake. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Flipping too early breaks the noodle cake; let it set and brown first.
  • A thin gravy soaks the noodles; make it thick and glossy.

Authentic tips

  • Diners add Chinkiang vinegar at the table to cut the richness.
  • Use a plate to flip the noodle cake, like a tortilla.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or char siu replace seafood.
  • Use frozen seafood mix.

Storage

Leftovers: Store topping separately for 2 days; re-crisp the noodles in a pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Red vinegar and chili oil
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Almond jelly
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
