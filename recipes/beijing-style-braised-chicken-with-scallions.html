Beijing-Style Braised Chicken with Scallions 葱烧鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Cong shao (scallion braising) is a Beijing and Shandong technique that uses a generous quantity of charred scallion as both an aromatic base and a nearly vegetable-forward component of the finished dish.

  • 8 stalks8 stalksGreen onion, cut into 5cm segments
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce

  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 250 ml1 cupChicken stock or water

Method

  1. Char the green onion segments in a dry pan or with a little oil until lightly blackened in spots; set half aside for garnish.
  2. Brown chicken pieces in the same pan until golden.
  3. Add ginger and the remaining charred scallion, stirring briefly.
  4. Add both soy sauces, wine, and sugar, tossing to coat.
  5. Add stock, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 20 minutes.
  6. Uncover and reduce the sauce 5 minutes; garnish with the reserved fresh-charred scallion before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little scallion — this dish is meant to be scallion-forward, not just lightly seasoned with it.
  • Skipping the char step, which is what gives the scallion its distinctive smoky-sweet flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The charred scallion is as much a vegetable component of this dish as an aromatic — don't skimp on quantity.
  • A cast-iron pan gives the best char on the scallions if you have one.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks work with 10 extra minutes of braising.
  • Leeks can replace some of the scallions for a sweeter finish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed buns or rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pear
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
