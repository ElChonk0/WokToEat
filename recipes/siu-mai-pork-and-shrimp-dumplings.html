Siu Mai (Pork and Shrimp Dumplings) 烧卖

▾

Surprise me with

Siu mai, open-topped steamed dumplings of pork and shrimp, are among the most common dim sum dishes in Guangdong and Hong Kong, traditionally topped with a dot of roe or a goji berry.

Method

  1. Chop the pork by hand into small bits, or pulse briefly, keeping some texture rather than a paste. Rinse it in water, massage for a minute, drain and squeeze it as dry as you can in a cloth. Pat the shrimp dry and chop them into pea-size pieces. Excess water makes siu mai soggy.
  2. Mix the soy sauce, oyster sauce, salt, sugar, pepper, cornstarch, baking soda and water in a bowl. Add the pork and stir hard in one direction for 2 to 3 minutes until sticky and springy, then rest 10 minutes.
  3. Add the shrimp, mushrooms, neutral oil and sesame oil and mix for 1 to 2 minutes more. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or up to 8; chilled filling is firmer and easier to shape.
  4. If using square wonton wrappers, trim them into circles. Make a circle with your thumb and index finger, lay a wrapper over it and press about 1 heaping tablespoon of filling into the center with a butter knife, letting the wrapper rise around it.
  5. Squeeze gently to form a cup with pleated sides and an open top, pressing the filling down level and tapping the base flat on the board so it stands up. Top each with a pinch of carrot.
  6. Line a steamer basket or a rack in a flat-bottom wok with parchment with holes cut in it. Arrange the siu mai so they do not touch.
  7. Bring the water to a full boil before the basket goes on, then steam covered over high heat for 10 to 12 minutes, until the filling is firm and cooked through. Serve straight from the steamer. To freeze, freeze them raw on a tray, then steam from thawed.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring in both directions or for too long makes the filling tough instead of bouncy.
  • Overfilling stretches the wrapper and splits it in the steamer.

Authentic tips

  • Use pork with about 20 percent fat; lean pork turns dry.
  • Fresh round siu mai wrappers are best, but wonton wrappers trimmed into circles work well.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken thigh replaces the pork for a lighter filling.
  • Use all shrimp for a seafood-only version; add a little extra cornstarch to bind.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam for 5 minutes, never microwave.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray, then bag for 2 months; steam from frozen for 14 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and rice rolls for a dim sum spread
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
