Cōng Bào Jī Sī (Scallion Flash-Fried Chicken) 葱爆鸡丝

▾

Surprise me with

Bao, flash-frying over fierce heat, is a technique central to Beijing and Shandong cooking, and scallion bao is a classic pairing with strips of meat.

  • Cook 6 min

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes. Cut the scallions on an angle, whites and greens separate.
  2. Mix the sauce soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and sesame oil in a bowl.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat until smoking. Add the chicken and toss for 1 minute until it just changes color. Cong bao means 'scallion explode-fried': fierce heat and speed.
  4. Add the garlic and scallion whites and toss for 20 seconds, then the greens.
  5. Pour the sauce around the edge of the wok and toss for 15 to 20 seconds, until the scallions just wilt and everything is glossy. Serve at once; it is done in under 3 minutes from start to finish.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on moderate heat steams the chicken; bao means very hot and very fast.
  • Overcooking the scallions makes them limp; they should still have bite.

Authentic tips

  • Use the white and light green parts of large scallions for sweetness.
  • Prepare everything before the wok goes on the heat.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works if sliced thin and velveted.
  • Leeks replace part of the scallions.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the dish is meant to be eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
