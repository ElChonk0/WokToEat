Tonkatsu 豚カツ

▾

Surprise me with

Tonkatsu is Japan's breaded, deep-fried pork cutlet, adapted from Western cutlet dishes in the late 19th century and now a defining comfort food, served with shredded cabbage and tangy tonkatsu sauce.

Method

  1. Season pork chops with salt and pepper.
  2. Dredge each in flour, then egg, then panko, pressing gently to adhere.
  3. Heat oil to 175°C (350°F) in a wide pan.
  4. Fry the pork 4–5 minutes per side until deeply golden and cooked through.
  5. Drain on a rack, rest 3 minutes, then slice into strips.
  6. Serve over shredded cabbage with tonkatsu sauce drizzled on top.

Common mistakes

  • Not scoring the fat edge of the chop, which can cause the meat to curl and cook unevenly.
  • Overcrowding the pan, which drops the oil temperature and produces a greasy crust.

Authentic tips

  • Panko's larger, airier crumbs give tonkatsu its distinctively light, crunchy crust compared to regular breadcrumbs.
  • Draining on a wire rack instead of paper towels keeps the bottom crust from steaming and going soft.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh, pounded flat, makes a good chicken katsu variation.
  • No tonkatsu sauce: a mix of ketchup, Worcestershire sauce, and a little soy sauce approximates it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in an oven or air fryer to restore crispness.

Freezing: Freezes well, breaded and uncooked, for up to a month.
