Nikuman (Steamed Pork Buns) 肉まん

▾

Surprise me with

Nikuman, steamed buns filled with seasoned pork, are a Japanese convenience-store and winter street snack adapted from Chinese baozi.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, salt, baking powder and yeast, add the oil and warm water and knead for 10 minutes into a smooth, soft dough. Cover and leave in a warm place for 45 to 60 minutes until doubled.
  2. Squeeze the salted cabbage dry. Knead the pork with the mushrooms, cabbage, green onion, ginger, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sake, sugar, sesame oil, potato starch and pepper until sticky and pale. Chill while the dough rises.
  3. Punch down the dough, divide it into 10 pieces and rest them for 10 minutes. Roll each into a round about 12 cm (5 in) across, thinner at the edge than the center.
  4. Put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center and pleat the edge up and around it, pinching and twisting to close. Set each on a square of parchment and leave covered for 20 minutes to rise again.
  5. Steam over boiling water in a flat-bottom wok or steamer, with the lid wrapped in a towel so condensation does not drip on the buns, for 13 to 15 minutes, until puffed and the filling is cooked through.
  6. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 2 minutes so the buns do not deflate, then serve hot with mustard and soy sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the second rest makes dense buns.
  • Opening the lid wrinkles the buns.

Authentic tips

  • Low-protein flour gives whiter buns.
  • Freeze cooked buns and re-steam.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken replaces some pork.
  • Store-bought frozen buns are a quick shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; re-steam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze steamed buns for 2 months; steam from frozen for 15 minutes.
