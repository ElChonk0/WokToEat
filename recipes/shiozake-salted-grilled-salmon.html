Shiozake (Salted Grilled Salmon) 塩鮭

▾

Surprise me with

Shiozake, salted and grilled salmon, is the quintessential Japanese breakfast fish served with rice, miso soup and pickles.

  • Prep 10 min (plus overnight curing)

Method

  1. Pat the salmon dry and brush it with sake, then rub salt over both sides.
  2. Wrap each fillet in a paper towel and plastic wrap and refrigerate overnight, or up to 3 days. The cure firms the flesh, seasons it through and gives the dense texture of Japanese breakfast salmon.
  3. Unwrap and pat dry. Heat the broiler to high, or a fish grill, and line the tray with foil.
  4. Broil skin-side up for 7 to 10 minutes, until the skin crackles and browns and the flesh is just opaque at the center. A nonstick skillet over medium heat also works, 3 to 4 minutes per side.
  5. Serve with grated daikon and lemon alongside rice and miso soup.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the drying leaves the skin soggy.
  • Too much salt makes the fish harsh; salt lightly.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse off excess salt if it forms a crust.
  • A classic breakfast with rice, miso soup and pickles.

Substitutions

  • Mackerel or cod replace salmon.
  • Lightly salted salmon sold in Japanese markets is a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; flake into rice or onigiri.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
