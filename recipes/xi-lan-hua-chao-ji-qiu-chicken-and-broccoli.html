Xī Lán Huā Chǎo Jī Qiú (Chicken and Broccoli Stir-Fry) 西兰花炒鸡球

▾

Chicken and broccoli in a light oyster-garlic sauce is a Cantonese restaurant staple and the model for the takeout dish of the same name.

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the marinade oyster sauce, half the wine, the chicken cornstarch and the water, working it with your hand until the liquid is absorbed. Stir in 1 teaspoon of oil and leave for 15 minutes. The water is what keeps the meat juicy and tender. Mix the sauce oyster sauce, soy sauces, sugar, pepper, stock and cornstarch in a cup.
  2. Bring a pot of water to a boil with a pinch of salt and a teaspoon of oil. Blanch the broccoli for 1 to 1.5 minutes, until bright green and just tender at the stem, then drain well. The oil keeps it glossy, and blanching means it needs only a quick toss in the wok.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it just smokes and add 2 tablespoons of oil. Spread the chicken in one layer and leave it for 1 minute to brown, then turn and cook for 1 minute more, until golden outside and nearly cooked through. Lift it out.
  4. Add the remaining oil and fry the ginger and garlic for 15 to 20 seconds until fragrant. Add the broccoli and toss for 30 seconds, then splash the remaining wine around the edge of the wok.
  5. Stir the sauce and pour it in, then return the chicken and any juices. Toss for 30 to 60 seconds until the sauce bubbles, thickens and coats everything with a light gloss.
  6. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve at once over rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Stir-frying raw broccoli takes too long and burns the garlic; blanch it first.
  • A thick, gloopy sauce; it should just coat.

Authentic tips

  • A little oil in the blanching water keeps the broccoli glossy green.
  • Peel and slice the stems; they are as good as the florets.

Substitutions

  • Gai lan replaces broccoli.
  • Beef flank or shrimp replace chicken.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.
