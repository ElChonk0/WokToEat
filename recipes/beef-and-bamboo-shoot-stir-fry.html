Beef and Bamboo Shoot Stir-Fry 笋丝炒牛肉

▾

A springtime Jiangnan stir-fry that pairs tender beef with the delicate crunch of fresh bamboo shoots, lightly seasoned to let both ingredients' natural flavors come through.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch, baking soda and a tablespoon of water and leave for 20 minutes. Blanch the bamboo shoots for 2 minutes to remove any bitterness and drain.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and sear the beef in a single layer for 1 minute, then toss for 30 seconds until just browned. Lift it out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the garlic and ginger for 15 seconds, then the bamboo shoots for 1 to 2 minutes until lightly browned.
  4. Return the beef with the sauce soy sauces and sugar and toss for 30 seconds until glossy.
  5. Add the green onion, toss once more and serve. Shanghai cooking leans slightly sweet, so the sugar is there on purpose.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanching step for fresh bamboo shoots, which leaves an unpleasant bitter taste.
  • Overcooking the beef, which turns it tough — sear quickly over very high heat.

Authentic tips

  • Canned bamboo shoots (rinsed well) are a perfectly fine, widely available substitute for fresh.
  • Slicing the beef thinly against the grain is essential for tenderness in this quick stir-fry.

Substitutions

  • Blanched canned bamboo shoots work year round.
  • Pork loin takes the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.
