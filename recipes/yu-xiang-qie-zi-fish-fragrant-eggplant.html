Yu Xiang Qie Zi (Fish-Fragrant Eggplant) 鱼香茄子

Yuxiang eggplant carries the Sichuan "fish-fragrant" sauce of pickled chili, garlic, ginger and vinegar, which contains no fish; it is among the most popular Sichuan vegetable dishes.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbChinese eggplant, cut into batons
  • 30 g2 tbspPickled chili paste
  • 60 g4 tbspGarlic, ginger and scallion
  • 30 ml2 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 20 g4 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspCornstarch slurry
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Toss the eggplant with 1 teaspoon of salt and leave 10 minutes; squeeze out the liquid and pat dry. Microwaving it for 2 minutes first reduces oil absorption.
  2. Heat 3 tablespoons of the oil in a wok over medium-high heat and fry the eggplant for 5-6 minutes until golden and soft; lift out.
  3. Add the remaining oil, the chili paste and the aromatics and fry 1 minute until the oil turns red.
  4. Add the vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and 100 ml water and bring to a simmer.
  5. Return the eggplant, simmer 2 minutes and stir in the slurry until the sauce is glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Eggplant soaks up oil like a sponge; salting and squeezing it first reduces this.
  • An over-thick sauce turns gluey, so add the slurry slowly.

Authentic tips

  • Balance the sauce: it should taste sweet, sour, salty and hot in turn.
  • Long, thin Chinese eggplant gives creamier results than globe eggplant.

Substitutions

  • Zucchini replaces the eggplant; fry it less.
  • Regular eggplant works if peeled and cut thin.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
