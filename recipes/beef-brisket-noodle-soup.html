Beef Brisket Noodle Soup 牛腩面

A Hong Kong comfort food institution, beef brisket noodle soup slow-simmers brisket in a fragrant five-spice broth until fall-apart tender, served over noodles — found at cha chaan tengs and noodle shops across the city.

Method

  1. Blanch beef (and tendon if using) in boiling water 5 minutes; drain and rinse.
  2. Combine beef, star anise, cinnamon, and ginger in a pot with enough water to cover generously.
  3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer, cover, and cook 1.5 hours.
  4. Add both soy sauces and rock sugar; continue simmering 30 minutes more until the beef is very tender.
  5. Cook egg noodles and blanch bok choy separately according to package directions.
  6. Divide noodles among bowls, ladle the beef and broth over top, and add bok choy and green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long simmer, which is essential for breaking down brisket's connective tissue into tenderness.
  • Skipping the initial blanch, which leaves the broth cloudier and less clean-tasting.

Authentic tips

  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half for a quick future meal.
  • Beef tendon, if you can find it, adds a prized gelatinous texture that many brisket noodle enthusiasts seek out.

Substitutions

  • Beef shank can substitute for brisket with a similar cooking time.
  • No tendon: the dish is still excellent with brisket alone.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; store noodles separately.

Freezing: The broth and beef freeze excellently for up to 3 months; cook fresh noodles when serving.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
