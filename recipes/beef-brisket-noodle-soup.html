Beef Brisket Noodle Soup 牛腩面

▾

Surprise me with

A Hong Kong comfort food institution, beef brisket noodle soup slow-simmers brisket in a fragrant five-spice broth until fall-apart tender, served over noodles — found at cha chaan tengs and noodle shops across the city.

Method

  1. Put the brisket and tendon in a pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil for 1 to 2 minutes, skimming off the scum, then drain and rinse each piece. This keeps the braising liquid clean.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, star anise, cinnamon and bay leaves for 2 minutes until fragrant. Raise the heat to high, add the beef and sear for 6 to 8 minutes, turning, until the edges are lightly browned.
  3. Add the wine, soy sauces and oyster sauce and bring to a boil, then add the water and rock sugar. Cover, return to a boil, then lower to a slow, steady bubble and simmer for 2 hours, stirring now and then, until the brisket is tender and the tendon is soft and wobbly. Season with salt.
  4. Shortly before serving, heat the stock with a ladle or two of the braising liquid and taste; it should be savory and lightly spiced. Blanch the greens in it for 1 minute and lift them out.
  5. Boil the noodles for about 1 minute, dip them briefly in cold water for a springier texture, return to the boiling water for 15 seconds and drain.
  6. Divide the noodles among bowls, top with the greens, brisket and tendon, and pour over the hot soup with a little extra braising liquid. Scatter with green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long simmer, which is essential for breaking down brisket's connective tissue into tenderness.
  • Skipping the initial blanch, which leaves the broth cloudier and less clean-tasting.

Authentic tips

  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half for a quick future meal.
  • Beef tendon, if you can find it, adds a prized gelatinous texture that many brisket noodle enthusiasts seek out.

Substitutions

  • Beef shank can substitute for brisket with a similar cooking time.
  • No tendon: the dish is still excellent with brisket alone.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; store noodles separately.

Freezing: The broth and beef freeze excellently for up to 3 months; cook fresh noodles when serving.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
