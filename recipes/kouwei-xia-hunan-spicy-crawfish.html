Kǒuwèi Xiā (Hunan Spicy Crayfish) 口味虾

▾

Surprise me with

Kouwei xia, "flavor shrimp," is a Changsha street-food staple from the late 1990s: crayfish braised with chilies, spices and perilla until saturated with heat.

  • Cook 20 min

  • 1.5 kg3.3 lbLive or frozen crayfish, cleaned

  • 60 g4 tbspGarlic and ginger

  • 5 g2 tspSichuan peppercorns

  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 80 ml5 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Scrub the crayfish well, trim the heads and remove the intestinal line if you like; drain and pat dry.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat and stir-fry the crayfish for 3 minutes until the shells turn bright red; lift out.
  3. In the same oil, fry the dried chilies, fresh chilies, garlic, ginger and Sichuan peppercorns for 1 minute.
  4. Return the crayfish, add the beer, soy sauce and perilla, cover and braise 10 minutes.
  5. Uncover and cook over high heat for 3-4 minutes until the liquid reduces to a spicy coating.

Common mistakes

  • Frozen crayfish are often pre-cooked, so cook them only briefly to avoid toughness.
  • Skipping the beer loses the depth the braise needs.

Authentic tips

  • Perilla leaves are the signature herb of Hunan seafood cooking; Thai basil is the nearest substitute.
  • Provide plenty of napkins and a bowl for shells.

Substitutions

  • Large shell-on shrimp give a quicker version.
  • A spoonful of sugar rounds out the heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day; leftover meat can be picked into fried rice.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
