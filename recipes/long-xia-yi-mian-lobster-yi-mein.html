Lóng Xiā Yī Miàn (Ginger-Scallion Lobster with Yi Mein) 姜葱龙虾伊面

▾

Surprise me with

Lobster stir-fried with ginger and scallion over braised yi mein noodles is the showpiece of Cantonese wedding and birthday banquets; the noodles symbolize long life.

Method

  1. Boil the noodle cakes one at a time in a wok of boiling water over high heat for 2 minutes, loosening them with chopsticks, then drain and spread on a platter. Mix the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sugar, salt, pepper and half the wine, and the slurry cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  2. If using live lobster, chill it in the freezer for 30 minutes, then split and cut it into pieces, cracking the claws. Pat the pieces dry and dust only the exposed meat with cornstarch, which seals in the juices.
  3. Heat the wok over high heat for 2 minutes, add 4 tablespoons of oil and heat until it ripples, about 180 C (350 F). Fry the lobster in batches for 40 to 50 seconds, until the shells turn bright red and the meat turns white. Lift it out; it finishes in the sauce.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil and fry the garlic, ginger and green onion whites for about 50 seconds until fragrant. Return the lobster for 20 seconds and splash in the remaining wine.
  5. Add the boiling water and the sauce, cover and boil for 30 seconds, then uncover and cook 1 minute. Stir in the slurry until the sauce turns lightly glossy, then add the green onion greens for 30 seconds and the sesame oil.
  6. Lift the lobster onto the noodles and pour the sauce over; the spongy yi mein soaks it up. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked lobster turns rubbery; it needs only about 5 minutes in total.
  • Soggy noodles: braise them until nearly dry.

Authentic tips

  • Ask the fishmonger to cut the lobster if you are using a live one.
  • The cornstarch dusting seals in the juices and thickens the sauce slightly.

Substitutions

  • Large shrimp in the shell replace lobster.
  • Crab works the same way.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Braised shiitake with bok choy
  • Soup: Dried scallop and chicken soup
  • Dessert: Longevity peach buns
  • Beverage: Chilled white wine or tea
