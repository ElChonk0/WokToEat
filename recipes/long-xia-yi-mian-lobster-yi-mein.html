Long Xia Yi Mian (Ginger-Scallion Lobster with Yi Mein) 姜葱龙虾伊面

Lobster stir-fried with ginger and scallion over braised yi mein noodles is the showpiece of Cantonese wedding and birthday banquets; the noodles symbolize long life.

  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Blanch the yi mein 1 minute, drain and shake dry. Pat the lobster dry and dust the cut sides with cornstarch.
  2. Heat 4 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry the lobster pieces 2 to 3 minutes until the shells turn red. Lift out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the ginger and shallots 1 minute, then the green onion whites. Return the lobster, splash in the wine around the hot edge and add 250 ml of stock, the oyster sauce and sugar. Cover and cook 3 minutes.
  4. Lift out the lobster. Add the noodles, soy sauce and remaining stock to the wok and braise, tossing with tongs, for 2 to 3 minutes until the liquid is absorbed.
  5. Pile the noodles on a platter, top with the lobster and green onion tops and drizzle with sesame oil.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked lobster turns rubbery; it needs only about 5 minutes in total.
  • Soggy noodles: braise them until nearly dry.

Authentic tips

  • Ask the fishmonger to cut the lobster if you are using a live one.
  • The cornstarch dusting seals in the juices and thickens the sauce slightly.

Substitutions

  • Large shrimp in the shell replace lobster.
  • Crab works the same way.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Braised shiitake with bok choy
  • Soup: Dried scallop and chicken soup
  • Dessert: Longevity peach buns
  • Beverage: Chilled white wine or tea
