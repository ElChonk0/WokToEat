Moo Ping (Grilled Pork Skewers) หมูปิ้ง

▾

Surprise me with

A ubiquitous Thai street food, moo ping marinates pork in a coconut milk, palm sugar, and cilantro root mixture before grilling on skewers — sold from carts across Thailand, often eaten with sticky rice for breakfast.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 12 min

Method

  1. Pound cilantro root, garlic, and white pepper into a rough paste using a mortar and pestle or food processor.
  2. Whisk the paste with coconut milk, palm sugar, and fish sauce for the marinade.
  3. Coat pork slices and marinate at least 2 hours, or overnight for best flavor.
  4. Thread pork onto skewers.
  5. Grill over medium heat, turning occasionally, 10–12 minutes until charred in spots and cooked through.
  6. Serve with sticky rice.

Common mistakes

  • Grilling over too high direct heat, which chars the sugary marinade before the pork cooks through.
  • Skipping the cilantro root — it has a more intense, earthy flavor than the leaves and is central to the marinade.

Authentic tips

  • If cilantro root isn't available, the lower stems are the closest substitute — avoid using just the leafy tops.
  • Moving skewers to indirect heat once charred lets them finish cooking through without burning.

Substitutions

  • No grill: a hot grill pan or broiler both work well as substitutes.
  • Chicken thigh works well with the same marinade and method.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice and jaew dipping sauce
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot coffee or Thai iced tea
