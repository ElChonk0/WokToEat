San Bei Ji (Three Cup Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Named for its traditional ratio of one cup each of soy sauce, rice wine, and sesame oil, this Taiwanese dish is built around fresh basil and a generous amount of garlic and ginger, simmered until the sauce reduces to a glossy, intensely aromatic glaze.

Method

  1. Heat the sesame oil in a flat-bottom wok or clay pot over medium-low heat and fry the ginger slices for 3 to 4 minutes until they curl and brown at the edges. Add the garlic and dried chilies and fry for 2 minutes until golden.
  2. Raise the heat to medium-high, add the chicken and stir-fry for 4 to 5 minutes until the skin is browned.
  3. Add the sugar and let it caramelize slightly for 30 seconds, then the soy sauce and rice wine. The three 'cups' are sesame oil, soy sauce and rice wine in equal parts.
  4. Cover and simmer over medium heat for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked through.
  5. Uncover and cook over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes, tossing, until the sauce reduces to a thick, dark glaze. Turn off the heat, toss in the basil, cover for 30 seconds so it wilts in the steam, and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the basil too early: it should go in at the very end so it stays bright rather than turning dark and bitter from prolonged cooking.

Authentic tips

  • Whole, unpeeled (or lightly crushed) garlic cloves cooked slowly become soft and sweet — don't chop them too small.

Substitutions

  • No Taiwanese basil: Thai basil is the closest and most available substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3–4 days; flavor deepens overnight.

Freezing: Freezes well without the basil; add fresh basil after reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice to catch the sauce
  • Soup: Corn and pork rib soup
  • Dessert: Shaved ice with toppings
  • Beverage: Winter melon tea
