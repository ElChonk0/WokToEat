Kimchi-jeon (Kimchi Pancake) 김치전

▾

Surprise me with

Kimchi-jeon is the Korean way of using up sour, well-fermented kimchi; the crisp pancakes are traditionally eaten with makgeolli on rainy days.

Method

  1. Slice the kimchi and vegetables thinly so the pancake cooks evenly and stays thin. Mix the dipping soy sauce, vinegar and water.
  2. Whisk the flour, kimchi juice, gochugaru and sugar with the ice water, adding it a little at a time, just until the batter flows off a spoon; do not overmix, or the pancake turns chewy. Ice water and kimchi juice are what make it crisp.
  3. Stir in the kimchi, pork, onion and green onion.
  4. Heat a large nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium heat with 1 tablespoon of oil. Spread half the batter into a thin, even round. Cook for about 3 minutes, until the edges turn golden and the top looks set at the rim.
  5. Flip, drizzle another tablespoon of oil around the edge and press the pancake flat with a spatula. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, raising the heat for the last minute, until crisp and the pork is cooked through. Repeat with the rest.
  6. Cut into wedges and serve hot with the dipping sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • A thick pancake stays doughy; spread it thin.
  • Fresh kimchi tastes flat here; use sour, well-fermented kimchi.

Authentic tips

  • Ice-cold water and a little potato starch make the edges extra crisp.
  • Drizzle more oil around the edge when you flip for a lacy crust.

Substitutions

  • Canned tuna or squid replace the pork.
  • Leave out the pork and use vegan kimchi for a vegetarian pancake.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-crisp in a dry skillet.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month; reheat in a skillet from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Dipping sauce of soy and vinegar
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced Korean pear
  • Beverage: Makgeolli
