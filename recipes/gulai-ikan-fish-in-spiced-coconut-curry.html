Gulai Ikan (Padang Fish Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Gulai, a turmeric-yellow coconut curry, is a Minangkabau dish of West Sumatra eaten across Indonesia; gulai ikan uses firm fish.

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the lime juice and a pinch of salt and leave for 10 minutes, then pat dry. This freshens it and firms the flesh.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, ginger, turmeric, galangal and candlenuts into a smooth paste.
  3. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium heat and fry the spice paste with the lemongrass, turmeric leaf and lime leaves, stirring often, for 6 to 8 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried paste tastes sharp and grassy.
  4. Add the water, coconut milk, asam kandis and salt and bring to a gentle simmer, stirring constantly so the coconut milk does not split. Add the vegetables and simmer for 5 minutes.
  5. Slide in the fish and simmer gently for 8 to 10 minutes, spooning the sauce over without stirring, until it flakes. Serve with rice; gulai is the Minangkabau word for a coconut curry.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard splits the coconut milk.
  • Stirring breaks the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Add vegetables like eggplant or jackfruit.
  • Better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Mackerel or catfish replace the fish.
  • Eggplant and jackfruit join the curry.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; coconut milk may separate slightly.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
