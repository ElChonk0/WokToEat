Jiàohuā Jī (Beggar's Chicken) 叫花鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Beggar's chicken, chicken wrapped in lotus leaf and clay and baked, is a Jiangnan specialty associated with Hangzhou; legend says a beggar baked a stolen chicken in mud.

  • Cook 2 hr

  • 1.4 kg3 lbWhole chicken
  • 22Dried lotus leaves
  • 80 g3 ozShiitake mushrooms and ham
  • 60 ml4 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 60 g4 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 1 kg2.2 lbSalt dough or foil

Method

  1. Soak the dried lotus leaves in hot water for 30 minutes until pliable. Soak the shiitake if dried, slice them and dice the ham.
  2. Rub the chicken inside and out with the wine and both soy sauces, tuck the ginger and scallion into the cavity and marinate in the fridge for 4 hours.
  3. Stuff the cavity with the mushrooms and ham, then wrap the chicken tightly in the lotus leaves, tying with kitchen string.
  4. Wrap the parcel in a thick layer of salt dough (or two layers of heavy foil), sealing all seams so no steam escapes.
  5. Bake at 180 C (350 F) for 2 hours, until the juices run clear (75 C / 165 F at the thigh).
  6. Rest 15 minutes, crack open the casing at the table, unwrap the leaves and carve.

Common mistakes

  • A leak in the casing lets the steam escape, which leaves the chicken dry.
  • Cutting the casing open too soon sends the fragrant steam and juices away; rest it first.

Authentic tips

  • Lotus leaves give a tea-like fragrance; parchment makes a poor but workable substitute.
  • Serve with the stuffing and the juices spooned over.

Substitutions

  • Foil plus parchment works if you can't find dried lotus leaves.
  • Chicken legs stuffed the same way cook in 75 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 15 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes the cooked meat for 2 months; shred for soups.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or lotus-leaf buns
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
