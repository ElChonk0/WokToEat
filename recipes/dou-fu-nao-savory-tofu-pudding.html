Dòu Fu Nǎo (Beijing Savory Tofu Pudding) 豆腐脑

▾

Dou fu nao, silken tofu in a thick savory sauce of mushrooms and wood ear, is a Beijing breakfast classic eaten with fried dough sticks.

Method

  1. Warm the tofu gently in its container in hot water, or steam it for 5 minutes, then scoop it into bowls in large, soft spoonfuls.
  2. Make the lu (gravy): bring the stock to a boil with the shiitake, wood ear and daylily and simmer for 5 minutes. Season with the soy sauces and salt.
  3. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer until the gravy is thick and glossy. (Many Beijing versions use lamb or an egg drop; this one is vegan.)
  4. Ladle the hot gravy over the tofu.
  5. Top with garlic water, sesame oil, chili oil and cilantro. Savory tofu pudding is a classic northern breakfast; the south eats it sweet.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the tofu makes it spongy.
  • Adding the slurry too fast creates lumps.

Authentic tips

  • Scoop with a flat spoon for wide, thin curls.
  • Serve with fried dough sticks.

Substitutions

  • Soft tofu replaces silken tofu if it isn't available.
  • A vegetarian mushroom gravy replaces the traditional meat gravy.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the tofu pudding firms up when cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Youtiao (fried dough sticks)
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot soy milk
