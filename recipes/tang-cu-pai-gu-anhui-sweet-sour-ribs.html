Táng Cù Pái Gǔ (Anhui Sweet and Sour Ribs) 糖醋排骨

▾

Surprise me with

Sweet and sour ribs are popular across the lower Yangtze; Anhui's version tends to be darker and tangier with local vinegar.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 800 g1.75 lbPork ribs, cut into 4 cm (1.5 in) pieces
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and green onion
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 ml1 tspDark soy sauce
  • 45 g3.5 tbspSugar
  • 60 ml1/4 cupChinkiang vinegar
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsWater
  • 5 g2 tspToasted sesame seeds

Method

  1. Blanch the ribs for 3 minutes and pat dry.
  2. Fry the ribs in the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat for 5 to 6 minutes until golden, with the ginger and green onion.
  3. Add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces, sugar, two-thirds of the vinegar and the water. Cover and simmer for 35 to 40 minutes until tender.
  4. Uncover and reduce over medium-high heat, stirring, until the sauce is thick and sticky.
  5. Add the remaining vinegar in the last minute to keep its tang, and scatter with sesame seeds.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the vinegar too early cooks off its sourness; add it at the end.
  • Over-reducing makes the sauce burn.

Authentic tips

  • Chinkiang vinegar gives a darker, more complex tang.
  • Skim the fat before reducing.

Substitutions

  • Baby back ribs shorten the cooking by 10 minutes.
  • Rice vinegar replaces Chinkiang.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat in a pan to re-glaze.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
