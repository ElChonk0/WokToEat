Jiān Máo Dòu Fu (Pan-Fried Fuzzy Tofu) 煎毛豆腐

▾

Surprise me with

Mao doufu, "hairy tofu," is tofu fermented until it grows a fine white mold, then pan-fried; it is a signature of Huizhou in Anhui and a regional delicacy.

  • Cook 12 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 500 g1.1 lbMao doufu (fermented fuzzy tofu), cut into pieces
  • 60 ml1/4 cupNeutral oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili sauce or chopped chili paste
  • 15 g1/2 ozGarlic, minced
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion, chopped

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a cast-iron skillet over medium heat. Lay in the tofu pieces and fry for 3 to 4 minutes per side, without moving them, until both sides are golden and crisp and the inside is soft and almost molten.
  2. Mix the chili sauce, garlic, soy sauce and sugar.
  3. Dab a little sauce on each piece of tofu.
  4. Scatter with green onion.
  5. Serve straight from the hot pan. Pan-fried mao doufu is a famous Huizhou street snack, often cooked in front of the customer.

Common mistakes

  • Wet tofu spatters in the oil.
  • Cooking too fast burns the outside before the inside is warm.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh mao doufu has a fine white fuzz; avoid any with dark or colored mold.
  • Mao doufu is sold at Chinese markets, or use stinky tofu as a stand-in.

Substitutions

  • Stinky tofu replaces mao doufu.
  • Firm tofu with fermented bean curd approximates the flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.
