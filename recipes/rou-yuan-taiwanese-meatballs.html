Ròu Yuán (Taiwanese Steamed Pork Meatballs) 肉圓

▾

Surprise me with

Ba-wan, or rou yuan, is a translucent, chewy dumpling of sweet potato starch around a savory pork filling, a snack of Changhua and Taiwan's central coast.

Method

  1. Marinate the pork with the soy sauce, five-spice, pepper and sugar for 20 minutes, then stir-fry it with the shiitake, bamboo shoots and fried shallots for 4 to 5 minutes until cooked. Cool.
  2. Mix the starches and rice flour with the cold water, then pour in the boiling water while stirring quickly until it turns into a thick, translucent paste. The boiling water half-cooks the starch so the skin is chewy and clear.
  3. Oil 8 small heatproof bowls. Spread a layer of paste in each, add a spoon of filling and cover with more paste, smoothing the top with a wet spoon.
  4. Steam over boiling water for 15 to 20 minutes, until the skins turn fully translucent. Cool for 5 minutes and ease them out.
  5. Simmer the chili sauce, soy paste, sugar and rice flour slurry until thick and glossy. Serve the rou yuan in the sauce, Changhua style; in Hsinchu they are fried after steaming.

Common mistakes

  • Dough that is too dry cracks.
  • Overfilling splits the dough.

Authentic tips

  • Fry them afterward for a crisp skin (Taiwanese rou yuan are often fried).
  • Wet hands make shaping easier.

Substitutions

  • Fry the cooked rou yuan until crisp, as is common in central Taiwan.
  • Ground chicken replaces pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

  • Soup: Soy milk
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
