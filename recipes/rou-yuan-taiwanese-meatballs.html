Ròu Yuán (Taiwanese Steamed Pork Meatballs) 肉圓

▾

Surprise me with

Ba-wan, or rou yuan, is a translucent, chewy dumpling of sweet potato starch around a savory pork filling, a snack of Changhua and Taiwan's central coast.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 150 g1.25 cupsSweet potato starch
  • 60 g2 ozTapioca starch and rice flour
  • 300 g10 ozPork, ground
  • 80 g3 ozDried shiitake and bamboo
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce
  • 90 ml6 tbspSweet-savory sauce
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsHot water

Method

  1. Sift the sweet potato starch, tapioca starch and rice flour; stir in the hot water and knead into a smooth, sticky dough; rest 15 minutes.
  2. Stir-fry the pork, shiitake and bamboo shoots with the soy sauce for 5 minutes; cool.
  3. Press a spoonful of dough into a small bowl, add filling and seal with more dough.
  4. Steam 20 minutes on oiled parchment until translucent.
  5. Serve with the sweet-savory sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Dough that is too dry cracks.
  • Overfilling splits the dough.

Authentic tips

  • Fry them afterward for a crisp skin (Taiwanese rou yuan are often fried).
  • Wet hands make shaping easier.

Substitutions

  • Fry the cooked rou yuan until crisp, as is common in central Taiwan.
  • Ground chicken replaces pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Clear soup
  • Soup: Soy milk
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
  • Beverage: Winter melon tea
