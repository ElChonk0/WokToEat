Sinigang na Isda (Sour Tamarind Fish Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Sinigang is the Philippines' best-known sour soup, traditionally soured with tamarind and built around whatever protein is on hand — fish, pork, or shrimp — making it a flexible, everyday comfort dish across the islands.

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil with the onion and tomato and simmer for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tamarind (boiled pulp pressed through a strainer, or the powdered base) and the radish, and simmer for 5 minutes until the radish is almost tender.
  3. Add the long beans and green chili and simmer for 3 minutes.
  4. Lower the heat and slide in the fish. Simmer gently for 5 to 6 minutes, without stirring, until the fish flakes. Salmon belly or milkfish belly makes the richest sinigang.
  5. Add the water spinach for the last minute, season with fish sauce and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the fish, which falls apart and clouds the broth — add it last and simmer only until just opaque.
  • Under-seasoning the sourness — sinigang should taste assertively sour, not just lightly tangy; add more tamarind if needed.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh tamarind pulp gives a rounder sourness than the powdered packet mix, if available.
  • This soup is endlessly adaptable — swap in shrimp, pork belly, or beef short rib for the fish on a different week.

Substitutions

  • Kangkong (water spinach) can be swapped for regular spinach or bok choy with little change to the dish.
  • No tamarind — a mix of lime juice and a small amount of brown sugar gets reasonably close, though brighter and less rounded.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth's flavor deepens overnight; reheat gently to avoid further breaking up the fish.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably without the leafy greens; add fresh greens after reheating from frozen.
