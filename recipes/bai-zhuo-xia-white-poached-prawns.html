Bái Zhuó Xiā (White-Poached Prawns) 白灼虾

▾

Surprise me with

Bai zhuo, "white-blanching," is the Cantonese method of poaching live seafood in barely seasoned water and serving it with a soy dip, the purest way to show off freshness.

Method

  1. Rinse the prawns under cold water. Trim the long feelers and legs with scissors, but leave the shells and heads on: they keep the meat juicy and sweet. Set out a large bowl of ice water.
  2. Make the dip: put the julienned ginger, green onion and chili in a heatproof bowl. Heat the oil until it just smokes and pour it over so it sizzles, then stir in the soy sauce and sugar.
  3. Bring the water to a rolling boil with the sliced ginger, knotted green onion, wine and salt, and let it boil for 2 minutes so the aromatics flavor the water.
  4. Add the prawns all at once and stir. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, depending on size, just until the shells turn bright pink-orange and the bodies curl into a loose C. A tight O means they are overcooked and rubbery.
  5. Lift them straight into the ice water for 30 seconds to stop the cooking and firm the flesh, then drain. Serve warm or at room temperature with the dip, peeling them at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked prawns turn rubbery and lose their sweetness; pull them the moment they curl.
  • Prawns that are not very fresh will taste fishy since nothing masks them.

Authentic tips

  • Choose heads-on prawns; the heads add sweetness to the cooking water.
  • Add a pinch of sugar and a few slices of ginger to the dip.

Substitutions

  • Squid or scallops can be poached in the same way for 1 minute.
  • Add a little sugar and sesame oil to the soy dip for a sweeter finish.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat immediately; leftover prawns keep 1 day and are best cold in salad.

Freezing: Not recommended; poached prawns turn mealy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Choy sum with oyster sauce
  • Dessert: Mango pomelo sago
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
