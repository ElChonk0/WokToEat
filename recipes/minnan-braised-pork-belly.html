Minnan Braised Pork Belly 焢肉

Southern Fujian’s soy-braised pork belly, cooked in thick slabs with garlic and a little five-spice, is the ancestor of the kong rou fan sold over rice across Quanzhou, Zhangzhou and Taiwan.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 800 g1.75 lbSkin-on pork belly, in 1.5 cm thick slabs
  • 8 cloves8 clovesGarlic, whole

  • 60 ml1/4 cupLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce

  • 25 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 2 ml1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater
  • 44Hard-boiled eggs (optional)

Method

  1. Sear the pork slabs skin-side down in a dry pot until the skin blisters slightly; turn and brown the other side.
  2. Add garlic and shallots and fry until golden in the rendered fat.
  3. Add both soy sauces, wine, rock sugar, five-spice and water; bring to a boil.
  4. Cover and simmer gently 1 hour 15 minutes, turning the slabs once; add the eggs for the last 30 minutes.
  5. Uncover and reduce until the sauce is glossy. Serve a slab over rice with sauce and an egg.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the belly into cubes; this dish is meant to be thick slabs that hold their shape.

Authentic tips

  • Make it a day ahead, chill, and lift off the solid fat before reheating.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner version; add 15 minutes to the braise.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the flavour deepens.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
