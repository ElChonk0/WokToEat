Huáinán Dòu Fu Nǎo (Huainan Savory Tofu Pudding) 淮南豆腐脑

▾

Surprise me with

Huainan is the legendary birthplace of tofu, and savory dou fu nao, silky tofu pudding in a seasoned sauce, is a common breakfast there and across northern China.

Ingredients

  • 600 g1.3 lbSilken tofu or tofu pudding (douhua)
  • 20 g3/4 ozDried shiitake, soaked and sliced
  • 15 g1/2 ozWood ear mushrooms, soaked and sliced
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsMushroom soaking liquid and water
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspCornstarch slurry (1 tbsp starch + 2 tbsp water)
  • 40 g1.5 ozPickled mustard greens, chopped
  • 20 g2 tbspScallion
  • 15 ml1 tbspChili oil
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt

Method

  1. Warm the tofu gently in a pan of barely simmering water for 5 minutes so it is hot but intact.
  2. Simmer the shiitake and wood ear in the mushroom liquid for 8 minutes; season with the soy sauce and salt.
  3. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and cook 1 minute until the sauce is glossy and slightly thick.
  4. Scoop the tofu in thin, wide slices into bowls with a flat spoon.
  5. Ladle the sauce over and top with pickled greens, scallion, chili oil and sesame oil.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the tofu makes it spongy and watery.
  • A sauce that is too thick masks the delicate texture of the pudding.

Authentic tips

  • Slice with a flat, wide spoon in thin ribbons.
  • Savory (xian) versions are typical in the north; sweet versions are found in the south.

Substitutions

  • Soft tofu replaces tofu pudding.
  • Add dried shrimp for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Youtiao (fried dough sticks)
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot soy milk
