Baechu-kimchi (Napa Cabbage Kimchi) 배추김치

▾

Surprise me with

Napa cabbage kimchi is the everyday kimchi of Korea; families make it in bulk each autumn in the communal kimjang tradition, listed by UNESCO in 2013. This version is vegetarian.

Method

  1. Quarter the cabbage lengthwise, keeping the core attached so the leaves hold together. Dissolve half the salt in 1.2 L (5 cups) of water, dip each quarter in it, then sprinkle the remaining salt between the leaves, concentrating on the thick white stems.
  2. Leave the cabbage at room temperature for 6 to 8 hours, turning it every 2 to 3 hours, until the white parts bend without snapping. That bend means it has drawn out enough water to stay crunchy and absorb the seasoning.
  3. Rinse the quarters three times in cold water, shaking out the salt between the leaves, and drain them cut-side down for at least 30 minutes.
  4. Simmer the glutinous rice flour with the porridge water over low heat, stirring, for 2 to 3 minutes until it thickens into a thin paste, then cool it. The paste helps the seasoning cling and feeds the fermentation.
  5. Mix the cooled paste with the gochugaru, garlic, ginger, onion, pear, soy sauce and sugar and let it stand for 10 minutes until the chili flakes soften. Toss in the radish and green onion. Taste: it should be a little saltier than you would like to eat, as the cabbage dilutes it.
  6. Wearing gloves, spread 1 to 2 tablespoons of the seasoning between every leaf of each quarter, working from the core outward. Fold each quarter in half, wrap the outer leaf around it and pack it cut-side up into a clean jar or container, pressing out air pockets.
  7. Leave it at room temperature for 1 to 2 days, until it smells pleasantly sour and you see small bubbles, then refrigerate. It is good fresh, at its best after about two weeks, and keeps for months, turning more sour over time.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Under-salted cabbage stays stiff and goes watery; it must bend easily.
  • Packing loosely lets air in and invites mold.

Authentic tips

  • Traditional kimchi uses fish sauce and salted shrimp (saeujeot) in the paste; add 45 ml of fish sauce and 30 g salted shrimp in place of the soy sauce if it does not need to be vegetarian.
  • Fresh kimchi is for eating as is; sour, aged kimchi is for stews and fried rice.

Substitutions

  • Kelp or mushroom stock in place of water in the porridge adds depth.
  • Use less gochugaru for children.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 to 3 months in the refrigerator, growing more sour.

Freezing: Not recommended; it loses its crunch.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Served with every Korean meal
  • Soup: Kimchi jjigae when it is sour
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
