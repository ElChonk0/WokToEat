Korean Beef Bulgogi Hot Pot (Bulgogi Jeongol)

▾

Surprise me with

A hot pot variation of standard bulgogi, jeongol simmers marinated beef with vegetables and noodles together in a shared pot at the table — a more communal, soupier take on the familiar grilled dish.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, honey, mirin, pear, garlic, sesame oil and pepper and marinate the beef in it while you prepare the broth and vegetables. Partly freezing the beef first makes thin slicing easy.
  2. Bring the broth water, kelp and radish to a boil, then simmer over medium heat for about 15 minutes. Discard the solids and season with the soup soy sauce and a little salt. Skip any extra sugar: the marinade sweetens the broth as it cooks.
  3. Cut the vegetables into thin, bite-size pieces. Arrange the beef in the center of a wide, shallow pot or Dutch oven, with the vegetables, mushrooms and noodles in separate clusters around it.
  4. Pour in about 600 ml (2.5 cups) of the broth and bring to a boil over high heat at the table or on the stove, separating the beef with chopsticks as it cooks.
  5. Simmer for 5 to 8 minutes, until the beef is just cooked and the cabbage is tender, adding more broth as it reduces. Ladle into bowls with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the glass noodles too early, which makes them overcook and turn mushy.
  • Overcrowding the pot, which prevents even cooking of the beef and vegetables.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is meant to be cooked and eaten communally, ideally at the table with a portable burner.
  • Soaking the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes before adding ensures they cook evenly in the remaining time.

Substitutions

  • Pork works well with the same marinade for a variation.
  • Any mushroom variety can substitute for enoki, though its delicate texture is traditional.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store noodles separately if possible.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle and vegetable texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and kimchi
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
