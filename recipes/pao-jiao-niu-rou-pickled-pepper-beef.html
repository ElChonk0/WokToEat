Pào Jiāo Niú Ròu (Pickled Pepper Beef) 泡椒牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Pickled chilies lend a sour heat to this quick Sichuan stir-fry of beef, a staple of home cooking and small restaurants.

  • Cook 10 min

  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspCornstarch
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the beef 4 mm thick across the grain and toss with the soy sauce, cornstarch and 1 tablespoon of water; marinate 15 minutes. Chop the pickled chilies and ginger and slice the celery.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat until it smokes and sear the beef 45 seconds, stirring, until just browned; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, then the pickled chilies, pickled ginger and garlic and fry 1 minute.
  4. Return the beef with the celery and a splash of the pickle brine and toss over high heat for 45 seconds.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough; it should still be slightly pink when it leaves the pan.
  • Skipping the pickled ginger loses the sour, fresh edge that defines the dish.

Authentic tips

  • Taste before seasoning; the pickles already supply salt.
  • Choose a tender cut like sirloin or flank for a quick stir-fry.

Substitutions

  • Pork loin works with the same method.
  • Fresh red chilies plus extra vinegar replace the pickled ones.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
