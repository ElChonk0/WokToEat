Pào Jiāo Niú Ròu (Pickled Pepper Beef) 泡椒牛肉

▾

Pickled chilies lend a sour heat to this quick Sichuan stir-fry of beef, a staple of home cooking and small restaurants.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and 1 tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the beef for 1 minute, until it just changes color. Lift it out.
  3. Add the remaining oil, lower the heat to medium and fry the pickled chilies and pickled ginger for 1 minute, until the oil turns orange and smells tangy. Pickled chilies give a sour, bright heat rather than the deep heat of doubanjiang.
  4. Add the garlic and celery and stir-fry over high heat for 1 minute.
  5. Return the beef with the sauce soy sauce and sugar and toss for 30 seconds until glossy. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough; it should still be slightly pink when it leaves the pan.
  • Skipping the pickled ginger loses the sour, fresh edge that defines the dish.

Authentic tips

  • Taste before seasoning; the pickles already supply salt.
  • Choose a tender cut like sirloin or flank for a quick stir-fry.

Substitutions

  • Pork loin works with the same method.
  • Fresh red chilies plus extra vinegar replace the pickled ones.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.
