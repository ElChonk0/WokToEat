Beef and Turnip Braise 萝卜烧牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

A homestyle Jiangnan braise pairing beef with Chinese white turnip (daikon), which absorbs the sweet soy braising liquid while adding a light, slightly peppery freshness to balance the meat's richness.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 800 g1.75 lbBeef chuck or shank, cut into chunks
  • 400 g14 ozDaikon radish, cut into chunks
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 20 g1.5 tbspRock sugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 500 ml2 cupsWater

Method

  1. Blanch beef in boiling water 3 minutes; drain.
  2. Brown beef in a little oil, add ginger, stirring briefly.
  3. Add both soy sauces, rock sugar, and wine, stirring to coat.
  4. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 1 to 1.25 hours until the beef is nearly tender.
  5. Add daikon and continue simmering 20–25 minutes until the daikon is tender and the sauce has reduced.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the daikon too early, which lets it turn mushy by the time the beef is fully tender.
  • Cutting the daikon too small, which causes it to fall apart before the braise finishes.

Authentic tips

  • Daikon absorbs the braising liquid's flavor beautifully — cut it into fairly large chunks so it holds its shape.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Chuck replaces the shank with a shorter braise.
  • Carrots can join the daikon for sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the daikon, which turns spongy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
