Beef and Turnip Braise 萝卜烧牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

A homestyle Jiangnan braise pairing beef with Chinese white turnip (daikon), which absorbs the sweet soy braising liquid while adding a light, slightly peppery freshness to balance the meat's richness.

  • Cook 2.5 hr

Method

  1. Put the beef in a pot of cold water, bring it to a boil and boil for 5 minutes. Drain and rinse off the scum; starting in cold water draws out more of the blood.
  2. Heat a tablespoon of oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and star anise for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the rock sugar and stir over medium-low heat until it melts into an amber caramel, then add the beef and turn it until glossy and evenly colored. Caramelized sugar, not just dark soy, gives Shanghai red-braising its lacquered color.
  4. Pour in the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the pot so it sizzles, then the soy sauces and enough hot water to almost cover. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 hours, until the beef is almost tender.
  5. Add the daikon and simmer for 30 to 40 minutes more, until it is translucent and soft and has soaked up the sauce. The radish sweetens the braise and is often the best part.
  6. Uncover, raise the heat to medium-high and reduce the sauce for 5 to 10 minutes, turning and basting, until it is thick, glossy and clings to the meat. This final reduction (shou zhi) is what makes the dish 'nong you chi jiang': rich in oil, dark in sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the daikon too early, which lets it turn mushy by the time the beef is fully tender.
  • Cutting the daikon too small, which causes it to fall apart before the braise finishes.

Authentic tips

  • Daikon absorbs the braising liquid's flavor beautifully — cut it into fairly large chunks so it holds its shape.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Chuck replaces the shank with a shorter braise.
  • Carrots can join the daikon for sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the daikon, which turns spongy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
