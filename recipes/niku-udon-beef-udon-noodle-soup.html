Niku Udon (Beef Udon Noodle Soup) 肉うどん

▾

Surprise me with

Niku udon, udon in dashi broth topped with sweet simmered beef, is a Kansai noodle-shop classic; in eastern Japan pork is more common.

Method

  1. Bring the dashi to a simmer and season it with the broth soy sauce, mirin and salt. Kansai-style niku udon uses light soy sauce for a pale, delicate broth.
  2. In a small skillet, simmer the soy sauce, mirin, sugar and water for the beef. Add the onion and cook for 3 minutes, then add the beef and simmer for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until it is cooked and coated in a sweet glaze.
  3. Cook the udon in boiling water according to the packet, usually 1 minute for frozen, and drain.
  4. Divide the noodles among warmed bowls and ladle the hot broth over.
  5. Top with the sweet beef and onion, a little of their sauce, green onion and shichimi. The beef sauce slowly sweetens the broth as you eat.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the thin beef makes it tough.
  • Dark soy sauce makes the Kansai broth too dark; use light soy.

Authentic tips

  • Kansai broth is pale and dashi-forward; let the dashi lead.
  • Add a soft-boiled egg or tempura flakes.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly for buta udon.
  • Instant dashi granules make quick stock.

Storage

Leftovers: Store broth and beef separately for 3 days.

Freezing: The beef topping freezes for 2 months.
