Gān Guō Huā Cài (Dry-Pot Cauliflower) 干锅花菜

▾

Surprise me with

Dry pot, or gan guo, is a Sichuan and Hunan style where ingredients are stir-fried with dried chilies and aromatics and served in a pot over a small flame; cauliflower is a classic vegetarian version.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbCauliflower, in florets
  • 10 g1/3 ozDried chilies
  • 3 g1 tspSichuan peppercorns
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and ginger
  • 20 g1.5 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 150 g5 ozOnion and celery
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Cut the cauliflower into small florets and dry them well. Slice the onion and celery.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over medium-high heat and fry the cauliflower 5-6 minutes, until browned at the edges but still firm; lift out.
  3. In the same oil fry the dried chilies and peppercorns for 20 seconds, then add the doubanjiang, garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute until the oil turns red.
  4. Return the cauliflower with the onion, celery and soy sauce and toss over high heat 2 minutes.
  5. Serve in a pre-warmed cast-iron skillet or heavy pot so it stays hot.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the cauliflower makes it soggy; frying gives the browned edges that carry the flavor.
  • Too many chilies dominate; they are mainly for aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Cutting florets small and even helps them brown.
  • Roasting the cauliflower at 220 C (425 F) for 20 minutes is a lighter alternative.

Substitutions

  • Broccoli or cabbage replace the cauliflower.
  • Fried tofu puffs make it heartier.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
