Shanghai Red-Braised Chicken 红烧鸡腿

▾

Surprise me with

Hong shao (red braising) is the signature Shanghainese technique of slow-simmering in soy sauce, wine, and rock sugar until glossy and deeply savory-sweet — the same method used for the region's famous red-braised pork.

Method

  1. Put the chicken in a pot of cold water, bring it to a boil and boil for 2 minutes. Drain and rinse off the scum; starting in cold water draws out more of the blood.
  2. Heat a tablespoon of oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and star anise for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the rock sugar and stir over medium-low heat until it melts into an amber caramel, then add the chicken and turn it until glossy and evenly colored. Caramelized sugar, not just dark soy, gives Shanghai red-braising its lacquered color.
  4. Pour in the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the pot so it sizzles, then the soy sauces and enough hot water to almost cover. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, until the chicken is tender.
  5. Uncover, raise the heat to medium-high and reduce the sauce for 5 to 10 minutes, turning and basting, until it is thick, glossy and clings to the meat. This final reduction (shou zhi) is what makes the dish 'nong you chi jiang': rich in oil, dark in sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Using only light soy sauce, which won't give the deep mahogany color hong shao dishes are known for.
  • Rushing the final reduction, which leaves the sauce thin instead of glossy and clinging.

Authentic tips

  • Rock sugar melts more slowly than granulated sugar, giving the sauce a more subtle sweetness and better sheen.
  • This dish reheats beautifully and, like many braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks work with 10 extra minutes of simmering.
  • A splash of rice vinegar at the end brightens the sweet sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently with a splash of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; thaw overnight.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with the sauce
  • Soup: Blanched baby bok choy
  • Dessert: Sweet osmanthus jelly
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
