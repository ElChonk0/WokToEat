Bún Bò Huế (Hue Spicy Beef Noodle Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Bun bo Hue, a spicy, lemongrass-rich beef and pork noodle soup from the imperial city of Hue, is considered one of Vietnam's most flavorful noodle soups.

Method

  1. Blanch the bones, shank and hock in boiling water for 10 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Return them to a clean stockpot with the water, lemongrass and onion. Simmer gently, skimming, for 1.5 hours, then lift out the shank when it is tender and leave the hock and bones to simmer for 1 hour more.
  3. Let the dissolved shrimp paste settle for 10 minutes, then pour only the clear liquid into the broth, leaving the grit behind. Season with fish sauce, rock sugar and salt. Mam ruoc gives the funky depth that makes bun bo Hue distinct.
  4. Make the sate: heat the oil with the annatto seeds until red, strain, then fry the minced lemongrass, garlic, shallot and chili flakes in it for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant. Stir half into the broth.
  5. Cook the thick noodles according to the packet, often 10 to 15 minutes, and rinse. Slice the shank.
  6. Put noodles, shank and hock in each bowl, ladle over the boiling, red-flecked broth and serve with the remaining sate, banana blossom, sprouts, herbs and lime.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanch gives a muddy broth.
  • Adding the shrimp paste undissolved makes it gritty.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with lime, banana blossom and chili.
  • The red oil is the Hue signature.

Substitutions

  • Beef bones plus pork hock give the richest broth.
  • Store-bought bun bo Hue base paste saves time.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 4 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lime, banana blossom and herbs
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
