Yóu Yú Gēng (Taiwanese Squid Thick Soup) 魷魚羹

▾

Squid thick soup, a starchy broth with squid, bamboo shoots and mushrooms seasoned with black vinegar, is a Taiwanese street food.

  • 400 g14 ozSquid (or rehydrated dried squid), cleaned and cut into strips
  • 150 g5 ozFish paste (optional, to coat the squid)
  • 80 g3 ozBamboo shoots, shredded
  • 33Dried shiitake, soaked and shredded
  • 50 g2 ozCarrot, shredded
  • 100 g3.5 ozNapa cabbage, shredded
  • 1 L4 cupsStock
  • 10 g1/3 ozBonito flakes
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 30 g1/4 cupSweet potato starch, mixed with 60 ml water
  • 30 ml2 tbspBlack vinegar
  • to tasteto tasteBasil or cilantro, white pepper and fried shallots

Method

  1. If using fish paste, rub it around the squid strips so each is coated; this is the night-market style. Blanch the squid in boiling water for 1 minute and drain.
  2. Simmer the stock with the bonito flakes for 2 minutes and strain.
  3. Add the bamboo shoots, shiitake, carrot and cabbage and simmer for 5 minutes until the cabbage softens and sweetens the broth. Season with soy sauce, sugar and salt.
  4. Stir in the starch slurry until the soup is thick and glossy, then add the squid and simmer for 1 minute.
  5. Ladle into bowls and finish with black vinegar, basil, white pepper and fried shallots.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the squid makes it rubbery.
  • Adding the slurry too fast makes lumps.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with noodles or rice.
  • Black vinegar is essential.

Substitutions

  • Cuttlefish replaces squid.
  • Stock with a splash of fish sauce enriches the broth.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; thickens as it sits.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is a thick soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Winter melon tea
