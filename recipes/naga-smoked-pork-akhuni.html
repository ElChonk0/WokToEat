Naga Smoked Pork with Akhuni (Nagaland Smoked Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

Smoked pork cooked with akhuni, fermented soybean paste, is a staple of Naga cooking in India's northeast, where pork is the most important meat.

Method

  1. Cut the smoked pork into bite-size pieces and wash it in warm water to remove soot.
  2. Put the pork in a heavy pot with the water, bring to a boil and simmer, covered, for 45 minutes until it begins to soften.
  3. Pound the ginger, garlic and chilies coarsely. Add them with the akhuni and bamboo shoot and simmer for 20 to 30 minutes more. Akhuni is pungent and earthy; that smell is the point of the dish.
  4. Add the greens for the last 5 minutes, then simmer uncovered until the broth reduces to a thick gravy.
  5. Season with salt and serve with plain rice. Naga cooking uses almost no oil or spices beyond chilies and fermentation.

Common mistakes

  • Underboiled smoked pork stays chewy.
  • Overdoing the chilies: bhut jolokia is extremely hot.

Authentic tips

  • Ordinary bacon is a poor stand-in; use smoked pork belly.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Bacon replaces smoked pork belly.
  • Miso replaces akhuni.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
