Naga Smoked Pork with Akhuni (Nagaland Smoked Pork)

Smoked pork cooked with akhuni, fermented soybean paste, is a staple of Naga cooking in India's northeast, where pork is the most important meat.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 70 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 400 g14 ozSmoked pork belly
  • 45 g1.5 ozAkhuni or miso
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and garlic
  • 15 g1/2 ozBhut jolokia or chilies
  • 100 g3.5 ozBamboo shoots
  • 700 ml3 cupsWater
  • 5 g1 tspSalt

Method

  1. Cover the smoked pork with water and simmer 40 minutes until tender.
  2. Mash the akhuni with a little hot water.
  3. Add the akhuni, ginger, garlic and chilies to the pot.
  4. Add the rinsed bamboo shoots.
  5. Cook 30 minutes until the broth thickens; season with the salt.

Common mistakes

  • Underboiled smoked pork stays chewy.
  • Overdoing the chilies: bhut jolokia is extremely hot.

Authentic tips

  • Ordinary bacon is a poor stand-in; use smoked pork belly.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Bacon replaces smoked pork belly.
  • Miso replaces akhuni.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice
  • Soup: Boiled greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Black tea
