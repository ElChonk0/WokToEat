Spicy Beef and Mushroom Ramyeon (Shin Ramyun-Style)

▾

Surprise me with

This homestyle version recreates the beloved flavor profile of Korea's most famous instant noodle export — a fiery beef broth loaded with mushrooms and scallion — built from scratch rather than a packet, for a fresher, more customizable bowl.

Method

  1. Warm the sesame oil in a pot over low heat, add the gochugaru and garlic and stir for 20 to 30 seconds, until the oil turns red and smells toasty; take it off the heat before the chili darkens. This chili oil gives the broth its depth and color.
  2. Add the beef and stir for 30 seconds, then pour in the stock, gochujang and soy sauce and bring to a boil.
  3. Add the mushrooms, zucchini and onion and simmer for 3 minutes so they flavor the broth.
  4. Add the noodles and cook for 1 minute less than the packet suggests, lifting them with chopsticks a few times so they get air and stay springy.
  5. Crack in the eggs and let them poach for 1 minute without stirring, or stir them in for a cloudier broth. Add the green onion and serve straight away, with kimchi.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles, which turns them mushy — they continue softening slightly even after removing from heat.
  • Adding the egg too early, which overcooks it before the noodles are ready.

Authentic tips

  • A good beef stock base is what gives this bowl its rich depth — a quality store-bought stock works well if homemade isn't on hand.
  • Adjust the gochugaru up or down depending on how spicy you like it; this ratio gives a solid medium-hot heat.

Substitutions

  • Sliced rice cakes (tteok) are a popular addition for extra chew.
  • Vegetable or chicken stock can replace beef stock for a lighter bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; noodles continue absorbing broth and soften if stored.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle and broth texture don't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi and a fried egg
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
