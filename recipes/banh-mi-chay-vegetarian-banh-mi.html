Bánh Mì Chay (Vegetarian Banh Mi)

▾

Surprise me with

Banh mi, the Vietnamese baguette sandwich, came from French colonial bakeries; the chay version fills it with fried tofu, pickles and herbs, common near Buddhist temples.

  • 10 ml2 tspMaggi or soy sauce, for drizzling

Method

  1. Press the tofu for 15 minutes. Mix the lemongrass, garlic, soy sauce and sugar and marinate the tofu for 20 minutes.
  2. Fry the tofu in the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-high heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side until golden and crisp, brushing with the remaining marinade at the end so it caramelizes.
  3. Warm the baguettes in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 3 to 5 minutes until the crust crackles.
  4. Split the bread, spread with vegan mayonnaise and drizzle with Maggi.
  5. Fill with the tofu, pickles, cucumber, cilantro and chilies and eat right away.

Common mistakes

  • Soggy bread: toast it.
  • Wet tofu makes a soggy sandwich.

Authentic tips

  • Pickled carrot and daikon are essential.
  • Use a crusty baguette.

Substitutions

  • Baked tofu replaces fried.
  • Mushroom pate replaces mayonnaise.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the pickles keep a week.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled carrot and daikon
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Vietnamese iced coffee
