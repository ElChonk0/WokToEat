Kè Jiā Yóu Jī (Hakka Salted Soaked Chicken) 客家油鸡

Hakka salted chicken is poached, then steeped in a cold, lightly salted and sweetened wine liquid so the meat stays tender and picks up seasoning; it is served with a sharp kumquat sauce to cut the richness.

Method

  1. Bring the water, sliced ginger, green onion and 30 ml of the wine to a gentle simmer in a wide pot, with only small bubbles breaking the surface.
  2. Add the chicken thighs, return to a bare simmer, cover and poach for 20 minutes, until the juices run clear and the thickest piece reads 74 C (165 F).
  3. Strain 500 ml of the poaching liquid into a bowl, stir in the salt, sugar and remaining wine until dissolved, and cool it completely in the refrigerator.
  4. Lift the thighs into a bowl of ice water for 2 minutes to set the skin, then submerge them in the cooled salted liquid and refrigerate for 1 hour.
  5. Crush the chopped kumquats with the soy sauce, sugar and minced ginger into a loose, tart sauce.
  6. Lift the chicken out, chop through the bone into bite-size pieces and serve with the kumquat sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Poaching at a rolling boil toughens the thighs and clouds the liquid; keep it at a bare simmer.
  • Soaking in warm liquid makes the skin soggy; chill the salted liquid first.

Authentic tips

  • Soaking for 1 to 4 hours deepens the seasoning, but beyond that the meat turns salty.
  • Keep the kumquat peel in the sauce, because it carries most of the aroma.

Substitutions

  • Calamansi or a mix of lime zest and juice replaces kumquats.
  • A whole small chicken works if you add 15 minutes to the poach.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator in its soaking liquid; serve cold.

Freezing: Not recommended, because the skin turns rubbery after thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched choy sum
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth from the poaching liquid
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot chrysanthemum or jasmine tea
