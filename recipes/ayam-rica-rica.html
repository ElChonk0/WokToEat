Ayam Rica-Rica

▾

Surprise me with

Hailing from Manado in North Sulawesi, rica-rica is known for its fiery, tangy sambal-like sauce built on chili, shallot, ginger, and lime leaf — among the spiciest regional Indonesian chicken preparations.

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with the lime juice and a pinch of salt and leave for 10 minutes; Manado cooks do this to freshen the meat.
  2. Coarsely grind the chilies, shallots, garlic and ginger. Rica-rica means 'chili-chili', so expect real heat.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the spice paste (bumbu) with the lemongrass, lime leaves and pandan, stirring often, for 6 to 8 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried bumbu tastes sharp and grassy; this step is the base of Indonesian cooking.
  4. Add the chicken and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until it changes color, then add the tomato, water, salt and sugar. Simmer for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked and the sauce is thick.
  5. Turn off the heat and stir in the basil and green onion. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the paste-frying step, which leaves the sauce tasting raw and sharp instead of rounded.
  • Using too little chili out of caution — rica-rica is meant to be assertively spicy; adjust to taste but don't underdo it entirely.

Authentic tips

  • A mix of dried and fresh red chilies, if you have both, gives a more complex heat than fresh alone.
  • This dish is traditionally quite fiery — balance it with plenty of plain rice on the side.

Substitutions

  • A mix of bird's eye and mild red chilies adjusts the heat.
  • Thai basil replaces the Manado basil (kemangi).

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the heat mellows.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
