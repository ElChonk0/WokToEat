Kalguksu (Knife-Cut Noodle Soup) 칼국수

Kalguksu ("knife noodles") refers to the hand-cut, knife-trimmed noodles at the heart of this comforting soup, traditionally simmered in a chicken or anchovy broth and considered a humble, homestyle dish across Korea.

Method

  1. Combine chicken, garlic, and onion in a pot with water; bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook 25 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  2. Remove chicken, shred the meat once cool enough to handle, and discard bones; strain the broth and return it to the pot.
  3. Bring the broth back to a simmer and add zucchini and potato, cooking 5 minutes.
  4. Add noodles and cook according to package directions, usually 3–5 minutes, until tender.
  5. Stir in soy sauce and sesame oil, then return shredded chicken to the pot.
  6. Garnish with green onion before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles, which turns them mushy and clouds the broth with excess starch.
  • Skipping the strain step, which leaves the broth cloudier and less clean-tasting.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh kalguksu noodles are sold at Korean grocers and cook much faster than dried — adjust cooking time if substituting.
  • This soup is intentionally simple and homestyle — resist the urge to over-season the broth.

Substitutions

  • Anchovy and kelp stock is a traditional alternative broth base if you'd like a lighter, seafood-forward version.
  • No fresh noodles: dried wide wheat noodles work, though texture differs slightly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; noodles continue absorbing broth and soften.

Freezing: The broth and chicken freeze well without noodles; cook fresh noodles when serving.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
