Wuxi Pai Gu (Wuxi Braised Spare Ribs) 无锡排骨

Wuxi ribs, spare ribs braised in a sweet soy and spice sauce until the meat falls off the bone, are a specialty of Wuxi, known for its sweet Jiangnan cooking.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 90 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 900 g2 lbPork spare ribs, cut
  • 60 ml4 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 45 g3 tbspRock sugar
  • 60 ml4 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 8 g3 tspStar anise, cinnamon, bay
  • 60 g4 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 800 ml3.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Cut the ribs into 4 cm pieces, cover with cold water, boil 5 minutes and drain; rinse off the scum.
  2. Melt the rock sugar in a dry pot over low heat until amber, add the ribs and turn to coat, 2 minutes.
  3. Add the ginger, scallion, wine, both soy sauces, star anise, cinnamon and bay, then pour in the 800 ml water.
  4. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer on low for 60 minutes until the meat is tender.
  5. Uncover and boil over high heat for 8-10 minutes, stirring, until the sauce is thick and the ribs are glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanching leaves a scummy, cloudy sauce.
  • Burning the sugar makes the whole pot bitter; take it off the heat when it turns amber.

Authentic tips

  • Wuxi cooking is known for sweetness, so the sauce should be sticky and a little sweet.
  • A pressure cooker does the braise in 25 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Baby back ribs shorten the braise by 20 minutes.
  • More sugar and less soy sauce gives the sweeter Wuxi style.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
