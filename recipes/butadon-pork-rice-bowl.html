Butadon (Pork Rice Bowl) 豚丼

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty rice bowl especially popular in Hokkaido, butadon tops steamed rice with sweet-savory glazed pork slices, offering a faster, everyday alternative to the more involved kakuni or tonkatsu.

Method

  1. Whisk soy sauce, mirin, sake, sugar, and ginger for the glaze.
  2. Sear pork slices in a hot pan with a little oil, 2–3 minutes per side.
  3. Pour in the glaze and reduce over medium heat, turning the pork to coat, 3–4 minutes until thick and glossy.
  4. Divide rice among bowls and top with the glazed pork.
  5. Garnish with green onion and pickled ginger before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams rather than sears the pork, missing out on browning.
  • Letting the glaze reduce too far, which can turn it overly sticky and hard to serve neatly.

Authentic tips

  • This dish comes together very quickly — have the rice ready before you start cooking the pork.
  • A little extra grated ginger stirred in at the end brightens the finished glaze.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same method for a different but equally satisfying bowl.
  • No mirin: a mix of sake and a touch more sugar approximates it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently to avoid drying out.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled ginger

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
