Butadon (Pork Rice Bowl) 豚丼

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty rice bowl especially popular in Hokkaido, butadon tops steamed rice with sweet-savory glazed pork slices, offering a faster, everyday alternative to the more involved kakuni or tonkatsu.

Method

  1. Make the sauce, Obihiro style: heat the sugar and water in a small, heavy pan over medium heat, stirring until dissolved, then stop stirring and let it caramelize to a deep amber, swirling the pan. Take it off the heat and carefully add the boiling water; it will spit, so stand back. Stir in the soy sauce, sake, mirin and ginger and simmer for 1 minute.
  2. Snip the fat-to-meat seams of the pork so the slices lie flat.
  3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and sear the pork in batches for 1 minute per side until browned at the edges but not cooked through.
  4. Return all the pork, pour in most of the sauce and toss for 1 minute until glossy and just cooked.
  5. Drizzle the remaining sauce over bowls of hot rice, lay the pork on top and garnish with shredded negi, black pepper and pickled ginger.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams rather than sears the pork, missing out on browning.
  • Letting the glaze reduce too far, which can turn it overly sticky and hard to serve neatly.

Authentic tips

  • This dish comes together very quickly — have the rice ready before you start cooking the pork.
  • A little extra grated ginger stirred in at the end brightens the finished glaze.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same method for a different but equally satisfying bowl.
  • No mirin: a mix of sake and a touch more sugar approximates it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently to avoid drying out.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled ginger
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
