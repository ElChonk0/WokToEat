Kuay Teow Rua (Thai Boat Noodles) ก๋วยเตี๋ยวเรือ

▾

Surprise me with

Boat noodles, a dark spiced beef broth served in small bowls, were once sold from boats on Bangkok's canals and are now a street staple near Victory Monument.

Method

  1. Blanch the bones and brisket for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Toast the whole spices in a dry Dutch oven for 1 minute, then add the bones, brisket, water, galangal, lemongrass and pounded cilantro root paste. Simmer gently, skimming, for 2.5 to 3 hours.
  3. Season the broth with the soy sauces, fermented bean curd and palm sugar. It should be dark, rich, spiced and slightly sweet. (Boat noodle stalls traditionally finish each bowl with a spoon of fresh beef or pork blood to thicken and darken it; it is left out here.) Slice the brisket.
  4. Cook the meatballs in the broth for 5 minutes. Blanch a portion of noodles with the vegetables in boiling water for 10 to 20 seconds and drop into a small bowl.
  5. Lay raw sliced beef over the noodles and ladle the boiling broth over it, which cooks it pink in seconds. Add brisket and meatballs.
  6. Top with fried garlic, basil and pork rinds. Boat noodles are served in small bowls, so people eat several.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough.
  • Too dilute a broth lacks the characteristic depth.

Authentic tips

  • Authentic bowls are tiny; people eat several.
  • Add a spoonful of pork-blood or sugar and vinegar.

Substitutions

  • Pork blood is traditional in the broth; omit for a milder version.
  • Ready-made boat noodle seasoning saves time.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 4 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Crispy pork rinds
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
