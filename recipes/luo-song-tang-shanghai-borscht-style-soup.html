Luó Sòng Tāng (Shanghai Russian Soup) 罗宋汤

▾

Luo song tang, a tomato and cabbage beef soup adapted by Russian emigres in 1920s Shanghai, became a Shanghai-style Western restaurant staple.

Method

  1. Blanch the brisket for 5 minutes, rinse, then simmer it in the water for 1.25 hours until tender. Keep the broth.
  2. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 minutes until soft, then the tomatoes for 5 minutes until they collapse.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and flour and cook for 2 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens. This red roux gives Shanghai borscht its thick, tangy body.
  4. Scrape the tomato mixture into the broth with the potatoes, carrots and celery and simmer for 20 minutes.
  5. Add the cabbage and simmer for 10 minutes more, until everything is soft and the soup is thick. Season with sugar, salt and pepper; it should be sweet-sour. Russian émigrés brought borscht to Shanghai, where it lost its beets.

Common mistakes

  • Under-cooking the beef leaves it chewy; the vegetables go in only once it is nearly tender.
  • Too thin a broth lacks body; brown the tomatoes first.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with a slice of bread, as in Shanghai Western-style restaurants.
  • A spoonful of vinegar at the end brightens the soup.

Substitutions

  • Beef short rib adds more flavor than brisket.
  • Beets are not traditional but add color if you like.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Crusty bread or rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot black tea
