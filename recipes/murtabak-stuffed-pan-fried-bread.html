Murtabak (Malaysian Stuffed Pan-Fried Bread)

▾

Surprise me with

Murtabak, a thin pan-fried bread stuffed with spiced minced meat, egg and onion, is a Malaysian and Singaporean Indian-Muslim street food from Arabia and India.

Method

  1. Knead the flour, water, salt and 1 tablespoon of ghee into a very soft, smooth dough. Divide it into 4 balls, coat them generously with ghee and rest them, covered, for at least 2 hours, or overnight in the refrigerator. The long rest lets the dough stretch paper-thin without tearing.
  2. Stir-fry the onion, leek and garlic in a little ghee for 3 minutes, add the beef and cook for 3 minutes, then the curry powder, salt and sugar, until the mixture is very dry. Cool it, then mix in the beaten eggs.
  3. On an oiled surface, press and stretch a ball of dough into a very thin square about 30 cm (12 in) across, pulling gently from the edges.
  4. Spread a quarter of the filling in the center and fold the edges over it like an envelope into a square parcel.
  5. Heat a little ghee on a flat griddle or large skillet over medium heat and fry the murtabak seam-side down for 3 to 4 minutes until golden and crisp, then flip and cook 3 to 4 minutes more until the egg filling is set. Keep the heat moderate so the outside does not burn before the filling cooks.
  6. Cut into squares and serve hot with pickled onion and curry gravy.

Common mistakes

  • A torn sheet leaks the filling; stretch gently.
  • High heat burns the outside before the egg sets.

Authentic tips

  • Spring roll skins are a shortcut for roti dough.
  • Serve with dhal or curry sauce.

Substitutions

  • Ground lamb or chicken replace beef.
  • Spring roll wrappers replace roti dough.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Freeze cooked murtabak for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Curry sauce and pickled onions
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Cendol
