Gyeran-mari (Korean Rolled Omelet) 계란말이

Gyeran-mari, a rolled omelet with finely chopped vegetables, is one of the most common Korean banchan and lunchbox dishes.

Method

  1. Beat the eggs thoroughly until no strands of white remain, which gives an even yellow roll. Stir in the salt, pepper and vegetables, chopped very finely so they do not tear the egg as you roll.
  2. Wipe a 23 to 25 cm (9 to 10 in) nonstick skillet, or a rectangular omelet pan, with oil and preheat it over medium-low heat. The pan is ready when you feel the warmth a few centimeters above it.
  3. Pour in about a third of the egg, tilting to coat the pan. When the bottom is set but the top is still wet, lift one edge and fold it over about 4 cm (1.5 in), then keep rolling it toward the other side. Lower the heat if the bottom browns.
  4. Push the roll to one end, oil the empty part of the pan and pour in half of the remaining egg, lifting the roll slightly so the new egg runs underneath and joins it. When it is nearly set, roll the log back across it. Repeat with the last of the egg.
  5. Press the roll gently into a neat log with the spatula, cook it for 30 seconds on each side, then rest it for 5 minutes, ideally wrapped in a bamboo mat. Slice it 2 cm (3/4 in) thick and serve warm or at room temperature.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too high a heat browns and toughens the omelet.
  • Rolling while the egg is still runny makes it split.

Authentic tips

  • A rectangular tamagoyaki pan makes neat rolls, but any non-stick skillet works.
  • Wrap the hot roll in a bamboo mat for a firm shape.

Substitutions

  • Add a sheet of seaweed or cheese between layers.
  • Chopped ham or crab sticks for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; good cold in lunchboxes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and kimchi
  • Soup: Miyeokguk (seaweed soup)
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
