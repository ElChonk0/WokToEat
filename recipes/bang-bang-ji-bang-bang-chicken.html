Bàng Bàng Jī (Bang Bang Chicken) 棒棒鸡

Bang bang ji, poached chicken shredded and dressed with a sesame and chili sauce, takes its name from the stick used to tenderize the meat and is a classic Sichuan cold dish.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 700 g1.5 lbChicken thighs, poached
  • 45 g3 tbspSesame paste
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 3 g1 tspSichuan pepper powder
  • 200 g7 ozCucumber, julienned
  • 30 g2 tbspScallion
  • 20 g1 tbspGinger, sliced

Method

  1. Bring a pot of water to a boil with the ginger, add the chicken thighs, cover and simmer very gently for 18-20 minutes, until the juices run clear (75 C / 165 F).
  2. Lift the chicken into a bowl of ice water for 10 minutes to firm the meat and set the skin; drain well.
  3. Bang the chicken lightly with a rolling pin, which loosens the fibers, then tear the meat into thick shreds along the grain.
  4. Whisk the sesame paste with the soy sauce, vinegar, chili oil and Sichuan pepper, thinning with 2-3 tablespoons of the poaching liquid until pourable.
  5. Pile the cucumber on a plate, top with the chicken, spoon over the sauce and scatter with scallion.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the chicken hard makes it dry and stringy; keep the water at a bare simmer.
  • Dressing the cucumber early makes the plate watery, so sauce just before serving.

Authentic tips

  • The ice bath is what gives the meat its springy, juicy texture.
  • Thin the sesame paste gradually; it seizes if you add all the liquid at once.

Substitutions

  • Smooth peanut butter thinned with a little warm water stands in for sesame paste.
  • Chicken breast works if poached gently and cooled in its liquid.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; bring to room temperature and re-dress before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended; the poached chicken turns watery on thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cold noodles or steamed rice
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh watermelon
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
