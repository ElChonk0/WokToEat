Sardines with Garlic & Black Bean Sauce

▾

Surprise me with

Steaming or braising small, bony fish with fermented black beans is a frugal, flavor-forward Cantonese technique traditionally used to make inexpensive fish taste rich and savory — sardines and dace are common choices in home cooking.

Method

  1. Scale and gut the sardines, rinse them and pat them completely dry inside and out. Cut two shallow slashes on each side, season lightly with salt and dust with cornstarch, shaking off the excess. A dry, dusted skin is what stops oily fish sticking and tearing.
  2. Heat a heavy skillet or flat-bottom wok over high heat, add 2 tablespoons of oil and lower to medium. Lay in the fish and fry without moving for 2 to 3 minutes until the skin is golden and releases on its own, then turn carefully and fry 2 minutes more. Lift them onto a plate.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the garlic, ginger, chili and black beans over medium heat for 30 seconds, until fragrant; do not let the garlic brown.
  4. Splash in the wine, then add the soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and water and bring to a simmer. Return the fish and spoon the sauce over them, then simmer for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce reduces to a glaze and the fish is cooked through to the bone.
  5. Scatter with green onion and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking small, delicate fish like sardines, which dries them out quickly — check at the 8-minute mark.
  • Using too much black bean paste, which can overwhelm the dish with saltiness; rinsing the beans first removes excess salt.

Authentic tips

  • This technique works beautifully with any small, oily fish — a flavorful, budget-friendly way to get fish onto the table twice a week without repeating the same recipe.
  • Fermented black beans keep for months in the fridge once opened, so this becomes an easy weeknight option once you have a jar on hand.

Substitutions

  • Mackerel fillets or smelt work well in place of whole sardines.
  • No fermented black beans — a tablespoon of doubanjiang gives a different but still flavorful result.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten fresh, as with most steamed fish; flake any extra into fried rice.

Freezing: Freeze raw, uncooked sardines if needed; the cooked dish doesn't freeze well.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
