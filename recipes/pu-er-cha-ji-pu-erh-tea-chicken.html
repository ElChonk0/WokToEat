Pǔ'ěr Chá Jī (Pu'er Tea Braised Chicken) 普洱茶鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Yunnan's Pu'er tea region gives its name to the earthy fermented tea used to braise chicken in local home cooking.

  • Cook 40 min

Method

  1. Rinse the Pu'er leaves with a splash of boiling water and discard it, then steep them in the boiling water for 5 minutes. Strain and keep the tea. Pu'er, the fermented tea named after a Yunnan town, gives an earthy depth.
  2. Heat the oil and rock sugar in a Dutch oven until it caramelizes, then turn the chicken in it until glossy.
  3. Add the ginger, green onion, star anise, Shaoxing wine and soy sauces.
  4. Pour in the tea, cover and simmer for 25 to 30 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  5. Uncover and reduce until the sauce is thick and glossy. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Over-steeped tea turns the sauce bitter; strain after 5 minutes.
  • Letting the sauce burn after reducing ruins the dish.

Authentic tips

  • Aged ripe (shu) Pu'er gives a mellow, earthy depth.
  • Skim the fat before reducing for a cleaner sauce.

Substitutions

  • Strong black tea replaces Pu'er.
  • Boneless thighs shorten the braise by 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
