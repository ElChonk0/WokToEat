Gān Guō Niú Ròu (Hunan Dry-Pot Beef) 干锅牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Dry pot, a Hunan and Sichuan style, serves stir-fried ingredients in a hot pot without much sauce; beef is a favorite.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the lotus root and onion for 3 minutes until lightly browned. Lift out and spread them in a small pot or skillet that will go to the table.
  3. Add 2 more tablespoons of oil and sear the beef for 1 to 2 minutes until browned. Lift it out.
  4. Add the remaining oil and fry the doubanjiang over medium heat for 1 minute, then the chilies, peppercorns, garlic and ginger for 30 seconds.
  5. Return the beef with the celery, soy sauce and sugar and toss over high heat for 1 minute. Pile it over the vegetables, scatter with cilantro and sesame, and keep it sizzling over a burner at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it chewy; it is cooked once at the start and returns only briefly.
  • Burning the dried chilies turns the dish bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Serve in a pre-warmed cast-iron skillet or heavy pot to keep it hot.
  • Blanch the lotus root first if you like it tender.

Substitutions

  • Lamb takes the same spices.
  • Firm tofu and mushrooms make a vegetarian dry pot.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
