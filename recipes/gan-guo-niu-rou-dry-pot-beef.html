Gān Guō Niú Ròu (Hunan Dry-Pot Beef) 干锅牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Dry pot, a Hunan and Sichuan style, serves stir-fried ingredients in a hot pot without much sauce; beef is a favorite.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbBeef, sliced
  • 15 g1/2 ozDried chilies
  • 5 g2 tspSichuan peppercorns
  • 300 g10 ozOnion, celery, lotus root
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and ginger
  • 30 g2 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the beef thin and toss with a splash of soy sauce and cornstarch if you have it. Cut the lotus root into 5 mm slices, the celery on the diagonal and the onion into wedges.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat and sear the beef 1-2 minutes until browned; lift out.
  3. Fry the dried chilies and peppercorns for 20 seconds, then the doubanjiang, garlic and ginger for 1 minute until the oil turns red.
  4. Add the lotus root and stir-fry 3 minutes, then the celery and onion for 2 minutes.
  5. Return the beef, toss 30 seconds and serve in a hot pot.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef makes it chewy; it is cooked once at the start and returns only briefly.
  • Burning the dried chilies turns the dish bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Serve in a pre-warmed cast-iron skillet or heavy pot to keep it hot.
  • Blanch the lotus root first if you like it tender.

Substitutions

  • Lamb takes the same spices.
  • Firm tofu and mushrooms make a vegetarian dry pot.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
