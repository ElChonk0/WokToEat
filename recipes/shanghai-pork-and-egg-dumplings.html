Shanghai Pork and Egg Dumplings 蛋饺

▾

Surprise me with

A Chinese New Year staple in Jiangnan households, egg dumplings wrap a savory pork filling inside thin egg "skins" instead of dough, traditionally cooked and served in a hot pot or simmered in broth.

Method

  1. Mix the pork with the green onion, ginger, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar and sesame oil until sticky.
  2. Heat a ladle or small skillet over low heat and brush it with oil. Pour in 2 tablespoons of egg and swirl into a thin round about 8 cm (3 in) across.
  3. While the top is still runny, put a teaspoon of filling on one half, fold the egg over and press the edge to seal; the wet egg acts as glue. Lift it out and repeat. These egg dumplings (dan jiao) are a Lunar New Year dish.
  4. Line a pot with the cabbage and glass noodles, arrange the egg dumplings on top and add the stock, soy sauce and sugar.
  5. Simmer, covered, for 10 minutes, until the filling is cooked through and the cabbage is soft. Serve in the broth.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the egg skin too long before folding, which makes it too firm to seal properly around the filling.
  • Overfilling the dumplings, which makes them prone to bursting during simmering.

Authentic tips

  • A small ladle works better than a full pan for forming individual egg skins of the right size.
  • These dumplings are traditionally part of a hot pot spread — feel free to add other hot pot ingredients to the broth.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken or shrimp can replace or supplement pork in the filling.
  • No dedicated ladle: a small non-stick pan works fine for forming the egg skins.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated in the broth.

Freezing: Freezes well, uncooked, for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black vinegar and ginger dip
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
