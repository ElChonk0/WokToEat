Green Curry Chicken

▾

Surprise me with

Green curry gets its color and herbal brightness from fresh green chilies, Thai basil, and kaffir lime leaf blended into the paste — among the spicier and more aromatic of Thailand's curry family compared to the milder red and yellow versions.

Method

  1. Spoon the thick cream from the top of the coconut milk into a pot (if separated) and fry over medium heat with the curry paste until fragrant and the oil starts to separate, about 2 minutes.
  2. Add the chicken and stir to coat in the paste; cook 3–4 minutes.
  3. Pour in the remaining coconut milk and bring to a gentle simmer — don't boil hard, which can cause the coconut milk to curdle.
  4. Add eggplant, bamboo shoots, and kaffir lime leaves; simmer 10 minutes until the chicken and vegetables are cooked through.
  5. Season with fish sauce and palm sugar, tasting and adjusting the balance of salty, sweet, and spicy.
  6. Stir in Thai basil leaves just before serving; garnish with sliced red chili.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the curry hard, which can split the coconut milk into an oily, less appealing texture — keep it at a gentle simmer.
  • Adding the basil too early, which wilts it into a dull green and loses its bright aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Frying the curry paste in the coconut cream until the oil separates ("cracking" the curry) is the single biggest flavor difference between good and average curry.
  • Store-bought green curry paste varies widely in heat — start with less than the package suggests and add more after tasting.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or firm tofu can replace chicken with little change to the method.
  • No kaffir lime leaves — a strip of lime zest is a partial substitute, though less aromatically complex.

Storage

Leftovers: Flavor deepens overnight; reheat gently without boiling.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months; the eggplant softens further but remains pleasant.
