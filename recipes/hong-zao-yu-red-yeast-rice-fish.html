Hóng Zāo Yú (Red Yeast Rice Fish) 红糟鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Red yeast rice lees (hong zao) are the defining flavor of Fuzhou cuisine; fish marinated and fried with them turns red-brown and wine-scented.

  • Prep 15 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbFish steaks (mackerel, cod or eel)
  • 60 g1/4 cupRed wine lees (hong zao)
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing or Fujian red rice wine
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic, minced
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, minced
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt
  • 30 g1/4 cupSweet potato starch or cornstarch
  • 120 ml1/2 cupNeutral oil, for shallow-frying

Method

  1. Mix the red wine lees with the wine, garlic, ginger, sugar, five-spice and salt and coat the fish. Marinate for at least 2 hours. The lees give a deep red color and sweet, winey flavor typical of Fuzhou.
  2. Dust the fish lightly in starch, keeping some of the marinade on.
  3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat and shallow-fry the fish for 3 to 4 minutes per side until cooked and dark red-brown. The sugar burns easily, so keep the heat moderate.
  4. Drain on a rack.
  5. Serve hot, or at room temperature as a cold dish.

Common mistakes

  • Excess lees burn and taste bitter; wipe it off before frying.
  • Turning too early tears the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Mackerel's oiliness pairs well with the winey lees.
  • Serve with steamed rice and greens.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus a pinch of paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • Chicken pieces take the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
