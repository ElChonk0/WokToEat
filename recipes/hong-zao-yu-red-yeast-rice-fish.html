Hóng Zāo Yú (Red Yeast Rice Fish) 红糟鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Red yeast rice lees (hong zao) are the defining flavor of Fuzhou cuisine; fish marinated and fried with them turns red-brown and wine-scented.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbFish steaks (mackerel or cod)
  • 60 g4 tbspRed yeast rice lees (hong zao)
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 30 g2 tbspCornstarch
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Mix the lees with the wine, sugar and garlic and coat the fish; marinate 2 hours in the fridge.
  2. Wipe off excess lees and pat the fish dry; dust with the cornstarch.
  3. Heat the oil in a wide nonstick pan over medium heat and fry the steaks 3-4 minutes per side until crisp and cooked through.
  4. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Excess lees burn and taste bitter; wipe it off before frying.
  • Turning too early tears the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Mackerel's oiliness pairs well with the winey lees.
  • Serve with steamed rice and greens.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus a pinch of paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • Chicken pieces take the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
