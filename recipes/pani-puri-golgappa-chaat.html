Pani Puri (Golgappa Chaat)

▾

Surprise me with

Pani puri, also called golgappa, is an Indian street snack of crisp hollow shells filled with spiced potato and chickpeas and topped with a tangy, spiced water.

Method

  1. Make the pani: blend the mint, cilantro, green chilies, roasted cumin, black salt, chaat masala, half the tamarind, the lemon juice and a little of the water to a smooth paste, then stir in the rest of the cold water. Chill for at least 1 hour; ice-cold pani is essential.
  2. Mix the potatoes and chickpeas with the onion, a pinch of black salt, cumin and chaat masala for the filling.
  3. Mix the remaining tamarind with the sugar and a splash of water to make the sweet chutney, or use ready-made sweet tamarind chutney.
  4. Just before eating, crack a small hole in the top of each puri with your thumb, fill it with a spoonful of potato-chickpea filling and a few boondi, and add a little sweet chutney.
  5. Dip each puri into the chilled pani so it fills, and eat it in one bite before it goes soggy. A street snack across India, called golgappa in the north and puchka in Bengal.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Filling the puris ahead makes them soggy; fill and eat immediately.
  • Warm pani loses its sharp, fresh taste; keep it ice cold.

Authentic tips

  • Make the pani a day ahead because its flavor deepens.
  • Adjust the heat with the number of green chilies.

Substitutions

  • Sprouted mung beans replace chickpeas.
  • Dates replace sugar in the pani for a deeper sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: The pani keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; the filling keeps 2 days.

Freezing: The pani freezes for 1 month; do not freeze the puris.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sev puri or bhel puri
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Kulfi
  • Beverage: Masala chai
