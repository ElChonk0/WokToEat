Sisig (Sizzling Chopped Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

Originating in Pampanga, sisig transforms pig face and other cuts into a sizzling, chopped dish seasoned with calamansi and chili, traditionally served on a hot plate with a raw egg cracked on top.

Method

  1. Boil pork in water until tender, about 25–30 minutes; drain and cool.
  2. Chop the cooled pork finely by hand.
  3. Sear the chopped pork in a very hot pan without oil until crisp and browned at the edges, 6–8 minutes.
  4. Stir in onion, calamansi juice, soy sauce, and green chili; cook 2–3 minutes more.
  5. Stir in mayonnaise off heat for creaminess (optional but common), and transfer to a sizzling plate if available.
  6. Top with a raw egg, cracked directly on top while the dish is still very hot, and garnish with green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Chopping the pork too coarsely, which loses sisig's signature fine, crispy-chewy texture.
  • Skipping the browning step, which is what gives sisig its characteristic crispy, charred edges.

Authentic tips

  • A genuinely hot pan or sizzling plate is key — the dish should still be audibly sizzling when the egg is cracked over it.
  • Calamansi's distinct tart-sweet flavor is central to sisig; regular lime is a reasonable but not identical substitute.

Substitutions

  • Pork liver is a traditional addition for those who enjoy it, adding extra richness.
  • Chicken can be used for a lighter version, though pork is far more traditional.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan to re-crisp.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the crispy texture softens.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and a raw egg on top
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
