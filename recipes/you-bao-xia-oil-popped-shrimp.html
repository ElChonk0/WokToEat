Yóu Bào Xiā (Shanghai Oil-Popped Shrimp) 油爆虾

▾

Surprise me with

You bao xia, shrimp fried in hot oil until the shells crisp, then glazed with a sweet-savory sauce, is a Shanghai cold appetizer and home classic.

  • 600 g1.3 lbSmall shell-on freshwater shrimp, legs and feelers trimmed
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 15 g1/2 ozGinger, minced
  • 15 g1/2 ozGreen onion, minced
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 ml1 tspDark soy sauce
  • 25 g2 tbspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater

Method

  1. Trim the shrimp legs and feelers with scissors, rinse and dry thoroughly; any water makes the oil spit.
  2. Heat the oil to 200 C (390 F) in a flat-bottom wok and fry the shrimp for 30 to 45 seconds, until they turn red and the shells separate from the meat. Lift them out.
  3. Reheat the oil to 200 C and fry them again for 15 to 20 seconds, until the shells are crisp enough to eat. This 'oil-popping' is what the name means.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Fry the ginger and green onion for 10 seconds, then add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces, sugar, vinegar and water and bubble until syrupy.
  5. Toss in the shrimp over high heat for 15 seconds until coated in the sweet glaze. Serve hot or at room temperature; the shells are eaten.

Common mistakes

  • Wet shrimp spatter dangerously and do not crisp.
  • Oil that is not hot enough steams the shrimp rather than popping the shell.

Authentic tips

  • Eat shell and all; freshwater shrimp shells are thin.
  • Serve warm or at room temperature as an appetizer.

Substitutions

  • Large shrimp replace the river shrimp; cut cooking time slightly.
  • Add a little ginger vinegar dip on the side.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; eat while still crisp.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
