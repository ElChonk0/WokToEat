Doenjang Dakgui (Soybean Paste Grilled Chicken) 된장 닭구이

▾

Surprise me with

Marinating chicken in doenjang, the Korean fermented soybean paste, before grilling is a homestyle alternative to soy or chili marinades.

  • Prep 15 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 25 min

Method

  1. Pat the thighs dry and cut each one in half crosswise if large, so they cook evenly. Score the thick parts of the meat side lightly so the marinade gets in.
  2. Mix the doenjang, syrup, mirin, soy sauce, grated onion, garlic, ginger, sesame oil and pepper into a smooth paste. Coat the chicken and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or overnight. Doenjang is salty and savory, so the marinade needs no extra salt.
  3. Heat a grill pan or heavy skillet over medium heat, or a grill to medium, and oil it lightly. Lay the chicken in skin-side down, scraping off the thickest clumps of paste, which burn before the meat is cooked.
  4. Cook for 6 to 8 minutes, until the skin is browned and crisp, then turn and cook 5 to 6 minutes more, brushing with the scraped-off marinade. The chicken is done when it reads 74 C (165 F) and the paste is caramelized in spots. Lower the heat if the paste blackens.
  5. Rest for 3 minutes, slice and scatter with green onion. Serve with rice and lettuce for wrapping.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • High heat burns the doenjang and syrup before the chicken cooks.
  • Moving the chicken too soon tears the skin.

Authentic tips

  • Oven or broiler: roast at 220 C (425 F) for 20 to 25 minutes on a rack.
  • Wrap in lettuce with rice and ssamjang.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder slices work with the same marinade.
  • Miso replaces doenjang for a milder flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes raw in the marinade for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lettuce wraps and rice
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
