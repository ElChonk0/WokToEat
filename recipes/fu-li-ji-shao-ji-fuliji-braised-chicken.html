Fúlíjí Shāo Jī (Fuliji Roast Chicken) 符离集烧鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Fuliji roast chicken, from a town in northern Anhui, is a famous deli chicken fried and then simmered in spices until tender; it is eaten cold and is considered one of the best-known chicken dishes in China.

Method

  1. Tuck the legs into a slit in the belly and the wing tips through the beak, a fold that keeps the bird compact.
  2. Brush the chicken all over with honey dissolved in a little hot water and let it dry for 30 minutes.
  3. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) in a large Dutch oven and fry the chicken, turning, for 3 to 4 minutes until deep golden red. Lift it out carefully.
  4. Bring the water, soy sauces, rock sugar, salt, spices, ginger and green onion to a boil in a clean pot, add the chicken and simmer very gently, covered, for 2 to 2.5 hours, until the meat is so tender it falls from the bone.
  5. Cool in the liquid. Fuliji chicken, from Suzhou in northern Anhui, is one of China's 'four famous chickens'; serve it cold or at room temperature.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard tears the skin and toughens the meat; keep the stock barely moving.
  • Skipping the frying gives pale skin that does not take on the characteristic color.

Authentic tips

  • Fuliji chicken is traditionally eaten cold, torn from the bone.
  • The stock can be reused as a master sauce.

Substitutions

  • Chicken legs only cook in 60 minutes.
  • Skip the frying and roast at 220 C (425 F) for 15 minutes before braising.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; serve cold, torn from the bone.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed buns, torn chicken eaten by hand
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
