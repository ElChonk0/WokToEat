Jjajangmyeon (Black Bean Noodles) 짜장면

Jjajangmyeon developed among Chinese immigrants in Incheon in the early 20th century, adapting northern Chinese zhajiangmian into a sweeter, darker Korean-Chinese staple — now one of Korea's most beloved comfort foods, especially for delivery.

Method

  1. Fry black bean paste in oil in a separate small pan over medium heat 3–4 minutes to mellow its raw bitterness; set aside.
  2. Brown pork in a large pot or wok until cooked through; add garlic, stirring briefly.
  3. Add onion, zucchini, and potato, stir-frying 3–4 minutes.
  4. Add the fried black bean paste and sugar, stirring to coat everything.
  5. Add 400 ml water, bring to a simmer, cover, and cook 12–15 minutes until the vegetables are tender.
  6. Stir in cornstarch slurry to thicken; cook noodles separately, divide among bowls, and top with the sauce and cucumber.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the step of frying the black bean paste separately first, which leaves the sauce tasting raw and overly bitter.
  • Making the sauce too thin, which won't cling to the noodles properly — the cornstarch slurry step matters.

Authentic tips

  • Frying the chunjang paste on its own before adding it to the stew is the single most important step for authentic flavor.
  • This dish is traditionally mixed together thoroughly at the table just before eating.

Substitutions

  • Diced beef can replace pork, though pork is the more traditional choice for this dish.
  • No chunjang: hoisin sauce mixed with a little miso approximates the flavor, though less authentically.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; store sauce and noodles separately.

Freezing: The sauce freezes well for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Yellow pickled radish and raw onion
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold cola or barley tea
