Jjajangmyeon (Black Bean Noodles) 짜장면

▾

Surprise me with

Jjajangmyeon developed among Chinese immigrants in Incheon in the early 20th century, adapting northern Chinese zhajiangmian into a sweeter, darker Korean-Chinese staple — now one of Korea's most beloved comfort foods, especially for delivery.

Method

  1. Cut the pork and vegetables into even 1.5 cm (1/2 in) cubes and mix the pork with the ginger, rice wine, salt and pepper. Put a large pot of water on to boil for the noodles.
  2. Heat the paste oil in a small pan over medium heat, add the chunjang, sugar and oyster sauce and fry for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring constantly. Frying the raw paste takes away its bitterness and deepens its flavor. Scrape it into a bowl.
  3. Heat the stir-fry oil in a flat-bottom wok or deep skillet over medium-high heat and cook the pork for 3 to 4 minutes until no pink remains, adding the soy sauce halfway.
  4. Add the onion and cook until soft and translucent, about 3 minutes, then the potato, cabbage and zucchini for 3 to 4 minutes until tender.
  5. Stir in the fried paste until everything is coated, then add the stock and boil for 3 to 4 minutes. Stir in the starch mixed with the slurry water and simmer until the sauce is thick and glossy.
  6. Cook the noodles just until tender, drain and divide among bowls. Ladle the sauce over and top with cucumber. Mix everything together just before eating.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the step of frying the black bean paste separately first, which leaves the sauce tasting raw and overly bitter.
  • Making the sauce too thin, which won't cling to the noodles properly — the cornstarch slurry step matters.

Authentic tips

  • Frying the chunjang paste on its own before adding it to the stew is the single most important step for authentic flavor.
  • This dish is traditionally mixed together thoroughly at the table just before eating.

Substitutions

  • Diced beef can replace pork, though pork is the more traditional choice for this dish.
  • No chunjang: hoisin sauce mixed with a little miso approximates the flavor, though less authentically.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; store sauce and noodles separately.

Freezing: The sauce freezes well for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Yellow pickled radish and raw onion
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold cola or barley tea
