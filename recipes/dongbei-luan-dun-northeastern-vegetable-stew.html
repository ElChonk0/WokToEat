Dōngběi Luàn Dùn (Northeastern Vegetable Stew) 东北乱炖

▾

Surprise me with

Luan dun, "chaotic stew," is a Dongbei one-pot dish of beans, potatoes and cabbage simmered with soybean paste, born of cold winters and sparse produce.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the green onion, ginger and garlic for 30 seconds, then the soybean paste for 1 minute until fragrant.
  2. Add the potatoes, green beans and corn and stir for 2 minutes to coat. Northeastern green beans are always cooked through; undercooked raw beans are unsafe.
  3. Add the water, soy sauce and sugar, cover and simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Add the eggplant, cabbage and tomatoes and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes more, until everything is soft and the sauce has thickened. Luan dun means 'chaotic stew': whatever the garden gives.
  5. Season with salt and serve with rice or cornbread.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the vegetables makes them mushy.
  • Too much soybean paste makes it too salty.

Authentic tips

  • Add the vegetables in order of cooking time.
  • Serve with steamed buns.

Substitutions

  • Any seasonal vegetables replace the list.
  • Add tofu or pork belly for richness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or cornbread
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
