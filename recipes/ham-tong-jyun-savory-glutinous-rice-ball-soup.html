Ham Tong Jyun (Savory Glutinous Rice Ball Soup) 咸汤圆

▾

Surprise me with

Savory tong jyun, plain glutinous rice balls in a pork, shrimp and radish broth, is a Toisanese and Cantonese dish for the winter solstice and Lunar New Year.

Method

  1. Soak the dried shrimp for 10 minutes and the mushrooms for 15 minutes in warm water, then drain and slice. Mix the pork with the cornstarch and water.
  2. Stir the hot water into the rice flour with chopsticks, then knead in the cold water by hand until the dough is smooth and soft. The hot water partly cooks the flour so the dough is elastic and easy to roll. Add a spoon of flour if it sticks.
  3. Roll the dough into ropes, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) pieces and roll each into a ball between your palms.
  4. Boil the balls in plenty of water, stirring so they do not stick to the bottom, for 6 to 8 minutes until they float and puff slightly. Lift them into a bowl of ice water. The cold bath firms them, keeps them from sticking together and stops the starch from clouding the soup.
  5. Heat a Dutch oven over high heat with the oil. Fry the ginger and garlic for 15 seconds, the dried shrimp and sausage for 30 seconds, then the radish for 1 minute and the mushrooms for 30 seconds, until fragrant.
  6. Add the stock, cover and boil for 3 minutes until the radish turns translucent. Add the cabbage and cook 2 minutes, then lower to medium and add the pork, stirring to separate, for 1 minute until it turns pale.
  7. Season with salt, sugar, fish sauce and white pepper. Drain the rice balls, add them to the pot and simmer for 1 minute to warm through. Finish with sesame oil, celery and green onion and serve hot.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the rice balls in the soup clouds it with starch; boil them separately.
  • A dry dough cracks; add water a spoonful at a time until it feels like soft clay.

Authentic tips

  • Chinese celery is the traditional fragrant finish.
  • Make extra balls and freeze them on a tray.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork balls replace sliced pork.
  • Napa cabbage replaces daikon.

Storage

Leftovers: Keep the soup and rice balls separately for 2 days; the balls harden when chilled, so reboil them.

Freezing: Uncooked rice balls freeze for 3 months; boil from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice is not needed
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Sweet sesame tong jyun
  • Beverage: Hot tea
