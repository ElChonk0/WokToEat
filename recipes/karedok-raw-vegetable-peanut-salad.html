Karedok (Raw Vegetable Salad with Peanut Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

Karedok, raw vegetables in a peanut-chili sauce, is a Sundanese (West Java) dish related to gado-gado, using the vegetables fresh rather than blanched.

Method

  1. Pound the garlic, chilies and kencur in a mortar into a paste. Kencur gives karedok its distinctive camphor-like aroma; this is the vegan version, without the shrimp paste often added.
  2. Add the peanuts and pound until coarsely ground, then the palm sugar, tamarind and salt.
  3. Stir in the warm water a little at a time until the sauce is thick and spoonable.
  4. Toss all the raw vegetables and the basil with the sauce just before serving, so they stay crunchy.
  5. Top with fried tofu and serve with rice or krupuk. Karedok is the raw Sundanese cousin of gado-gado.

Common mistakes

  • Dressing too early makes the vegetables soggy.
  • Raw long beans should be young and tender.

Authentic tips

  • Kencur gives the Sundanese flavor.
  • Serve immediately.

Substitutions

  • Peanut butter plus chili replaces homemade sauce.
  • Raw long beans should be young and tender.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the sauce keeps 5 days separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
