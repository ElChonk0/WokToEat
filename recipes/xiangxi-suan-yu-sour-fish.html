Xiāngxī Suān Yú (Western Hunan Sour Fish) 湘西酸鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Western Hunan's Miao and Tujia communities preserve fish with salt, rice flour and chilies; sour fish stews are a signature of their cooking, with a fermented tang that sets them apart from the rest of Hunan.

  • 800 g1.75 lbFish fillets or steaks (carp or tilapia)
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 45 ml3 tbspTea oil or neutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and garlic, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozPickled red chilies, chopped
  • 100 g3.5 ozSour pickled vegetables (or Xiangxi pickled fish), chopped
  • 150 g5 ozTomatoes, chopped
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater or stock
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice vinegar (if the pickles are mild)
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce
  • 30 g1 ozScallions
  • 10 g1/3 ozPerilla or cilantro

Method

  1. Rub the fish with salt and leave for 10 minutes. Pat dry and pan-fry in a little of the oil for 2 minutes per side until golden. Lift it out.
  2. Fry the ginger, garlic, pickled chilies and sour vegetables in the remaining oil for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant and the oil turns orange.
  3. Add the tomatoes for 3 minutes, then the water, and boil for 5 minutes into a sour, spicy broth. Xiangxi's Miao people ferment fish and vegetables in rice for this sharp sourness.
  4. Return the fish and simmer for 5 to 6 minutes until cooked. Season with vinegar and soy sauce to taste.
  5. Scatter with scallions and perilla and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard breaks the fish apart.
  • Adding extra vinegar masks the fermented flavor; rely on the pickles for sourness.

Authentic tips

  • Tea oil gives a distinctive nutty finish common in western Hunan.
  • Sauerkraut can stand in for sour vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Sauerkraut replaces sour pickled vegetables.
  • Chili paste adds heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freeze the broth only, for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
