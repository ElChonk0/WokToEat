Xiangxi Suan Yu (Western Hunan Sour Fish) 湘西酸鱼

Western Hunan's Miao and Tujia communities preserve fish with salt, rice flour and chilies; sour fish stews are a signature of their cooking, with a fermented tang that sets them apart from the rest of Hunan.

  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 45 ml3 tbspTea oil or neutral oil

Method

  1. Cut the fish into 4 cm pieces and pat dry. Chop the tomatoes.
  2. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium-high heat and fry the pickled chilies, ginger and garlic for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the tomatoes and sour vegetables and cook 3 minutes until they begin to break down.
  4. Pour in the water, bring to a simmer and cook 8 minutes to build the sour broth.
  5. Slide in the fish, simmer gently for 6-8 minutes until just opaque, and finish with scallions.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard breaks the fish apart.
  • Adding extra vinegar masks the fermented flavor; rely on the pickles for sourness.

Authentic tips

  • Tea oil gives a distinctive nutty finish common in western Hunan.
  • Sauerkraut can stand in for sour vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Sauerkraut replaces sour pickled vegetables.
  • Chili paste adds heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freeze the broth only, for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
