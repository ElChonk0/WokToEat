Gan Guo Ji (Sichuan Dry-Pot Chicken) 干锅鸡

Dry pot, or gan guo, is a Sichuan and Hunan style where ingredients are stir-fried with dried chilies and spices and served in a pot over a small flame; chicken is the most common version.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, chopped bone-in
  • 20 g2/3 ozDried chilies
  • 5 g2 tspSichuan peppercorns
  • 30 g2 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 250 g9 ozLotus root and potato
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and ginger
  • 100 g3.5 ozCelery
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Chop the chicken into 4 cm pieces through the bone. Cut the lotus root into 5 mm slices and the potato into batons; blanch both 3 minutes and drain.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over medium-high heat and fry the chicken for 10-12 minutes, turning, until the skin is golden and the meat is cooked through; lift out.
  3. In the same oil fry the dried chilies and peppercorns for 20 seconds until they darken slightly, then add the doubanjiang, garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute until the oil turns red.
  4. Return the chicken with the lotus root, potato and celery and toss over high heat for 3 minutes so the sauce clings.
  5. Serve straight from the wok, or in a pre-warmed cast-iron skillet or heavy pot so it stays hot at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the chilies turns the dish bitter; pull them out if they blacken.
  • Cutting the bone-in chicken too large leaves the centers undercooked.

Authentic tips

  • Blanching the vegetables first means they finish with the chicken in a few minutes.
  • The dried chilies are for aroma; most people do not eat them.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thigh cooks faster and is easier to eat; cut the first fry to 6 minutes.
  • Halve the dried chilies for a milder pot.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan with a splash of oil.

Freezing: Not recommended; the potatoes and lotus root turn mealy.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Cold cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
