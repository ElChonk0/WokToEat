Curry Laksa

▾

Surprise me with

Laksa blends Chinese noodle traditions with Malay and Peranakan spice pastes, and varies significantly by region — this curry laksa version, rich with coconut milk and a spiced chili paste, is among the most widely recognized internationally.

Method

  1. Fry the laksa paste in a little oil over medium heat 2–3 minutes until fragrant and the oil separates slightly.
  2. Add the stock and coconut milk; bring to a gentle simmer.
  3. Add the chicken and tofu puffs; simmer 12–15 minutes until the chicken is cooked through.
  4. Season with fish sauce and sugar; taste and adjust.
  5. Blanch bean sprouts briefly in the simmering broth, or separately, just until barely wilted.
  6. Divide cooked vermicelli among bowls, ladle the hot broth and chicken over top, and add bean sprouts and a halved egg to each bowl.
  7. Garnish with cilantro and serve with lime wedges.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the broth hard once the coconut milk is added, which can cause it to separate into an oily, less appealing texture.
  • Overcooking the noodles ahead of time so they turn mushy once combined with the hot broth — cook them just shy of done if assembling ahead.

Authentic tips

  • Good store-bought laksa paste is a perfectly legitimate shortcut, even for cooks who make most things from scratch.
  • This is an excellent Sunday prep dish: make the broth in batches and freeze portions, assembling fresh noodles and toppings on the night you serve it.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp can replace or be added alongside chicken.
  • No fish tofu puffs — regular fried tofu cubes work as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the broth separately from noodles and toppings; combine fresh when reheating.

Freezing: The broth (without noodles) freezes very well for up to 3 months.
