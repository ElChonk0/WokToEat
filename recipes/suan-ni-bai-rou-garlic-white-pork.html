Suàn Ní Bái Ròu (Garlic White Pork Slices) 蒜泥白肉

▾

Surprise me with

Suan ni bai rou, thinly sliced boiled pork belly in garlic, chili oil and soy sauce, is a Sichuan cold appetizer famed for the contrast of rich pork and sharp sauce.

  • Cook 40 min

  • 400 g14 ozPork belly, skin-on
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic, crushed
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 150 g5 ozCucumber
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger and scallion

Method

  1. Put the pork in a pot of cold water with the ginger and scallion, bring to a boil, skim, and simmer on low for 30 minutes, until a chopstick slides in easily.
  2. Remove from the heat and let the pork cool in its liquid for 30 minutes so it stays moist; refrigerate 1 hour to firm.
  3. Slice the cold pork as thinly as you can, about 2 mm, and fan it over the sliced cucumber.
  4. Crush the garlic with a pinch of salt to a paste and stir it into the soy sauce, chili oil, sugar and sesame oil.
  5. Spoon the sauce over the pork just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing warm pork gives ragged, thick slices; chill it first.
  • Boiling hard toughens the lean layers, so keep to a gentle simmer.

Authentic tips

  • Choose a belly with even layers of fat and lean for the classic "streaky" ribbons.
  • Chilling the pork overnight makes the slices paper-thin.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder works but is less rich; slice thinner.
  • Add a few drops of Sichuan pepper oil to the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; serve cold, with the sauce kept separate.

Freezing: Not recommended; the cooked pork turns watery.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Fresh watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
