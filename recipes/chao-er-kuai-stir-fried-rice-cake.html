Chǎo Ěr Kuài (Stir-Fried Yunnan Rice Cake) 炒饵块

▾

Surprise me with

Er kuai, a chewy cake of pounded rice, is a Yunnan breakfast staple, sliced and stir-fried with greens, egg and chilies.

  • 500 g1.1 lbEr kuai rice cakes, sliced
  • 22Eggs
  • 200 g7 ozGreens
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspChili oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozPickled mustard greens
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Soak the er kuai slices in warm water 10 minutes if hard; drain.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok and scramble the eggs; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the greens and pickled greens 2 minutes.
  4. Add the er kuai and soy sauce and toss 3-4 minutes until soft and slightly charred.
  5. Return the egg and finish with chili oil.

Common mistakes

  • Fresh rice cakes stick to the pan; use a well-oiled or nonstick wok.
  • Cooking too long makes them gummy.

Authentic tips

  • Korean tteok or Chinese nian gao can stand in.
  • Er kuai is a Yunnan breakfast.

Substitutions

  • Korean tteok replaces er kuai.
  • Chinese nian gao works too.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
