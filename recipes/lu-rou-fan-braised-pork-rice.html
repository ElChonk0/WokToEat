Lǔ Ròu Fàn (Braised Pork Rice) 滷肉飯

▾

Surprise me with

Taiwan's quintessential comfort food — minced or diced pork belly braised slowly in soy sauce, five-spice, and shallots until deeply savory, ladled over plain rice.

  • 600 g1.3 lbPork belly, skin-on, cut into small strips or 1 cm dice
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil or lard
  • 66Shallots, thinly sliced
  • 15 g1/2 ozGarlic, minced
  • 20 g1.5 tbspRock sugar (or brown sugar)
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing or rice wine
  • 60 ml1/4 cupLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 2 g1 tspFive-spice powder
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper
  • 44Dried shiitake, soaked and diced (optional)
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater or stock
  • 44Hard-boiled eggs
  • 800 g5 cupsCooked rice, for serving
  • 150 g5 ozPickled daikon and blanched greens, for serving

Method

  1. Fry the shallots in the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-low heat for 8 to 10 minutes until golden, then lift them out. Fried shallots are the soul of lu rou fan.
  2. Raise the heat to medium and cook the pork in the shallot oil for 6 to 8 minutes, stirring, until it renders fat and lightly browns. Add the garlic for 30 seconds.
  3. Push the pork aside and melt the rock sugar in the fat until amber, then stir it through the pork with the rice wine, soy sauces, five-spice and pepper.
  4. Add the water, shiitake, fried shallots and eggs, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over very low heat for 1.5 hours, stirring occasionally, until the skin is gelatinous and the sauce is thick and sticky.
  5. Spoon the pork and plenty of sauce over hot rice with a halved egg, pickled daikon and greens.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the braise: the deep flavor comes specifically from the long, slow reduction — a shorter time leaves the pork tough and the sauce thin.

Authentic tips

  • Letting the dish rest, covered, for 10–15 minutes after cooking — or making it a day ahead — noticeably improves the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Leaner cut instead of pork belly: works, but loses some richness; a 50/50 mix of belly and shoulder is a reasonable middle ground.

Storage

Leftovers: Improves over several days; keeps a week refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes excellently; a great make-ahead batch dish.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled radish and a braised egg
  • Soup: Clear pork bone soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
