Lǔ Ròu Fàn (Braised Pork Rice) 滷肉飯

▾

Surprise me with

Taiwan's quintessential comfort food — minced or diced pork belly braised slowly in soy sauce, five-spice, and shallots until deeply savory, ladled over plain rice.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 600g1.3 lbPork belly, diced small

  • 15g3 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 60ml4 tbspSoy sauce
  • 15ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 20g1.5 tbspRock sugar (or brown sugar)
  • 2g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 500ml2 cupsWater or stock
  • 44Hard-boiled eggs (optional)
  • 800g4 cupsCooked rice for serving

Method

  1. In a pot, render the pork belly over medium heat until it releases some fat and turns lightly golden, 5–6 minutes — no extra oil needed.
  2. Add shallots and garlic, frying until fragrant and slightly caramelized.
  3. Add soy sauce, dark soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, and five-spice; stir to coat.
  4. Add water or stock, bring to a simmer, then reduce heat to low.
  5. Simmer uncovered, stirring occasionally, 45–60 minutes until the pork is tender and the sauce reduces to a thick, glossy braise. Add hard-boiled eggs in the last 15 minutes if using.
  6. Ladle generously over bowls of rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the braise: the deep flavor comes specifically from the long, slow reduction — a shorter time leaves the pork tough and the sauce thin.

Authentic tips

  • Letting the dish rest, covered, for 10–15 minutes after cooking — or making it a day ahead — noticeably improves the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Leaner cut instead of pork belly: works, but loses some richness; a 50/50 mix of belly and shoulder is a reasonable middle ground.

Storage

Leftovers: Improves over several days; keeps a week refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes excellently; a great make-ahead batch dish.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled radish and a braised egg
  • Soup: Clear pork bone soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
