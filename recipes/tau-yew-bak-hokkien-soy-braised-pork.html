Tau Yew Bak (Hokkien Soy-Braised Pork) 豆油肉

▾

Surprise me with

Tau yew bak, pork belly braised in dark soy sauce with garlic, is a Hokkien dish brought to Malaysia and Singapore, eaten with rice or in buns.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over high heat and brown the pork in batches for 3 to 4 minutes, turning, until golden on the outside.
  2. Lower the heat to medium and fry the garlic, shallots, ginger, star anise, cinnamon and taucheo for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant, scraping the bottom of the pot.
  3. Return the pork, add the soy sauces, rock sugar, pepper and water to just cover and bring to a boil.
  4. Cover and simmer gently for 1 hour, stirring now and then. Add the eggs and tofu puffs and simmer, partly covered, for 30 minutes more, until the pork is meltingly tender and the gravy is dark and slightly syrupy.
  5. Taste for salt and sugar. It is even better made a day ahead: chill it, lift off the set fat and reheat. Serve with rice and sambal belacan.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the sugar makes it bitter.
  • Boiling hard toughens the pork.

Authentic tips

  • Add boiled eggs for the last 20 minutes.
  • Serve with rice or in steamed buns.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.
  • Hard-boiled eggs added for the last 20 minutes soak up the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or buns
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Chinese tea
