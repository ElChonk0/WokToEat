Jiān Bǐng Guǒ Zi (Beijing Egg Crepe) 煎饼果子

▾

Surprise me with

Jianbing guozi, a thin mung bean and wheat crepe filled with egg, crisp wafer, scallion and sauces, is the quintessential street breakfast of Tianjin and Beijing.

  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Whisk the flours, five-spice, salt and water into a thin, smooth batter and rest for 30 minutes. Mung bean flour gives the crepe its savory, slightly nutty taste.
  2. Heat a large nonstick skillet or crepe pan over medium heat and wipe it with oil. Pour in a ladle of batter and spread it quickly into a thin round with the back of the ladle or a spatula.
  3. When it sets, after 30 seconds, crack an egg on top, break the yolk and spread it over the crepe. Sprinkle with green onion, cilantro and sesame seeds and cook until the egg sets, about 1 minute.
  4. Flip the crepe egg-side down for 10 seconds, then turn it back and brush with sweet bean sauce, chili sauce and fermented bean curd.
  5. Lay two crispy wafers in the center, fold the sides over and fold in half. Eat at once, while the wafer is still crunchy; a Tianjin and Beijing street breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Batter that is too thick gives a gummy crepe.
  • A griddle that is too hot sets the batter before it spreads.

Authentic tips

  • Spread the batter quickly in a circular motion.
  • The crisp wafer, or fried wonton skin, is essential for crunch.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought crepe batter mix saves time.
  • A fried dough stick (youtiao) replaces the crispy wafer.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crepe softens within minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Hot soy milk
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot soy milk
