Gān Chǎo Sù Hé Fěn (Vegetable Dry-Fried Rice Noodles) 干炒素河粉

▾

Surprise me with

Gan chao niu he, dry-fried beef rice noodles, is the benchmark of Cantonese wok skill; this vegetarian version keeps the smoky wok hei with bean sprouts and mushrooms.

  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbFresh wide rice noodles
  • 200 g7 ozBean sprouts
  • 100 g3.5 ozShiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozScallions
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Gently separate the rice noodles by hand, microwaving them 30 seconds first if they are cold and stiff.
  2. Heat a wok until smoking. Add 2 tablespoons of the oil, then the mushrooms, and sear 2 minutes until browned; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the noodles, spreading them out; leave 30 seconds, then toss gently so they pick up some char.
  4. Add the light and dark soy sauces and sugar and toss to coat evenly.
  5. Return the mushrooms and add the sprouts and scallion; toss 30 seconds and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the wok steams the noodles into a clump; cook in two batches if needed.
  • Stirring constantly breaks the noodles and prevents any char.

Authentic tips

  • Wok hei, the smoky breath of the wok, comes from very high heat and not overloading the pan.
  • Work with room-temperature noodles; cold ones snap.

Substitutions

  • Pressed tofu strips add protein.
  • Dried rice noodles, soaked until pliable, replace fresh ones.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; reheat in a very hot wok with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
