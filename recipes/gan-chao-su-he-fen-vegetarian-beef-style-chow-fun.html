Gān Chǎo Sù Hé Fěn (Vegetable Dry-Fried Rice Noodles) 干炒素河粉

▾

Surprise me with

Gan chao niu he, dry-fried beef rice noodles, is the benchmark of Cantonese wok skill; this vegetarian version keeps the smoky wok hei with bean sprouts and mushrooms.

Method

  1. Peel the noodles apart into single strands by hand; if they are cold and stiff, microwave them covered for 1 minute first so they do not break. Mix the soy sauces, mushroom sauce and sugar in a cup.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat with 2 tablespoons of oil. Sear the mushrooms and tofu in one layer for 1 to 2 minutes until browned, toss with a splash of the sauce and lift them out. The browned mushrooms stand in for the beef.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the onion and scallion whites for 20 to 30 seconds until fragrant. Add the noodles, spread them out and cook for about 2 minutes, turning them gently only every 30 seconds, until they char in spots and smell toasty.
  4. Pour the rest of the sauce over and toss gently with a lifting motion for 1 minute until evenly brown. Add the bean sprouts and scallion greens for 30 to 45 seconds, then return the mushrooms and tofu for 30 seconds.
  5. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve at once while the sprouts are still crunchy.

Common mistakes

  • Overcrowding the wok steams the noodles into a clump; cook in two batches if needed.
  • Stirring constantly breaks the noodles and prevents any char.

Authentic tips

  • Wok hei, the smoky breath of the wok, comes from very high heat and not overloading the pan.
  • Work with room-temperature noodles; cold ones snap.

Substitutions

  • Pressed tofu strips add protein.
  • Dried rice noodles, soaked until pliable, replace fresh ones.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; reheat in a very hot wok with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
