Som Tam Jay (Green Papaya Salad)

▾

Surprise me with

Som tam, pounded green papaya salad, comes from Isaan in northeastern Thailand and Laos; this vegetarian version swaps fish sauce for soy sauce and salt.

  • 400 g14 ozGreen papaya, peeled and shredded
  • 3 cloves3 clovesGarlic
  • 33Bird's eye chilies
  • 80 g3 ozLong beans, cut into 4 cm (1.5 in) lengths
  • 100 g3.5 ozCherry tomatoes, halved
  • 20 g1.5 tbspPalm sugar
  • 60 ml1/4 cupLime juice
  • 20 ml4 tspLight soy sauce (in place of fish sauce)
  • 10 ml2 tspTamarind paste (optional)
  • 40 g1/3 cupRoasted peanuts

Method

  1. Shred the papaya with a julienne peeler, or the traditional way: hack long cuts into it with a cleaver, then slice them off. Soak in ice water for 10 minutes for extra crunch and drain well.
  2. Pound the garlic and chilies in a large clay or wooden mortar to a coarse paste. Add the long beans and bruise them lightly.
  3. Add the palm sugar and pound until it dissolves, then the lime juice, soy sauce and tamarind.
  4. Add the papaya, tomatoes and half the peanuts. Pound gently with one hand while turning the salad with a spoon in the other, for about 1 minute, so the papaya bruises and absorbs the dressing without turning to mush.
  5. Taste and adjust; it should be sour, sweet, salty and hot. Serve topped with the remaining peanuts, with sticky rice and raw cabbage.

Common mistakes

  • Over-pounding makes it mushy.
  • Waiting makes the salad watery.

Authentic tips

  • Adjust the heat to taste.
  • Serve with sticky rice.

Substitutions

  • Shredded green mango or raw carrot replace papaya.
  • Reduce the chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the salad waters out.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
