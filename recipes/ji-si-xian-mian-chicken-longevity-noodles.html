Jī Sī Xiàn Miàn (Fuzhou Chicken Longevity Noodles) 鸡丝线面

▾

Surprise me with

Xian mian, very thin dried wheat noodles, are a Fuzhou specialty served at birthdays for long life, often in a clear chicken broth.

  • 250 g9 ozDried thin wheat noodles (Fuzhou xian mian / mee sua)
  • 500 g1.1 lbChicken thighs, bone-in
  • 1.5 L6 cupsChicken stock
  • 30 g1 ozGinger and green onion

  • 44Eggs, hard-boiled (or fried, Fuzhou style)
  • 44Dried shiitake, soaked
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken with the stock, ginger, green onion, Shaoxing wine and shiitake for 25 minutes. Lift out the chicken and shred the meat.
  2. Season the broth with salt; xian mian are already salty, so go lightly.
  3. Cook the noodles in a separate pot of boiling water for 1 to 2 minutes, then drain; boiling them in the broth would make it starchy and salty.
  4. Divide the noodles among bowls and top with chicken, shiitake and an egg.
  5. Ladle over the hot broth and finish with sesame oil and white pepper. Long, unbroken noodles with eggs are served for birthdays in Fuzhou to wish long life.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the thin noodles dissolve; they need only two minutes.
  • Cooking them in the broth turns it cloudy and starchy.

Authentic tips

  • Never cut the noodles; long noodles symbolize long life.
  • Add a few shiitake to the stock for flavor.

Substitutions

  • Dried thin wheat noodles replace xian mian.
  • Add dried shrimp for flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
