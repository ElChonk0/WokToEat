Ji Si Xian Mian (Fuzhou Chicken Longevity Noodles) 鸡丝线面

Xian mian, very thin dried wheat noodles, are a Fuzhou specialty served at birthdays for long life, often in a clear chicken broth.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozChicken thighs
  • 22Eggs
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 1.2 L5 cupsChicken stock
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken thighs in the stock with the ginger for 20 minutes until cooked through; lift out and shred.
  2. Season the stock with the salt and bring to a gentle boil.
  3. Cook the xian mian in a separate pot of boiling water for 2 minutes until tender; drain.
  4. Divide the noodles among bowls, ladle over the hot stock, and slip a soft-poached or boiled egg into each bowl.
  5. Top with the chicken, scallion and a few drops of sesame oil.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the thin noodles dissolve; they need only two minutes.
  • Cooking them in the broth turns it cloudy and starchy.

Authentic tips

  • Never cut the noodles; long noodles symbolize long life.
  • Add a few shiitake to the stock for flavor.

Substitutions

  • Dried thin wheat noodles replace xian mian.
  • Add dried shrimp for flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
