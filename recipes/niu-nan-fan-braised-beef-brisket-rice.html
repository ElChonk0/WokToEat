Niú Nǎn Fàn (Taiwanese Beef Brisket Rice) 牛腩飯

▾

Surprise me with

Braised beef brisket spooned over rice, with greens and a boiled egg, is a Taiwanese lunch-shop staple that brings the flavors of beef noodle soup to a rice bowl.

  • Cook 2.5 hr

  • 650 g1.4 lbBeef brisket, cut into 4 cm (1.5 in) cubes
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 80 g3 ozGinger, green onion and star anise
  • 30 g2 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 20 g1.5 tbspRock sugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspRice wine (mijiu)
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 11Tomato, cut into wedges (optional)
  • 1 L4 cupsWater
  • 300 g10 ozDaikon, cut into chunks
  • 600 g4 cupsCooked rice
  • 150 g5 ozBlanched greens and pickled mustard greens, for serving

Method

  1. Blanch the brisket in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and star anise for 1 minute, then the doubanjiang for 1 minute until the oil turns red. Add the rock sugar and stir until it melts.
  3. Add the brisket, rice wine and soy sauces and toss for 2 minutes until coated and glossy.
  4. Add the tomato and water, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 1.5 hours.
  5. Add the daikon and simmer uncovered for 30 to 40 minutes, until the beef is very tender and the daikon has turned brown and soft.
  6. Spoon the beef, daikon and plenty of sauce over rice and serve with blanched greens and pickled mustard greens.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.
  • Adding daikon too early makes it dissolve.

Authentic tips

  • Better the next day.
  • Add a boiled egg.

Substitutions

  • Short rib replaces brisket.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the daikon.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched greens
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
