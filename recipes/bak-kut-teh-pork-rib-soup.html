Bak Kut Teh (Pork Rib Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

A Hokkien-Chinese dish born among Malaysian dock workers needing hearty, restorative meals, bak kut teh ("meat bone tea") simmers pork ribs in a fragrant herbal broth of star anise, cinnamon, and garlic.

Method

  1. Blanch pork ribs in boiling water 3 minutes to remove impurities; drain.
  2. Combine ribs, garlic, star anise, cinnamon, and white peppercorns in a pot with water.
  3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer, cover, and cook 1 hour.
  4. Add both soy sauces and continue simmering 20–30 minutes until the ribs are very tender.
  5. Add tofu puffs and mushrooms if using in the last 10 minutes.
  6. Serve hot with rice or you tiao (fried dough sticks) for dipping.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the initial blanch, which leaves the broth cloudier and less clean-tasting.
  • Rushing the simmer — this broth needs real time for the garlic and spices to fully infuse.

Authentic tips

  • Whole garlic cloves become soft and mellow after the long simmer — they're meant to be eaten alongside the ribs.
  • A store-bought bak kut teh spice packet is a reliable shortcut if you don't have all the individual spices.

Substitutions

  • Pork neck bones or spare ribs both work.
  • Pre-mixed bak kut teh spice packets save effort.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the broth improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, youtiao and braised tofu puffs
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the main
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Chinese tea
