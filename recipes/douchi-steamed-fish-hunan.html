Douchi Zheng Yu (Hunan Steamed Fish with Black Beans) 豆豉蒸鱼

Fermented black beans (douchi) are a cornerstone of Hunan cooking, and steaming fish over them with chilies and ginger yields a fragrant, mildly spicy dish typical of Hunan home kitchens.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 700 g1.5 lbWhite fish fillets or whole fish
  • 30 g2 tbspFermented black beans, rinsed
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger and garlic
  • 30 g1 ozFresh red chilies, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 g2 tbspScallions
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry and rub with the wine on a plate; lay the ginger slices underneath.
  2. Spread the rinsed black beans, garlic and chilies over the fish.
  3. Steam over high heat for 8-10 minutes, until the thickest part flakes and the eye turns opaque if using whole fish.
  4. Pour off any liquid that has collected on the plate.
  5. Drizzle with the soy sauce, scatter the scallions on top and pour smoking-hot oil over to sizzle.

Common mistakes

  • Oversteaming dries the fish; check at 8 minutes.
  • Skipping the step of pouring off the steaming liquid leaves a fishy flavor.

Authentic tips

  • Use the freshest fish; steaming offers nowhere to hide.
  • Rinse the black beans quickly if they taste very salty.

Substitutions

  • Cod, sea bass or snapper all work.
  • Dried chili flakes replace fresh chilies.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
