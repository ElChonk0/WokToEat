Chicken Korma

▾

Surprise me with

Korma traces back to Mughal royal kitchens, where meat was braised slowly in a rich, yogurt- or nut-based sauce — today one of the mildest and creamiest curries in the North Indian repertoire.

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken in the yogurt, ginger-garlic paste and salt for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Fry the onions in the oil over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes until light golden (not dark; korma should be pale). Lift out half and blend it with the soaked cashews and a little water into a smooth paste.
  3. Add the cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and bay leaf to the remaining onions and fry for 30 seconds, then the coriander and pepper for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the chicken with its marinade and cook over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring, until the yogurt is absorbed and the oil starts to separate. Keep the heat moderate so the yogurt does not curdle.
  5. Stir in the onion-cashew paste and stock, cover and simmer for 15 minutes until the chicken is tender and the sauce is creamy.
  6. Finish with garam masala and kewra water. Korma, a Mughal braise, is rich and mild rather than hot. Serve with naan or pulao.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the yogurt over high heat, which can cause it to curdle — stir it in over lower heat and simmer gently.
  • Skipping the cashew paste, which is central to korma's signature creamy texture.

Authentic tips

  • This curry should be mild and creamy rather than spicy — it's designed as a gentler counterpoint on a table with spicier dishes.
  • Whisking the yogurt smooth before adding it, and adding it gradually, helps prevent curdling.

Substitutions

  • No cashews: blanched almonds work as a substitute for the paste.
  • Coconut milk can replace some of the yogurt for a slightly different, equally rich result.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the sauce may need re-blending after thawing to restore smoothness.
