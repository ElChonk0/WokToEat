Chicken Korma

▾

Surprise me with

Korma traces back to Mughal royal kitchens, where meat was braised slowly in a rich, yogurt- or nut-based sauce — today one of the mildest and creamiest curries in the North Indian repertoire.

Method

  1. Blend soaked cashews with a little water into a smooth paste.
  2. Fry onion, cardamom, and cinnamon in oil until the onion turns golden.
  3. Add ginger-garlic paste, garam masala, and coriander; cook 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
  4. Add chicken and stir to coat, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  5. Stir in yogurt and cashew paste, then add stock; bring to a gentle simmer.
  6. Cover and cook 18–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is tender and the sauce is creamy and thickened.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the yogurt over high heat, which can cause it to curdle — stir it in over lower heat and simmer gently.
  • Skipping the cashew paste, which is central to korma's signature creamy texture.

Authentic tips

  • This curry should be mild and creamy rather than spicy — it's designed as a gentler counterpoint on a table with spicier dishes.
  • Whisking the yogurt smooth before adding it, and adding it gradually, helps prevent curdling.

Substitutions

  • No cashews: blanched almonds work as a substitute for the paste.
  • Coconut milk can replace some of the yogurt for a slightly different, equally rich result.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the sauce may need re-blending after thawing to restore smoothness.
