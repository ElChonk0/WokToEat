Goan Fish Curry

▾

Surprise me with

Goan fish curry is the everyday curry of Goa, built on a paste of coconut and dried red chilies soured with tamarind or kokum and served with rice.

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the turmeric and a pinch of salt and leave for 15 minutes; this firms the flesh and removes any fishy smell.
  2. Grind the coconut, chilies, coriander, cumin, pepper, garlic and tamarind with about 150 ml (2/3 cup) of the water into a very smooth, bright orange paste. A silky paste gives Goan curry its velvety texture.
  3. Heat the coconut oil in a wide pan over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 to 6 minutes until soft and lightly golden. Add the tomato and green chilies for 3 minutes.
  4. Add the coconut paste and fry for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until fragrant. Add the remaining water and the salt and simmer for 5 minutes into a pourable gravy.
  5. Slide in the fish and simmer gently for 5 to 6 minutes, shaking the pan rather than stirring, until it flakes. Scatter with cilantro and serve with rice; Goans call this 'xit kodi' (rice and curry).

Cook’s notes

Written for haddock fillet. Goan cooks use local fish such as kingfish, pomfret or mackerel; haddock is a good firm, mild substitute.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring breaks haddock into flakes; shake the pan and spoon sauce over.
  • Boiling the sauce after adding the coconut paste can split it; keep it at a simmer.

Authentic tips

  • The curry tastes better after resting, so make it ahead if you can.
  • Add kokum if you have it for a sharper sourness.

Substitutions

  • Cod or any firm white fish replaces the haddock.
  • Unsweetened desiccated coconut soaked in warm water replaces fresh coconut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator and tastes better the next day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with
