Yáng Zhōu Chǎo Fàn (Yeung Chow Fried Rice) 扬州炒饭

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, Yeung Chow fried rice with char siu, shrimp and egg is the house fried rice of Cantonese restaurants worldwide, distinct from the Yangzhou original.

  • 800 g5 cupsCooked jasmine rice, cold and slightly dry
  • 150 g5 ozChar siu (Chinese BBQ pork), diced
  • 150 g5 ozShrimp, peeled and halved
  • 33Eggs, beaten
  • 50 g2 ozCarrot, finely diced
  • 60 g1/2 cupFrozen peas
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 3 stalks3 stalksGreen onion, white and green parts separated, chopped
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/2 tspGround white pepper

Method

  1. Break up the rice with wet hands so no clumps remain. Day-old rice, or rice cooked with a little less water than usual, gives the best fried rice because the grains are firm and dry.
  2. Bring a small pot of water to a boil, blanch the carrot and peas for 30 to 40 seconds, then add the shrimp for 1 to 2 minutes until just pink. Drain well. Blanching keeps the vegetables tender and the shrimp juicy, so they need only warming in the wok.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat for 1 minute, add 1 tablespoon of oil and wait until it ripples. Pour in the eggs and let them set for 10 to 15 seconds, then add the rice before they firm up.
  4. Stir-fry over high heat for 2.5 to 3 minutes, pressing and tossing to break up the rice and coat it with egg, until the grains are separate and you hear them crackle.
  5. Lower the heat to medium, add the salt and soy sauce and toss. Push the rice aside, fry the char siu and green onion whites in the center for 30 seconds, then mix everything together and stir-fry for 1.5 minutes.
  6. Add the vegetables and shrimp, raise the heat to high and drizzle the remaining oil around the edge of the wok so the rice glistens. Toss for 1 minute, add the green onion greens and white pepper, and turn off the heat. Taste and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too much soy turns it brown and soggy; Yeung Chow fried rice should be pale and golden.
  • Overloading a small wok steams the rice; cook in two batches.

Authentic tips

  • On induction, keep the wok on the surface and toss with a spatula rather than lifting it.
  • Day-old rice gives separate grains; spread fresh rice on a tray and chill for an hour if needed.

Substitutions

  • Ham or Chinese sausage replace char siu.
  • Add diced lettuce at the end for crunch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic gai lan
  • Soup: West Lake beef soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
