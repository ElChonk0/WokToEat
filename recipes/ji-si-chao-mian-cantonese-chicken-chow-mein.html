Jī Sī Chǎo Miàn (Cantonese Chicken Chow Mein) 鸡丝炒面

▾

Surprise me with

Cantonese chow mein pan-fries thin egg noodles until crisp in places, then tosses them with a light, savory stir-fry; it is the dish behind the takeout chow mein known worldwide.

Method

  1. If the noodles are raw rather than pre-steamed, steam them in a loose nest over boiling water for 10 minutes. Dip them in boiling water for 15 to 30 seconds, drain well and fluff with chopsticks, then leave to cool and dry for 5 minutes. Dry noodles crisp; wet ones steam and stick.
  2. Slice the chicken across the grain and mix with the marinade salt, cornstarch and water. Mix the gravy slurry cornstarch with its water.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok or large skillet over medium-high heat until the oil shimmers, using 2 tablespoons of oil. Spread the noodles into an even cake and fry without stirring for 5 to 7 minutes, shaking the pan now and then, until the underside is golden and crisp. Flip, add 1 tablespoon of oil around the rim and fry the second side the same way. Slide onto a platter.
  4. Add the last of the oil and fry the chicken for about 1 minute until it turns white, then push it aside and fry the ginger and green onion for 45 seconds until fragrant. Lift everything out.
  5. Add the bok choy, mushrooms, carrot and boiling water, cover and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the bok choy stems are tender-crisp.
  6. Return the chicken and aromatics and season with the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sugar and salt. Stir in the slurry and simmer for 30 seconds until the gravy is glossy and coats a spoon, then add the sesame oil.
  7. Spoon the vegetables, chicken and gravy over the noodle cake and serve at once; the gravy softens the noodles within minutes, so the contrast of crisp and soft is best at the table.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet noodles steam instead of crisping; drain and dry them well.
  • Overcrowding a small pan stews everything; cook in two batches if your wok is under 30 cm (12 in).

Authentic tips

  • Toss with chopsticks or tongs rather than a spatula to keep the noodles long.
  • On induction, preheat the wok fully and keep it in contact with the surface instead of tossing it.

Substitutions

  • Dried thin egg noodles: boil 3 minutes, then dry and fry the same way.
  • Use char siu or shrimp in place of the chicken.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot pan to re-crisp.

Freezing: Not recommended; the vegetables turn limp.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed gai lan with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
