Jian Zhu Pa (Hong Kong Pan-Fried Pork Chops) 煎猪扒

▾

Surprise me with

Tenderized, marinated pork chops pan-fried and served over rice or with fries are a Hong Kong cha chaan teng classic, a product of the city's Western-influenced diner cooking.

  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Pound the chops with the back of a cleaver or a meat mallet to about 1 cm (1/2 in) thick and nick the fat edge so they stay flat.
  2. Mix the soy, oyster sauce, wine, sugar, garlic, pepper, baking soda, egg and cornstarch, coat the chops and marinate for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat and fry the chops for 3 to 4 minutes per side until browned and just cooked through. Rest on a board.
  4. Add the remaining oil and the onion to the pan and cook for 2 minutes. Stir in the Worcestershire sauce, ketchup and water and simmer for 1 minute until glossy.
  5. Slice the chops and serve over rice with the onion sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the pounding leaves the lean loin chewy.
  • Too much baking soda gives a soapy taste; a quarter teaspoon is plenty.

Authentic tips

  • The baking-soda marinade is the diner trick for very tender chops.
  • Serve the sauce on the side for the crispest surface.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in chops: cook 1 to 2 minutes longer per side.
  • Chicken thigh fillets work with the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: Freezes well raw in the marinade for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched lettuce with oyster sauce
  • Soup: Hong Kong borscht
  • Dessert: Pineapple bun
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
