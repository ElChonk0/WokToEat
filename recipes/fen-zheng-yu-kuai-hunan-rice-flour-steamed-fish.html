Fěn Zhēng Yú Kuài (Rice-Flour Steamed Fish) 粉蒸鱼块

▾

Surprise me with

Fen zheng, steaming food in a coating of coarsely ground toasted rice, is a Hunan and Hubei technique usually applied to pork and beef; fish is cooked the same way.

Method

  1. Toast the rice, star anise and peppercorns in a dry pan over medium-low heat for 5 minutes until golden, cool, and grind coarsely.
  2. Mix the doubanjiang, soy sauce, wine, ginger, garlic, chili oil and sugar and marinate the fish for 15 minutes.
  3. Toss the fish with the ground rice and 3 tablespoons water until each piece is coated.
  4. Spread the pumpkin in a heatproof dish or bamboo steamer, arrange the fish on top, and steam over high heat for 15 minutes until the fish is cooked and the coating soft.
  5. Scatter the scallions on top and serve in the steaming dish.

Cook’s notes

Written for Chilean sea bass. Hunan cooks use carp or river fish.

Common mistakes

  • Grinding the rice too fine makes a paste; keep it coarse like sand.
  • Too much water turns the coating gluey.

Authentic tips

  • Put the pumpkin underneath to soak up the juices.
  • Sea bass is rich, so the chili and rice balance it.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin replaces the sea bass.
  • Taro replaces the pumpkin.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator; reheat by steaming.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched greens
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
