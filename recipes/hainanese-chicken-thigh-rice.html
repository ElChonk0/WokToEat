Hainanese Chicken Thigh Rice

▾

Surprise me with

Singapore's national dish in miniature: chicken poached in ginger-scallion-infused broth, served with rice cooked in the poaching fat and two dipping sauces — here made quicker using thighs instead of a whole bird.

Method

  1. Rub the chicken skin with the salt, rinse and pat dry; this cleans and seasons the skin. Bring the poaching water to a boil with the ginger and green onion.
  2. Lower in the thighs, return to a boil, then cover and keep at a bare simmer for 15 minutes. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 15 minutes more, until the thickest part by the bone reads 74 C (165 F). Lift the chicken into ice water for 5 minutes to set the skin silky and tight, then drain and brush with sesame oil. Keep the poaching broth.
  3. Heat the chicken fat in a pot over medium heat and fry the garlic, shallots and minced ginger for 1 to 2 minutes until golden and fragrant. Add the drained rice and stir for 2 minutes until every grain is coated and turns opaque.
  4. Add 480 ml (2 cups) of the poaching broth, the pandan and salt, bring to a boil, cover and cook on low for 15 minutes (or transfer to a rice cooker). Rest 10 minutes, then fluff. The broth and fat are what make chicken rice taste of chicken.
  5. Blend the chilies, garlic, ginger, lime juice and sugar with a few spoons of broth into the chili sauce. Pound or blend the grated ginger with a pinch of salt and a spoon of hot oil for the ginger sauce.
  6. Chop the chicken through the bone, arrange it over the rice with cucumber, drizzle with light soy sauce and sesame oil, and serve with the sauces, dark soy and a bowl of the hot broth with green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the chicken hard instead of gently poaching it, which toughens the meat.
  • Cooking the rice in plain water instead of the reserved poaching stock, which is where most of this dish's flavor comes from.

Authentic tips

  • The ice bath step genuinely matters for texture — it's the difference between silky, tender skin and an ordinary poached chicken.
  • Make extra stock and freeze what's not used for the rice; it's a flavorful base for future soups.

Substitutions

  • A whole chicken can replace thighs for a more traditional presentation, poaching about 35–40 minutes.
  • No fresh chilies for the sauce: a good store-bought chili-garlic sauce loosened with lime juice works as a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Store chicken, rice, and sauce separately; the rice reheats best with a splash of water or stock.

Freezing: Freeze the poaching stock for future use; the cooked chicken and rice are best fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili-ginger sauce and cucumber
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
