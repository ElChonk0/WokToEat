Kare-Kare (Beef in Peanut Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

A distinctly Filipino dish with pre-colonial roots, kare-kare simmers beef (traditionally oxtail) in a rich peanut sauce with vegetables, served with fermented shrimp paste (bagoong) on the side.

Method

  1. Boil the oxtail in the water with the quartered onion, skimming, for 2.5 to 3 hours, until very tender. Keep 1 L (4 cups) of the broth.
  2. Heat the oil with the annatto seeds over low heat for 3 to 4 minutes until the oil turns deep orange, then discard the seeds. Annatto gives kare-kare its color, not flavor.
  3. Saute the garlic and onion in the annatto oil in a Dutch oven for 3 minutes, then add the oxtail and broth and bring to a simmer.
  4. Stir in the peanut butter and toasted rice and simmer, stirring often, for 10 to 15 minutes until thick and creamy. Season lightly with salt; the dish is deliberately mild because the bagoong seasons it.
  5. Blanch the eggplant, long beans and bok choy separately until just tender and arrange them over the stew.
  6. Serve with rice and plenty of sauteed shrimp paste on the side, to add to each bite.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial beef simmer, since oxtail or shank needs real time to become properly tender.
  • Skipping the bagoong on the side, which is an essential flavor contrast, not merely a garnish.

Authentic tips

  • The annatto seeds give kare-kare its signature orange color — don't skip steeping them.
  • This dish is always served with bagoong (fermented shrimp paste) alongside — the salty funk balances the sauce's richness.

Substitutions

  • Peanut butter replaces ground peanuts.
  • Oxtail takes longer; shank is quicker.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; thin with water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and bagoong
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
