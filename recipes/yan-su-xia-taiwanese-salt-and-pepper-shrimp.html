Yán Sū Xiā (Taiwanese Salt-and-Pepper Shrimp) 鹽酥蝦

▾

Surprise me with

Yan su ji (salt-crispy chicken) is a Taiwan night-market icon; the same seasoning, with fried basil and garlic, is applied to shrimp.

  • Cook 8 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbShrimp, shell on, legs trimmed and deveined
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice wine
  • 60 g1/2 cupSweet potato starch
  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 30 g1 ozGarlic, minced
  • 11Red chili, minced
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, chopped
  • 30 g1 cupThai basil
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 2 g1 tspWhite pepper
  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder

Method

  1. Cut along the back of each shrimp through the shell to devein it, leaving the shell on. Toss with the rice wine and pat very dry, then coat thinly in sweet potato starch.
  2. Mix the salt, white pepper and five-spice into pepper salt.
  3. Heat the oil to 190 C (375 F) and fry the shrimp in batches for 1 to 2 minutes until pink and the shells are crisp enough to eat. Drain. Fry the basil for 5 seconds until crisp.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Fry the garlic, chili and green onion over medium heat for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  5. Toss in the shrimp, basil and pepper salt for 20 seconds and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Wet shrimp spatter and fry soggy.
  • Overcooking makes them rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • Eat the shells too if the shrimp are small.
  • Serve hot with beer.

Substitutions

  • Squid or tofu take the same coating.
  • An air fryer at 200 C (400 F) for 6 minutes works.

Storage

Leftovers: Best within minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Corn soup
  • Dessert: Shaved ice
  • Beverage: Cold beer
