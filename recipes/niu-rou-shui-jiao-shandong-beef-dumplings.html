Shāndōng Niú Ròu Shuǐ Jiǎo (Shandong Beef Dumplings) 山东牛肉水饺

▾

Dumplings are the New Year food of northern China; beef versions are made by Shandong's Hui communities.

Method

  1. Chop the thick scallions finely and toss them with the sesame oil first; the oil coats them so they stay juicy and do not weep.
  2. Mix the beef with the ginger, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, salt and pepper. Add the Sichuan pepper water a tablespoon at a time, stirring hard in one direction, until it is absorbed and the filling turns sticky and springy. This makes juicy dumplings. Fold in the vegetables and sesame oil.
  3. Put a heaping teaspoon of filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it in half, pinch the middle and pleat or press the edges to seal tightly.
  4. Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and add 15 to 20 dumplings, stirring gently so they do not stick. When it returns to a boil, add 250 ml (1 cup) of cold water; repeat twice. They are done when they float and look puffed, 6 to 8 minutes.
  5. Lift out with a slotted spoon and serve with black vinegar, garlic and chili oil. Shandong is famous for its dumplings, made thick-skinned and generously filled.

Common mistakes

  • Overmixing makes the filling tough.
  • Adding scallions early makes it watery.

Authentic tips

  • Add water gradually for a juicy filling.
  • Lamb is a common alternative.

Substitutions

  • Ground lamb is a traditional alternative.
  • Chopped celery lightens the filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; pan-fry leftovers.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; boil from frozen for 8 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black vinegar and chili oil dip
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
