Kerala Beef Fry (Beef Ularthiyathu)

▾

Surprise me with

A defining dish of Kerala's Syrian Christian and broader coastal cuisine, beef ularthiyathu slow-cooks beef with black pepper and coconut pieces until deeply dark and dry-roasted — among Kerala's most beloved beef preparations.

Method

  1. Mix the Beef chuck, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) cubes with the turmeric, chili, coriander, half the pepper, half the garam masala, the ginger, garlic, green chilies, vinegar and salt. Marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Pressure-cook the Beef chuck, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) cubes with 60 ml (1/4 cup) water for 4 to 5 whistles, or simmer it covered in a Dutch oven over low heat for 1.5 hours, until tender and the liquid has almost gone. It cooks in its own juices.
  3. Heat the coconut oil in a wide, heavy skillet or cast-iron pan over medium heat and fry the coconut slivers for 3 to 4 minutes until golden. Add the shallots and curry leaves and fry for 8 to 10 minutes until the shallots are deep brown.
  4. Add the cooked Beef chuck, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) cubes with any remaining juices and the fennel. Ularthiyathu means 'dry-roasted': fry it over medium-low heat, stirring and scraping every few minutes, for 20 to 30 minutes, until the meat turns dark brown and the masala clings to it.
  5. Stir in the remaining pepper and garam masala for the last 5 minutes and add another sprig of curry leaves.
  6. Serve hot with Kerala parotta, appam or rice. Kerala Christians and Muslims cook this for feasts.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the final dry-roasting stage, which is what develops this dish's characteristic dark color and concentrated flavor.
  • Using too little black pepper — this dish is meant to be assertively peppery, a defining Kerala flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The small pieces of fried coconut add texture and nutty sweetness that's central to this dish's character.
  • Beef takes longer than pork to become tender in this style — don't rush the initial covered cooking stage.

Substitutions

  • Dried coconut slivers fried in oil replace fresh coconut.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the cooking to 30 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
