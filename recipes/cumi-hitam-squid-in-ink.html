Cumi Hitam (Squid in Its Ink)

▾

Surprise me with

Cumi hitam, squid cooked in its own ink with garlic and chilies, is a Madurese and East Javanese coastal dish.

Method

  1. Clean the squid, setting the ink sacs aside in a small bowl; pierce them and mix the ink with the water. Cut the bodies into rings and the tentacles into pieces.
  2. Coarsely grind the shallots, garlic, chilies and ginger.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the spice paste with the lemongrass and bay leaves, stirring often, for 5 to 6 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried paste tastes sharp and grassy.
  4. Add the tomato and cook for 2 minutes until soft, then add the squid and stir-fry for 1 minute.
  5. Pour in the ink water, kecap manis, salt and sugar and simmer for 3 to 5 minutes, until the squid is just tender and the sauce is black and glossy. Squid is tender either very briefly cooked or braised for 30 minutes or more; anything in between is rubbery.
  6. Stir in the lime juice and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking too long makes the squid rubbery.
  • Cooking too short leaves it tough; either 2 minutes or 20.

Authentic tips

  • Ink sacs are sold in some fishmongers; sachets also work.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Ink sachets are sold at fishmongers.
  • Cooking time is 5 minutes or 25; avoid the middle.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
