Steamed Minced Pork with Salted Egg 咸蛋蒸肉饼

▾

Surprise me with

A beloved Cantonese home-style dish, this steamed pork patty is enriched with salted duck egg, whose rich, briny yolk melts into the meat as it steams — simple, fast, and deeply comforting.

Method

  1. If using pork shoulder, dice it and chop it with a cleaver until finely minced but not a paste; hand-chopped pork gives a looser, juicier patty than machine-ground. Use a fatty cut: lean pork turns dry and hard.
  2. Crack the salted eggs, separating them: keep the yolks whole and beat the whites. Crush the water chestnuts with the flat of a knife and chop them roughly so they stay crunchy.
  3. Mix the pork with the soy sauce, oyster sauce, wine, sugar, pepper, cornstarch, water and the salted egg whites, stirring in one direction for about 1 minute until sticky. Fold in the water chestnuts, ginger, green onion whites and sesame oil; do not overmix after this.
  4. Oil a rimmed heatproof plate and spread the pork over it in an even layer about 2 cm (3/4 in) thick, with a slight dip in the center. Halve the yolks and press them into the top, cut side up.
  5. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer and bring the water to a full boil. Steam the plate, covered, over high heat for 15 to 18 minutes, until the patty is firm, the yolks are set and orange oil pools on the plate; the juices should run clear.
  6. Scatter with the green onion greens and serve on the plate, spooning the juices over rice.

Common mistakes

  • Not mixing the pork vigorously enough, which leaves the finished patty crumbly instead of springy.
  • Steaming at too low heat, which extends cooking time unnecessarily and can dry the pork out.

Authentic tips

  • Mixing in one consistent direction develops the meat's protein structure for a bouncier, more cohesive texture.
  • Water chestnuts add a pleasant crunch that contrasts nicely with the tender pork — don't skip them.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken works as a leaner substitute, though the texture is slightly less rich.
  • No salted duck egg: a regular egg cracked on top in the last 5 minutes of steaming is a reasonable, if less traditional, substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain steamed rice
