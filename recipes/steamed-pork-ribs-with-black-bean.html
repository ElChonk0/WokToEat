Steamed Pork Ribs with Black Bean 豉汁蒸排骨

▾

Surprise me with

A dim sum staple, these small steamed rib pieces are marinated with fermented black beans and steamed quickly at high heat — a dish found on the trolley of nearly every Cantonese yum cha restaurant.

Method

  1. Massage the rib pieces with the rinsing cornstarch, then rinse under running water for 1 to 2 minutes, drain and squeeze dry in a thick towel. This removes bone fragments and surface blood so the sauce stays clean.
  2. Rinse the black beans for 15 seconds and drain. Soak the mandarin peel in warm water for 10 minutes, scrape off the white pith and mince it.
  3. Massage the ribs with the salt, sugar, soy sauce, oyster sauce, wine, garlic, ginger and mandarin peel for about 1 minute, then the black beans for 30 seconds. Finally add the sesame oil and marinade cornstarch and massage for 1 minute more; the cornstarch coating keeps the meat silky. Leave for 30 minutes.
  4. Spread the ribs in a single layer on a rimmed heatproof plate, larger pieces on top, and scatter with the chili and neutral oil.
  5. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer with boiling water just below it. Put in the plate, cover and steam over high heat for 18 to 20 minutes, topping up with boiling water if needed, until the meat is opaque at the bone and comes away easily.
  6. Scatter with green onion and serve on the plate with rice, spooning over the black bean juices.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the soaking step, which leaves the finished dish with a slightly muddy, less clean flavor.
  • Cutting the rib pieces too large, which extends steaming time and risks uneven cooking.

Authentic tips

  • Ask your butcher to chop the ribs into small (about 3cm) pieces — this is standard for dim sum-style preparations.
  • A thin layer of cornstarch in the marinade helps the sauce that pools on the plate turn glossy rather than watery.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly cut into chunks works if ribs aren't available; steam 10 minutes longer.
  • Without fermented black beans, use a little extra soy sauce plus a pinch of miso for umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month in the sauce; thaw overnight and re-steam.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear tofu and spinach soup
