Steamed Pork Ribs with Black Bean 豉汁蒸排骨

A dim sum staple, these small steamed rib pieces are marinated with fermented black beans and steamed quickly at high heat — a dish found on the trolley of nearly every Cantonese yum cha restaurant.

Method

  1. Soak rib pieces in water 20 minutes, changing water once, to remove excess blood; pat dry.
  2. Combine ribs with black beans, garlic, ginger, soy sauce, wine, sugar, and cornstarch; marinate 15 minutes.
  3. Spread the marinated ribs in a single layer on a heatproof plate.
  4. Steam over high heat, covered, 18–20 minutes until the pork is tender and cooked through.
  5. Garnish with sliced chili before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the soaking step, which leaves the finished dish with a slightly muddy, less clean flavor.
  • Cutting the rib pieces too large, which extends steaming time and risks uneven cooking.

Authentic tips

  • Ask your butcher to chop the ribs into small (about 3cm) pieces — this is standard for dim sum-style preparations.
  • A thin layer of cornstarch in the marinade helps the sauce that pools on the plate turn glossy rather than watery.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly cut into chunks works if ribs aren't available; steam 10 minutes longer.
  • Without fermented black beans, use a little extra soy sauce plus a pinch of miso for umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month in the sauce; thaw overnight and re-steam.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear tofu and spinach soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
