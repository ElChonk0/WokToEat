Satay Ayam (Malaysian Chicken Satay)

▾

Surprise me with

Satay is a hawker and street staple of Malaysia and Singapore; Malay satay is often sweeter and more fragrant with lemongrass and turmeric.

Method

  1. Blend the lemongrass, galangal, shallots and garlic with the turmeric, coriander, cumin, fennel, palm sugar, salt and oil into a paste. Toss the chicken in it and marinate for at least 2 hours, or overnight. Turmeric gives satay its golden color.
  2. Soak the skewers in water for at least 30 minutes so they do not burn. Thread 3 or 4 small pieces of chicken onto each, packing them tightly near the pointed end.
  3. For the peanut sauce, fry the chili paste and blended shallots and lemongrass in a little oil over medium-low heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until dark and the oil separates. Add the peanuts, tamarind, palm sugar and water and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring often, until thick. It should be sweet, nutty and gently spicy.
  4. Grill the skewers over hot charcoal, on a grill pan or under a broiler for 3 to 4 minutes per side, brushing them with oil (traditionally with a lemongrass brush) so they char at the edges and stay juicy, until cooked through.
  5. Serve hot with the peanut sauce, cucumber, red onion and ketupat (compressed rice cakes).

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking dries the chicken.
  • Dry skewers burn; soak them.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with ketupat (rice cakes).
  • Brush with coconut oil while grilling.

Substitutions

  • Beef or lamb take the same marinade.
  • Peanut butter plus chili replaces homemade peanut sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat under a broiler.

Freezing: Freeze raw skewers for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Ketupat and onion-cucumber
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Cendol
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
