Lor Mee (Braised Noodles in Thick Gravy)

▾

Surprise me with

A Hokkien-Chinese dish popular in both Malaysia and Singapore, lor mee bathes thick yellow noodles in a starch-thickened, five-spice-scented gravy along with braised pork and hard-boiled egg — a hearty hawker-stall favorite.

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil with the garlic, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, five-spice, soy sauces, oyster sauce, sugar and pepper. Add the pork belly, mushrooms and eggs and simmer, covered, for 30 to 40 minutes until the pork is tender.
  2. Lift out the pork, eggs and mushrooms. Slice the pork and mushrooms and halve the eggs. Strain out the whole spices and taste the stock: it should be savory and gently sweet.
  3. Bring the stock back to a simmer. Stir the slurry and pour it in gradually, stirring, until the gravy is thick and gloopy, almost like a thin custard; that thick, glossy gravy is what defines lor mee.
  4. With the gravy at a gentle simmer, not a boil, pour in the beaten egg in a thin stream while stirring slowly so it sets into fine ribbons.
  5. Blanch the noodles and bean sprouts briefly in boiling water and divide among bowls. Ladle the hot gravy over and top with the pork, egg and mushrooms.
  6. Serve with black vinegar, garlic paste, chili and fried shallots so each person seasons their own bowl.

Common mistakes

  • Making the gravy too thin, which misses the defining thick, clinging texture that gives lor mee its name.
  • Skipping the black vinegar on the side, which is an essential tangy counterpoint many diners add generously.

Authentic tips

  • The gravy should be notably thick — close to a light sauce rather than a thin broth — don't be shy with the cornstarch slurry.
  • Fried shallot adds crucial crunch and aroma; make a double batch since it keeps well and is useful elsewhere.

Substitutions

  • Braised chicken can replace pork for a lighter version.
  • No thick yellow noodles: fresh ramen-style noodles are a reasonable substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the gravy continues thickening as it sits.

Freezing: Not recommended — gravy texture changes upon freezing and reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black vinegar and crushed garlic
  • Soup: Not needed; the gravy noodles are the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Kopi
