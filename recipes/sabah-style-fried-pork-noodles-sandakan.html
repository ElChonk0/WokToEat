Sabah-Style Fried Pork Noodles (Sandakan)

Fried noodles with pork are a Hakka-influenced dish of Sabah on Borneo, where Sandakan is known for pork noodle stalls; this is a home interpretation of the style, with lard-scented egg noodles, sliced pork and greens in a dark soy sauce.

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the 15 ml soy sauce and cornstarch and rest for 10 minutes.
  2. Loosen the noodles and blanch for 30 seconds in boiling water, then drain well so they are dry.
  3. Heat half the lard in a wok over high heat, stir-fry the pork for 2 minutes until just cooked, and lift out.
  4. Add the remaining lard and the garlic for 15 seconds, then the choy sum and stir-fry for 1 minute. Push to the side, crack in the eggs and scramble until just set.
  5. Add the noodles, both soy sauces, the oyster sauce and white pepper and toss over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes until the noodles are glossy and slightly charred.
  6. Return the pork, add the bean sprouts, toss for 30 seconds, and serve topped with the fried shallots and the chilies in soy sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet noodles steam and clump instead of frying; drain and shake them dry.
  • Crowding the wok loses the char; cook in two batches for 4 servings.

Authentic tips

  • Lard gives the authentic fragrance, but oil works.
  • Keep the heat high and the noodles moving.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh replaces pork.
  • Fresh flat rice noodles replace egg noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled green chilies
  • Soup: Clear pork bone broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot kopi or teh
