Black Pepper Crab (Simplified Home Version)

▾

Surprise me with

One of Singapore's most famous seafood dishes, contrasting sweet crab meat with a bold, glossy black pepper sauce — traditionally made with whole crab, though this version works well with crab legs or claws for an easier weeknight version.

Method

  1. If using live crab, put it in the freezer for 30 minutes so it goes still, then clean it, cut the body into pieces and crack the claws and legs with the back of a cleaver so the sauce gets in.
  2. Toast the cracked pepper in a dry flat-bottom wok over medium heat for 1 minute until fragrant, then tip it out. Toasting wakes up the pepper's aroma, which is the whole point of the dish.
  3. Heat the oil and butter in the wok over medium-high heat and fry the garlic for 30 seconds, then add the pepper back for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the crab and toss to coat, then pour in the stock, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar. Cover and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, tossing once or twice, until the shells are bright red and the meat is opaque. Precooked crab needs only 2 to 3 minutes.
  5. Uncover and toss over high heat for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce reduces to a glossy coating. Scatter with scallion and serve at once, with bread or buns for the sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Using pre-ground fine pepper: the coarse, freshly cracked texture is essential to both flavor and name — grind whole peppercorns just before cooking.

Authentic tips

  • Cracking the shell before cooking, rather than after, lets the sauce penetrate the meat far better.

Substitutions

  • No whole crab: crab legs or claws (often easier to find and less expensive) work very well.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh, but keeps a day refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended — cooked crab texture suffers significantly when frozen and reheated.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or fried mantou
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
