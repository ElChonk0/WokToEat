Omu Katsu Curry Rice (Chicken Cutlet, Omelette and Curry) オムカツカレー

▾

Surprise me with

Omu katsu curry rice is a Japanese yoshoku combination of a fried cutlet, a soft omelette over rice and Japanese curry, popular in casual curry shops and food courts.

Method

  1. Fry the onion in the 30 ml oil over medium heat for 8 minutes until golden, add the carrot and potato, then the water, and simmer for 15 minutes until tender.
  2. Turn off the heat, stir in the curry roux and ketchup until dissolved, then simmer for 5 minutes to thicken.
  3. Season the chicken with the salt, dredge in flour, dip in the beaten egg and press in the panko.
  4. Heat the frying oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry the cutlets for 5 to 6 minutes per side until deep golden and cooked through, then drain and slice.
  5. Beat 3 eggs with a splash of the milk and cook each omelette in a quarter of the butter over medium heat until barely set; repeat to make two omelettes, folding each over.
  6. Plate the rice with an omelette on each, top with the sliced cutlet and ladle over the curry.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the omelette makes it dry; take it off while the center is still creamy.
  • Adding the roux over high heat can scorch it; turn the heat off first.

Authentic tips

  • Use skin-on thigh for juicier cutlets.
  • The curry improves if made a day ahead.

Substitutions

  • Pork loin replaces chicken for tonkatsu.
  • Store-bought katsu curry sauce replaces homemade.

Storage

Leftovers: The curry keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; fry cutlets fresh.

Freezing: The curry freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fukujinzuke pickles
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
