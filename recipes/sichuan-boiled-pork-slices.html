Sichuan Boiled Pork Slices 水煮肉片

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name suggesting a bland dish, shui zhu ("water boiled") is one of Sichuan's spiciest preparations — thin pork slices are poached, then drowned in hot chili oil poured tableside for maximum aromatic impact.

Method

  1. Toss the pork with a pinch of salt, the Shaoxing wine, egg white and cornstarch until each slice is coated in a thin, silky film. This velveting keeps the meat tender in the hot broth.
  2. Toast the dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns in a dry flat-bottom wok over low heat for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the chilies darken slightly and smell toasty but do not blacken. Cool and chop them finely with a knife; this is the ci ba la jiao topping.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in the wok over high heat and stir-fry the vegetables with a pinch of salt for 1 to 2 minutes until just wilted. Lay them in a deep serving bowl.
  4. Lower the heat to medium, add another 2 tablespoons of oil and fry the doubanjiang for 1 to 2 minutes until the oil turns red. Add the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds, then the stock and soy sauce, and bring to a boil.
  5. Lower the heat to a gentle simmer and add the pork a slice at a time, separating them. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, just until cooked. Pour the pork and broth over the vegetables.
  6. Scatter the chopped chili and peppercorn over the top with the green onion and garlic. Heat the remaining oil until it just begins to smoke and pour it over the spices; they sizzle and release their fragrance. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the pork slices in the broth, which toughens them — they only need a minute or two given how thin they are.
  • Pouring the oil before it's hot enough, which fails to properly bloom the chilies and peppercorns for maximum aroma.

Authentic tips

  • Slicing the pork paper-thin (partially frozen meat is easier to slice thinly) is essential to this dish's signature silky texture.
  • The final hot-oil pour is the dramatic centerpiece of this dish — do it just before serving, at the table if possible.

Substitutions

  • Beef gives the classic shui zhu niu rou version with the same technique.
  • Reduce the dried chilies for a considerably milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the pork toughens on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is soupy
