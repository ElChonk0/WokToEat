Jiàng Ròu (Beijing Sauce-Braised Pork) 酱肉

▾

Surprise me with

Jiang rou, pork braised in a master sauce and sliced cold, is a Beijing delicatessen staple, served with scallion and sweet bean sauce.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork whole in boiling water for 10 minutes and rinse. Some Beijing cooks rub it with salt and leave it overnight first, which firms the texture.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry the sweet bean sauce for 2 minutes, stirring, until it darkens and smells toasty. Frying the jiang (sauce) is what gives jiang rou its name and deep flavor.
  3. Add the rock sugar, soy sauces, Shaoxing wine, spices, ginger, green onion and water, bring to a boil, and add the pork.
  4. Simmer gently, covered, for 1.5 hours, turning occasionally, until a chopstick goes in easily. Turn off the heat.
  5. Let the pork cool in the liquid for at least 4 hours, or overnight, so the sauce soaks into the center.
  6. Slice thinly against the grain and serve cold with crushed garlic, a little braising liquid and sesame oil. Keep the strained liquid (lao tang) in the freezer for the next batch.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the pork.
  • Slicing while warm shreds the meat; chill it first.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the sauce as a master braise.
  • Serve with steamed buns and sweet bean sauce.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly gives a richer version; trim any excess fat.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Thin pancakes or steamed buns
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
