Jiàng Ròu (Beijing Sauce-Braised Pork) 酱肉

▾

Surprise me with

Jiang rou, pork braised in a master sauce and sliced cold, is a Beijing delicatessen staple, served with scallion and sweet bean sauce.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 90 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 45 g3 tbspSweet bean sauce
  • 8 g3 tspStar anise, cinnamon, bay
  • 60 g4 tbspGinger and scallion

Method

  1. Cut the pork into 8 cm chunks, cover with cold water, boil 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Return to the pot with the soy sauce, sweet bean sauce, sugar, spices, ginger, scallion and the water.
  3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a bare simmer, cover and cook 80 minutes, until a chopstick slides in easily.
  4. Cool in the sauce, then chill 1 hour.
  5. Slice thinly and serve with scallion and a spoonful of the sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the pork.
  • Slicing while warm shreds the meat; chill it first.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the sauce as a master braise.
  • Serve with steamed buns and sweet bean sauce.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly gives a richer version; trim any excess fat.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Thin pancakes or steamed buns
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
