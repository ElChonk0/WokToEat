Jiāo Yán Xuě Yú (Salt-and-Pepper Cod) 椒鹽鱈魚

▾

Surprise me with

Salt-and-pepper frying, crisp pieces tossed with white pepper, salt and fried garlic, is a Taiwanese night-market style also found in Cantonese cooking.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozIcelandic cod fillet, in 3 cm pieces
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 70 g1/2 cupSweet potato starch or cornstarch
  • 600 ml2 1/2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 4 cloves4 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 30 g1 ozScallions, sliced
  • 15 g1 tbspRed chilies, minced
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, to finish
  • 2 g3/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 1 g1/4 tspFive-spice powder

Method

  1. Toss the cod with the salt and wine, rest 10 minutes, pat dry and coat thickly in the starch.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok to 180°C (350°F) and fry the fish in two batches for 3 minutes until golden and crisp; drain on a rack.
  3. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil, fry the garlic, scallions and chilies over medium heat for 30 seconds until fragrant and golden.
  4. Return the fish, add the finishing salt, white pepper and five-spice powder, and toss for 20 seconds.
  5. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet.

Common mistakes

  • Wet fish makes the coating gummy; pat it dry before starching.
  • Overcrowded frying makes the coating soft.

Authentic tips

  • Use a thermometer on electric and induction stoves.
  • Press the starch on firmly for a crisp shell.

Substitutions

  • Haddock replaces the cod.
  • Squid or shrimp take the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; re-crisp in a hot oven for 5 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold oolong tea
