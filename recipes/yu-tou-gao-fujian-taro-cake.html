Yù Tou Gāo (Fujian Taro Cake) 芋头糕

▾

Surprise me with

Taro cake, steamed rice flour and taro cut into slices and pan-fried, is a Fujian and Minnan dim sum and New Year snack.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater
  • 30 g1 ozFried shallots and green onion, for topping

Method

  1. Fry the shallots in the oil until golden, then the shiitake and taro for 5 minutes. Season with soy sauce, five-spice, pepper, salt and sugar.
  2. Whisk the rice flour with the water until smooth and pour it into the pan. Stir over low heat for 3 to 5 minutes until it thickens into a heavy paste. Pre-cooking the batter keeps the taro evenly suspended.
  3. Spread the mixture in an oiled square pan and smooth the top.
  4. Steam over boiling water, covered, for 45 to 50 minutes, until a chopstick comes out clean.
  5. Top with fried shallots and green onion. Cool completely before slicing; it is often pan-fried in slices until crisp, for Lunar New Year.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting while warm makes messy, sticky slices; chill first.
  • Undercooking the batter leaves it raw tasting.

Authentic tips

  • Chill overnight for clean slices.
  • Serve with sweet chili or soy sauce.

Substitutions

  • Daikon makes a similar savory cake (lo bak go).
  • Add dried shrimp for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; pan-fry slices.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Congee
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
