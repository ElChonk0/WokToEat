Yù Tou Gāo (Fujian Taro Cake) 芋头糕

▾

Surprise me with

Taro cake, steamed rice flour and taro cut into slices and pan-fried, is a Fujian and Minnan dim sum and New Year snack.

  • Cook 45 min

  • 180 g1.5 cupsRice flour
  • 30 g1 ozDried shiitake, diced
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 20 g2 tbspFried shallots
  • 6 g1 tspSalt
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Peel the taro and dice into 1 cm cubes. Soak and slice the mushrooms.
  2. Fry the shallots in the oil until crisp, then the mushrooms and taro for 3 minutes; add the soy sauce and salt.
  3. Whisk the rice flour with the water and pour into the pan, stirring constantly over medium heat for 3-5 minutes until the batter thickens to a paste.
  4. Pour into an oiled 20 cm pan, smooth the top and steam over high heat for 40 minutes until a toothpick comes out clean.
  5. Cool completely (preferably chilled), slice and pan-fry the slices in a little oil until crisp on both sides.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting while warm makes messy, sticky slices; chill first.
  • Undercooking the batter leaves it raw tasting.

Authentic tips

  • Chill overnight for clean slices.
  • Serve with sweet chili or soy sauce.

Substitutions

  • Daikon makes a similar savory cake (lo bak go).
  • Add dried shrimp for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; pan-fry slices.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Congee
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
