Sichuan Chicken in Chili Oil (口水鸡-Style)

Adapted from the famous "mouth-watering chicken" (kou shui ji), this dish poaches chicken until tender, then drenches it in a garlicky, numbing chili oil sauce designed to make your mouth water at the sight of it.

Method

  1. Poach chicken thighs gently in water with ginger and green onion, covered, 18–20 minutes until cooked through.
  2. Transfer chicken to an ice bath to cool and firm the skin, then debone and slice into bite-size pieces.
  3. Whisk together garlic, chili oil, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, and Sichuan peppercorn oil for the sauce.
  4. Arrange sliced chicken on a plate and pour the sauce generously over top.
  5. Garnish with crushed peanuts and cilantro; serve at room temperature.

Common mistakes

  • Serving the chicken hot straight from poaching, when this dish is meant to be enjoyed cool or at room temperature.
  • Using thin, low-quality chili oil without sediment, which lacks the depth the sauce needs.

Authentic tips

  • Good chili oil with plenty of toasted chili sediment (not just the oil) is the single most important ingredient here.
  • The ice bath step keeps the chicken skin pleasantly firm rather than flabby.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs work fine and are easier to slice, though bone-in gives more flavor to the poaching liquid.
  • No Sichuan peppercorn oil: a light dusting of ground toasted Sichuan peppercorn works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; serve cold, it only improves as flavors meld.

Freezing: Not recommended — best fresh.
