Jīng Dū Pái Gǔ (Peking-Style Pork Chops) 京都排骨

Despite the name, "Peking" pork chops are a Cantonese restaurant dish: fried pork pieces glazed in a sweet-sour sauce of Chinkiang vinegar, ketchup and Worcestershire sauce.

Method

  1. Pound the pork lightly, then marinate it with the soy sauce, wine, sugar, baking soda and egg for 30 minutes. Toss with the cornstarch to coat.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 175 C (350 F) and fry the pork in batches for 3 to 4 minutes until golden and cooked through. Drain on a rack, then refry all of it for 1 minute at 190 C (375 F).
  3. Stir the ketchup, vinegar, Worcestershire sauce, sugar and water together.
  4. Pour off the oil, leaving a tablespoon in the wok. Simmer the sauce in it over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes until thick and bubbling.
  5. Add the pork and toss quickly to coat, sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too thin a sauce slides off; reduce it until it bubbles thickly before the pork goes in.
  • Over-tossing softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Spare ribs cut into short pieces are the other classic cut.
  • Worcestershire sauce is a Hong Kong pantry staple and gives the fruity depth.

Substitutions

  • Rice vinegar plus a teaspoon of dark soy replace black vinegar.
  • Air fryer: cook the coated pork at 200 C (400 F) for 12 minutes, shaking twice.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Almond tofu
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
