Jīng Dū Pái Gǔ (Peking-Style Pork Chops) 京都排骨

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, "Peking" pork chops are a Cantonese restaurant dish: fried pork pieces glazed in a sweet-sour sauce of Chinkiang vinegar, ketchup and Worcestershire sauce.

Method

  1. Pound the pork slices across the grain with the back of a cleaver until slightly thinner and evenly flat. Knead in the marinade cornstarch, oyster sauce, wine, water, pepper and salt by hand until absorbed, and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Whisk the flour, batter cornstarch, baking powder and salt, then add the water a little at a time until smooth and lump-free, about the thickness of pancake batter. Mix the sauce ingredients, taste them, and mix the slurry cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  3. Stir the batter oil into the batter and coat the pork lightly. Heat the frying oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 165 to 175 C (330 to 350 F).
  4. Fry the pork in batches for 1.5 to 2 minutes, until set and pale golden, keeping the oil in that range and skimming out loose bits. Drain on a rack.
  5. Raise the oil to 190 C (370 F) and fry all the pieces again for 20 to 30 seconds, until deep golden and crisp. Drain.
  6. Wipe out the wok, pour in the sauce and bring to a boil over medium heat, then simmer 1 to 2 minutes. Lower the heat and add the slurry a little at a time until it is thick and glossy enough to coat a spoon; you may not need it all.
  7. Turn off the heat, toss the pork through the sauce until every piece is coated, plate and sprinkle with sesame seeds. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too thin a sauce slides off; reduce it until it bubbles thickly before the pork goes in.
  • Over-tossing softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Spare ribs cut into short pieces are the other classic cut.
  • Worcestershire sauce is a Hong Kong pantry staple and gives the fruity depth.

Substitutions

  • Rice vinegar plus a teaspoon of dark soy replace black vinegar.
  • Air fryer: cook the coated pork at 200 C (400 F) for 12 minutes, shaking twice.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Almond tofu
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
