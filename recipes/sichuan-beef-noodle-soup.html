Sichuan Beef Noodle Soup 川味牛肉面

▾

Surprise me with

A spicier, more numbing cousin of Taiwan's beef noodle soup, the Sichuan version builds its broth on doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorn for a fiery, tongue-tingling bowl found throughout Chengdu's noodle shops.

Method

  1. Blanch the beef in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the doubanjiang for 2 minutes until the oil turns red. Add the ginger, garlic, green onion, spices, peppercorns and chilies and fry for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the beef and Shaoxing wine and toss to coat, then add the soy sauce, sugar and water. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 2 hours, until the beef is very tender.
  4. Fish out the spices and green onion and taste the broth; it should be rich, spicy and savory.
  5. Cook the noodles according to the packet, blanching the bok choy in the same water for the last minute. Drain and divide among bowls.
  6. Ladle over the beef and broth and top with pickled mustard greens, cilantro and chili oil.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the initial blanch-and-rinse of the beef, which leaves the broth cloudier and slightly muddier.
  • Rushing the braise — beef shank and short rib need real time to break down properly.

Authentic tips

  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half.
  • Pickled mustard greens add a sour, crunchy contrast that cuts through the richness of the broth.

Substitutions

  • Brisket can replace shank or short rib, with a similar braising time.
  • For a milder version, reduce the doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorn by half.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth and beef improve overnight; store noodles separately.

Freezing: The braised beef and broth freeze excellently for up to 3 months; cook fresh noodles when serving.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
