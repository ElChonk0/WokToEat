Tteokbokki (Korean Spicy Rice Cakes with Fish Cake) 떡볶이

▾

Surprise me with

Tteokbokki is a Korean street food of chewy rice cakes simmered in a sweet and spicy gochujang sauce with fish cake, a staple of snack stalls and school-street vendors.

Method

  1. Soak the rice cakes in cold water for about 20 minutes, longer if they were refrigerated, so they soften evenly. Cut the fish cake, cabbage and green onion into 5 cm (2 in) pieces.
  2. Simmer the anchovies and kelp in the water over medium-high heat for 10 minutes, then remove them. Anchovy broth gives street-stall depth; water works but tastes flatter.
  3. Stir the gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar and garlic into the broth until dissolved and bring to a boil.
  4. Add the rice cakes and boil for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often so they do not stick to the bottom, until they are very soft and the sauce has thickened. Add a splash of water if it reduces too fast.
  5. Add the fish cake, cabbage and eggs and boil for 4 to 6 minutes more, then add the green onion for the last 2 minutes. The sauce should be thick and glossy, coating each rice cake.
  6. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve hot.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Walking away lets the rice cakes stick to the pan; stir often.
  • Boiling too long makes the rice cakes mushy.

Authentic tips

  • A spoonful of corn syrup gives the sauce its glossy finish.
  • Add a slice of cheese on top for a popular variation.

Substitutions

  • Thin sausage or ramen noodles (rabokki) add bulk.
  • Anchovy stock replaces with vegetable stock for a lighter version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; the rice cakes harden, so add water when reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi and fried dumplings
  • Soup: Fish cake soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
