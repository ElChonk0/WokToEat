Zhū Jiǎo Jiāng (Pork Knuckle in Ginger and Black Vinegar) 猪脚姜

▾

Surprise me with

Pork trotters and eggs stewed with old ginger in sweet black vinegar is the essential Cantonese confinement dish for new mothers, also shared with visitors to celebrate a birth.

  • 1.5 kg3.3 lbPork knuckle or trotters, chopped into pieces
  • 500 g1.1 lbOld ginger, smashed and sliced 6 mm (1/4 in) thick
  • 15 ml1 tbspSesame oil (optional)
  • 1.2 L5 cupsSweetened black vinegar (Chinese)
  • 90 ml6 tbspChinkiang black vinegar
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar or brown sugar
  • 4 g1 tspSalt
  • 66Eggs

Method

  1. Put the pork in a pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil uncovered for 2 to 3 minutes, then lift the pieces into a bowl of cold water and scrub them, scraping off any hairs. Rinse and drain. This parboil removes the scum that would otherwise make the stew cloudy and gamey.
  2. Smash the ginger lightly and slice it. Dry-fry it in a clean nonreactive wok or Dutch oven over medium heat for about 5 minutes, stirring, until it darkens, the edges char slightly and it smells strongly of ginger. Add the sesame oil for the last minute if using. Use enamel, stainless steel or clay; vinegar reacts with bare iron.
  3. Pour in the sweetened vinegar and the Chinkiang vinegar over medium heat, then stir in the sugar and salt. Bring to a simmer, cover and cook on low for 5 to 7 minutes, then taste: it should be sweet, sour and gingery in balance. Adjust with sugar or Chinkiang vinegar.
  4. Add the pork with the bony pieces at the bottom and top up with sweetened vinegar so everything is covered. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover and cook on low for 1 hour.
  5. Meanwhile, put the eggs in cold water, bring to a boil and cook 5 minutes, then turn off the heat for 5 minutes and plunge them into cold water. Peel them.
  6. Add the eggs to the pot and simmer for 1 hour more, until the knuckle skin is soft and sticky and the eggs are dark. Keep the heat low throughout; a fast boil reduces the vinegar too much.
  7. The stew is best made a day or more ahead: the flavor deepens as it rests. Reheat gently and serve the pork, ginger and eggs in bowls with some of the sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Bare metal pots react with the vinegar; use enamel, stainless or a clay pot.
  • Young ginger lacks the heat this dish needs; use mature, fibrous ginger.

Authentic tips

  • The vinegar stock is often kept and reheated over days, adding more eggs.
  • Pat Chun is the classic Hong Kong sweet vinegar brand for this dish.

Substitutions

  • Pork hocks or ham hocks work; trim extra fat.
  • A pressure cooker cooks the pork in 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps a week in the refrigerator and improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the eggs.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Dried scallop and chicken soup
  • Dessert: Red bean soup
  • Beverage: Warm water
