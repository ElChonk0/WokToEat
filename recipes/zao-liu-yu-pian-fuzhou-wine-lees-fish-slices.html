Zāo Liū Yú Piàn (Fuzhou Wine-Lees Fish Slices) 糟溜鱼片

▾

Surprise me with

Zao liu, a light soup-sauce flavored with fermented rice lees, is a classic Fuzhou dish; the lees give a faint wine fragrance to quickly poached fish.

Method

  1. Mix the fish slices with the egg white, cornstarch and 1 g of the salt and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Blanch the fish in barely simmering water for 30 to 40 seconds until it turns white; drain gently.
  3. Heat the oil in a wok over medium heat, add the lees and stir for 30 seconds, then add the stock, wood ear, bamboo shoots, wine, sugar, the remaining salt and white pepper and simmer 3 minutes.
  4. Thicken with the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water until lightly glossy.
  5. Slide in the fish, fold gently for 20 seconds, scatter with the scallions and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling water at the blanching step breaks the delicate slices.
  • Too much lees makes the sauce bitter and gritty.

Authentic tips

  • Strain the lees through a sieve if the grains bother you.
  • Dry sherry plus a pinch of sugar works if you cannot find lees.

Substitutions

  • Haddock or cod loin replaces the cod.
  • Sweet fermented rice (jiu niang) replaces the lees.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
