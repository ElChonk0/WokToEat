Yú Xiāng Ròu Sī (Fish-Fragrant Pork) 鱼香肉丝

▾

Surprise me with

Despite containing no fish, "fish-fragrant" describes a classic Sichuan flavor combination of sweet, sour, salty, and spicy originally used for cooking fish, applied here to shredded pork with wood ear mushroom and bamboo shoot.

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the salt, Shaoxing wine and marinade cornstarch. Mix the vinegar, sugar, soy sauce, sauce cornstarch and water in a bowl.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the pork for 1 to 2 minutes, separating the shreds, until pale. Lift it out.
  3. Add the remaining oil, lower the heat to medium and fry the chili paste for 30 seconds until the oil turns red, then the garlic and ginger for 20 seconds.
  4. Raise the heat, add the wood ear and bamboo shoots and stir-fry for 1 minute.
  5. Return the pork, stir the sauce and pour it in. Toss for 20 seconds until thick and glossy, add the green onion and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the marination step, which leaves the pork tougher and less silky in the finished dish.
  • Using too little vinegar, since the balance of sweet-sour-salty-spicy is the entire point of this flavor profile.

Authentic tips

  • The yu xiang sauce (garlic, ginger, doubanjiang, vinegar, sugar) is a foundational Sichuan flavor combination worth mastering — it appears in many other dishes.
  • Shredding all ingredients to a similar size ensures even cooking and a pleasant texture.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or beef can be prepared the same way with this classic sauce.
  • No wood ear mushroom: shiitake mushroom, shredded, works as a substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the vegetables suffers.

  • Dessert: Fresh orange
