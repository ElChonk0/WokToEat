Smoked Pork with Dried Tofu 烟熏肉炒香干

▾

Surprise me with

Smoked and cured meats are central to Hunan cuisine, a legacy of preserving pork through the region's humid mountain winters; this dish pairs smoky pork with firm dried tofu for texture contrast.

Method

  1. Toss pork slices with smoked paprika and let sit 10 minutes.
  2. Sear the pork in a hot, dry pan without oil until lightly charred at the edges and the fat renders, 4–5 minutes; remove.
  3. Add garlic and green chili to the rendered fat, stir-frying until fragrant.
  4. Add dried tofu and stir-fry 2–3 minutes until lightly browned.
  5. Return pork to the pan with soy sauce and wine; toss over high heat until combined.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry-sear step, which is what stands in for the traditional smoking process and adds depth.
  • Overcrowding the pan, which steams the pork instead of properly rendering and charring it.

Authentic tips

  • A well-seasoned cast-iron pan gets closest to the smoky char this dish approximates.
  • Look for pressed dried tofu (dou gan), which holds its shape and texture well in stir-fries.

Substitutions

  • Smoked bacon replaces pork belly for a smokier shortcut.
  • Firm regular tofu works if dried tofu isn't available, though it is softer.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Sliced orange
  • Beverage: Hot black tea
