Kaya Toast with Soft-Boiled Eggs

▾

Surprise me with

Kaya toast, thin toasted bread with coconut-and-egg jam and butter, served with soft-boiled eggs and strong coffee, is the classic Singapore and Malaysian breakfast set.

Method

  1. Take the eggs out of the refrigerator 10 minutes ahead so they do not crack. Bring a pot of water to a rolling boil, turn off the heat, lower the eggs in gently, cover and leave for 7 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, toast the bread until golden and crisp, then trim the crusts if you like and split each slice in half horizontally for the thin, kopitiam-style toast.
  3. Spread a generous layer of kaya on the warm toast and lay thin slices of cold butter on top, then sandwich and cut into strips. Cold butter half-melts against the warm toast, which is the point.
  4. Crack the eggs into small bowls: the whites should be just set and custardy, the yolks runny. If the whites are still clear, return the eggs to the hot water for another minute.
  5. Season the eggs with a dash of soy sauce and white pepper, stir and dip the toast into them. Serve with strong kopi or teh.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the eggs sets the yolk; keep to 6 minutes off the heat.
  • Cold butter on warm toast melts and soaks in; use it straight from the refrigerator.

Authentic tips

  • Run the eggs under cold water for 20 seconds after cooking to stop the cooking.
  • Make the kaya at home by whisking coconut milk, eggs, sugar and pandan over low heat for 30 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Peanut butter or condensed milk replaces kaya.
  • Brioche or milk bread replaces white bread.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the toast softens quickly.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: None; the set is complete
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Not needed; the kaya is sweet
  • Beverage: Hot kopi or teh with evaporated milk
