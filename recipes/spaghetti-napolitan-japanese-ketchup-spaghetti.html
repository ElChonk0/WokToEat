Spaghetti Napolitan (Japanese Ketchup Spaghetti) ナポリタン

▾

Spaghetti Napolitan is a Japanese yoshoku dish of spaghetti stir-fried with ketchup, sausage and vegetables, created in the 1940s and still served in kissaten cafes.

Method

  1. Cook the spaghetti in salted water for 1 to 2 minutes longer than al dente, then drain and toss with a little oil. Napolitan is traditionally made with soft noodles, cooked ahead in kissaten cafes, so they soak up the sauce.
  2. Melt half the butter with the oil in a large skillet over medium heat and cook the onion for 3 minutes, then the sausages for 2 minutes until browned, then the mushrooms and pepper for 2 minutes.
  3. Push everything to the side, add the ketchup to the empty part of the pan and fry it for 1 to 2 minutes until it darkens and thickens. Cooking the ketchup takes away its raw acidity and deepens its sweetness.
  4. Add the spaghetti, Worcestershire, sugar, salt and pepper and toss over medium-high heat for 2 to 3 minutes until every strand is coated and glossy.
  5. Stir in the remaining butter and serve with grated Parmesan and Tabasco.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the ketchup to cold noodles gives a raw tomato taste; cook it in the pan first.
  • Under-cooking the pasta leaves it hard because it keeps cooking in the pan.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking the noodles a little past al dente and letting them rest gives the soft Japanese texture.
  • A fried egg on top is common.

Substitutions

  • Bacon replaces sausage.
  • Tomato sauce and a spoon of sugar replace ketchup.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan with a splash of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Simple green salad
  • Soup: Corn potage
  • Dessert: Purin (caramel pudding)
  • Beverage: Iced melon soda
