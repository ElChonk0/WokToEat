Chicken Inasal

▾

Surprise me with

Hailing from Bacolod, chicken inasal marinates chicken in calamansi, lemongrass, and annatto oil before grilling over charcoal — the annatto oil gives it a signature orange hue and is brushed on throughout cooking.

Method

  1. Mix the calamansi, vinegar, lemongrass, garlic, ginger, brown sugar, salt and pepper. Slash the chicken to the bone, toss it in the marinade and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight. Unlike most Filipino barbecue, inasal uses no soy sauce.
  2. Make the annatto oil: warm the annatto seeds in the oil over low heat for 5 minutes until the oil is deep orange, then strain. Mix half of it with the melted butter for basting.
  3. Skewer the chicken pieces flat. Grill over medium charcoal or a medium grill for 30 to 40 minutes, turning every 5 minutes and basting with the annatto butter each time, until the skin is golden-orange and charred in spots and the meat reaches 74 C (165 F).
  4. Medium heat is essential; the sugar and butter burn if the fire is too hot.
  5. Serve with rice drizzled with the remaining annatto oil and a dip of soy sauce, calamansi and chilies. A specialty of Bacolod.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the annatto oil, which is what gives inasal its signature color and subtle earthy flavor — without it, it's just a lemongrass-marinated chicken.
  • Basting too infrequently, which misses out on building the dish's characteristic glossy, flavorful crust.

Authentic tips

  • Annatto seeds (achuete) are inexpensive and widely available at Filipino or Latin grocers; a little goes a long way.
  • Frequent basting with the annatto oil while grilling is what builds the dish's signature color and flavor.

Substitutions

  • No calamansi: a mix of lime and a touch of orange juice approximates its unique tart-sweet flavor.
  • No annatto: a small amount of paprika in the basting oil gives a similar (if less authentic) color.
  • No grill: roast on a wire rack over a sheet pan at 230°C (450°F) for 25 to 30 minutes, basting with the annatto oil every 10 minutes, then broil 2 to 3 minutes for char; boneless thighs take about 18 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.
