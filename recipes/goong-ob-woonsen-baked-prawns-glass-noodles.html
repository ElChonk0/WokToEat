Goong Ob Woonsen (Baked Prawns with Glass Noodles)

▾

Surprise me with

Goong ob woonsen, prawns baked on glass noodles with ginger and pepper, is a Chinese-Thai restaurant dish, often cooked in a clay pot.

Method

  1. Soak the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes, drain and cut into 15 cm (6 in) lengths. Mix the soy sauces, oyster sauce, sesame oil, sugar and stock and toss the noodles in it to soak up the flavor.
  2. Melt the butter in a Dutch oven or clay pot over medium heat, line the bottom with the pork slices, and scatter the ginger, garlic and cilantro root paste over them. Fry for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant. The pork fat bastes the noodles from below.
  3. Pile the noodles with all their liquid on top and lay the prawns over them.
  4. Cover tightly and cook over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until the prawns are pink and the noodles have absorbed the sauce. Listen for a gentle sizzle; if it smells of scorching, lower the heat.
  5. Scatter the green onion and cilantro over, cover for 1 minute more, then toss and serve from the pot with seafood dipping sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the prawns rubbery.
  • Dry noodles absorb all the sauce; add enough liquid.

Authentic tips

  • Serve in the pot.
  • Add cilantro root to the sauce for authentic flavor.

Substitutions

  • Large shrimp replace prawns.
  • A Dutch oven replaces the clay pot on any stove, induction included.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
