Chicken Tikka

▾

Surprise me with

Chicken tikka — boneless chunks marinated in yogurt and spice, traditionally cooked in a tandoor — predates and inspired the later invention of tikka masala, and remains a staple grilled appetizer across South Asia.

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the lemon juice, salt and first chili powder and leave for 20 minutes. This first marinade seasons the meat through.
  2. Heat the mustard oil until it just smokes, then cool it slightly; this mellows its pungency. Mix it with the hung yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, remaining chili, garam masala, cumin, turmeric and kasuri methi. Hung yogurt is thick enough to cling and char.
  3. Coat the chicken in the yogurt marinade and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight.
  4. Thread the chicken onto skewers, alternating with onion and pepper. Grill over hot charcoal or under a broiler on high, 10 cm (4 in) from the heat, for 12 to 15 minutes, turning every few minutes.
  5. Baste with melted butter in the last 2 minutes and cook until charred at the edges and cooked through (74 C / 165 F).
  6. Sprinkle with chaat masala and serve with lemon, sliced onion and mint chutney.

Common mistakes

  • Marinating too briefly, which doesn't give the yogurt and spices enough time to tenderize and flavor the meat.
  • Cooking over too gentle heat, which misses the light char that defines tandoor-style cooking.

Authentic tips

  • The acidity and enzymes in yogurt genuinely tenderize the chicken over time — longer marinating (up to 24 hours) gives noticeably better results.
  • A very hot grill or broiler, close to the heat source for the last minute, best mimics tandoor-style charring.

Substitutions

  • No grill: a hot cast-iron pan or oven broiler both work as substitutes.
  • Add a few drops of red or orange food coloring to the marinade for a more restaurant-style vivid color, if desired.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; great chopped into a wrap or salad.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.
