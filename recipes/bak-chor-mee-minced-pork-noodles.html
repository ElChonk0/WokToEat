Bak Chor Mee (Singapore Minced Pork Noodles)

▾

Surprise me with

Bak chor mee, noodles dressed in black vinegar, chili and minced pork, is a Singapore hawker dish of Teochew origin.

Method

  1. Soak the mushrooms in 500 ml (2 cups) of hot water for 30 minutes, then simmer them in their soaking water with the dark soy sauce and sugar, covered, for 45 minutes to 1 hour until soft and glossy. Keep the braising liquid; it seasons the noodles. Mix the minced and sliced pork with the soy sauce and cornstarch.
  2. Simmer the stock with the anchovies for 30 minutes, then strain. This anchovy-rich broth is what gives the dish its savory depth.
  3. In each serving bowl, mix 1 tablespoon of black vinegar, a teaspoon of lard, some chili paste, a teaspoon of light soy, a little fish sauce and 2 tablespoons each of mushroom liquid and broth.
  4. Bring the broth to a boil and poach the pork, liver and minced pork in batches for 1 to 2 minutes until just cooked, lifting them out with a strainer.
  5. Cook the noodles in a strainer basket in boiling water for about 45 seconds, dip them in cold water, then back in the hot water for a few seconds. Toss them straight into the sauce in each bowl. This quick double dip keeps them springy.
  6. Top with the pork, liver, sliced mushrooms, crisp lard, fried shallots and green onion, and serve with a small bowl of the hot broth on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles go limp.
  • Drain the noodles well or the sauce dilutes.

Authentic tips

  • Mee pok (flat noodles) is traditional.
  • Lard gives the authentic flavor.

Substitutions

  • Chicken replaces pork for a halal-friendly version.
  • Spaghetti replaces mee pok.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Ais kacang
