Beef Chow Fun 干炒牛河

▾

Surprise me with

A Cantonese wok-hei showcase, beef chow fun stir-fries wide rice noodles with beef and bean sprouts over ferociously high heat — the hallmark of a great version is the smoky "breath of the wok" flavor.

Method

  1. Rinse the sliced beef in a bowl of water, drain and squeeze it dry. Mix it with the baking soda, cornstarch, marinade oyster sauce and soy sauce, sugar, wine, pepper and water until absorbed, then stir in the marinade oil and leave for 15 minutes. The baking soda is what makes takeout beef so tender. Mix the noodle soy sauces and oyster sauce in a cup.
  2. Peel the noodles apart into single strands by hand. If they are cold and stiff, microwave them covered for 1 minute first; cold noodles crack and break in the wok.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat until the oil ripples, using 2 tablespoons. Spread the beef out and sear for 40 to 60 seconds, turning once, until browned but still slightly pink. Lift it out and wipe the wok clean.
  4. Add the remaining oil and fry the onion and green onion whites for 20 to 30 seconds until fragrant. Add the noodles and spread them out. Cook for about 2 minutes, turning them gently with a spatula only every 30 seconds or so, until they char in spots and smell toasty; stirring too much breaks them up.
  5. Pour the sauce over and toss gently with a lifting motion for 1 minute until evenly brown. Add the bean sprouts and green onion greens for 30 to 45 seconds, then return the beef for 1 minute.
  6. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking over insufficient heat, which fails to achieve the smoky "wok hei" flavor that defines this dish.
  • Stirring the noodles too vigorously, which breaks the delicate wide rice noodles apart.

Authentic tips

  • The wok needs to be genuinely very hot — a home stove's maximum heat, used for the shortest possible cooking time.
  • Fresh rice noodles (not dried) are essential; separate them gently by hand before cooking if they're stuck together.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or pork can replace beef with the same high-heat technique.
  • No fresh rice noodles: soak dried flat rice noodles until pliable, though texture differs slightly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; noodles firm up but reheat reasonably in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture doesn't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
