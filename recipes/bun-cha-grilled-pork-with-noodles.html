Bun Cha (Grilled Pork with Noodles)

A Hanoi specialty famously enjoyed by Barack Obama and Anthony Bourdain together in 2016, bun cha grills seasoned pork patties and slices, served in a sweet-tangy fish sauce broth alongside vermicelli and herbs.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Combine ground pork with half the shallot, garlic, fish sauce, and sugar; form into small patties.
  2. Marinate pork belly slices in the remaining shallot, garlic, fish sauce, and sugar; marinate both, patties and belly, at least 1 hour.
  3. Grill both the patties and belly slices over medium heat, turning occasionally, 10–12 minutes until charred and cooked through.
  4. Whisk fish sauce, sugar, lime juice, and warm water to make the dipping broth; add the grilled pork to the warm broth just before serving.
  5. Cook rice vermicelli according to package directions; drain.
  6. Serve the pork and broth alongside vermicelli, fresh herbs, and pickled carrot and daikon for dipping and assembling.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the pickled vegetables in the dipping broth, which are a key part of the dish's sweet-tangy balance.
  • Under-charring the pork, which misses the smoky depth that defines good bun cha.

Authentic tips

  • Adding the hot grilled pork directly to the dipping broth just before serving lets it perfume the whole bowl.
  • This dish is meant to be assembled at the table — set out all components separately.

Substitutions

  • No grill: a hot broiler or grill pan both work as substitutes.
  • Ground chicken can replace pork for the patties, though pork is more traditional.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store components separately.

Freezing: The pork patties freeze well, raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fresh herbs and nuoc cham are included
  • Soup: Fried crab spring rolls
  • Dessert: Che
  • Beverage: Iced tea
