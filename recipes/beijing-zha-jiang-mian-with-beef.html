Beijing Zha Jiang Mian with Beef 牛肉炸酱面

Zha jiang mian ("fried sauce noodles") tops wheat noodles with a rich, savory fermented soybean paste sauce studded with diced meat and vegetables — a Beijing home-cooking staple, here made with beef instead of the more traditional pork.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 30 ml2 tbspYellow bean sauce

  • 10 g2 tspGinger, minced

  • 400 g14 ozWheat noodles
  • 10 g2 tspSugar

Method

  1. Stir-fry ground beef in oil over medium-high heat until browned.
  2. Add garlic and ginger, stirring briefly.
  3. Stir in sweet bean sauce, yellow bean sauce, and sugar; simmer 8–10 minutes, adding a splash of water if too thick, until rich and fragrant.
  4. Cook noodles according to package directions; drain.
  5. Top noodles with the beef sauce and julienned cucumber; toss together before eating.

Common mistakes

  • Using only one type of bean sauce, which misses the layered savory-sweet depth from combining sweet and yellow bean sauces.
  • Simmering the sauce too briefly, which leaves it tasting raw rather than deeply savory.

Authentic tips

  • A combination of sweet bean sauce and yellow bean sauce gives the most authentic, balanced flavor.
  • This dish is meant to be mixed thoroughly at the table, sauce and cucumber tossed into the noodles just before eating.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork is the more traditional protein, if you'd like to try the classic version.
  • No tianmianjiang: hoisin sauce diluted slightly works as a rough substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store sauce and noodles separately.

Freezing: The sauce freezes well for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Shredded cucumber, radish and bean sprouts
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
