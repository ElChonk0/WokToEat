Samgyetang (Ginseng Chicken Soup) 삼계탕

▾

Surprise me with

Samgyetang, a whole young chicken stuffed with glutinous rice, ginseng and jujubes, is eaten in the hottest days of summer in Korea on the principle of treating heat with heat.

Method

  1. Rinse the hens inside and out, trim any excess fat at the cavity and pat dry. Soak the rice for 1 hour and drain.
  2. Stuff each cavity with half the rice, 2 garlic cloves and a jujube, filling it only about three quarters full; the rice swells as it cooks and will burst out if packed tight.
  3. Cross the legs and tie them together with kitchen string, or pin the cavity closed with a toothpick, so the stuffing stays in.
  4. Put the hens in a pot just big enough to hold them, add water to cover most of each bird and add the remaining garlic, the ginger, the remaining jujubes, the ginseng and the green onion white.
  5. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, skim off the foam, then cover and boil for 15 minutes. Lower to medium-low and simmer, covered, for 25 to 30 minutes more, until the rice inside is soft and a skewer slides easily into the thigh. Turn off the heat and rest for 10 minutes.
  6. Serve each hen whole in a large bowl with plenty of broth. Set out salt, pepper and chopped green onion so each person seasons their own; the meat comes off the bone with a spoon.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling with rice leaves it raw; fill only about 70 percent.
  • Boiling hard makes the broth cloudy and the chicken tough.

Authentic tips

  • Koreans eat samgyetang on the three hottest days of summer (boknal).
  • Season at the table with salt and pepper; the soup is intentionally mild.

Substitutions

  • A Cornish hen replaces the poussin.
  • Dried ginseng slices replace fresh roots.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended; the rice softens.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi and radish kimchi
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Ginseng or barley tea
