Buddha Jumps Over the Wall, Home Version 佛跳墙

▾

Surprise me with

Fuzhou’s most celebrated banquet dish is a rich soup of many luxury ingredients sealed in a wine jar and slow-cooked; the name comes from the legend that its aroma could tempt a meditating monk over a wall.

Method

  1. Soak the dried scallops and shiitake overnight; keep the soaking water.
  2. Blanch the chicken and ribs 3 minutes; rinse. Fry the taro cubes until golden.
  3. Layer the chicken, ribs, shiitake, scallops (with their water), ginger and taro in a heavy lidded pot or Dutch oven.
  4. Add the stock, wine, oyster sauce and soy; bring to a simmer, seal tightly and cook on the lowest heat 2.5 hours.
  5. Add the abalone, sea cucumber, shrimp and quail eggs and cook 15 minutes more.
  6. Taste, adjust with salt, and serve in bowls with plenty of broth.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the abalone and shrimp at the start, which turns them rubbery; they only need the last 15 minutes.

Authentic tips

  • Sealing the lid with foil keeps in the wine aroma, the effect the traditional sealed jar is meant to create.

Substitutions

  • Skip sea cucumber and use extra scallops; the dish is still unmistakably rich.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days; reheat gently without boiling.

Freezing: The broth and meats freeze well for a month; add the seafood fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
