Beef Bhuna

▾

Surprise me with

Bhuna describes a slow, dry-roasting technique that concentrates spices into meat before adding liquid — this beef version produces a thick, deeply spiced curry with minimal sauce.

Method

  1. Fry onion in oil over medium heat until deeply golden and starting to caramelize, 10–12 minutes.
  2. Add ginger-garlic paste and cook 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Add coriander, cumin, turmeric, and chili powder, stirring briefly, then add tomato.
  4. Cook the tomato-spice mixture until it breaks down and the oil begins to separate at the edges, 8–10 minutes.
  5. Add beef and stir to coat thoroughly, cooking covered over low heat, stirring occasionally, 55–60 minutes until the beef is tender and the mixture is thick and dry.
  6. Stir in garam masala in the last 2 minutes before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the onion-browning step, which is the foundation of bhuna's deep flavor — it genuinely needs 10+ minutes.
  • Adding too much liquid, which defeats the point of the dry, concentrated bhuna technique.

Authentic tips

  • The oil separating from the tomato-spice mixture is the visual cue that the base is properly cooked — wait for it before adding the beef.
  • Beef needs a longer covered cooking time than pork or chicken to become properly tender in this technique.

Substitutions

  • Lamb takes the same method.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the cooking to 40 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
