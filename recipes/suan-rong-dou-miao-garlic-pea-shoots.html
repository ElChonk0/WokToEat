Suàn Róng Dòu Miáo (Garlic Pea Shoots) 蒜蓉豆苗

▾

Surprise me with

Pea shoots are a prized Cantonese vegetable, quickly stir-fried with garlic and eaten at banquets and everyday meals for their sweet, grassy flavor.

  • Cook 3 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozPea shoots, tough stems trimmed
  • 6 cloves6 clovesGarlic, half sliced, half minced
  • 3 slices3 slicesGinger, sliced
  • 5 ml1 tspShaoxing wine
  • 4 g1 tspSugar
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Wash the pea shoots and spin or shake them very dry. Pick off any tough stems; the tender tips and leaves are what you want.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat, add 1 tablespoon of oil and lower to medium-low. Fry the ginger and garlic for 15 seconds, until fragrant but still pale.
  3. Raise the heat to high, add the pea shoots and the wine and sugar, and toss constantly for 30 to 60 seconds, until they just collapse and turn bright green.
  4. Add the salt only now, at the end: salting early draws out water and the greens stew instead of stir-frying. Drizzle in the remaining oil, toss for 20 seconds and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking.
  • A wet wok, which steams the shoots.

Authentic tips

  • Cook in one fast stir-fry.
  • Add stock for a saucier dish.

Substitutions

  • Baby bok choy or spinach replace the pea shoots.
  • Snow pea tendrils are an excellent alternative.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; eat within the hour.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Steamed egg custard
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
