Jiǔ Zhuǎn Dà Cháng (Nine-Turn Braised Pork Intestines) 九转大肠

▾

Surprise me with

Jiu zhuan da chang, braised pork intestines, is a famous Jinan dish said to have been refined in the Qing dynasty; its name, "nine turns," refers to the repeated cooking steps and the alchemical elixir.

Method

  1. Turn the intestines inside out, rub thoroughly with salt, flour and vinegar, and rinse several times until clean.
  2. Boil in water with ginger, scallion and wine for 10 minutes; drain and rinse. Then simmer in fresh water with the aromatics for 90 minutes until tender.
  3. Cut into 3 cm sections and pat dry.
  4. Fry in hot oil for 2 minutes until the surface is golden and crisp; drain.
  5. In the pan, melt the sugar until amber, add the vinegar, soy sauce and 60 ml water, return the pieces and toss until glazed.

Common mistakes

  • Poorly cleaned intestines taste strong; do not skimp on the scrubbing.
  • Burning the sugar makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • The name "nine turns" refers to the many steps of cleaning, boiling, frying and glazing.
  • Buy pre-cleaned intestines from a good butcher.

Substitutions

  • Cleaned intestines can be bought ready-prepared.
  • Pork belly slices can replace the intestines for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
