Japchae (Glass Noodle Stir-Fry)

▾

Surprise me with

Japchae is a celebratory Korean dish of stir-fried sweet potato glass noodles and vegetables, traditionally served at holidays, valued for its glossy, slightly sweet, sesame-forward flavor.

  • 300g10 ozSweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon)

  • 150g5 ozSpinach

  • 1/21/2Onion, sliced

  • 60ml4 tbspSoy sauce
  • 20g1.5 tbspSugar
  • 30ml2 tbspSesame oil
  • 10g2 clovesGarlic, minced

Method

  1. Soak glass noodles in warm water until pliable, then boil 5–6 minutes until tender; drain and toss with a little sesame oil and soy sauce.
  2. Blanch spinach 30 seconds, squeeze dry, and season with sesame oil and salt.
  3. Stir-fry carrot, onion, and mushrooms separately until just tender; set aside.
  4. Marinate beef briefly in soy sauce, sugar, garlic, and sesame oil, then stir-fry until cooked.
  5. Combine noodles with all vegetables and beef in a large bowl; add remaining soy sauce, sugar, and sesame oil to taste and toss thoroughly.
  6. Finish with sesame seeds and serve warm or at room temperature.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles: they should be tender but still chewy — check a minute before the package time.

Authentic tips

  • Sautéing vegetables separately keeps colors and textures distinct in the final toss.

Substitutions

  • No glass noodles: thin rice noodles are the closest substitute, though the chewy texture differs.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated; best served cold or at room temperature, since reheating can make noodles gummy.

Freezing: Not recommended; noodle texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
