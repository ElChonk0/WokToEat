Suān Là Tāng (Hot and Sour Soup) 酸辣汤

Hot and sour soup, sharp with vinegar and hot with white pepper, is eaten across northern and western China and is claimed by Sichuan, Henan and Beijing alike; Chinese restaurants abroad made it famous.

Method

  1. Bring the stock to a boil with the shiitake, wood ear, bamboo shoots and carrot and simmer for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tofu, both soy sauces and the sugar and simmer for 2 minutes.
  3. Stir in the cornstarch mixed with the water and simmer for 1 minute until the soup coats a spoon.
  4. Lower the heat, stir the soup into a slow whirlpool and drizzle in the eggs; leave for 10 seconds, then stir gently.
  5. Off the heat, stir in the vinegar and white pepper, then taste and adjust: it should be distinctly sour and peppery. Finish with sesame oil, chili oil and green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the vinegar drives off its sharpness; add it off the heat.
  • Black pepper is not a substitute; white pepper gives the characteristic heat.

Authentic tips

  • Pork shreds and chicken stock are the classic non-vegetarian version.
  • Day lily buds (golden needles) are a traditional addition.

Substitutions

  • Rice vinegar plus a little extra dark soy if you have no black vinegar.
  • Fresh shiitake instead of dried; add them with the tofu.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; add a splash of vinegar when reheating.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month, without the egg if possible.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Potstickers or scallion pancakes
  • Soup: This is the soup course
  • Dessert: Sesame balls
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
