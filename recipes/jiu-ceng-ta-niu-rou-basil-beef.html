Jiǔ Céng Tǎ Niú Ròu (Beef with Taiwanese Basil) 九層塔牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Jiu ceng ta, Taiwanese Thai basil, features in many local stir-fries and is cooked with beef for a fragrant weeknight dish.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

4servings (recipe serves 4)

  • 450 g1 lbBeef sirloin, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozThai basil
  • 40 g3 tbspGarlic and chilies
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice wine
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Slice the beef thin against the grain and toss with half the soy sauce and the wine; leave 10 minutes.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat and sear the beef 1 minute; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil, garlic and chilies for 15 seconds.
  4. Return the beef with the remaining soy sauce and the sugar and toss 30 seconds.
  5. Take off the heat and fold in the basil to wilt it.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the basil too long makes it dull.
  • Overcooking the beef makes it tough.

Authentic tips

  • Thai basil is the closest to jiu ceng ta.
  • Serve with rice.

Substitutions

  • Thai basil is the closest to Taiwanese basil.
  • Pork takes the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the basil darkens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
