Fěn Zhēng Niú Ròu (Anhui Rice-Flour Steamed Beef) 粉蒸牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Rice flour steaming is common across the Yangtze region; in Anhui it's used for beef over sweet potato or taro.

  • 600 g1.3 lbBeef brisket or flank, sliced thin
  • 90 g1/2 cupLong-grain rice, for the steaming rice flour
  • 3 g1 tspStar anise and Sichuan peppercorns, for the rice
  • 30 g2 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 10 g2 tspFermented bean curd, mashed (optional)
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and garlic, minced
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 60 ml1/4 cupWater
  • 300 g10 ozSweet potato, cubed
  • 20 g3/4 ozCilantro and green onion

Method

  1. Toast the rice with the star anise and peppercorns in a dry pan over medium-low heat for 10 minutes until golden, then grind it coarsely.
  2. Marinate the beef with the doubanjiang, bean curd, ginger, garlic, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar and oil for 30 minutes.
  3. Add the rice powder and water and toss until each slice is coated in a moist, sandy layer.
  4. Line a steamer basket with the sweet potato and spread the beef on top.
  5. Steam over boiling water, covered, for 40 to 50 minutes, until the beef is tender and the coating soft. Scatter with cilantro and green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the beef too thick leaves it chewy.
  • Too much rice flour makes it pasty.

Authentic tips

  • Brisket with some fat gives a rich result.
  • The sweet potato soaks up the juices.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly gives a richer version.
  • Pumpkin replaces sweet potato.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; re-steam for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Not needed; the steamed dish is rich
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
