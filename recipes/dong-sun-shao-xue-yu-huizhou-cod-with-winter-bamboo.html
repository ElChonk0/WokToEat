Dōng Sǔn Shāo Xuě Yú (Cod Braised with Winter Bamboo Shoots) 冬笋烧鳕鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Winter bamboo shoots are among the best-known ingredients of the Huangshan mountains, and braising with soy sauce and lard or oil is the typical Anhui (Hui) way of using them.

  • Cook 20 min

  • 500 g1 lb 2 ozCod loin, in 5 cm pieces
  • 200 g7 ozWinter bamboo shoots, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozPork belly or ham, thinly sliced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 15 g1/2 ozGinger, sliced
  • 30 g1 ozScallions, cut in lengths
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 300 ml1 1/4 cupsWater

Method

  1. Pat the cod dry. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat and sear the cod for 1 minute per side until lightly golden; lift out.
  2. Fry the pork slices in the same pan for 2 minutes until they release some fat, then add the ginger and scallions for 30 seconds.
  3. Add the bamboo shoots, wine, both soy sauces, sugar and water and simmer 8 minutes.
  4. Return the cod, cover and simmer on low for 6 minutes, basting, until cooked through.
  5. Uncover and reduce the sauce for 2 minutes, then serve with rice.

Cook’s notes

Written for cod loin. Anhui cooks braise freshwater fish this way.

Common mistakes

  • Bamboo shoots from a tin taste tinny; boil them 3 minutes first.
  • Too much heat toughens the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Fresh bamboo shoots need blanching for 5 minutes to remove bitterness.
  • Salty ham makes the dish salty, so cut back on soy sauce.

Substitutions

  • Haddock replaces the cod.
  • Shiitake mushrooms replace the bamboo shoots.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
