Miso-Glazed Chicken Thigh 味噌焼き

Misoyaki (miso-broiling) marinates fish or meat in a sweetened miso paste before broiling until caramelized — traditionally more common with fish like miso-marinated black cod, but equally well suited to chicken.

  • Prep 10 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 700 g1.5 lbChicken thighs, boneless, skin-on
  • 60 g1/4 cupMiso paste (white or red)
  • 30 ml2 tbspMirin
  • 15 ml1 tbspSake
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • to tasteto tasteToasted sesame seeds and green onion, for garnish

Method

  1. Whisk together miso, mirin, sake, and sugar to make the marinade.
  2. Coat chicken thoroughly and marinate at least 2 hours, or overnight for best flavor.
  3. Preheat the broiler; wipe excess marinade off the chicken (it burns easily) and place skin-side up on a lined tray.
  4. Broil 6–8 minutes, watching closely, until caramelized and cooked through, moving to a lower rack if it browns too fast.
  5. Rest 2 minutes, garnish with sesame seeds and green onion, and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving too much marinade on the chicken before broiling, which causes it to burn before the chicken cooks through.
  • Broiling too close to the heat source, which chars the sugar-rich miso before the interior is done.

Authentic tips

  • Wiping off excess marinade before broiling, then basting again partway through, prevents burning while keeping flavor.
  • White miso gives a milder, sweeter result; red miso is more intense and savory — either works well here.

Substitutions

  • Salmon or black cod are the more traditional proteins for this marinade if you'd like to try it beyond chicken.
  • Pan-searing instead of broiling also works well if you don't have a broiler.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Clear dashi soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
