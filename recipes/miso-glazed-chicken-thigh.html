Miso-Glazed Chicken Thigh 味噌焼き

▾

Surprise me with

Misoyaki (miso-broiling) marinates fish or meat in a sweetened miso paste before broiling until caramelized — traditionally more common with fish like miso-marinated black cod, but equally well suited to chicken.

  • Prep 10 min (plus 4 hr marinating)
  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Simmer the miso, mirin, sake and sugar in a small pan over low heat for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until smooth and glossy, then cool. This becomes both marinade and glaze.
  2. Prick the chicken skin with a fork, coat the thighs in half the miso mixture and refrigerate for 4 to 24 hours. Longer than a day and the salt makes it too strong.
  3. Wipe most of the marinade off the chicken and pat the skin dry; miso burns easily. Put the thighs skin-side down in a cold skillet with the oil, then turn the heat to medium-low.
  4. Cook undisturbed for 7 to 8 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and crisp, then turn and cook 5 minutes more, pouring off the fat, until the chicken reaches 74 C (165 F).
  5. Lift out the chicken and rest it. Wipe the pan, add the remaining miso mixture and the water, and simmer over low heat until it thickens enough to coat a spoon. Slice the chicken, spoon the glaze over and sprinkle with sesame seeds and green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving too much marinade on the chicken before broiling, which causes it to burn before the chicken cooks through.
  • Broiling too close to the heat source, which chars the sugar-rich miso before the interior is done.

Authentic tips

  • Wiping off excess marinade before broiling, then basting again partway through, prevents burning while keeping flavor.
  • White miso gives a milder, sweeter result; red miso is more intense and savory — either works well here.

Substitutions

  • Salmon or black cod are the more traditional proteins for this marinade if you'd like to try it beyond chicken.
  • Pan-searing instead of broiling also works well if you don't have a broiler.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Clear dashi soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
