Qì Guō Jī (Yunnan Steam-Pot Chicken) 汽锅鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Qi guo ji, chicken steamed in a special earthenware pot with a central chimney, is Yunnan's most famous dish, made without added water so the broth forms purely from the chicken's juices.

Method

  1. Blanch the chicken for 3 minutes and rinse.
  2. Arrange the chicken in a Jianshui qi guo (a clay pot with a central chimney) or a heatproof bowl with the ginger, green onion, sanqi and Shaoxing wine. Do not add any water.
  3. Set the qi guo over a pot of boiling water so steam rises through the chimney, condenses and drips onto the chicken. With a bowl, set it on a rack in a large pot of simmering water and cover tightly.
  4. Steam for 2.5 to 3 hours, topping up the water below. The broth that collects is made entirely of condensed steam and chicken juices, so it is pure and intense.
  5. Season with salt only at the end and serve the chicken in its clear broth. Qi guo ji is a famous dish of Jianshui and Kunming.

Common mistakes

  • Adding water dilutes the broth; the whole point is the chicken's own juices.
  • Skipping the blanching leaves a cloudy, scummy broth.

Authentic tips

  • A bone-in, skin-on local chicken gives the sweetest broth.
  • Without a steam pot, use a heatproof bowl covered tightly with foil in a steamer.

Substitutions

  • A heatproof lidded bowl set in a steamer replaces the special steam pot.
  • Add sanqi root only if you have it; goji and dried jujube are a gentler substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming.

Freezing: Freezes with its broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: The clear broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
