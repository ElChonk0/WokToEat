Cantonese Char Siu–Style Chicken Thighs 叉烧鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Borrowing the sweet, lacquered glaze of classic char siu pork, this version applies the same hoisin-and-five-spice marinade to chicken thighs for a faster weeknight take on a barbecue-shop favorite.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 35 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 25 g2 tbspSugar

  • 2 cloves2 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 10 g2 tspGinger, minced

Method

  1. Pat the thighs dry and trim off any loose flaps of fat. Prick the thickest part of each one two or three times with the tip of a knife so the marinade can get inside.
  2. Stir the hoisin, bean sauce, mashed bean curd, soy sauce, wine, sugar, five-spice, shallot, garlic and ginger together until smooth. Coat the chicken thoroughly, rubbing the marinade into the cuts.
  3. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight for the deepest color and flavor. Take the chicken out 20 minutes before cooking.
  4. Heat the oven to 195 C (385 F) with a rack in the middle. Line a tray with foil, set a wire rack on it and lay the thighs on the rack skin-side down, scraping the excess marinade back into the bowl. The rack lets hot air reach the underside so the meat roasts rather than stews in its juices.
  5. Roast for 15 minutes, then turn the thighs skin-side up and brush them with the oil and a little of the reserved marinade. Roast for 10 to 12 minutes more, until the edges darken and the thickest piece reads 74 C (165 F).
  6. Stir the honey with the hot water until runny and brush it over the skin. Roast or broil for 3 to 5 minutes, watching closely, until the glaze bubbles and catches in a few spots. Honey burns quickly, so it goes on only at the end.
  7. Rest for 5 minutes, then slice across into strips and spoon any pan juices over the top.

Common mistakes

  • Basting too late in the process, which doesn't give the glaze enough time to caramelize properly.
  • Skipping the wire rack, which lets the chicken steam in its own juices instead of developing a lacquered surface.

Authentic tips

  • Maltose gives the most authentic sticky-glossy char siu texture, but honey is a fine, more accessible substitute.
  • Basting with a separate portion of marinade (not the one that touched raw chicken) keeps things food-safe.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in thighs work with 5–7 extra minutes of roasting time.
  • No red fermented bean curd: a few drops of red food coloring or simply omitting it both work fine.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; excellent sliced cold into a rice bowl.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with blanched gai lan
  • Soup: Wonton soup
