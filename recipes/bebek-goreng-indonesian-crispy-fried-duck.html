Bebek Goreng (Indonesian Crispy Fried Duck)

▾

Surprise me with

Bebek goreng is an Indonesian dish of duck simmered in spices until tender and then fried until crisp, usually served with sambal and rice.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal, coriander, turmeric and candlenuts into a paste.
  2. Put the duck in a pot with the paste, lemongrass, bay leaves, water, salt and sugar. Simmer, covered, for 1 to 1.5 hours, until the meat is tender, then uncover and cook until the liquid has almost evaporated. Long braising renders the fat under the skin so it fries crisp.
  3. Lift out the duck and pat dry; keep the spice residue in the pot.
  4. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) in a Dutch oven and fry the duck for 5 to 6 minutes, turning, until deep brown and crisp.
  5. Fry the leftover spice paste for 1 to 2 minutes until crumbly and golden, for a kremes-like topping.
  6. Serve the duck with the crisp spices, sambal, cucumber and rice, the Balinese and Javanese way.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Frying wet duck makes the oil spit; dry it well first.
  • Boiling the duck hard dries the meat; simmer gently.

Authentic tips

  • Reduce the leftover braising liquid to a thick sauce to serve alongside.
  • Fry just before serving so the skin stays crisp.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thighs replace duck and braise for 25 minutes.
  • Almonds replace candlenuts.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; refry or roast to recrisp.

Freezing: The braised duck freezes for 2 months before frying.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, fresh vegetables and sambal
  • Soup: Sayur asem
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh manis
