Xiāng Jiān Yú (Pan-Fried Fish with Soy and Scallion) 香煎鱼

▾

Surprise me with

A whole fish or fillets pan-fried crisp and dressed with sweet soy and scallion is a staple of the Cantonese family dinner table.

  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce

  • 2 g1/2 tspCornstarch, for the slurry

Method

  1. Trim the fins, pat the fish dry inside and out and cut 4 or 5 angled slashes on each side. Rub it all over with the salt and pepper and leave for 10 minutes, then pat dry again and dust thinly with cornstarch. A dry, dusted skin is what keeps it from sticking.
  2. Mix the soy sauces, oyster sauce, sugar, water and half the wine, and the slurry cornstarch with 3 tablespoons of water.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok or large skillet over high heat, add 1 tablespoon of oil and swirl, then lay in the fish and turn the heat to medium. Fry without moving for 2 minutes until golden, turn and fry 2 minutes more.
  4. Lower the heat, splash the rest of the wine around the fish, cover and let it steam for 1 minute. Uncover, turn once more and cook 45 seconds. It is done when the flesh at the thickest slash is opaque to the bone. Move it to a plate.
  5. Add the remaining oil and fry the ginger, garlic and shallot over medium heat for 45 seconds, then the onion and pepper for 20 seconds. Pour in the sauce and stir for 30 seconds over medium-high heat.
  6. Lower the heat, stir in the slurry slowly until the sauce thickens and turns glossy, add the green onion and pour it over the fish.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • A damp fish sticks and tears; dry it thoroughly.
  • Moving the fish too soon rips the skin.

Authentic tips

  • Rubbing the hot pan with a slice of ginger before adding the oil helps stop sticking.
  • Salmon fillets are a popular modern version: cook them skin-side down 5 to 6 minutes, turn for 1 to 2 minutes, and spoon the sauce around rather than over the skin.

Substitutions

  • Salmon, cod, trout or black cod fillets.
  • Add a little chopped chili to the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Bok choy and pork soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
