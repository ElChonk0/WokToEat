Singapore Prawn Mee (Hae Mee)

▾

Surprise me with

Singapore prawn mee is a noodle soup built on a broth simmered with prawn heads and shells and pork bones, served with prawns, pork and kangkong; the dry version is tossed in chili.

Method

  1. Peel the prawns, keeping the heads and shells. Blanch the pork ribs in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium-high heat and fry the garlic for 30 seconds, then the prawn heads and shells for 5 to 8 minutes, pressing on the heads, until deep orange and smelling roasted. This step decides how rich and sweet the broth is.
  3. Add the ribs, water and rock sugar, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer for 1 to 1.5 hours until the ribs are tender. Strain the broth, keeping the ribs, and season with the fish sauce, salt and white pepper.
  4. Poach the peeled prawns in the simmering broth for 1 to 2 minutes until just pink, then lift them out and halve them.
  5. Blanch the noodles, vermicelli, kangkong and bean sprouts and divide among bowls. Add the ribs and prawns, ladle the hot broth over, and top with fried shallots and chili paste.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the broth hard turns it cloudy and bitter; keep it at a gentle simmer.
  • Poaching the prawns too long makes them rubbery; 2 minutes is enough.

Authentic tips

  • Pressing the prawn heads releases the roe and gives the broth its orange color.
  • A spoonful of pork lard in the bowl rounds out the broth.

Substitutions

  • Frozen shell-on prawns work if fresh heads are unavailable.
  • Skip the ribs for a lighter prawn-only broth.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; cook the noodles fresh.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sliced red chilies in soy sauce
  • Soup: The broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced lime juice
