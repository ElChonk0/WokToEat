Ayam Goreng Berempah (Malaysian Spiced Fried Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Ayam goreng berempah, chicken fried in a coating of spices and shallots, is a Malay classic often served at Hari Raya feasts.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 35 min

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal and lemongrass into a paste with a splash of water. Mix in the turmeric, coriander, cumin, fennel, chili powder, salt and sugar.
  2. Score the thick pieces of chicken to the bone and rub the paste in well. Marinate in the refrigerator for at least 2 hours, or overnight, so the spices reach the meat.
  3. Mix the egg, rice flour and cornstarch into the chicken until each piece has a thin, sticky coating. Rice flour is what gives the berempah crust its crackle.
  4. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok to 160 C (320 F). Fry the chicken in batches for 12 to 15 minutes, turning, until cooked through and deep golden; the thickest piece should read 74 C (165 F). Lower the heat if the paste on the surface darkens too fast.
  5. At the end, scrape the marinade remnants into the oil with the curry leaves and fry them for 1 to 2 minutes until crisp, then lift them out. These crunchy spiced bits (rempah) are scattered over the chicken.
  6. Drain the chicken on a rack and serve with the fried rempah, rice, cucumber and sambal.

Common mistakes

  • Frying at too high a heat burns the spices before the chicken cooks.
  • Skipping the rice flour gives a soft coating.

Authentic tips

  • Bone-in pieces stay juicier.
  • Serve with sambal and cucumber.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in thighs and drumsticks both work.
  • Air-fry at 190 C (375 F) for 20 minutes for a lighter version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Nasi lemak or steamed rice with sambal
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Ais kacang
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
