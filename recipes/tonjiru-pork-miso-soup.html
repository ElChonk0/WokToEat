Tonjiru (Pork Miso Soup) 豚汁

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty, substantial miso soup loaded with pork and root vegetables, tonjiru is often served as a filling meal on its own rather than a side dish, especially popular in colder months.

  • 300 g10.5 ozPork belly, sliced thin
  • 200 g7 ozDaikon radish, sliced into half-moons
  • 11Carrot, sliced into half-moons
  • 1 medium1 mediumPotato, cut into chunks
  • 50 g1.75 ozBurdock root, sliced (optional)
  • 1 L4 cupsDashi stock
  • 80 g1/3 cupMiso paste
  • to tasteto tasteGreen onion, sliced, for garnish

Method

  1. Stir-fry pork slices in a little oil in a pot until lightly browned.
  2. Add daikon, carrot, potato, and burdock root, stirring 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add dashi stock, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer.
  4. Cover and cook 15–18 minutes until the vegetables are tender.
  5. Remove from heat briefly, whisk in miso paste until dissolved (don't boil after adding miso), and return to low heat to warm through.
  6. Garnish with green onion before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the soup after adding miso, which destroys some of its delicate flavor and aroma.
  • Adding all the vegetables at once regardless of size, which cooks them unevenly.

Authentic tips

  • Whisking the miso through a small strainer directly into the pot helps it dissolve smoothly without lumps.
  • This soup is hearty enough to serve as a light meal with just a bowl of rice alongside.

Substitutions

  • No burdock root: extra carrot or daikon work as substitutes.
  • Pork shoulder, sliced thin, can replace belly for a leaner soup.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently without boiling hard.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the vegetable texture softens further.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
