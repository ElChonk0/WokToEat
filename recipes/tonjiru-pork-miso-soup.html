Tonjiru (Pork Miso Soup) 豚汁

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty, substantial miso soup loaded with pork and root vegetables, tonjiru is often served as a filling meal on its own rather than a side dish, especially popular in colder months.

Method

  1. Soak the shaved burdock and the taro in water for 5 minutes to prevent browning, then drain. Rub the konnyaku with salt and boil it for 2 minutes to remove its smell.
  2. Heat the sesame oil in a large pot over medium heat and stir-fry the pork for 2 minutes until it loses its pink.
  3. Add the onion, daikon, carrot, burdock, taro, konnyaku and aburaage and stir for 2 minutes, until coated in the pork fat. This short stir-fry gives tonjiru its rich, nutty flavor.
  4. Pour in the dashi, bring to a boil and skim off the foam, then cover and simmer gently for 10 to 15 minutes, until a skewer passes easily through the daikon and taro.
  5. Turn off the heat and dissolve the miso into the soup through a small strainer, then add the ginger. Reheat over low heat until just steaming; boiling miso dulls its aroma. Serve topped with green onion and shichimi.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the soup after adding miso, which destroys some of its delicate flavor and aroma.
  • Adding all the vegetables at once regardless of size, which cooks them unevenly.

Authentic tips

  • Whisking the miso through a small strainer directly into the pot helps it dissolve smoothly without lumps.
  • This soup is hearty enough to serve as a light meal with just a bowl of rice alongside.

Substitutions

  • No burdock root: extra carrot or daikon work as substitutes.
  • Pork shoulder, sliced thin, can replace belly for a leaner soup.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently without boiling hard.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though the vegetable texture softens further.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
