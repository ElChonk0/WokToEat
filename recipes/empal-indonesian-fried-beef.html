Empal (Indonesian Fried Beef)

▾

Surprise me with

A Sundanese specialty, empal simmers beef until tender in coconut water and spices, then fries it until crisp at the edges — a beloved dish often served alongside sambal and steamed rice.

Method

  1. Combine beef, coconut water, garlic, coriander, galangal, palm sugar, and salt in a pot.
  2. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook 45–50 minutes until very tender.
  3. Remove beef and let cool slightly, then lightly pound or flatten each piece with the back of a knife.
  4. Heat oil and fry the beef pieces until deeply golden and crisp at the edges, 2–3 minutes per side.
  5. Drain and serve with sambal and steamed rice.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the pounding step, which helps the beef absorb more of the frying oil's crispness and makes it easier to eat.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, which produces a greasy rather than crisp result.

Authentic tips

  • The initial simmer in coconut water and spices is what makes empal tender and flavorful before it's ever fried.
  • Lightly flattening the beef before frying increases surface area for maximum crispness.

Substitutions

  • No coconut water: regular water with a touch more palm sugar works as a substitute.
  • This dish is very traditionally paired with a sambal on the side; don't skip it if possible.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan to re-crisp.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, cooked but unfried; fry fresh after thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and sambal

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
