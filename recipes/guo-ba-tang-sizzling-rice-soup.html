Guō Bā Tāng (Sizzling Rice Soup with Shrimp) 锅巴汤

▾

Surprise me with

Sizzling rice soup, hot broth poured over fried rice crusts at the table so they crackle, is an old Chinese restaurant showpiece that is a favorite in Cantonese American restaurants.

Method

  1. If making your own rice crusts, press cooked rice into a thin, even layer on a tray and dry it completely in a 95 C (200 F) oven for about 3 hours, then break it into pieces. They must be bone dry, or they will not puff.
  2. Mix the chicken with its salt, half the cornstarch and the water; toss the shrimp with the rest of the cornstarch. Cut the vegetables into similar small pieces.
  3. Bring the stock and water to a full boil over high heat and add the oil, which keeps the vegetables glossy. Add the carrot, then the rest of the vegetables, and return to a boil. Season with the salt and sugar.
  4. Add the chicken, stirring to separate it, then the shrimp 20 to 30 seconds later. Once the shrimp turn pink, after about 20 seconds more, add the fish sauce, pepper and sesame oil. Keep the soup at a simmer.
  5. Just before serving, heat the frying oil in a clean wok or Dutch oven to 190 C (375 F) and fry the rice crusts for 40 to 50 seconds until puffed and lightly golden. Drain and pile them into a heatproof serving bowl.
  6. Bring the crusts and the boiling soup to the table separately and pour the soup over the crusts in front of everyone, so they crackle and sizzle. Both must be very hot for the sizzle; serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • If the crusts or soup are not both very hot, there is no sizzle.
  • Moist rice crusts will not puff; they must be fully dry.

Authentic tips

  • Store dried crusts in a sealed jar for weeks.
  • Toss in a few more crusts at the end for crunch.

Substitutions

  • Rice cakes or puffed rice crackers stand in for homemade crusts.
  • Make it vegetarian with tofu and vegetable stock.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the soup separately for 2 days; fry fresh crusts when serving.

Freezing: The soup freezes for 2 months without the snow peas.
