Ayam Kapitan (Nyonya Chicken Curry)

▾

Surprise me with

Ayam kapitan, a mild Nyonya (Peranakan) coconut curry with a dry spice paste, is a Penang and Malacca specialty.

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with the turmeric, salt and a little oil and leave it for at least 30 minutes. For extra depth, brown it under the broiler or in a hot pan until the skin is golden, without cooking it through.
  2. Blend the candlenuts, lemongrass, fresh turmeric, galangal, ginger, garlic, onion and both chilies into a smooth paste, adding a little oil to help the blender.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the paste for 1 minute, then add the lime leaves, belacan and tamarind. Keep frying, stirring, for 5 to 8 minutes, until the paste darkens and the oil separates from it.
  4. Add the coconut milk, water, sugar, salt and the chicken. Bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered for 30 to 40 minutes, stirring now and then, until the chicken is tender and the gravy is thick. Add the potatoes for the last 15 minutes.
  5. Taste and adjust the salt and sugar: kapitan should be rich, gently tart from the tamarind, a little sweet and spicy. Serve with rice or roti jala.

Common mistakes

  • Under-frying the paste leaves a raw taste.
  • Boiling hard splits the coconut milk.

Authentic tips

  • Kapitan is a dry curry; reduce until the sauce clings.
  • Better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs shorten the cooking by 10 minutes.
  • Kaffir lime leaves can be replaced by a squeeze of lime.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
