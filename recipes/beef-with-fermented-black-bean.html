Beef with Fermented Black Bean 豆豉牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

A savory, deeply aromatic stir-fry built on fermented black beans, a Hunanese and broader southern Chinese pantry staple that adds umami depth to quickly cooked beef and vegetables.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Put the green chilies in a dry flat-bottom wok over medium heat with a pinch of salt and press them against the wok with a spatula for 3 to 4 minutes, until blistered and softened. Lift them out. Dry-frying the chilies first (gan bian) is the Hunan way; it tames their rawness and deepens their flavor.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in the wok over high heat until smoking and stir-fry the beef for 1 to 2 minutes, until just cooked and browned at the edges. Push it aside.
  4. Add the remaining oil, the garlic, ginger and fermented black beans and fry for 20 seconds until fragrant.
  5. Return the chilies, add the sauce soy sauce and a pinch of sugar and toss everything over the highest heat for 30 to 60 seconds, until the beef is coated and smoky. Hunan home cooking (xiao chao, 'small-wok stir-fry') is all about this fierce, quick heat. Serve with plenty of rice.

Common mistakes

  • Not rinsing the fermented black beans, which can leave the dish overly salty.
  • Overcooking the beef, which turns it tough — sear quickly over very high heat.

Authentic tips

  • Rinsing the fermented black beans briefly softens their intensity without losing their signature umami.
  • This dish comes together very quickly — have all ingredients prepped before you start cooking.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or pork replace the beef.
  • Any bell pepper color works; a mix adds visual appeal.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Simple egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Sliced watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
