Kung Pao Chicken 宫保鸡丁

Named for a Qing-dynasty governor (gongbao), this Sichuan classic balances numbing peppercorn, dried chili, and a sweet-sour-savory sauce with crunchy peanuts — one of the most internationally recognized Sichuan dishes.

Method

  1. Marinate diced chicken in 5 ml soy sauce and half the cornstarch for 15 minutes.
  2. Mix remaining soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, and remaining cornstarch with 30 ml water for the sauce.
  3. Stir-fry dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns in oil over medium heat until fragrant, about 30 seconds — don't let them burn.
  4. Add chicken and stir-fry over high heat until just cooked, 3–4 minutes.
  5. Add garlic, ginger, and green onion; stir-fry 30 seconds more.
  6. Pour in the sauce, tossing until it thickens and coats everything glossy.
  7. Stir in peanuts at the very end to keep them crunchy; serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the dried chilies and peppercorns, which turns the whole dish bitter.
  • Adding peanuts too early, which lets them go soft in the sauce instead of staying crisp.

Authentic tips

  • Toasting the chilies and peppercorns briefly in dry oil is the flavor foundation of the whole dish — don't rush it.
  • Velvet the chicken (marinate with cornstarch) for the silkiest texture, standard practice in Sichuan stir-fries.

Substitutions

  • Peanuts can be swapped for cashews for a different but still authentic-adjacent crunch.
  • Reduce dried chilies by half for a milder, family-friendly version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the peanuts soften somewhat on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended — the peanuts and glossy sauce don't hold texture after freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice to temper the heat
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh orange slices
  • Beverage: Cold Tsingtao or barley tea
