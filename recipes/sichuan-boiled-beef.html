Sichuan Boiled Beef 水煮牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

The original and most famous version of the shui zhu technique, this dish poaches thin beef slices in broth before drowning them in a mountain of chilies and a dramatic pour of hot oil — one of Sichuan's most iconic dishes.

Method

  1. Toss the beef with a pinch of salt, the Shaoxing wine, egg white and cornstarch until each slice is coated in a thin, silky film. This velveting keeps the meat tender in the hot broth.
  2. Toast the dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns in a dry flat-bottom wok over low heat for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the chilies darken slightly and smell toasty but do not blacken. Cool and chop them finely with a knife; this is the ci ba la jiao topping.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in the wok over high heat and stir-fry the vegetables with a pinch of salt for 1 to 2 minutes until just wilted. Lay them in a deep serving bowl.
  4. Lower the heat to medium, add another 2 tablespoons of oil and fry the doubanjiang for 1 to 2 minutes until the oil turns red. Add the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds, then the stock and soy sauce, and bring to a boil.
  5. Lower the heat to a gentle simmer and add the beef a slice at a time, separating them. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, just until cooked. Pour the beef and broth over the vegetables.
  6. Scatter the chopped chili and peppercorn over the top with the green onion and garlic. Heat the remaining oil until it just begins to smoke and pour it over the spices; they sizzle and release their fragrance. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef slices in the broth, which toughens them — they only need a minute or two given how thin they are.
  • Pouring the oil before it's hot enough, which fails to properly bloom the chilies and peppercorns.

Authentic tips

  • Slicing the beef paper-thin (partially frozen meat is easier to slice thinly) is essential to this dish's signature silky texture.
  • The final hot-oil pour is the dramatic centerpiece of this dish — do it just before serving, at the table if possible.

Substitutions

  • Pork works with the same technique.
  • Reduce the dried chilies for a considerably milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the beef toughens on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is soupy
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
