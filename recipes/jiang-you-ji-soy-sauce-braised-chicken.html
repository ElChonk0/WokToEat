Jiàng Yóu Jī (Beijing Soy-Braised Chicken) 酱油鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Soy-braised chicken, sold at Beijing delis and eaten cold, is a staple northern dish simmered in a master sauce with star anise.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 45 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced

  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Blanch the chicken legs for 3 minutes and pat dry.
  2. Heat the oil and rock sugar in a Dutch oven over low heat until it caramelizes to amber, then turn the chicken in it until glossy.
  3. Add the ginger, spices and green onion, then the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces and water. Bring to a boil.
  4. Cover and simmer gently for 25 minutes, turning once, then turn off the heat and leave the chicken in the liquid for 20 minutes so it soaks up the color and flavor.
  5. Lift out the chicken, chop it and serve warm or cold, drizzled with a little of the reduced braising liquid.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard tightens the meat and clouds the sauce.
  • Skipping the steep leaves the chicken less flavorful and less juicy.

Authentic tips

  • Reuse the sauce as a master stock: strain, refrigerate and top up with fresh aromatics.
  • Serve cold with steamed buns, as in Beijing delis.

Substitutions

  • Whole bone-in thighs work with the same timing.
  • Hard-boiled eggs added for the last 15 minutes soak up the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
