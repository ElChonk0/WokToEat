Dòu Chǐ Chǎo Gé Lí (Clams in Black Bean Sauce) 豉汁炒蛤蜊

▾

Clams stir-fried in fermented black bean sauce are a Cantonese seafood-restaurant standby.

Method

  1. Scrub the clams and soak them in cold salted water for 30 minutes so they spit out sand, then drain. Discard any that stay open when tapped. Mix the soy sauces, oyster sauce, sugar, pepper and water in a cup, and the cornstarch with 3 tablespoons of water.
  2. Bring a wok or pot of water to a rolling boil, add the clams and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, lifting them out as they open. Pry open any that are only slightly ajar and throw away any that stay shut or smell off. Par-cooking them first means they only need a quick toss in the sauce and stay tender. Drain and wipe the wok dry.
  3. Heat the wok over high heat with the oil, then lower to medium and fry the ginger, garlic, green onion whites and chilies for 15 to 20 seconds until fragrant. Lower the heat and add the black bean sauce for 5 to 10 seconds, then the onion and bell pepper for 30 seconds.
  4. Raise the heat to medium-high, return the clams and toss for 30 seconds. Add the sauce over the highest heat and stir for about 50 seconds, so the clam juices mix into it.
  5. Re-stir the slurry and add it a little at a time until the sauce clings to the shells; you may need only half. Turn off the heat, add the sesame oil and green onion greens and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the soak leaves sand in every clam.
  • Cooking clams after they open makes them tough; take each batch off the heat as it opens.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse the fermented black beans briefly if they taste very salty.
  • Add a splash of stock if you want more sauce for rice.

Substitutions

  • Mussels replace the clams; cook until just open.
  • Halve the chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat immediately; cooked clams turn rubbery on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Choy sum
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
