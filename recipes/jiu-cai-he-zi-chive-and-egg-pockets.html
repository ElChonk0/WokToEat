Jiǔ Cài Hé Zi (Chive and Egg Pockets) 韭菜盒子

Jiu cai he zi, pan-fried dough pockets filled with chives, egg and glass noodles, are a classic Shandong and northern street breakfast.

  • Prep 40 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 300 g2.5 cupsFlour
  • 180 ml3/4 cupHot water
  • 250 g9 ozChinese chives, chopped
  • 44Eggs, scrambled
  • 60 g2 ozGlass noodles, soaked and chopped
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Mix the flour with the hot water into a dough, knead 5 minutes and rest, covered, 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the chopped chives, scrambled eggs and noodles with the sesame oil, and add the salt just before filling.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 pieces, roll each into a 12 cm circle, fill the center and fold over into a half-moon; crimp the edge.
  4. Heat the oil in a nonstick pan over medium heat and fry the pockets 3-4 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the salt early makes the chives release water.
  • Overfilling tears the dough.

Authentic tips

  • Toss the chives with oil first to coat them.
  • Seal the edges well so no filling escapes.

Substitutions

  • Garlic chives replace Chinese chives.
  • Add soaked dried shrimp for the traditional filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a dry pan.

Freezing: Freeze cooked pockets for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Millet porridge
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
