Jī Róng Sù Mǐ Gēng (Chicken and Sweetcorn Soup) 鸡蓉粟米羹

▾

Surprise me with

Thick chicken and creamed-corn soup with egg ribbons is a Hong Kong home and restaurant favorite, made possible by canned creamed corn arriving in the colony in the mid-20th century.

Method

  1. Dice the chicken very small, then mix it with the salt, cornstarch and water until the liquid is absorbed; the coating keeps the pieces silky in the soup. Blend the corn with the blending water for 15 to 30 seconds into a coarse puree. Stir the slurry cornstarch into its water and beat the egg.
  2. Bring the stock to a boil in a large pot over high heat. Add the corn puree and carrot and return to a boil, then simmer for 3 minutes, stirring the bottom so the corn does not catch.
  3. Re-stir the slurry and pour it in slowly while stirring, then bring the soup back to a boil; it should turn glossy and lightly thick. Add the slurry a little at a time if you prefer a thinner soup.
  4. Add the chicken a spoonful at a time, stirring to separate the pieces, and simmer for 1 to 2 minutes until it turns white.
  5. Turn the heat to low. Drizzle in the egg in two or three additions while stirring the soup slowly in one direction, so it sets into thin, soft ribbons rather than a clump.
  6. Season with salt and white pepper and finish with the sesame oil; use it sparingly, as too much overpowers the corn. Serve topped with cilantro.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the chicken in a clump makes chewy lumps; stir it in so it disperses.
  • Boiling hard after the egg breaks up the ribbons.

Authentic tips

  • Mincing the chicken very finely by hand gives the silky "chicken velvet" texture.
  • Add a few kernels of whole corn for bite.

Substitutions

  • Crab meat replaces chicken for the banquet version.
  • Vegetable stock and tofu make it vegetarian.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended; the thickened soup turns watery.
