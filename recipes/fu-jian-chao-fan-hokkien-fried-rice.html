Fú Jiàn Chǎo Fàn (Hokkien Fried Rice) 福建炒饭

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, Hokkien fried rice, egg fried rice under a thick gravy of seafood, chicken and mushrooms, is a Hong Kong restaurant dish.

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with its salt and a third of the cornstarch and a tablespoon of water; toss the shrimp and scallops with the rest of the cornstarch and the pepper. Soak the mushrooms for at least 15 minutes until fully soft. Mix the oyster sauce, gravy soy sauces and sugar, and the slurry cornstarch with 2 tablespoons of water.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat with 1 tablespoon of oil, pour in the eggs and, after 10 to 15 seconds, add the rice. Lower the heat to medium and stir-fry for 2 minutes, pressing out clumps. Drizzle a little oil around the edge, season with the rice salt and soy sauce and toss for 30 seconds, then add half the green onion greens. Pile the rice onto a deep platter.
  3. Clean the wok and heat it over high heat with the remaining oil, then lower the heat. Fry the garlic, ginger and green onion whites for 15 seconds, the mushrooms for 30 seconds, then the chicken for 45 seconds.
  4. Add the scallops for 30 seconds, then the shrimp, carrot and greens for 30 seconds, until the shrimp start to turn pink. Splash the wine around the edge of the wok.
  5. Pour in the boiling water and boil for 30 seconds, then add the sauce and cook 30 seconds more. Lower the heat and stir in the slurry gradually until the gravy is glossy and coats a spoon; stop before it turns gluey.
  6. Turn off the heat, stir in the sesame oil and ladle the seafood gravy over the rice. Scatter with the remaining green onion and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • A thin gravy turns the rice to mush; it should coat a spoon thickly.
  • Seasoning the rice heavily makes the dish too salty with the gravy.

Authentic tips

  • Add a few pieces of roast duck for the classic restaurant version.
  • Keep the rice lightly seasoned; the gravy carries the flavor.

Substitutions

  • All shrimp or all chicken.
  • Add diced roast duck or char siu.

Storage

Leftovers: Store the gravy separately for 2 days.

Freezing: Not recommended.
