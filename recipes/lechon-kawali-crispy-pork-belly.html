Lechon Kawali (Crispy Pork Belly)

A pan-fried alternative to whole roasted lechon (spit-roasted pig), lechon kawali boils pork belly until tender, then deep-fries it until the skin blisters into an impossibly crisp crackling.

Method

  1. Combine pork belly, bay leaves, garlic, peppercorns, and salt in a pot with water.
  2. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook 45 minutes until tender.
  3. Remove pork and pat very dry; let air-dry uncovered in the refrigerator at least 1 hour, or overnight for best results.
  4. Heat oil to 180°C (350°F) and deep-fry the pork, skin-side down first, until the skin blisters and crisps, 8–10 minutes total, turning as needed.
  5. Drain, rest 5 minutes, then chop into pieces.

Common mistakes

  • Not drying the pork thoroughly before frying, which prevents the skin from properly blistering and crisping.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, which produces a greasy result instead of genuine crackling.

Authentic tips

  • The air-drying step (uncovered, in the fridge) removes surface moisture and is essential for good crackling — don't rush it.
  • Frying in two stages — once to cook through, once hotter to crisp — gives the most reliable crackling.

Substitutions

  • No deep fryer: a wide, deep pot with enough oil works fine.
  • Pork shoulder can substitute for belly, though it lacks the skin for crackling.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 2 days refrigerated but loses crispness.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, cooked; reheat in a hot oven to partially restore crispness.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and liver sauce
  • Soup: Sinigang
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
