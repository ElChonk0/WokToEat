Woo Tau Zheng Pai Gu (Steamed Spare Ribs with Taro) 芋头蒸排骨

Small pieces of spare rib steamed over taro in a black bean or garlic marinade is a dim sum and home dish; the taro soaks up the meat juices.

  • Prep 40 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 700 g1.5 lbPork spare ribs, cut into small pieces
  • 400 g14 ozTaro, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 5 cloves5 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 15 g1 tbspFermented black beans, rinsed and mashed
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 8 g2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 g1.5 tbspCornstarch
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 11Fresh red chili, sliced
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, chopped

Method

  1. Soak the ribs in cold water 15 minutes, drain and pat dry.
  2. Mix the ribs with the garlic, black beans, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and wine, then the cornstarch and oil. Marinate 20 minutes.
  3. Lay the taro in a shallow heatproof dish and spread the ribs on top.
  4. Steam over high heat for 22 to 25 minutes, until the ribs are cooked and the taro is tender.
  5. Scatter with chili and green onion and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Thick taro chunks stay hard; cut them about 2 cm (3/4 in).
  • Skipping the soak leaves the steaming juices cloudy.

Authentic tips

  • Fry the taro briefly first for a richer, firmer version.
  • Steaming on top of rice in a pot makes it a one-pot meal.

Substitutions

  • Potato or pumpkin replace taro.
  • Leave out the black beans for a plain garlic version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; re-steam.

Freezing: Freezes raw in the marinade for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
