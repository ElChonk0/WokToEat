Thai Red Curry

▾

Surprise me with

Built on a paste of dried red chilies, lemongrass, and galangal, red curry is one of Thailand's most common curries, simmered in coconut milk and adaptable to nearly any protein or vegetable on hand.

Method

  1. Heat the thickest coconut cream from the top of the can in a pot or flat-bottom wok over medium heat and simmer, stirring, for 3 to 5 minutes, until it thickens and the oil starts to separate ('cracking'). Fry the curry paste in it for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring constantly, until deeply fragrant and the oil turns red. Frying the paste in separated coconut fat is what develops a Thai curry's flavor.
  2. Add the chicken and stir for 2 minutes until coated and no longer pink outside.
  3. Pour in the rest of the coconut milk with a splash of water, bring to a gentle simmer and add the eggplant, bamboo shoots and torn lime leaves. Simmer for 8 to 10 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and the vegetables tender.
  4. Season with fish sauce and palm sugar, tasting until it is salty and rich with a little sweetness.
  5. Turn off the heat, stir in the Thai basil and serve with jasmine rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding all the coconut milk at once without frying the paste first: skipping this step gives a flatter, less aromatic curry.

Authentic tips

  • Tasting and adjusting fish sauce and sugar at the end is essential — bottled curry pastes vary widely in saltiness and heat by brand.

Substitutions

  • No Thai eggplant: zucchini or regular eggplant works in a pinch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3–4 days; flavor deepens overnight.

Freezing: Freezes well; coconut-based curries hold up better frozen than dairy-based sauces.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Mango sticky rice
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
