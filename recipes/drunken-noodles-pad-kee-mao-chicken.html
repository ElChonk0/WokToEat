Drunken Noodles (Pad Kee Mao, Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, drunken noodles contain no alcohol — the dish is said to have been invented as a fiery, aromatic way to cure a hangover, tossing wide rice noodles with holy basil and chili in a very hot wok.

Method

  1. Separate the noodles by hand; if they are stiff from the refrigerator, microwave them for 30 seconds so they do not break. Mix the oyster sauce, soy sauces, fish sauce and sugar. Pound the garlic and chilies into a coarse paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and fry the garlic-chili paste for 15 seconds until fragrant.
  3. Add the chicken and stir-fry for 2 to 3 minutes until cooked through, then the onion, bell pepper, baby corn and peppercorns for 1 minute.
  4. Add the noodles and the sauce and toss gently with a spatula for 2 minutes, letting the noodles sit against the hot wok between tosses so they char in spots. Cook in two batches if your wok is small; crowding makes them gummy.
  5. Turn off the heat and fold in the holy basil until just wilted. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking over insufficient heat, which fails to achieve the smoky char that defines this dish.
  • Adding the basil too early and overcooking it, which turns it dark and loses its peppery aroma.

Authentic tips

  • This dish should genuinely be very spicy — adjust chili count for your tolerance, but don't be too conservative if aiming for authenticity.
  • Very high heat and quick, constant tossing keep the wide noodles from clumping or breaking.

Substitutions

  • Beef or shrimp both work well with the same method.
  • Thai sweet basil works if holy basil isn't available, though the flavor will be milder.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the basil loses some vibrancy.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle and basil texture don't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cucumber slices
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Singha beer
