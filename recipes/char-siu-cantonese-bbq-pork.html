Char Siu (Cantonese BBQ Pork) 叉烧

▾

Surprise me with

Char siu is Cantonese roast pork glazed with maltose, hoisin, and red fermented bean curd, traditionally hung on hooks and roasted in specialized ovens — the iconic dish hanging in every Cantonese barbecue shop window.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 6 hr marinating)
  • Cook 50 min

Method

  1. Cut the pork into even strips about 2.5 cm (1 in) thick, so they roast at the same rate, and prick them all over on both sides with a fork. A fatty cut such as shoulder stays juicy; lean loin dries out.
  2. Mash the bean curd and stir in the garlic salt, sugar, oyster sauce, soy sauce, hoisin, wine, five-spice and garlic until smooth.
  3. Massage the pork with the marinade in a zip bag for 2 minutes, squeeze out the air and refrigerate for 6 to 8 hours or overnight, but no longer than a day, as the salt starts to cure the meat. Take it out 1 hour before roasting.
  4. Heat the oven to 220 C (425 F). Line a tray with foil, set a rack on it and pour a few tablespoons of water into the tray so the dripping sugars do not burn and smoke. Lay the pork on the rack, keeping the leftover marinade.
  5. Roast for 15 minutes, turn the strips and baste with the marinade, then roast 15 minutes more and baste again. Add a splash of water to the tray if it dries out.
  6. Stir the honey with the hot water. Brush it over the pork and roast for 10 minutes, then raise the oven to 230 C (450 F), brush again and roast for 5 minutes, until the edges char in spots and the center reads 63 to 70 C (145 to 160 F). Watch closely at the end; the glaze burns fast.
  7. Brush with honey once more, rest for 5 minutes, then slice across the grain.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting the pork too thick, which prevents the marinade from penetrating and slows even cooking.
  • Skipping the wire rack, which lets the meat steam in its own juices instead of developing a proper glaze.

Authentic tips

  • Maltose gives the most authentic sticky-glossy texture; honey is a fine, more accessible substitute.
  • Basting with a separate portion of marinade (not one that touched raw pork) keeps things food-safe.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly can be used for a fattier, richer version.
  • No red fermented bean curd: a few drops of red food coloring or simply omitting it both work fine.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; excellent sliced into fried rice.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with the glaze drizzled over
  • Soup: Wonton soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
