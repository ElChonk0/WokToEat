Chòu Guì Yú (Huizhou Stinky Mandarin Fish) 臭鳜鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Chou gui yu, mandarin fish cured with brine until it develops a pungent aroma, is Anhui's most famous dish; it dates to the days when fish carried from the Yangtze to mountainous Huizhou fermented en route.

Method

  1. Rinse the cured fish and pat dry; if you are curing your own, rub a sea bass with 3 percent salt and refrigerate 3 days, uncovered.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat and fry the fish 2-3 minutes per side until golden; lift out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the pork, ginger, garlic and chilies 3 minutes.
  4. Pour in the wine, soy sauce, sugar and 200 ml water, return the fish, cover and braise 10 minutes, spooning the sauce over.
  5. Reduce the sauce 2 minutes and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the fish too much breaks the soft flesh.
  • Using a fish that is very salty without rinsing makes the dish inedible.

Authentic tips

  • The smell is strong but the flesh is delicate and savory.
  • The traditional cure takes about a week; ready-made cured fish is available in Chinese markets.

Substitutions

  • Cured fish is sold at Chinese markets; sea bass fresh-cured for 3 days is the DIY option.
  • Pork belly can be omitted for a lighter dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
