Chòu Guì Yú (Huizhou Stinky Mandarin Fish) 臭鳜鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Chou gui yu, mandarin fish cured with brine until it develops a pungent aroma, is Anhui's most famous dish; it dates to the days when fish carried from the Yangtze to mountainous Huizhou fermented en route.

Method

  1. Traditional chou gui yu is mandarin fish brined lightly and left at cool room temperature under a weight for 6 to 8 days, until it smells pungent and the flesh turns firm and clings in garlic-clove-like flakes. To approximate it, rub fresh fish with the salt, wrap it and refrigerate under a weight for 3 days. Rinse and pat dry.
  2. Score the fish lightly. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and fry the fish for 3 to 4 minutes per side until golden. Lift it out.
  3. Fry the pork belly in the oil until it renders, then the bamboo shoots, ginger, garlic and chilies for 1 minute.
  4. Return the fish, add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces, sugar and water and simmer over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes, spooning the sauce over, until it reduces to a thick glaze.
  5. Scatter with green onion and serve. The famous Huizhou paradox: it smells strong but tastes savory and fragrant.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the fish too much breaks the soft flesh.
  • Using a fish that is very salty without rinsing makes the dish inedible.

Authentic tips

  • The smell is strong but the flesh is delicate and savory.
  • The traditional cure takes about a week; ready-made cured fish is available in Chinese markets.

Substitutions

  • Cured fish is sold at Chinese markets; sea bass fresh-cured for 3 days is the DIY option.
  • Pork belly can be omitted for a lighter dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
