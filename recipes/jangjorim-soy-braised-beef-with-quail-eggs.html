Jangjorim (Soy-Braised Beef with Quail Eggs) 메추리알 장조림

▾

Surprise me with

Jangjorim, beef braised in soy sauce until it can be pulled into shreds, is a long-keeping Korean banchan; quail eggs and shishito peppers are common additions.

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil with the onion, radish, garlic, ginger, green onion and peppercorns and boil, covered, for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the beef, return to a gentle boil and skim. Cover and simmer over medium heat for about 30 minutes, until the beef is cooked through. Do not add the soy sauce yet: salting the meat early tightens it and keeps it tough.
  3. Lift out the beef, strain the broth and return 600 ml (2.5 cups) of it to the pot with both soy sauces, the sugar and rice wine.
  4. Add the beef, bring to a boil over high heat, then cover and simmer over medium heat for about 20 minutes, until the beef is tender and the sauce has reduced to about a third.
  5. Add the kelp, quail eggs and peppers and simmer for 10 minutes more, turning the eggs so they color evenly. Discard the kelp and let everything cool in the sauce.
  6. Shred the beef along the grain into thin strips with your fingers and store it with the eggs and peppers in the sauce. It is a banchan (side dish), eaten cold or at room temperature with rice, and keeps for up to a week in the refrigerator.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding soy at the start toughens the beef; cook it until tender first.
  • Overcooking the peppers makes them drab.

Authentic tips

  • Spoon a little of the liquid over rice with butter for a childhood favorite.
  • Lean cuts are traditional so it keeps well cold.

Substitutions

  • Chicken eggs, halved, replace quail eggs.
  • Pork shoulder for dwaeji jangjorim.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 weeks in the refrigerator in its liquid.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Rice and other banchan
  • Soup: Miyeokguk
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
