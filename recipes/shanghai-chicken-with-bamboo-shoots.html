Shanghai Chicken with Bamboo Shoots 笋尖烧鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Bamboo shoots are prized in Jiangnan cooking for their delicate crunch and ability to soak up braising liquid, making this a popular springtime dish when fresh shoots are at their sweetest.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 700 g1.5 lbChicken thighs, bone-in, cut into pieces
  • 250 g9 ozBamboo shoots, sliced (fresh or canned)
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 g1 tbspRock sugar
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 250 ml1 cupChicken stock or water

Method

  1. If using fresh bamboo shoots, blanch in boiling water 5 minutes to remove bitterness; drain.
  2. Brown chicken pieces in a little oil until golden; add ginger and stir briefly.
  3. Add both soy sauces, wine, and rock sugar, stirring to coat the chicken.
  4. Add stock and bamboo shoots, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 20 minutes.
  5. Uncover and reduce 5 minutes more until the sauce thickens slightly.
  6. Serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanching step for fresh bamboo shoots, which leaves an unpleasant bitter or chalky taste.
  • Overcooking the bamboo shoots, which can turn them mushy instead of pleasantly crisp-tender.

Authentic tips

  • Canned bamboo shoots (rinsed well) are a perfectly fine, widely available substitute for fresh.
  • This dish is traditionally made in spring but works well year-round with canned shoots.

Substitutions

  • Shiitake mushrooms can be added alongside or instead of bamboo shoots for extra umami.
  • Bone-in drumsticks braise well with the same method and timing.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though bamboo shoots soften somewhat.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried baby bok choy
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
