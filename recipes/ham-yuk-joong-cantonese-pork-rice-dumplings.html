Ham Yuk Joong (Cantonese Salted Pork Rice Dumplings) 咸肉粽

▾

Surprise me with

Joong (zongzi), glutinous rice parcels wrapped in bamboo leaves, are eaten across China for the Dragon Boat Festival; the Cantonese and Toisanese style is savory, with marinated pork belly, mung beans and salted egg yolk.

Method

  1. Soak the glutinous rice and the mung beans in separate bowls of water overnight. Soak the bamboo leaves in hot water for at least 2 hours, weighted down, then scrub each one on both sides and trim off the stiff stem end. Soak the string with them so it does not snap.
  2. Toss the pork belly with the salt, five-spice and wine and refrigerate for at least 4 hours or overnight. Soak the mushrooms in warm water for 20 minutes and halve them.
  3. Drain the rice and mung beans well. Toss the rice with the rice salt and oil; the oil keeps it from sticking to the leaves. Keep the mung beans separate.
  4. Overlap two leaves, smooth sides up, and fold them across the middle into a cone with a pointed bottom. Add a spoonful of rice, then a spoonful of mung beans, a piece of pork, a mushroom half and a salted egg yolk, another spoonful of mung beans and enough rice to cover. Press down gently; leave a little room, as the rice swells.
  5. Fold the top of the leaves down over the filling, then fold the sides in to form a pyramid. Wrap the string around it several times and tie it firmly, tight enough that the rice cannot leak but not so tight that the leaves split. Repeat to make 8.
  6. Put the dumplings in a large pot, cover with boiling water by at least 5 cm (2 in) and set a plate on top to keep them submerged. Bring back to a boil, then simmer, covered, for 4 hours, topping up with boiling water whenever the level drops so they are always covered. Long, gentle cooking turns the rice and beans into a dense, creamy whole.
  7. Lift the dumplings out and drain for 10 minutes. Cut the string and unwrap at the table. They keep in the refrigerator for a week and reheat by steaming for 20 minutes.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Loose wrapping lets water in and the rice turns mushy; tie them firmly.
  • Letting the water drop below the dumplings leaves hard spots.

Authentic tips

  • A pressure cooker cuts the cooking time to 1 hour on high.
  • Wrapping is easier with a partner tying the string.

Substitutions

  • Add chestnuts or dried shrimp to the filling.
  • Leave out the salted yolks if unavailable.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days in the refrigerator; reboil or steam for 20 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 6 months; boil from frozen for 40 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Light soy sauce or sugar for dipping
  • Soup: Not needed; the dumplings are a meal
  • Dessert: Sweet alkaline dumplings (gan shui zong)
  • Beverage: Hot tea
