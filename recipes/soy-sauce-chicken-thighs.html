Soy Sauce Chicken Thighs 豉油鸡

▾

Surprise me with

A Cantonese restaurant and home-cooking classic, soy sauce chicken is built on a reusable master stock (lo sui) of soy, rock sugar, and spices — the same stock can be topped up and reused for weeks.

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, bone-in skin-on
  • 10 g2 tspSalt, for rubbing the skin
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 25 g1 ozGinger, sliced
  • 22Shallots, quartered
  • 3 stalks3 stalksGreen onion, cut into lengths
  • 22Star anise, lightly crushed
  • 60 ml1/4 cupShaoxing wine
  • 150 ml2/3 cupLight soy sauce
  • 45 ml3 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 50 g1/4 cupRock sugar (or brown sugar)
  • 500 ml2 cupsWater
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch, for the sauce
  • 3 ml1/2 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Take the thighs out of the refrigerator 30 minutes ahead. Rub the skin firmly with the salt, rinse it off and pat dry; this cleans the skin so it takes the soy color evenly instead of in blotches. For tighter, glossier skin, pour a kettle of boiling water over them in a colander and drain.
  2. Choose a pot just wide enough to hold the thighs in one snug layer, so the liquid covers them. Heat the oil over medium heat and fry the ginger for 20 seconds, the shallots for 20 seconds, then the star anise and green onion for 10 seconds, until fragrant.
  3. Add half the wine, then the soy sauces, water and rock sugar, then the rest of the wine. Simmer gently for 5 to 10 minutes, stirring until the sugar dissolves, so the spices flavor the liquid before the chicken goes in.
  4. Lower in the thighs skin-side down and bring back to a bare simmer, with only a few bubbles breaking the surface; a rolling boil tears the skin and toughens the meat. Cover and cook 15 minutes, spooning liquid over any exposed parts and turning the thighs skin-side up halfway.
  5. Turn off the heat and leave the thighs covered in the hot liquid for 15 minutes; they finish cooking gently and darken further. They are done when a chopstick pushed into the thickest part by the bone gives clear juices, or a thermometer reads 74 C (165 F).
  6. Lift the thighs onto a rack and let them cool for 10 minutes; resting them on a bag of ice speeds this and firms the skin. Meanwhile bring 3 ladles of the braising liquid to a boil in a small pan, stir in the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons of water and simmer until glossy, then add the sesame oil.
  7. Chop the thighs through the bone into bite-size pieces and spoon the thickened sauce over. Strain the rest of the braising liquid, boil it and keep it in the refrigerator for up to two weeks or freeze it as a master stock; it gets richer each time you reuse it.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard instead of gently poaching, which toughens the thigh meat.
  • Discarding the poaching liquid — strained and refrigerated, it becomes a reusable master stock that only improves with age.

Authentic tips

  • The resting step off heat is what keeps the chicken silky rather than dry; don't skip it.
  • Save and freeze the master stock; each use deepens its flavor.

Substitutions

  • Chicken drumsticks work identically.
  • No rock sugar: brown sugar in the same quantity is a fine substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; slice cold over rice or noodles.

Freezing: The meat freezes acceptably for a month; the master stock freezes excellently.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed jasmine rice with the pan sauce spooned over
  • Soup: Blanched choy sum with oyster sauce
  • Dessert: Chilled mango pudding
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
