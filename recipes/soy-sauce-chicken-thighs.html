Soy Sauce Chicken Thighs 豉油鸡

A Cantonese restaurant and home-cooking classic, soy sauce chicken is built on a reusable master stock (lo sui) of soy, rock sugar, and spices — the same stock can be topped up and reused for weeks.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken thighs, bone-in skin-on
  • 125 ml1/2 cupLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar (or brown sugar)
  • 60 ml1/4 cupShaoxing wine
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 3 stalks3 stalksGreen onion
  • 22Star anise
  • 500 ml2 cupsWater

Method

  1. Combine soy sauces, sugar, wine, ginger, green onion, star anise, and water in a pot; bring to a simmer.
  2. Lower the chicken thighs into the simmering liquid, skin-side down.
  3. Poach gently, partially covered, 18–20 minutes, turning once, until just cooked through.
  4. Turn off heat and let the chicken rest in the hot liquid 10 minutes to finish cooking gently and absorb flavor.
  5. Remove chicken and brush with a little of the reduced sauce for shine.
  6. Slice and serve over rice with the sauce spooned on top; reserve remaining sauce (the master stock) for next time.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard instead of gently poaching, which toughens the thigh meat.
  • Discarding the poaching liquid — strained and refrigerated, it becomes a reusable master stock that only improves with age.

Authentic tips

  • The resting step off heat is what keeps the chicken silky rather than dry; don't skip it.
  • Save and freeze the master stock; each use deepens its flavor.

Substitutions

  • Chicken drumsticks work identically.
  • No rock sugar: brown sugar in the same quantity is a fine substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; slice cold over rice or noodles.

Freezing: The meat freezes acceptably for a month; the master stock freezes excellently.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed jasmine rice with the pan sauce spooned over
  • Soup: Blanched choy sum with oyster sauce
  • Dessert: Chilled mango pudding
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
