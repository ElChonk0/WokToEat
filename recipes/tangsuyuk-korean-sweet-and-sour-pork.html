Tangsuyuk (Korean Sweet and Sour Pork) 탕수육

▾

Surprise me with

Tangsuyuk is a cornerstone of Korean-Chinese (junghwa) restaurants, brought by Shandong immigrants to Incheon; its crust is a chewy-crisp potato starch batter and the sauce is lighter than Cantonese versions.

Method

  1. Soak the batter starch in the soaking water for 1 to 3 hours until it settles into a thick layer, then pour off the clear water on top. This wet-starch batter is what gives tangsuyuk its distinctive glassy, chewy-crisp coating.
  2. Season the pork with the salt, pepper and ginger. Heat the sauce water with the soy sauce, sugar, vinegar and salt until the sugar dissolves, then turn off the heat. Mix the sauce starch with the slurry water.
  3. Mix the settled starch with the egg and batter oil into a thick paste and coat the pork in it.
  4. Heat the frying oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok to 175 C (350 F). Fry the pork in two or three batches for about 3 minutes, separating the pieces as they go in, until set and pale golden. Drain on a rack.
  5. Bring the oil back to 175 C (350 F) and fry all the pork again for 2 to 3 minutes until crisp and golden. The second fry is what keeps it crunchy.
  6. Bring the sauce back to a boil, add the onion, carrot and mushrooms, then stir in the slurry until thick and glossy. Turn off the heat and add the cucumber so it stays green and crisp.
  7. Pour the sauce over the pork or serve it alongside for dipping, and eat at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the starch soak gives a thin, powdery crust instead of the chewy-crisp Korean shell.
  • Pouring sauce over long before eating softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Korea famously argues over dipping versus pouring; serving the sauce on the side keeps everyone happy.
  • Wood ear and fruit like pineapple or apple are common additions to the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh strips work the same way.
  • Cornstarch can replace potato starch, but the crust is less chewy.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator, sauce separate; re-crisp the pork in a hot oven or air fryer.

Freezing: The fried pork freezes for 2 months; make the sauce fresh.
