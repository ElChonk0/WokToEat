Tangsuyuk (Korean Sweet and Sour Pork) 탕수육

Tangsuyuk is a cornerstone of Korean-Chinese (junghwa) restaurants, brought by Shandong immigrants to Incheon; its crust is a chewy-crisp potato starch batter and the sauce is lighter than Cantonese versions.

Method

  1. Season the pork with the salt, pepper and ginger. Soak the potato starch in the water for 30 minutes until it settles, pour off the clear water on top, and mix the thick starch with the egg white into a heavy batter. Coat the pork.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 170 C (340 F). Fry the pork in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until pale golden, drain, then refry everything at 185 C (365 F) for 2 minutes until crisp and hard.
  3. Bring the sauce water, sugar, vinegar and soy sauce to a boil in a pan with the onion, carrot and wood ear and cook 1 minute.
  4. Stir in the potato starch mixed with a little water and cook until thick and glossy, then add the cucumber.
  5. Serve the pork with the sauce on the side for dipping (jjikmeok) or poured over (bumeok), as each diner prefers.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the starch soak gives a thin, powdery crust instead of the chewy-crisp Korean shell.
  • Pouring sauce over long before eating softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Korea famously argues over dipping versus pouring; serving the sauce on the side keeps everyone happy.
  • Wood ear and fruit like pineapple or apple are common additions to the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh strips work the same way.
  • Cornstarch can replace potato starch, but the crust is less chewy.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator, sauce separate; re-crisp the pork in a hot oven or air fryer.

Freezing: The fried pork freezes for 2 months; make the sauce fresh.
