Beef Pares (Filipino Beef Stew with Garlic Rice)

▾

Surprise me with

Pares, beef braised in soy sauce and star anise and served with garlic rice and soup, started in Manila's Chinese-Filipino eateries and is a 24-hour street meal.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and saute the garlic, onion and ginger for 3 minutes. Add the beef and brown it for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the star anise, soy sauce, brown sugar and water, bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 2 hours, until the beef is very tender. Star anise is the signature aroma of pares.
  3. Thicken the sauce with the cornstarch slurry and simmer for 2 minutes until glossy. Season with pepper.
  4. Make sinangag: fry the rice garlic in a little oil over medium-low heat until golden, add the rice and a pinch of salt and toss over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes until hot.
  5. Serve the beef and sauce with the garlic rice and a bowl of the hot broth (sabaw) on the side, topped with green onion and fried garlic. Pares means 'pair', for the stew and rice served together.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.
  • Burning the sugar makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Add a little cornstarch slurry for a thicker sauce.
  • Pares means pair: the beef is paired with garlic rice.

Substitutions

  • Short rib replaces brisket.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic rice and the beef broth
