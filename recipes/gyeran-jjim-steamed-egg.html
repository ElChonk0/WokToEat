Gyeran Jjim (Korean Steamed Egg) 계란찜

▾

Gyeran jjim, a fluffy steamed egg served in an earthenware pot, is a staple Korean banchan.

  • 55Eggs

  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt or salted shrimp (saeujeot)
  • 3 ml1/2 tspNeutral oil, for the pot
  • 30 g2 tbspScallion, finely chopped
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 2 g1/2 tspToasted sesame seeds

Method

  1. Beat the eggs well, then strain them through a sieve for a silky texture. Stir in the stock and salt; about 60 ml (1/4 cup) of liquid per egg gives a soft, fluffy set.
  2. Lightly oil a small earthenware pot (ttukbaegi) or heavy saucepan and preheat it over medium heat for a minute.
  3. Pour in the egg mixture and cook, uncovered, stirring gently from the bottom, until the edges start to set and the mixture thickens to a loose custard, about 2 minutes.
  4. Turn the heat to low, cover with a lid (a small bowl over the top makes it puff up dramatically) and cook for 5 to 7 minutes without lifting the lid, until the egg has risen and set in the center. Add the scallion in the last minute. Keep the heat low, or the bottom scorches before the center sets.
  5. Drizzle with sesame oil, sprinkle with seeds and serve bubbling in the pot. For a gentler, smoother version, steam it in a covered bowl over simmering water for 12 minutes instead.

Common mistakes

  • High heat makes it tough and pitted.
  • Not straining leaves a lumpy texture.

Authentic tips

  • Use an earthenware pot (ttukbaegi) if it is rated for your stove; on induction use a heavy lidded pot or an induction-safe version.
  • The correct ratio is about 1:1 egg to stock.

Substitutions

  • Water replaces stock.
  • A microwave bowl works.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
