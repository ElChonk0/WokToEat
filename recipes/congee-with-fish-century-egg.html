Congee with Fish & Century Egg

Congee (jook) is one of the oldest and most universal comfort foods across China, a slow-cooked rice porridge that becomes a blank canvas for whatever protein and toppings a household has on hand — fish and century egg is a classic Cantonese combination.

Method

  1. Rinse the rice briefly, then combine with the stock in a large pot.
  2. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer; cook uncovered, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking, 45–50 minutes until the rice breaks down into a thick, creamy porridge.
  3. If the congee thickens too much before serving, loosen it with a little extra hot stock or water.
  4. Stir in half the ginger and the chopped century egg in the last 10 minutes of cooking.
  5. Just before serving, stir in the raw sliced fish — the residual heat of the congee will gently cook it through in a minute or two.
  6. Season with soy sauce and white pepper, and finish with a drizzle of sesame oil.
  7. Garnish with the remaining ginger, green onion, and fried shallots; serve with youtiao on the side if desired.

Common mistakes

  • Not stirring occasionally during the long simmer, which lets the rice settle and scorch on the bottom of the pot.
  • Adding the raw fish too early and letting it overcook in the simmering congee, rather than off the heat at the very end.

Authentic tips

  • Freezing leftover rice and using it (slightly broken up) as a head start can speed up congee considerably, since previously cooked rice breaks down faster than raw grains.
  • This is a forgiving, endlessly adaptable dish — leftover roast chicken, shredded pork, or even a soft-boiled egg all work in place of fish.

Substitutions

  • Skip the century egg if it's not to your taste — it's a textural and flavor accent, not essential to the dish's structure.
  • Ground pork meatballs, simmered directly in the congee, make a heartier variation.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps for 2–3 days; thins out over time, so loosen with extra stock when reheating.

Freezing: Freezes well without the raw fish; add freshly sliced fish to the reheated congee.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Youtiao (fried dough sticks)
  • Soup: Not needed; the congee is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
