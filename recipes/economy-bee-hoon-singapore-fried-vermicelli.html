Economy Bee Hoon (Singapore Fried Vermicelli)

▾

Surprise me with

Economy bee hoon is a Singapore breakfast of fried rice vermicelli with cabbage, bean sprouts and egg, sold at hawker stalls with a choice of sides.

Method

  1. Soak the vermicelli in warm water for 20 to 30 minutes until pliable, then drain well. Mix the soy sauces, pepper and half the stock.
  2. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a nonstick pan over medium-high heat and fry the tofu until golden, then lift it out. Beat and fry the eggs into a thin omelet, then slice it into strips.
  3. Heat the remaining oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and fry the garlic for 10 seconds, then the cabbage and carrot for 2 minutes until wilting.
  4. Add the vermicelli and the soy mixture and toss with chopsticks, adding the rest of the stock 1 to 2 tablespoons at a time, until the noodles are evenly brown, soft and dry, not wet. Adding liquid gradually stops the noodles turning mushy.
  5. Toss in the bean sprouts for 1 minute, then pile onto plates with the tofu, omelet strips and green onion. Serve with sambal, the classic breakfast plate.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Soaking too long makes the noodles mushy; they need only 10 minutes.
  • Overcrowding the wok steams the noodles; fry in two batches if needed.

Authentic tips

  • Add the stock a little at a time so the noodles soften but do not stew.
  • Traditional versions add luncheon meat or fish cake.

Substitutions

  • Egg noodles replace vermicelli.
  • Shredded chicken or prawns make it non-vegetarian.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sambal and fried egg
  • Soup: Not needed
