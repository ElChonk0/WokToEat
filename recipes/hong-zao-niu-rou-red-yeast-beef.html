Hóng Zāo Niú Ròu (Red Yeast Rice Beef) 红糟牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Red yeast rice lees, the signature Fuzhou seasoning, flavor beef as well as pork and fish, giving a winey color and sweetness.

Method

  1. Red wine lees (hong zao) is the sediment left from brewing Fujian red rice wine with red yeast rice. It gives Fuzhou dishes their deep red color and a sweet, winey, slightly fermented aroma. Toss the beef with the marinade lees, half the wine and the cornstarch and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and sear the beef for 1 to 2 minutes until just cooked. Lift it out.
  3. Lower the heat, add the remaining oil and fry the garlic, ginger and sauce lees for 1 minute, stirring; frying the lees in oil is what releases their fragrance and color.
  4. Add the remaining wine, sugar, soy sauce and water and bubble for 30 seconds.
  5. Return the beef and toss until glossy and red. Scatter with green onion and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving excess lees burns in the pan.
  • Overcooking makes the beef tough.

Authentic tips

  • Red yeast rice lees are sold in Chinese groceries; red rice wine is a rough substitute.
  • The dish should be savory with a touch of sweetness.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus a pinch of paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • Pork takes the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
