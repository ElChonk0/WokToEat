Dan Dan Noodles with Pork 担担面

▾

Surprise me with

Named for the shoulder poles (dan dan) once used by street vendors to carry noodles through Chengdu, this dish tops wheat noodles with a spicy, numbing sauce and preserved vegetable-seasoned ground pork.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry the pork for 5 to 6 minutes, breaking it up, until it is dry, brown and crisp. Add the Shaoxing wine, sweet bean sauce, dark soy sauce and ya cai and fry for 1 minute more.
  2. Divide the sesame paste, chili oil, light soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, Sichuan pepper and garlic among four bowls.
  3. Cook the noodles according to the packet, blanching the greens for the last minute. Stir 2 tablespoons of the hot cooking water into each bowl to loosen the sauce.
  4. Drain the noodles and greens and divide them among the bowls.
  5. Top with the crispy pork, green onion and peanuts. Mix thoroughly from the bottom before eating so the sauce coats every strand.

Common mistakes

  • Making the sauce too thick without loosening it with noodle water, which makes it hard to coat the noodles evenly.
  • Skipping the preserved mustard greens (ya cai) — they're a defining, distinctly savory-tangy component of authentic dan dan noodles.

Authentic tips

  • Ya cai is sold in small packets at Chinese grocers and keeps for a long time — worth seeking out specifically for this dish.
  • This dish is meant to be mixed thoroughly right before eating, sauce and toppings combined into the noodles at the table.

Substitutions

  • No sesame paste: well-stirred natural peanut butter works as a substitute, giving a slightly different but still good flavor.
  • Ground chicken or beef can replace pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; store sauce, noodles, and topping separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Blanched greens or pickles
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold lager
