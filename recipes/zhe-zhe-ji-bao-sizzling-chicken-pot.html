Zhě Zhě Jī Bāo (Sizzling Ginger-Shallot Chicken Pot) 啫啫鸡煲

▾

Surprise me with

Jeejee (啫啫) pots are a Guangdong specialty named for the sizzling sound they make: chicken is cooked fast in a very hot covered pot with ginger, shallots, garlic and sauce, and brought to the table still hissing.

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the marinade oyster sauce, soy sauce, wine, cornstarch and pepper and leave for 20 minutes. Mix the sauce oyster sauce, soy sauces, sugar and salt in a cup, and the slurry cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water. Soak the mushrooms fully in warm water for 20 minutes first; a quick rinse leaves them leathery.
  2. Heat a heavy Dutch oven or cast-iron pot over medium-high heat until very hot, then add 2 tablespoons of oil. Lower the heat to medium and fry the ginger for 1 minute, then the shallots and garlic for 2 to 3 minutes, until golden at the edges but not burnt; scorched garlic turns bitter.
  3. Raise the heat to medium-high, spread the chicken over the aromatics skin-side down in one layer and leave it for 2 to 3 minutes to sear without stirring. Turn the pieces and add the mushrooms, sausage and remaining oil, tossing for 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the sauce and hot water, bring to a boil, then cover and cook over medium heat for 10 to 12 minutes, shaking the pot now and then, until the chicken is cooked through and the liquid has reduced to a few spoonfuls.
  5. Uncover, stir in the slurry and toss until the sauce turns into a sticky glaze. Add the sesame oil and scatter the green onion on top.
  6. Cover and raise the heat to high. When you see steam escaping, after 30 to 40 seconds, pour the finishing wine around the edge of the lid so it hits the hot rim and sizzles, then cook for 30 seconds more. Bring the pot to the table still covered and lift the lid there; the hiss and aroma of the wine is the point of the dish.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too low a heat stews the chicken; the pot must sizzle throughout.
  • Stirring too early tears the skin and stops it browning.

Authentic tips

  • A clay pot is the traditional vessel; a heavy Dutch oven holds heat just as well on electric and induction hobs.
  • Pouring the wine around the rim at the end is what makes the signature sizzle and aroma.

Substitutions

  • Boneless thighs: cook 7 to 8 minutes covered.
  • Dry sherry replaces Shaoxing wine.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat covered in a hot pan.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic lettuce
  • Soup: Tomato tofu soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Cold beer
