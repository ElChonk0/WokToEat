Liáng Miàn (Taiwanese Cold Sesame Noodles) 涼麵

▾

Surprise me with

Taiwanese cold noodles, with sesame paste, garlic and cucumber, are a summer street and convenience-store staple.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 6 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozAlkaline wheat noodles
  • 60 g4 tbspSesame paste
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspBlack vinegar
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 150 g5 ozCucumber, julienned
  • 100 g3.5 ozCarrot, julienned
  • 10 g2 tspSugar

Method

  1. Cook the noodles until just tender, drain and rinse under cold water. Toss with a little oil and spread them out, fanning to cool; this keeps them springy.
  2. Thin the sesame paste with 3 to 4 tablespoons of water, a spoonful at a time, stirring until smooth and pourable.
  3. Stir in the soy sauce, black vinegar, sugar and garlic. Taiwanese liang mian sauce is nutty, sweet and tangy, with less heat than the Sichuan version.
  4. Pile the noodles in bowls and top with the cucumber and carrot.
  5. Pour the sauce over and toss before eating. Add chili oil at the table if you like.

Common mistakes

  • Sticky noodles clump; toss with oil.
  • A thick sauce won't coat; thin it with water.

Authentic tips

  • Add shredded chicken for more protein.
  • A convenience-store staple.

Substitutions

  • Soba replaces alkaline noodles.
  • Tahini replaces sesame paste.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; loosen with water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cold cucumber salad
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
