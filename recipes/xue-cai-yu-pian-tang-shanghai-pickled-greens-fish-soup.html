Xuě Cài Yú Piàn Tāng (Pickled Greens Fish Slice Soup) 雪菜鱼片汤

▾

Surprise me with

Xue cai, salted pickled mustard greens, is a Jiangnan staple that pairs with fish in soups and noodle dishes.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

4servings (recipe serves 4)

  • 450 g1 lbHaddock fillet, sliced 5 mm thick
  • 10 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, for the fish
  • 100 g3.5 ozXue cai (pickled mustard greens), rinsed and chopped
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 1.2 L5 cupsWater or light stock
  • 200 g7 ozFirm tofu, in cubes
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, for the soup
  • 20 g3/4 ozScallions, sliced

Method

  1. Toss the fish slices with the cornstarch and salt and refrigerate while you make the broth.
  2. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat, fry the ginger and xue cai for 2 minutes until fragrant, then add the wine.
  3. Add the water, bring to a boil, add the tofu, and simmer 8 minutes.
  4. Season with the white pepper and soup salt, reduce to a gentle simmer, and slide in the fish for 2 minutes until opaque.
  5. Scatter with the scallions and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for haddock fillet.

Common mistakes

  • Unrinsed xue cai makes the soup too salty.
  • Boiling hard once the fish is in breaks the slices.

Authentic tips

  • Taste the xue cai before salting; brands vary widely.
  • Add noodles to make a Shanghai-style fish noodle soup.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin replaces the haddock.
  • Suan cai replaces xue cai for a sourer broth.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: The recipe is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
