Opor Ayam

▾

Surprise me with

A mild, fragrant coconut chicken curry traditionally served during Eid celebrations in Indonesia, opor ayam relies on coriander, cumin, and candlenut for warmth rather than chili heat.

Method

  1. Blend shallot, garlic, candlenut, coriander, and cumin into a paste.
  2. Fry the paste with galangal in a little oil until fragrant, about 3 minutes.
  3. Add chicken and stir to coat, cooking 2–3 minutes.
  4. Pour in coconut milk and add kaffir lime leaves; bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Cover and simmer 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is tender and the sauce has thickened slightly.
  6. Season with salt and sugar to taste before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the coconut milk hard, which can cause it to separate into an oily, curdled texture.
  • Skipping the paste-frying step, which leaves the final dish tasting flat and underdeveloped.

Authentic tips

  • This curry should be mild and fragrant rather than spicy — it's designed to be a gentler counterpoint to spicier dishes on a festive table.
  • Stirring occasionally during the simmer helps prevent the coconut milk from separating.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in thighs add flavor.
  • Light coconut milk makes a lighter opor.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month; the coconut milk may separate slightly.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Ketupat or lontong with sambal goreng
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is creamy
  • Dessert: Kue lapis
  • Beverage: Hot sweet tea
