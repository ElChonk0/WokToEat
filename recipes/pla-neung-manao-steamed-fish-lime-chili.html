Pla Neung Manao (Thai Steamed Fish with Lime and Chili)

▾

Surprise me with

Pla neung manao, fish steamed and drenched in a sharp lime, garlic and bird's eye chili sauce, is a Thai restaurant classic, especially associated with the seafood restaurants of Bangkok and the coast.

Method

  1. Score the fish on both sides down to the bone and stuff the cavity with lemongrass and celery. This helps it steam evenly and lets the sauce reach the flesh.
  2. Pound the garlic, chilies and cilantro roots into a coarse paste and stir in the lime juice, fish sauce, sugar and hot stock until dissolved. It should be very sour, salty and hot.
  3. Put the fish on a heatproof plate and steam over boiling water in a flat-bottom wok, covered, for 12 to 15 minutes, until the flesh at the thickest part flakes from the bone.
  4. Pour off the fishy steaming liquid from the plate.
  5. Pour the sauce over the hot fish, scatter with cilantro and lime slices and serve right away, ideally over a burner to keep it hot as you eat.

Common mistakes

  • Oversteaming makes the fish dry.
  • Prepared sauce sitting too long loses its sharpness.

Authentic tips

  • Serve at once from the plate.
  • Taste the sauce; it should be sharp, salty and hot.

Substitutions

  • Fillets steam in 8 minutes.
  • A bamboo steamer replaces a plate.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat within the hour.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
