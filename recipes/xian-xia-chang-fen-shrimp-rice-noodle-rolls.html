Xiān Xiā Cháng Fěn (Shrimp Rice Noodle Rolls) 鲜虾肠粉

▾

Surprise me with

Cheung fun are silky steamed rice-noodle sheets wrapped around fillings; shrimp is the classic version found in Cantonese dim sum restaurants.

  • 180 g1.5 cupsRice flour

  • 30 g4 tbspWheat starch
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the batter
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil, for the batter
  • 350 ml1.5 cupsWater, room temperature
  • 240 ml1 cupWater, boiling
  • 300 g10 ozShrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 g1/4 tspSalt, for the shrimp
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspGround white pepper
  • 3 g1 tspCornstarch, for the shrimp
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil, for the pans
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 45 ml3 tbspWater, for the sweet soy
  • 10 g2 tspSugar

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, wheat starch, salt, batter oil and room-temperature water until smooth, then stir in the boiling water. Rest the batter for at least 30 minutes; a rested batter makes sheets that roll without cracking.
  2. Pat the shrimp dry, halve any large ones lengthwise and toss with the salt, pepper, cornstarch and sesame oil. Simmer the soy sauce, water and sugar for 1 minute to make the sweet soy.
  3. Oil two 23 cm (9 in) square metal pans. In a wide pot with a tight lid, set a small rack and 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of water and bring it to a full boil.
  4. Warm one pan in the steamer, stir the batter, pour in about 120 ml (1/2 cup) and tilt to coat the base. Scatter a sixth of the shrimp over it, cover and steam for 3 to 4 minutes, until the sheet is translucent and puffed and the shrimp are pink.
  5. Lift the pan out onto a towel and start the next sheet in the second pan. Loosen the sheet with a spatula and roll it up, then slide it onto a plate. Oil the pan again before each sheet.
  6. Cut the rolls in half and drizzle with the sweet soy just before serving. Eat warm.

Common mistakes

  • A batter that has settled makes thick, rubbery sheets, so stir before every ladle.
  • Overcooking makes the shrimp tough; 3 minutes is enough.

Authentic tips

  • Serve immediately for the silkiest texture.
  • Slice the shrimp in half lengthwise if large so they cook evenly.

Substitutions

  • Char siu or beef make a classic alternative filling.
  • Chopped scallion and dried shrimp add flavor to the batter.

Storage

Leftovers: Best within an hour; leftovers keep 1 day and re-steam for 3 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended; the rice rolls turn hard.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Congee
  • Soup: Clear chicken soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
