Relyenong Bangus (Stuffed Milkfish)

▾

Surprise me with

Relyenong bangus, milkfish deboned, stuffed with its own flaked meat, vegetables and raisins, and fried, is a festive Filipino dish.

Method

  1. Pound the fish gently all over with the back of a knife to loosen the flesh from the skin, then break the collarbone and push the handle of a long spoon down between skin and flesh to separate them. Pull the flesh and spine out through the neck, keeping the skin intact like a bag. Marinate the skin in soy sauce and calamansi.
  2. Boil the flesh for 5 minutes, then flake it and pick out every bone; milkfish has many fine ones, so take your time.
  3. Saute the garlic, onion and tomato in the oil for 3 minutes, add the flaked fish, carrot, peas, raisins, soy sauce, salt and pepper and cook for 5 minutes. Cool, then mix in one beaten egg and the flour.
  4. Stuff the mixture back into the fish skin, pressing it into a fish shape, and close the opening. Wrap it in foil or banana leaf and chill for 30 minutes to firm.
  5. Unwrap, brush with the remaining egg and fry in the oil over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes per side until golden. A Christmas and fiesta dish.
  6. Rest for 10 minutes, then slice and serve with banana ketchup.

Common mistakes

  • Overstuffing splits the skin.
  • Oil that is too hot browns the skin before the filling cooks.

Authentic tips

  • Ask the fishmonger to debone the fish.
  • Serve with banana ketchup.

Substitutions

  • Ask the fishmonger to debone the fish.
  • Tilapia replaces milkfish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Banana ketchup and rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Leche flan
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
