Málà Xiāng Guō (Sichuan Spicy Dry Pot with Pork) 麻辣香锅

▾

Surprise me with

Mala xiang guo is a Sichuan and Chongqing stir-fry of meat and vegetables chosen by the diner and tossed in a numbing, spicy sauce of chilies and Sichuan peppercorn.

Method

  1. Blanch the lotus root, potatoes and broccoli in boiling water for 1 minute each, then drain and dry.
  2. Heat 45 ml of the oil in a wok over medium heat, add the dried chilies, Sichuan peppercorns and star anise and fry for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the pork belly and stir-fry for 4 minutes until the fat renders and the edges crisp, then the doubanjiang, garlic and ginger for 1 minute.
  4. Add the remaining oil and the vegetables, tofu and enoki and stir-fry over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes.
  5. Season with the soy sauce, sugar and salt, toss for 1 minute, and finish with the cilantro and peanuts.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the dried chilies makes the dish bitter; fry them over medium heat for only a minute.
  • Crowding the wok steams the vegetables; cook in two batches if necessary.

Authentic tips

  • Blanch hard vegetables first so they finish at the same time as the soft ones.
  • Add chili oil at the end if you want more heat.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh or shrimp replaces pork.
  • Any firm vegetables, such as cauliflower or bamboo shoots, work.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced suan mei tang (sour plum drink)
