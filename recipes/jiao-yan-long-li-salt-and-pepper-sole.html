Jiāo Yán Lóng Lì (Salt-and-Pepper Sole) 椒盐龙脷

▾

Surprise me with

Strips of sole fried crisp and tossed with garlic, chili and spiced salt are a Hong Kong seafood restaurant staple of the jiu yim style.

Method

  1. Season the fish with the salt, pepper and two thirds of the ginger. Mix the spiced salt ingredients in a small bowl.
  2. Mix the egg yolk into the fish, then roll each piece in cornstarch until evenly coated. Cornstarch, not flour, gives the light, glassy crust.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 150 C (300 F). Lower the fish in in batches and leave it for 20 to 30 seconds before moving it, so the coating sets. Fry for about 4 minutes, until pale yellow and cooked through, and drain.
  4. Raise the oil to 200 C (400 F) and fry the pieces again for 30 to 40 seconds until golden and crisp. Drain on a rack.
  5. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Over high heat fry the garlic, remaining ginger and chili for 15 seconds, the red onion for 10 seconds and the green onion for 10 seconds.
  6. Lower the heat to medium, return the fish and toss gently for 15 to 20 seconds, sprinkling in the spiced salt a little at a time. Splash the wine around the edge, toss once more and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet fish spatters and the coating slides off; pat it dry.
  • Tossing too long breaks the delicate strips.

Authentic tips

  • Fry the garlic until just golden for "golden garlic" (gam sun), a popular variation.
  • Serve on shredded lettuce.

Substitutions

  • Cod or tilapia strips work.
  • Air fryer: spray and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; re-crisp leftovers in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic gai lan
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Cold beer
