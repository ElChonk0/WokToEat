Jiāo Yán Lóng Lì (Salt-and-Pepper Sole) 椒盐龙脷

Strips of sole fried crisp and tossed with garlic, chili and spiced salt are a Hong Kong seafood restaurant staple of the jiu yim style.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbSole or flounder fillets, cut into strips
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the fish
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 11Egg white
  • 60 g1/2 cupCornstarch
  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 6 cloves6 clovesGarlic, finely minced
  • 22Fresh red chili, chopped
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, chopped
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, toasted, for the spiced salt
  • 2 g1 tspGround white pepper
  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder

Method

  1. Season the fish with the salt and wine, then mix with the egg white and toss in the cornstarch.
  2. Mix the toasted salt, pepper and five-spice.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 180 C (350 F) and fry the fish in batches for 3 minutes until golden and crisp. Drain.
  4. Pour off all but a tablespoon of oil and fry the garlic, chili and green onion over medium heat for 30 seconds.
  5. Add the fish, sprinkle with the spiced salt and toss 20 seconds. Serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Wet fish spatters and the coating slides off; pat it dry.
  • Tossing too long breaks the delicate strips.

Authentic tips

  • Fry the garlic until just golden for "golden garlic" (gam sun), a popular variation.
  • Serve on shredded lettuce.

Substitutions

  • Cod or tilapia strips work.
  • Air fryer: spray and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; re-crisp leftovers in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic gai lan
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Cold beer
