Red-Braised Beef Shank 红烧牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Shanghai's hong shao technique applied to beef shank produces a glossy, deeply savory-sweet braise, traditionally served over noodles or rice — a comforting dish across Jiangnan households.

Method

  1. Blanch beef shank in boiling water 5 minutes; drain and rinse.
  2. Melt rock sugar in a dry pot over medium heat until amber.
  3. Add beef and turn to coat in the caramel.
  4. Add both soy sauces, wine, ginger, and star anise, stirring to coat.
  5. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer, cover, and braise 1.5–2 hours until fork-tender.
  6. Uncover and reduce over medium heat 10–15 minutes until the sauce is thick and glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long braise, which is essential for breaking down shank's connective tissue into tenderness.
  • Letting the caramel burn before adding the beef, which makes the whole dish bitter.

Authentic tips

  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better reheated the next day.
  • The broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a double batch and freeze half.

Substitutions

  • Brisket replaces the shank with a similar braise.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; slice cold or reheat in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
