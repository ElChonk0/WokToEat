Singapore Chili Crab

▾

Surprise me with

Chili crab, mud crab in a sweet, tangy tomato-chili sauce thickened with egg, was created in Singapore in 1956 by Cher Yam Tian and is widely regarded as the country's national dish.

Method

  1. Put a live crab in the freezer for 30 minutes so it goes still, then clean it, cut the body into pieces and crack the claws so the sauce soaks in. Blend the chilies, shallots, garlic, ginger and belacan into a paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a large flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the paste for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant and darker, then add the tau cheo for 30 seconds.
  3. Add the ketchup, chili sauce, sugar and salt, stir for 30 seconds, then pour in the stock and bring to a boil.
  4. Add the crab, cover and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, turning the pieces once or twice, until the shells are bright red and the meat is opaque. Precooked crab needs only 2 minutes.
  5. Stir in the slurry until the sauce thickens, then turn the heat down and drizzle in the egg while stirring slowly, so it forms silky ribbons through the sauce. Taste: it should be sweet, savory, tangy and gently hot.
  6. Serve at once with cilantro and green onion and plenty of mantou buns for mopping up the sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the crab dry.
  • Stirring the eggs too much makes them scramble instead of ribbon.

Authentic tips

  • Crack the claws before cooking so they absorb the sauce.
  • Mantou for mopping up the sauce is essential.

Substitutions

  • Dungeness crab replaces mud crab.
  • Large shrimp also work.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Side dish: Fried mantou buns

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
