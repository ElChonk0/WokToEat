Dotorimuk Muchim (Acorn Jelly Salad) 도토리묵무침

Dotorimuk, a jelly made from acorn starch, is a traditional Korean mountain food seasoned with soy sauce and sesame and served as a side.

Method

  1. If the acorn jelly has been refrigerated and turned firm and opaque, drop the slices into boiling water for 30 seconds until they turn glossy and wobbly again, then cool them in cold water and drain. This restores the soft, jiggly texture.
  2. Mix the soy sauce, gochugaru, sugar, vinegar, sesame oil, garlic and sesame seeds into a dressing.
  3. Cut the jelly into bite-size rectangles and the vegetables into similar-size pieces.
  4. Put the vegetables in a bowl, add the jelly on top and pour the dressing over. Toss very gently with your fingertips, as the jelly breaks easily.
  5. Serve straight away, before the vegetables wilt.

Common mistakes

  • Rough tossing breaks the jelly.
  • Dressing too early makes it soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Serve cold as a side.
  • Acorn jelly is sold at Korean markets.

Substitutions

  • Buckwheat jelly replaces acorn.
  • Add a few drops of vinegar.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Makgeolli or barley tea
