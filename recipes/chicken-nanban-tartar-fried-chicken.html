Chicken Nanban (Fried Chicken with Tartar Sauce) チキン南蛮

▾

Surprise me with

Chicken nanban, fried chicken dipped in a sweet vinegar sauce and topped with tartar sauce, was created in Miyazaki, Kyushu, in the 1950s and is a Japanese diner staple.

Method

  1. Make the tartar: squeeze the soaked onion dry (soaking takes the sharp bite away), then mix it with the chopped eggs, pickles, mayonnaise, lemon juice and a pinch of salt and pepper. Chill.
  2. Simmer the soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and mirin in a small pan for 1 minute until the sugar dissolves, then pour it into a wide dish. This sweet-sour nanban sauce is what defines the dish.
  3. Season the cutlets, dust them with flour, then dip them in the beaten egg. The egg coating, rather than crumbs, gives a soft, golden crust that soaks up the sauce.
  4. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and shallow-fry the chicken for 4 to 5 minutes, turning once, until golden and cooked through (74 C / 165 F). Raise the heat for the last 30 seconds per side to crisp it.
  5. While it is still hot, lay the chicken in the nanban sauce and turn it to coat; it absorbs the sauce best straight from the oil.
  6. Slice the chicken, arrange it over shredded cabbage and spoon the tartar sauce generously on top.

Common mistakes

  • Soaking too long makes the coating soggy.
  • Frying at too high a heat browns the coating before the chicken cooks.

Authentic tips

  • Tartar sauce with chopped egg and pickles is the Miyazaki style.
  • Serve with shredded cabbage and rice.

Substitutions

  • Breast works if pounded thin and fried briefly.
  • Mayonnaise mixed with chopped pickles and egg replaces tartar sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the sauce makes the coating soft within an hour.

Freezing: Freeze plain fried chicken for 1 month; re-sauce after reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and shredded cabbage
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
