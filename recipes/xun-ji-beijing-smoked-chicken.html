Xūn Jī (Beijing Smoked Chicken) 熏鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Smoked chicken, braised then smoked over sugar and tea, is a northern Chinese deli classic served cold, with a reddish-brown skin and sweet smoky flavor.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar, for the brine
  • 10 g1/3 ozStar anise, cinnamon, Sichuan pepper and fennel

  • 2 L8 cupsWater

  • 20 g1/4 cupBlack tea leaves, for smoking
  • 40 g3 tbspBrown sugar, for smoking
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Simmer the soy sauces, sugar, spices, ginger, green onion and water for 10 minutes to make a master brine. Add the chicken and poach at a bare simmer for 35 minutes, turning once, then leave it in the liquid for 20 minutes until it reaches 74 C (165 F) in the thigh.
  2. Lift the chicken out and pat it dry.
  3. Line a flat-bottom wok and its lid with heavy foil, scatter the rice, tea leaves and brown sugar in the bottom and set a rack above them. Put the chicken on the rack.
  4. Cover tightly and heat over medium-high until it starts to smoke, then lower to medium and smoke for 12 to 15 minutes. Leave covered off the heat for 5 minutes. Open the window; the sugar smokes heavily.
  5. Brush with sesame oil, rest for 10 minutes and chop. Serve cold or at room temperature.

Common mistakes

  • Smoking longer than 10 minutes makes the skin bitter and the meat dry.
  • Poor ventilation: open a window and run the extractor fan.

Authentic tips

  • Cool the braised chicken before smoking so the skin dries and takes the smoke.
  • The braising liquid is a good master sauce for next time.

Substitutions

  • Skip the smoking and finish the braised chicken under a hot broiler for 5 minutes for a quicker version.
  • Chicken legs only cook in 35 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; serve cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed buns
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
