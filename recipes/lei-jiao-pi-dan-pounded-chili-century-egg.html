Léi Jiāo Pí Dàn (Pounded Chili with Century Egg) 擂辣椒皮蛋

▾

Surprise me with

Lei jiao pi dan, century egg served with grilled green chilies pounded to a paste in a mortar, is a Hunan appetizer whose smoky, spicy sauce pairs with the cheesy egg.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 44Century eggs
  • 200 g7 ozLong green chilies
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspChinkiang vinegar
  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Char the chilies in a dry pan over high heat for 5-6 minutes, turning, until blistered and soft.
  2. Pound them in a mortar with the garlic, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and oil into a coarse paste.
  3. Peel the century eggs, rinse and cut into quarters.
  4. Arrange the eggs on a plate and spoon the chili paste over.

Common mistakes

  • Under-charring the chilies loses the smoky flavor.
  • Using eggs that are too soft makes them hard to cut; chill them first.

Authentic tips

  • A mortar gives the right coarse texture; a blender makes it too smooth.
  • Dip the knife in water between cuts for clean slices.

Substitutions

  • Soft-boiled eggs replace century eggs.
  • Jalapenos replace Hunan long chilies.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the chili mixture keeps 2 days separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or congee
  • Soup: Tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
