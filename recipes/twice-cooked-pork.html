Twice-Cooked Pork 回锅肉

▾

Surprise me with

One of Sichuan's most famous dishes, hui guo rou boils pork belly first, then slices and stir-fries it with doubanjiang and leeks — the double-cooking technique renders excess fat while keeping the meat tender.

  • 600 g1.3 lbPork belly, skin-on, whole piece
  • 30 g1 ozGinger and green onion, for boiling
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 30 g2 tbspPixian doubanjiang
  • 10 g1 tbspFermented black beans, rinsed
  • 15 ml1 tbspSweet bean sauce (tian mian jiang)
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 3 cloves3 clovesGarlic, sliced
  • 10 g1 tbspGinger, sliced
  • 22Leeks or garlic sprouts, cut on an angle
  • 11Green bell pepper, sliced

Method

  1. Put the pork belly in a pot of cold water with the ginger and green onion. Bring to a boil and simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, until just cooked through. This is the first cooking.
  2. Cool the pork, then chill it for 1 hour so it firms; this lets you cut it into thin, even slices about 3 mm (1/8 in) thick.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the pork slices for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until the fat renders and the slices curl into 'lamp-dish' cups with crisp edges. This is the second cooking.
  4. Push the pork aside and fry the doubanjiang in the rendered fat for 1 minute until the oil is red. Add the black beans, sweet bean sauce, garlic and ginger and stir for 30 seconds.
  5. Toss the pork through the sauce, add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauce and sugar, then the leeks and pepper. Stir-fry over high heat for 1 minute, until the leeks are just wilted and everything is glossy. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the boiling step and stir-frying raw pork belly directly, which leaves it greasy rather than properly rendered.
  • Slicing the pork too thick, which prevents the classic curled, crispy-edged texture.

Authentic tips

  • Cooling the boiled pork belly before slicing makes it much easier to cut thin, even pieces.
  • The pork should render enough fat in the dry pan that little to no additional oil is needed for the rest of the stir-fry.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder can substitute for belly for a leaner, less rich version.
  • No leek: extra green onion or even cabbage work as substitutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of the crisped edges is lost.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
