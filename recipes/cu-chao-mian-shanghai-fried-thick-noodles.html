Cū Chǎo Miàn (Shanghai Fried Thick Noodles) 粗炒面

▾

Surprise me with

Cu chao mian, thick noodles stir-fried with dark soy sauce and vegetables, is a Shanghai street food and a popular breakfast.

  • 400 g14 ozFresh thick Shanghai noodles
  • 250 g9 ozNapa cabbage or bok choy, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozShiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozBean sprouts
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspVegetarian mushroom sauce
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Boil the noodles for 1 to 2 minutes, just until loosened, then rinse under cold water and drain very well. Toss with a little oil. Mix the soy sauces, mushroom sauce and sugar.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the shiitake for 2 minutes, then the cabbage stems for 1 minute and the leaves until wilted. Lift them out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the noodles and let them sit for 30 seconds to sear before tossing. Pour in the sauce and toss with chopsticks for 2 to 3 minutes until every noodle is a deep, even brown.
  4. Return the vegetables with the bean sprouts and toss for 1 minute.
  5. Finish with white pepper and sesame oil and serve hot. (The classic version adds pork; this one is vegan.)

Common mistakes

  • Overcooked noodles go mushy in the wok.
  • Too much dark soy turns the dish bitter and salty.

Authentic tips

  • A very hot wok gives the char that defines the dish.
  • Add a fried egg on top for a traditional breakfast.

Substitutions

  • Udon or thick spaghetti replaces the Shanghai noodles.
  • Add fried tofu for protein.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Clear soup
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
