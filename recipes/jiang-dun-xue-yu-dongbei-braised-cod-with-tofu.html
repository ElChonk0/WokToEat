Jiàng Dùn Xuě Yú (Dongbei Cod and Tofu in Bean Paste) 酱炖鳕鱼豆腐

▾

Surprise me with

Dongbei (northeastern) home cooking stews fish with tofu in soybean paste (dou jiang), a simple, salty, hearty style suited to cold winters.

  • 500 g1 lb 2 ozCod loin, in 5 cm pieces
  • 300 g10 ozFirm tofu, in thick slabs
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 45 g3 tbspSoybean paste (huang dou jiang)
  • 15 g1/2 ozGinger, sliced
  • 4 cloves4 clovesGarlic, sliced
  • 40 g1.5 ozScallions, cut in lengths

  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 400 ml1 3/4 cupsWater
  • 10 g1/3 ozCilantro

Method

  1. Pat the cod and tofu dry. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat and brown the tofu 2 minutes per side; lift out.
  2. Sear the cod 1 minute per side, then push aside and fry the soybean paste, ginger, garlic, scallions and chilies for 1 minute.
  3. Add the soy sauce, sugar and water and bring to a boil.
  4. Return the tofu, cover and simmer 8 to 10 minutes, spooning the sauce over, until the cod is cooked and the sauce reduced by half.
  5. Finish with the cilantro and serve with rice.

Cook’s notes

Written for cod loin. Dongbei cooks stew river fish or sea fish in bean paste.

Common mistakes

  • Soybean paste is salty; taste before adding soy sauce.
  • Stirring breaks the fish; shake the pan instead.

Authentic tips

  • Browned tofu absorbs the sauce without falling apart.
  • Add a splash of vinegar at the end to lift the flavor.

Substitutions

  • Haddock replaces the cod.
  • Doubanjiang replaces part of the soybean paste for a spicier dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or cornbread
  • Soup: Pickled cabbage soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
