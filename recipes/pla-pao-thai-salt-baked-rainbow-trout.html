Pla Pao (Thai Salt-Baked Trout with Seafood Dipping Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

Pla pao, fish stuffed with lemongrass and herbs, coated in salt and grilled over charcoal, is a Thai street-food classic served with a sharp seafood dipping sauce.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 220°C (425°F) with a rack in the upper third. Pat the trout dry and stuff each with the lemongrass, lime leaves, crushed garlic and cilantro stems.
  2. Rub the skin with the oil and press the coarse salt all over it so it clings.
  3. Lay the fish on a wire rack over a foil-lined sheet pan and bake 18 to 22 minutes, turning once, until the skin is crisp and the flesh near the bone is opaque.
  4. Meanwhile stir the lime juice, fish sauce, sugar, minced garlic and chilies until the sugar dissolves and add the cilantro.
  5. Rest the fish 3 minutes, brush off any loose salt, and serve with the sauce and rice.

Cook’s notes

Written for whole rainbow trout. Thai street vendors use tilapia and grill it over charcoal; this version bakes it.

Common mistakes

  • Too much salt makes the skin inedible; brush off the excess before serving.
  • A wet fish steams instead of crisping.

Authentic tips

  • A grill over charcoal gives the traditional smoke; the oven is the indoor route.
  • The dipping sauce should be sour, salty and fiercely hot.

Substitutions

  • Tilapia or sea bream replaces the trout.
  • Use a hot grill pan for 6 to 8 minutes per side if you prefer.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice and green papaya salad
  • Soup: Tom yum
  • Dessert: Fresh mango
  • Beverage: Iced lime soda
