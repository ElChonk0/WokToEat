Lo Mai Fan (Cantonese Sticky Rice with Chinese Sausage) 糯米饭

▾

Surprise me with

Lo mai fan, glutinous rice stir-fried with Chinese sausage, dried shrimp and mushrooms, is a Cantonese winter dish and a Lunar New Year and Thanksgiving-table favorite in Chinese American families.

Method

  1. Rinse both rices together three times and drain well. A little jasmine rice lightens the texture so the dish is chewy rather than gummy.
  2. Stir the salt and rice dark soy into the boiling water and cook the rice in it, in a rice cooker or covered pot over low heat for 18 to 20 minutes, until tender. Starting with boiling water keeps the grains distinct and chewy.
  3. Meanwhile soak the shrimp and mushrooms in warm water for 15 minutes, then dice them to the same size as the sausage and pork belly so everything mixes evenly.
  4. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat with 1 tablespoon of oil until it ripples. Fry the garlic and shallots for 10 seconds, the shrimp for 10 seconds, the sausage and pork belly for 30 seconds until they turn translucent, then the mushrooms for 1 minute. Add the light soy, topping dark soy, oyster sauce and sugar and stir for 40 seconds, then lift it all onto a plate.
  5. Stir 1 tablespoon of oil into the cooked rice. Heat the last tablespoon of oil in the wok over high heat, add the rice and stir-fry for about 1.5 minutes, pressing and folding with the spatula, until it smells toasty. Taste and add a splash of light soy if needed.
  6. Lower the heat, return most of the topping and stir-fry for 2 to 3 minutes with chopsticks or a spatula until it is evenly mixed through. Add the green onion and cilantro and turn off the heat.
  7. Pile onto a plate and scatter the reserved topping over the rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding all the liquid at once makes it mushy; sprinkle it in gradually.
  • Using high heat at the end burns the sticky rice onto the wok.

Authentic tips

  • A rice cooker can do the whole thing: add everything with 300 ml liquid and use the normal setting.
  • Toasted peanuts are a common addition.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh, diced, replaces some of the sausage.
  • Add chestnuts for a festive version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; re-steam or microwave with a sprinkle of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic gai lan
  • Soup: Dried scallop and chicken soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
