Rendang Ayam

▾

Surprise me with

A slow-braised Minangkabau specialty from West Sumatra, now claimed and beloved across both Malaysia and Singapore, rendang cooks chicken in coconut milk and toasted spice paste until nearly dry and deeply caramelized.

Method

  1. Toast the grated coconut in a dry pan over low heat, stirring constantly, for 10 to 15 minutes until deep golden brown, then pound or blend it into an oily paste. This kerisik is what gives rendang its nutty richness and thickens the sauce.
  2. Blend the fresh and dried chilies, shallots, garlic, galangal, ginger and turmeric into a smooth paste.
  3. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy wok over medium heat and fry the paste with the lemongrass and lime leaves for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring, until fragrant and the oil starts to separate.
  4. Add the coconut milk and bring to a simmer, then add the chicken. Cover and cook gently for about 20 minutes, until the chicken is no longer pink inside.
  5. Add the kerisik, tamarind, palm sugar, salt and turmeric leaf and cook uncovered over medium-low heat for 15 to 25 minutes, stirring often, until the sauce has reduced to a thick, dark coating that clings to the chicken and the oil glistens on top. Stir more as it thickens so it does not catch.
  6. Taste for salt and sugar and serve with rice, ketupat or lemang. Like all rendang it tastes better the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — rendang is defined by patience; the sauce needs real time to darken and concentrate.
  • Skipping the kerisik (toasted coconut), which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • True rendang should end up almost dry, with the sauce clinging to the meat rather than pooling — keep reducing until you reach that point.
  • Kerisik can be bought ready-made or made by slowly toasting grated coconut in a dry pan until deep golden brown, then pounding it into a paste.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks work well with the same long braising method.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably, a hallmark of good rendang.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.

Serve with
