Rendang Ayam

▾

Surprise me with

A slow-braised Minangkabau specialty from West Sumatra, now claimed and beloved across both Malaysia and Singapore, rendang cooks chicken in coconut milk and toasted spice paste until nearly dry and deeply caramelized.

Method

  1. Blend shallot, garlic, and soaked dried chilies into a smooth paste.
  2. Fry the paste with galangal and lemongrass in oil until deeply fragrant, 5–6 minutes.
  3. Add chicken and stir to coat, cooking 3–4 minutes.
  4. Pour in coconut milk, add kaffir lime leaves and tamarind paste, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Cook uncovered over low heat, stirring occasionally, 30–35 minutes until the sauce reduces significantly and darkens.
  6. Stir in the toasted coconut (kerisik) in the last 5 minutes to thicken further; season with sugar and salt to taste.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — rendang is defined by patience; the sauce needs real time to darken and concentrate.
  • Skipping the kerisik (toasted coconut), which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • True rendang should end up almost dry, with the sauce clinging to the meat rather than pooling — keep reducing until you reach that point.
  • Kerisik can be bought ready-made or made by slowly toasting grated coconut in a dry pan until deep golden brown, then pounding it into a paste.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in drumsticks work well with the same long braising method.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably, a hallmark of good rendang.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.
