Katsudon (Pork Cutlet and Egg Rice Bowl) カツ丼

▾

Surprise me with

Katsudon, a breaded pork cutlet simmered with onion and egg in a sweet dashi sauce over rice, has been a Japanese diner staple since the early 20th century and is a traditional good-luck meal before exams.

Method

  1. Pound the cutlets to 1.5 cm (1/2 in), nick the fat edge, and season. Dredge in flour, dip in beaten egg and press into the panko.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 170 C (340 F) and fry the cutlets 2 at a time for 5 to 6 minutes, turning once, until golden and cooked through to 63 C (145 F). Rest on a rack, then slice into strips.
  3. For each bowl, simmer a quarter of the dashi, soy sauce, mirin, sugar and onion in a small skillet for 2 minutes until the onion softens.
  4. Lay a sliced cutlet in the pan, pour 1.5 lightly beaten eggs around it, cover and cook 1 minute until the egg is just set but still soft.
  5. Slide everything over a bowl of hot rice and top with mitsuba. Repeat for the other bowls.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Over-beating the egg makes it uniform and rubbery; leave streaks of white and yolk.
  • Letting the cutlet sit in the sauce too long makes the whole crust soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Pour the egg in two stages, the second just before serving, for a soft, glossy top.
  • A dedicated small oyakodon pan makes it easy to slide onto the rice.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh cutlets make chicken katsudon.
  • Use leftover tonkatsu, reheated in the oven.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the fried cutlets keep 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Breaded raw cutlets freeze for 2 months; fry from frozen at 160 C (320 F).

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled daikon (takuan)
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Green tea ice cream
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
