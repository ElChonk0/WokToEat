Chettinad Prawn Masala

▾

Surprise me with

Chettinad cuisine from Tamil Nadu is known for its fiery roasted spices; the prawn masala showcases it in a thick dry gravy.

  • 700 g1.5 lbPrawns, peeled and deveined

  • 10 g1.5 tbspCoriander seeds
  • 5 g2 tspBlack peppercorns

  • 2 g3/4 tspCumin seeds

  • 40 g1/2 cupGrated coconut

  • 2 g1/2 tspFennel seeds, for tempering
  • 2 sprigs2 sprigsCurry leaves
  • 200 g7 ozShallots or onion, finely chopped
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger-garlic paste
  • 22Tomatoes, chopped
  • 120 ml1/2 cupWater

Method

  1. Toss the prawns with turmeric and a pinch of salt.
  2. Dry-roast the coriander, pepper, fennel, cumin, chilies, cinnamon and cloves over medium-low heat for 3 minutes until fragrant, add the coconut and roast for 2 minutes until golden. Cool and grind to a paste with a little water.
  3. Heat the sesame oil in a pan over medium heat and add the tempering fennel and curry leaves, then the shallots, and cook for 8 minutes until golden. Add the ginger-garlic paste for 1 minute and the tomatoes for 5 minutes until mushy.
  4. Stir in the masala paste and remaining salt and fry for 3 to 4 minutes until the oil separates. Add the water and simmer for 3 minutes to a thick gravy.
  5. Add the prawns and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until just pink and curled. Prawns toughen quickly, so add them last. Serve with rice or parotta.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the prawns rubbery.
  • Burning the spices makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Dry roast the spices for authentic flavor.
  • Serve with rice and rasam.

Substitutions

  • Large shrimp replace prawns.
  • Ground Chettinad masala from a market saves effort.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or parotta
  • Soup: Cucumber raita
  • Dessert: Payasam
  • Beverage: Filter coffee
