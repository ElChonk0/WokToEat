Inari Zushi (Tofu Pouch Sushi) いなり寿司

▾

Surprise me with

Inari zushi, sushi rice stuffed in sweet fried tofu pouches, is named for the fox deity Inari, whose messengers are said to love fried tofu; it's a picnic and bento staple.

Method

  1. Roll a chopstick over each aburaage sheet to loosen the inside, cut in half and gently open into pouches. Boil them for 2 minutes to remove the excess oil, then drain and squeeze gently.
  2. Simmer the pouches in the dashi, sugar, soy sauce and mirin under a drop lid over medium-low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, until almost all the liquid is absorbed. Cool them in the pan so they soak up the rest.
  3. Mix the vinegar, sugar and salt until dissolved, then fold into the warm rice with slicing strokes while fanning, until glossy. Fold in the sesame seeds.
  4. With wet hands, shape the rice into 16 loose ovals. Squeeze each pouch lightly so it is moist but not dripping.
  5. Open a pouch, push a rice ball into the corners and fold the edges over to close. Serve seam-side down with pickled ginger.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling splits the pouches.
  • Hot pouches tear; cool them first.

Authentic tips

  • Pre-seasoned pouches are sold in packets.
  • Add shredded ginger to the rice.

Substitutions

  • Pre-seasoned aburaage pouches save time.
  • Add shredded ginger to the rice.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Miso soup
  • Soup: Pickled ginger
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
