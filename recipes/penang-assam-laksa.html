Penang Assam Laksa

▾

Surprise me with

Assam laksa, rice noodles in a sour mackerel and tamarind broth with torch ginger and mint, is a Penang specialty regularly ranked among the world's best soups.

Method

  1. Simmer the mackerel in the water over medium-low heat for about 10 minutes, until the flesh flakes from the bone. Lift it out, flake the meat and return the bones and head to the pot.
  2. Blend the chilies, shallots, garlic, galangal, lemongrass whites and belacan into a paste.
  3. Add the paste, bruised lemongrass, tamarind, asam gelugur, laksa leaves and torch ginger to the fish stock, bring to a boil, then simmer, partly covered, for 30 minutes.
  4. Strain the broth through a sieve, pressing on the solids, and return it to the pot with the flaked fish. Season with the sugar and salt: it should be sharply sour, spicy, savory and a little sweet.
  5. Cook the noodles according to the packet and divide among bowls. Ladle the hot broth and fish over and top with cucumber, onion, pineapple, mint and torch ginger. Drizzle a spoonful of prawn paste thinned with hot water on top.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the noodles in the broth makes it cloudy.
  • Boiling hard breaks the fish too fine.

Authentic tips

  • Add a spoonful of thick prawn paste (hae ko) at the table.
  • Serve cold fruit and herbs on top.

Substitutions

  • Sardines in oil replace mackerel in a pinch.
  • Thick rice noodles (laksa noodles) are essential.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cold pineapple and cucumber
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced teh
