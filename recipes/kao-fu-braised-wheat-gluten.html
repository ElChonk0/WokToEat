Kǎo Fū (Shanghai Braised Wheat Gluten) 烤麸

▾

Kao fu, spongy wheat gluten braised in sweet soy sauce with wood ear and peanuts, is a Shanghai cold dish on the New Year table.

Method

  1. Tear the kao fu into chunks by hand rather than cutting; ragged edges soak up more sauce. Blanch it in boiling water for 2 minutes and squeeze it dry. Repeat once to remove the sour taste.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the kao fu for 5 minutes, turning, until golden at the edges.
  3. Add the ginger, star anise, shiitake, wood ear, daylily and peanuts and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Add the soy sauces, sugar, Shaoxing wine and mushroom water, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 25 to 30 minutes, until the peanuts are tender.
  5. Uncover and reduce, tossing, until almost all the sauce is absorbed. Finish with sesame oil. Si xi kao fu is served cold as a Shanghai appetizer.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the squeeze makes the kao fu bland and watery.
  • Under-braising leaves it bland inside.

Authentic tips

  • Better chilled; it is traditionally served cold on the New Year table.
  • Add extra sugar to taste; Shanghai cooking leans sweet.

Substitutions

  • Roasted peanuts can be omitted for a nut-free version.
  • Fresh wheat gluten from an Asian market gives the best texture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; served cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or congee
  • Soup: Tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
