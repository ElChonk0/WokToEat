Gyudon (Beef Rice Bowl) 牛丼

▾

Surprise me with

One of Japan's most beloved fast, cheap comfort foods, gyudon simmers thin beef slices and onion in a sweet soy-dashi broth over rice — a staple of Japan's beef bowl chain restaurants and home kitchens alike.

Method

  1. Cut the beef into 5 cm (2 in) pieces. Bring the dashi, sake, mirin and sugar to a boil in a wide skillet, then add the soy sauce.
  2. Add the onion and simmer over medium heat for 5 minutes, until it softens and turns translucent and sweet.
  3. Add the beef, spreading the slices apart, and bring back to a simmer. Skim off the foam so the sauce stays clean-tasting.
  4. Add the ginger and simmer gently for 3 to 5 minutes until the beef is cooked and has absorbed some of the sauce. Do not boil hard, which toughens it.
  5. Pile the beef and onion over hot rice and spoon over plenty of the sauce. Top with an egg yolk or onsen tamago, pickled red ginger and shichimi.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef, which toughens thin slices quickly — they need only a few minutes.
  • Using a cut too thick to slice thin at home; ask your butcher to slice it, or partially freeze the beef first for easier slicing.

Authentic tips

  • Partially freezing the beef for 20–30 minutes makes it much easier to slice thinly at home.
  • This dish comes together very quickly — have the rice ready before you start cooking.

Substitutions

  • Pork works well with the same method for a butadon variation.
  • No dashi: a light beef or chicken stock is a reasonable substitute, though it loses some traditional umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently to avoid toughening the beef.

Freezing: Not recommended — texture of thin beef slices suffers.

Serve with
