Xīng Zhōu Chǎo Mǐ (Singapore Noodles) 星洲炒米

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, curry-flavored Singapore noodles are a Hong Kong creation, found on Cantonese restaurant and cha chaan teng menus rather than in Singapore.

Method

  1. Soak the vermicelli in warm water for 10 minutes until pliable, drain well and cut into shorter lengths with scissors.
  2. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat, pour in the eggs, swirl into a thin omelet, cook until set, then roll and slice into ribbons.
  3. Add 1 tablespoon of oil, turn the heat to high and stir-fry the shrimp for 1 to 2 minutes until pink. Add the char siu, garlic, onion and pepper and stir-fry for 1 minute, then move everything to a plate.
  4. Add the last tablespoon of oil and the curry powder and stir for 15 seconds. Add the noodles, soy sauce, sugar and salt and toss with tongs for 2 minutes until evenly yellow; sprinkle with a little water if they stick.
  5. Return the shrimp mixture with the egg, bean sprouts and green onion and toss for 1 minute. Finish with sesame oil.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the vermicelli makes it break into mush in the wok.
  • Adding curry powder to a cold pan leaves it raw and dusty; bloom it in hot oil first.

Authentic tips

  • A light hand with soy keeps the noodles bright yellow, which is how they are served in Hong Kong.
  • Use tongs or chopsticks and lift rather than stir.

Substitutions

  • Cooked chicken or ham replaces char siu.
  • Make it vegetarian with tofu puffs and extra vegetables.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended; rice vermicelli turns brittle.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Stir-fried bok choy with garlic
  • Soup: Wonton soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Iced lemon tea
