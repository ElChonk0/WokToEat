Xīng Zhōu Chǎo Mǐ (Singapore Noodles) 星洲炒米

▾

Surprise me with

Despite the name, curry-flavored Singapore noodles are a Hong Kong creation, found on Cantonese restaurant and cha chaan teng menus rather than in Singapore.

Method

  1. Boil the vermicelli for 1 to 2 minutes, separating it with chopsticks, until just pliable. Drain well and toss for 30 to 60 seconds to let the steam escape, then cover with a plate. Do not rinse: drier noodles fry without clumping or turning soggy. Snip them a few times with scissors so they are easier to toss.
  2. Stir the curry powder into the water to make a paste, then mix in the oyster sauce, soy sauce, salt and sugar. Mixing the curry with water first means it coats the noodles evenly instead of in dusty patches.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat for 2 minutes, add 2 teaspoons of oil and pour in the eggs. Leave for 20 seconds, then scramble into soft curds and lift out. Add 1 tablespoon of oil and stir-fry the shrimp for about 1 minute until pink, then lift out.
  4. Add 1 tablespoon of oil and stir-fry the onion, pepper, carrot and celery for 60 to 90 seconds, until just tender-crisp, then lift out. Use only low-moisture vegetables and not too many, or the noodles steam.
  5. Add the remaining oil and the noodles. Spread them out and leave for 1 minute, then toss and fry for 2 to 3 minutes more, lifting with chopsticks or tongs, until hot and lightly toasted.
  6. Pour in the curry sauce and toss for 1 to 2 minutes until the noodles are evenly yellow. Return the vegetables, shrimp, char siu, bean sprouts, green onion and egg and toss for 1 to 2 minutes until hot.
  7. Drizzle with sesame oil, toss for 30 seconds and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the vermicelli makes it break into mush in the wok.
  • Adding curry powder to a cold pan leaves it raw and dusty; bloom it in hot oil first.

Authentic tips

  • A light hand with soy keeps the noodles bright yellow, which is how they are served in Hong Kong.
  • Use tongs or chopsticks and lift rather than stir.

Substitutions

  • Cooked chicken or ham replaces char siu.
  • Make it vegetarian with tofu puffs and extra vegetables.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended; rice vermicelli turns brittle.
