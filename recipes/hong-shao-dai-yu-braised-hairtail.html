Hóng Shāo Dài Yú (Shanghai Braised Hairtail) 红烧带鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Hairtail braised in soy sauce, sugar and rice wine is a Shanghai home staple and a classic of Zhejiang coastal cooking.

  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Cut the hairtail into 6 cm (2.5 in) pieces, rinse off the silvery coating and pat very dry. Toss lightly in flour.
  2. Heat the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-high heat and fry the fish for 3 minutes per side until golden and crisp. Frying first firms the delicate flesh so it survives the braise.
  3. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Add the ginger and green onion and fry for 30 seconds.
  4. Return the fish, add the Shaoxing wine around the edge, then the soy sauces, sugar and 150 ml (2/3 cup) water. Simmer for 6 to 8 minutes, turning once gently.
  5. Raise the heat and reduce for 2 to 3 minutes, spooning the sauce over, until thick and glossy. Hairtail is one of Shanghai's most common home-cooked fish.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the fish before it releases from the pan tears the skin.
  • Not drying the fish makes the oil spatter and prevents browning.

Authentic tips

  • Hairtail has a thin skin but few bones; the flour coating helps it keep its shape.
  • Serve with plenty of rice for the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Cut mackerel steaks replace the hairtail.
  • Flour coating can be skipped for a lighter dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
