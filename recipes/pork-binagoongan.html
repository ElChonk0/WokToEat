Pork Binagoongan

▾

Surprise me with

A robust Filipino dish that braises pork in shrimp paste (bagoong), binagoongan balances the funk of fermented shrimp paste with vinegar and sugar for a distinctly savory, tangy home-cooked favorite.

Method

  1. Brown pork chunks in oil until golden; set aside.
  2. In the same pot, fry garlic and onion until softened.
  3. Add tomato and cook until it breaks down, 3–4 minutes.
  4. Return pork to the pot with shrimp paste, vinegar, sugar, chili, and water; bring to a simmer.
  5. Cover and braise 20–25 minutes until the pork is tender and the sauce has reduced.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the vinegar too early and stirring it in vigorously, which can taste raw and sharp — let it simmer in gently.
  • Using too little shrimp paste, which is the dish's defining savory backbone.

Authentic tips

  • Frying the shrimp paste briefly with the aromatics mellows its raw fishiness into a deep savory note.
  • This dish is traditionally quite robust in flavor — balance it with plenty of plain rice.

Substitutions

  • Dried shrimp plus fish sauce replace bagoong.
  • Eggplant is a classic addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and green mango
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
