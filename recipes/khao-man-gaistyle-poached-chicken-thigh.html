Khao Man Gai–Style Poached Chicken Thigh

Thailand's version of Hainanese chicken rice, khao man gai poaches chicken in an aromatic broth then cooks rice in the resulting fat and stock, served with a distinctive ginger-soybean-chili dipping sauce.

Method

  1. Bring a pot of water to a boil with sliced ginger and smashed garlic; lower in the chicken.
  2. Reduce to a gentle simmer, cover, and poach 20–22 minutes until just cooked through.
  3. Remove chicken and reserve the poaching liquid.
  4. Rinse rice, then cook it in the reserved poaching liquid using standard rice-to-liquid ratios, for fragrant, savory rice.
  5. Whisk together minced ginger, garlic, soybean paste, soy sauce, lime juice, sugar, and chilies for the dipping sauce.
  6. Slice the chicken and serve over the rice with the dipping sauce alongside.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the chicken hard instead of gently poaching, which toughens the meat.
  • Cooking the rice in plain water instead of the reserved poaching liquid, which is where most of the dish's flavor comes from.

Authentic tips

  • The dipping sauce is what distinguishes khao man gai from other regional chicken rice dishes — don't skip the fermented soybean paste.
  • Reserve any extra poaching stock; it makes an excellent light soup on the side.

Substitutions

  • A whole chicken can replace thighs for a more traditional presentation, poaching for about 35–40 minutes.
  • No fermented soybean paste: white miso thinned slightly is a reasonable substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; store chicken, rice, and sauce separately.

Freezing: The poaching stock freezes well; chicken and rice are best fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chicken broth and cucumber slices
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
