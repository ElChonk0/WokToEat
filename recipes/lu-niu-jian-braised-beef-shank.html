Lu Niu Jian (Taiwanese Braised Beef Shank) 滷牛腱

Braised beef shank, simmered in a soy-based lu wei sauce and sliced cold, is a Taiwanese deli, noodle shop and snack staple.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 2.5 hr
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 10 g1/3 ozStar anise, cinnamon, bay
  • 60 ml4 tbspRice wine
  • 80 g5 tbspGinger, scallion, garlic
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater

Method

  1. Cover the shank with cold water, bring to a boil for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. In a clean pot, bring the soy sauce, sugar, wine, aromatics, spices and water to a boil.
  3. Add the shank, cover and simmer on low for 2 hours until a chopstick slides through.
  4. Turn off the heat and cool in the liquid.
  5. Chill, then slice thinly across the grain.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.
  • Slicing warm shreds the meat.

Authentic tips

  • Cooling in the liquid keeps it juicy.
  • Reuse the braise as a master stock.

Substitutions

  • Brisket works but slices less neatly.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 55 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the braise; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or noodles
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
