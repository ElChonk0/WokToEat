Lǔ Niú Jiàn (Taiwanese Braised Beef Shank) 滷牛腱

▾

Surprise me with

Braised beef shank, simmered in a soy-based lu wei sauce and sliced cold, is a Taiwanese deli, noodle shop and snack staple.

  • 700 g1.5 lbBeef shank, whole
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 80 g3 ozGinger, green onion and garlic
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 60 ml1/4 cupRice wine (mijiu)
  • 75 ml5 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 10 g1/3 ozStar anise, cinnamon, bay leaves and Sichuan peppercorns
  • 22Dried chili (optional)
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater

Method

  1. Blanch the shank in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse off the scum. This keeps the braising liquid clean.
  2. Heat a little oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and garlic for 2 minutes until fragrant. Add the rock sugar and stir until it melts and turns amber; caramelizing the sugar gives the deep color and gloss.
  3. Add the shank and toss to coat, then pour in the rice wine and soy sauce and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Add the water and spices, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 1.5 to 2 hours, turning occasionally, until a chopstick slides through easily.
  5. Turn off the heat and let the shank cool in the liquid for at least 2 hours, or overnight in the refrigerator; it absorbs flavor as it cools and firms up for slicing.
  6. Slice thinly across the grain and serve cold with a drizzle of the braising liquid, chili oil and sliced green onion. Keep the liquid for the next braise.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.
  • Slicing warm shreds the meat.

Authentic tips

  • Cooling in the liquid keeps it juicy.
  • Reuse the braise as a master stock.

Substitutions

  • Brisket works but slices less neatly.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 55 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the braise; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or noodles
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
