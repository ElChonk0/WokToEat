Gong Shi Luo Song Tang (Hong Kong Borscht) 港式罗宋汤

▾

Surprise me with

Hong Kong borscht, a tomato-red beef and cabbage soup without beets, came to the city with Russian emigres via Shanghai and became the standard soup of cha chaan teng set meals.

Method

  1. Put the beef in a pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil for 1 minute to bring up the scum, then drain, rinse the beef well under cold water and clean the pot. This keeps the soup clear and clean-tasting.
  2. Cut all the vegetables to about the same size so they cook evenly. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over low heat and fry the ginger and garlic for 15 to 20 seconds, the onion for 20 seconds, then half the tomatoes for 30 seconds.
  3. Raise the heat to medium-high, add the potatoes and carrots for 30 seconds, then the rest of the tomatoes and the celery. Stir in the ketchup and tomato paste, then the beef and black pepper, and cook for 1 minute so the tomato coats everything. Frying the tomato and ketchup at this stage gives the soup its deep red color.
  4. Add the stock and boiling water over high heat, then the cabbage, bay leaves and lemon. Bring to a boil, cover, lower to medium-low and simmer for 2 hours, stirring now and then, until the beef is tender and the vegetables are very soft.
  5. Remove the lemon and bay leaves and season with the sugar and salt, tasting: it should be tangy, gently sweet and peppery. Like most stews, it tastes even better the next day.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding raw tomato paste straight to the pot leaves a tinny taste; fry it first.
  • Too little cooking leaves the soup thin; it should be thick and soft.

Authentic tips

  • It tastes better the next day.
  • Cha chaan teng versions are thickened slightly with a roux of butter and flour.

Substitutions

  • Oxtail replaces brisket for a richer soup.
  • Add a beet for color if you like.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Buttered toast or a pork chop bun
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
