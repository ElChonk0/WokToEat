Gong Shi Luo Song Tang (Hong Kong Borscht) 港式罗宋汤

▾

Surprise me with

Hong Kong borscht, a tomato-red beef and cabbage soup without beets, came to the city with Russian emigres via Shanghai and became the standard soup of cha chaan teng set meals.

  • Cook 2 hr

Method

  1. Blanch the beef 5 minutes, drain and rinse, then simmer it in the water with the bay leaves for 1 hour.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the onion, carrot and celery 5 minutes, then add the tomatoes, tomato paste and ketchup and cook 5 minutes until jammy.
  3. Add the tomato mixture, potatoes and cabbage to the beef and simmer 45 minutes to 1 hour, until everything is soft and some potato has broken down to thicken the soup.
  4. Season with the sugar, salt and pepper; it should be gently sweet-sour.
  5. Remove the bay leaves and serve hot.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding raw tomato paste straight to the pot leaves a tinny taste; fry it first.
  • Too little cooking leaves the soup thin; it should be thick and soft.

Authentic tips

  • It tastes better the next day.
  • Cha chaan teng versions are thickened slightly with a roux of butter and flour.

Substitutions

  • Oxtail replaces brisket for a richer soup.
  • Add a beet for color if you like.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Buttered toast or a pork chop bun
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
