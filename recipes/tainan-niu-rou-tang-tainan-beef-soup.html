Táinán Niú Ròu Tāng (Tainan Fresh Beef Soup) 台南牛肉湯

▾

Surprise me with

Tainan's fresh beef soup, thin slices of just-slaughtered beef poured over with scalding beef bone broth, is a unique Tainan breakfast and is eaten at dawn near the old slaughterhouses.

Method

  1. Blanch the bones for 10 minutes, drain and rinse. Simmer them with the daikon, onion and water for 3 to 4 hours, skimming, until the broth is clear and rich. Strain and season with salt.
  2. Keep the broth at a rolling boil; it must be boiling hot to cook the beef.
  3. Slice the beef paper-thin and divide it raw among warmed bowls, with the ginger on top. In Tainan, beef from that morning's slaughter is used, so buy the freshest you can.
  4. Ladle the boiling broth straight over the beef; it cooks to pink in seconds. Taste and adjust with salt and white pepper.
  5. Sprinkle with green onion and cilantro and serve with rice and a dip of soy sauce paste and grated ginger. A Tainan breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Lukewarm broth leaves the beef raw.
  • Thick slices stay raw inside; cut paper-thin.

Authentic tips

  • Freeze the beef for 30 minutes to make it easier to slice thin.
  • Use very fresh beef.

Substitutions

  • Very thin beef from a good butcher replaces just-slaughtered beef.
  • Beef bones plus ginger make the broth in 2 hours.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the broth keeps 4 days, but slice and cook beef fresh.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with soy-ginger dip
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Oolong tea
