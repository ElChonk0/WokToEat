Nán Guā Sà Mù Sà (Pumpkin Samsa) 南瓜萨木萨

▾

Surprise me with

Samsa, baked pastries filled with lamb and onion, are a Silk Road food of Xinjiang; pumpkin samsa is a vegetarian variation common in the cooler months.

  • Prep 45 min
  • Cook 30 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 300 g10.5 ozFlour
  • 150 ml2/3 cupWater
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil, for dough and brushing
  • 400 g14 ozPumpkin, diced
  • 150 g5 ozOnion
  • 5 g2 tspCumin and black pepper
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 15 g2 tbspSesame seeds
  • 11Egg

Method

  1. Mix the flour, water and 15 ml oil into a dough, knead 5 minutes and rest 30 minutes; roll out thin and cut into 12 cm squares.
  2. Mix the finely diced pumpkin, onion, cumin, pepper, salt and 15 ml oil.
  3. Place a spoonful in each square and fold into triangles, sealing the edges with water.
  4. Brush with the egg and remaining oil and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  5. Bake at 200 C (400 F) for 30 minutes until golden.

Common mistakes

  • Watery pumpkin makes soggy samsa; dice small.
  • Overfilling bursts the seams.

Authentic tips

  • Traditional filling uses fat-rich lamb; the pumpkin version is sweeter.
  • Brush with oil rather than egg for a vegan version.

Substitutions

  • Butternut squash replaces pumpkin.
  • Brush with oil instead of egg for a vegan version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked samsa for 2 months; bake from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black tea and dried fruit
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Hami melon
  • Beverage: Black tea
