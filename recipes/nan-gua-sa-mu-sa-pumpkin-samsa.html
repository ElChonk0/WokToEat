Nán Guā Sà Mù Sà (Pumpkin Samsa) 南瓜萨木萨

▾

Surprise me with

Samsa, baked pastries filled with lamb and onion, are a Silk Road food of Xinjiang; pumpkin samsa is a vegetarian variation common in the cooler months.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt, water and a tablespoon of the oil into a smooth dough and rest it, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the pumpkin with the onion, cumin, pepper and salt. Uyghur samsa traditionally uses lamb with plenty of lamb fat and onion, which keeps the filling juicy.
  3. Divide the dough into 12 pieces, roll each into a thin square or round, brush with oil and put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center. Fold the corners in to make a square parcel and pinch to seal.
  4. Place seam-side down on a lined tray, brush with egg and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  5. Bake at 220 C (425 F) for 20 to 25 minutes until deep golden; in Xinjiang they are stuck to the walls of a tonur (clay oven). Serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Watery pumpkin makes soggy samsa; dice small.
  • Overfilling bursts the seams.

Authentic tips

  • Traditional filling uses fat-rich lamb; the pumpkin version is sweeter.
  • Brush with oil rather than egg for a vegan version.

Substitutions

  • Butternut squash replaces pumpkin.
  • Brush with oil instead of egg for a vegan version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; reheat in a 180 C (350 F) oven for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked samsa for 2 months; bake from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black tea and dried fruit
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Hami melon
  • Beverage: Black tea
