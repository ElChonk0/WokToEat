Beijing Chicken in Sweet Bean Sauce 京酱鸡丝

▾

Surprise me with

A chicken adaptation of jing jiang rou si (Beijing shredded pork in sweet bean sauce), this dish stir-fries strips of chicken in a rich, dark, slightly sweet fermented bean sauce, traditionally wrapped with scallion in thin pancakes.

  • 600 g1.3 lbChicken thighs, boneless, cut into thin strips
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 60 ml1/4 cupSweet bean sauce (tianmianjiang)
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, minced
  • 2 cloves2 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 150 g5 ozGreen onion and cucumber, julienned
  • 1212Thin pancakes or tofu skin, for serving

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with the soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch and a tablespoon of water and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and stir-fry the chicken for 2 to 3 minutes until cooked through. Lift it out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the sweet bean sauce and fry over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until it darkens and the oil turns glossy. Add the sugar, ginger and garlic and stir until the sugar dissolves into a thick, shiny sauce.
  4. Return the chicken and toss over medium heat until every strip is coated.
  5. Serve with julienned green onion and cucumber and thin pancakes or lettuce cups, rolling each bite like Peking duck. A Beijing classic, jing jiang rou si.

Common mistakes

  • Using hoisin sauce as a full substitute without adjusting seasoning — it's sweeter and less savory than proper tianmianjiang.
  • Cutting the chicken into pieces too thick, which won't wrap neatly in the pancakes.

Authentic tips

  • Tianmianjiang is worth seeking out at an Asian grocer; its flavor is distinctly different from hoisin.
  • Julienned cucumber alongside the green onion is a traditional and refreshing addition to the wrap.

Substitutions

  • Hoisin thinned with a little soy sauce replaces tianmianjiang.
  • Pork loin takes the same method.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Tofu skin wrappers or rice with shredded scallion
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pear
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
