Cong Shao Yin Tai Yu (Scallion-Braised Black Cod) 葱烧银鳕鱼

Cong shao, braising in a rich scallion-infused soy sauce, is a signature technique of Shandong (Lu) cuisine, most famous with sea cucumber.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozBlack cod, in 5 cm pieces
  • 200 g7 ozScallions, white and pale green, in 6 cm lengths
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil
  • 15 g1/2 ozGinger, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 15 g1 tbspSugar
  • 200 ml3/4 cupChicken stock
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch

Method

  1. Heat half the oil in a wide pan over medium heat and fry the scallions for 4 to 5 minutes until deep golden and sweet; lift out and keep the oil.
  2. Increase to medium-high, sear the black cod for 2 minutes per side until golden.
  3. Add the ginger, wine, both soy sauces, oyster sauce, sugar and stock and bring to a boil.
  4. Return half of the scallions, cover and simmer on low for 8 minutes.
  5. Thicken the sauce with the cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water, plate the fish, and top with the remaining scallions and the pan sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written for black cod. Shandong cooks use sea cucumber, carp or seabass.

Common mistakes

  • Burnt scallions turn bitter; fry them over steady medium heat.
  • Over-simmering breaks the fish apart.

Authentic tips

  • The scallion oil carries most of the flavor, so use the pan oil.
  • Sear skin-side down first for a crisp base.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin or Chilean sea bass replaces the black cod.
  • Leeks replace some of the scallions.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or mantou
  • Soup: Clear cabbage soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
