Gyutan Yaki (Sendai-Style Grilled Beef Tongue) 牛タン焼き

▾

Surprise me with

Gyutan, thick-cut grilled beef tongue with barley rice and oxtail soup, is the signature dish of Sendai, created there after the Second World War.

  • 400 g14 ozBeef tongue, peeled and sliced 5 mm (1/4 in) thick
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/2 tspGround black pepper
  • 15 ml1 tbspSake
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

  • 11Lemon, cut into wedges
  • 400 g2.5 cupsCooked rice mixed with barley (mugimeshi)
  • 30 g2 tbspNanban miso (miso with green chili), for serving

Method

  1. Score each slice of tongue lightly in a crosshatch on both sides, which keeps it tender and stops it curling. Rub with sake, salt and pepper and leave for 15 to 30 minutes.
  2. Heat a grill to very high or a cast-iron grill pan until smoking, and brush with sesame oil.
  3. Grill the slices for 45 to 60 seconds per side, until browned at the edges but still slightly pink in the center. Tongue goes rubbery if overcooked.
  4. Mix the green onion with a pinch of salt and a few drops of sesame oil to spoon on top, the Sendai way.
  5. Serve with lemon wedges, barley rice, nanban miso and a bowl of oxtail soup if you have it.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the tongue rubbery.
  • Slicing too thin makes it dry; 5-8 mm is classic.

Authentic tips

  • Sendai serves it with oxtail soup and pickles.
  • Buy pre-sliced tongue from a Japanese or Korean market.

Substitutions

  • Thick-sliced beef flank replaces tongue.
  • Regular rice replaces barley rice.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Barley rice and pickles
  • Soup: Oxtail soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
