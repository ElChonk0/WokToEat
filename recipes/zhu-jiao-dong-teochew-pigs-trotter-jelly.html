Zhu Jiao Dong (Teochew Pig's Trotter Jelly) 猪脚冻

Pig's trotter jelly is a Teochew cold dish in which trotters are boiled with spices until the collagen sets the broth into a savoury jelly; it is now rare in Singapore.

  • Cook 3 hr

Method

  1. Blanch the trotters in boiling water for 8 minutes, drain, rinse and scrub off any residue.
  2. Return them to a clean pot with the water, ginger, wine, star anise, cinnamon, peppercorns, soy sauce and salt.
  3. Bring to a boil, then simmer, partly covered, for 2 hours 30 minutes until the skin is very soft and the broth is reduced by about a third.
  4. Pull the meat and skin from the bones, discarding the bones, and pack it into a loaf pan or shallow dish.
  5. Strain the hot broth through a fine sieve over the meat to cover it, and cool, then chill for at least 6 hours until set.
  6. Unmold, slice, and serve cold with the black vinegar, shredded ginger and chili sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too much water gives a broth that will not set; reduce it until it coats a spoon.
  • Leaving bones in makes slicing difficult.

Authentic tips

  • Skim the surface fat once chilled for a cleaner jelly.
  • Add a pinch more salt, because cold dishes taste less seasoned.

Substitutions

  • Pork hock replaces trotters.
  • A few sheets of gelatin help if the jelly will not set.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended, because the jelly weeps on thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickled cucumber
  • Soup: Hot clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
