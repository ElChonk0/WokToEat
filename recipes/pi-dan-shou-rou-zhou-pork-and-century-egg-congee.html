Pí Dàn Shòu Ròu Zhōu (Pork and Century Egg Congee) 皮蛋瘦肉粥

▾

Surprise me with

Pork and century egg congee is a staple Cantonese breakfast of rice simmered into a smooth porridge with lean pork and preserved duck egg.

  • 170 g3/4 cup plus 2 tbspJasmine rice
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, for the rice

  • 20 g2/3 ozDried scallops (conpoy), rinsed (optional)

  • 1 g1/2 tspGround white pepper

  • 30 ml2 tbspWater, for the pork
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 33Century eggs, peeled
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, shredded
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, to finish
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion, sliced
  • 10 g1/3 ozCilantro
  • 100 g3.5 ozFried dough sticks (youtiao), sliced

Method

  1. Rinse the rice three times, then just cover it with water and stir in the rice salt and oil; leave it to soak while you prepare the pork. Break the scallops into pieces if using.
  2. Mix the pork with its salt, pepper, cornstarch and water until absorbed, then stir in the sesame oil. Partly freezing the pork first makes fine dicing easier. Dice two of the century eggs small and cut the third into wedges for the top.
  3. Bring the water to a hard boil in a Dutch oven over high heat. Add the scallops, then the rice with its soaking water, and return to a boil.
  4. Lower to medium, stir once to stop the rice settling, and set the lid ajar so it cannot boil over. Keep it at a steady bubble for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the grains have burst.
  5. Turn the heat down and whisk hard for 2 to 3 minutes, which breaks the rice into a smooth, creamy congee. Thin it with boiling water if it is thicker than heavy cream.
  6. With the heat at its lowest, drop in the pork in small clumps and stir gently to separate them, then simmer over medium-low for about 3 minutes until the pork just turns pale. Stir in the diced century egg and ginger, season with salt, and turn off the heat after 30 seconds.
  7. Ladle into bowls and top with the egg wedges, green onion, cilantro and youtiao for dipping.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Not stirring lets the rice stick and scorch on the bottom; stir every 10 minutes.
  • Adding the pork too early makes it tough; add it for the last 3 minutes.

Authentic tips

  • A ratio of about 1 part rice to 10 parts water gives a smooth, pourable congee.
  • Leftover cooked rice works: simmer 1 cup in 4 cups of water for 30 minutes.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork replaces sliced loin.
  • A rice cooker porridge setting replaces the stovetop.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; loosen with water when reheating.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months in portions.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried dough sticks and pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Not needed; the congee is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
