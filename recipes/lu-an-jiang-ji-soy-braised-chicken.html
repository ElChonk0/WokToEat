Lù Ān Jiàng Jī (Lu'an Soy-Braised Chicken) 六安酱鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Lu'an, in western Anhui, is known for Lu'an Guapian tea and for sauce-braised poultry in the Hui style.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 1 kg2.2 lbChicken legs, bone-in

  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g1.5 tbspSugar
  • 50 g2 ozGinger and green onion
  • 8 g1/4 ozStar anise and cinnamon
  • 45 ml3 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Rub the chicken legs with salt and leave them for 2 hours, or overnight; the cure firms the meat.
  2. Rinse and pat dry. Heat the oil and sugar in a Dutch oven until it caramelizes, then turn the chicken in it.
  3. Add the ginger, green onion and spices, then the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces and water.
  4. Simmer, covered, for 30 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave the chicken in the liquid for 30 minutes so the jiang (sauce) soaks in.
  5. Reduce some liquid to a glaze, chop the chicken and serve warm or cold.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard tightens the meat.
  • Skipping the steep leaves it less juicy.

Authentic tips

  • The sauce can be reused as a master stock.
  • Serve warm or cold.

Substitutions

  • Whole chicken legs, bone in, replace small chickens.
  • Reuse the sauce as a master stock.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Lu'an Guapian tea
