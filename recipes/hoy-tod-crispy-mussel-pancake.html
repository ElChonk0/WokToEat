Hoy Tod (Crispy Mussel Pancake)

▾

Surprise me with

Hoy tod, mussels in a crisp egg and starch batter, is a Thai street-food staple, associated with the Chinese-Thai (Teochew) cooking of Bangkok's Chinatown.

  • 300 g10 ozMussels or oysters, shucked
  • 60 g1/2 cupRice flour
  • 30 g1/4 cupTapioca starch
  • 150 ml2/3 cupWater, cold
  • 1 gpinchSalt
  • 33Eggs
  • 90 ml6 tbspNeutral oil
  • 2 cloves2 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 150 g5 ozBean sprouts
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion, cut into lengths
  • 15 ml1 tbspFish sauce and soy sauce
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper
  • 60 ml1/4 cupSriracha sauce (sauce Sriracha), for serving

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, water and salt into a thin batter. The rice flour crisps and the tapioca stays chewy; that contrast is hoy tod.
  2. Heat a cast-iron skillet or griddle over high heat with 3 tablespoons of oil until very hot. Pour in half the batter in a thin layer and fry for 1 to 2 minutes until the edges are crisp and golden.
  3. Scatter half the mussels over the batter, then crack over half the eggs and break them up with a spatula. Fry for 1 to 2 minutes, then chop the pancake into pieces and flip them, adding more oil, and fry until crisp on both sides.
  4. Push it aside and stir-fry half the garlic and bean sprouts in the pan for 30 seconds with a splash of fish sauce and soy sauce, just to warm them through.
  5. Pile the sprouts on a plate, lay the crisp pancake on top and scatter with green onion and white pepper. Repeat, and serve with Sriracha sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Too much batter makes a doughy pancake.
  • Stirring while cooking breaks the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Cook in two batches.
  • Oysters are the classic alternative.

Substitutions

  • Oysters are the classic alternative.
  • Shrimp also works.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crispness fades.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sweet chili sauce and bean sprouts
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
