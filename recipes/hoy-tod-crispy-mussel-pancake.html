Hoy Tod (Crispy Mussel Pancake)

Hoy tod, mussels in a crisp egg and starch batter, is a Thai street-food staple, associated with the Chinese-Thai (Teochew) cooking of Bangkok's Chinatown.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 300 g10 ozMussels or oysters, shucked
  • 60 g1/2 cupRice flour
  • 30 g1/4 cupTapioca starch
  • 33Eggs
  • 100 g3.5 ozBean sprouts
  • 30 g2 tbspScallion
  • 90 ml6 tbspNeutral oil
  • 60 ml4 tbspSweet chili sauce

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, 150 ml water and a pinch of salt into a smooth batter.
  2. Heat half the oil in a nonstick pan over medium-high heat and fry the mussels 1 minute.
  3. Pour in half the batter and cook until the edges crisp, 2 minutes.
  4. Pour the eggs over, flip and cook until golden and crisp.
  5. Top with the bean sprouts and scallion and serve with the sweet chili sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Too much batter makes a doughy pancake.
  • Stirring while cooking breaks the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Cook in two batches.
  • Oysters are the classic alternative.

Substitutions

  • Oysters are the classic alternative.
  • Shrimp also works.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crispness fades.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sweet chili sauce and bean sprouts
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
