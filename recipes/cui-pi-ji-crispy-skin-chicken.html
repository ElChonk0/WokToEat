Cuì Pí Jī (Cantonese Crispy-Skin Chicken) 脆皮鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Cui pi ji, a chicken scalded, air-dried and fried until the skin is glass-crisp, is a Cantonese banquet and Lunar New Year dish that signals celebration.

Method

  1. Rinse the legs and dry them thoroughly. Mix the underside salt, five-spice and white pepper and rub it only onto the meat side, keeping it off the skin. Rub the skin with the skin salt and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Set the legs skin-side up on a rack in the sink and ladle the boiling water slowly over the skin until it tightens and turns slightly translucent. Scalding shrinks the skin around the meat, which is the first step to a glassy, crackling finish.
  3. Air-dry for 20 minutes (a fan helps). Warm the honey with both vinegars for a few seconds until runny and brush it evenly over the skin.
  4. Refrigerate the legs uncovered on the rack for at least 3 hours, or overnight, until the skin feels dry and papery. Brush on a second coat of glaze halfway through if you like a deeper color. Dry skin is what crisps; damp skin stays leathery.
  5. Make the dip: stir the dip salt in a dry pan over low heat for about 45 seconds until it turns faintly yellow, then take it off the heat and stir in the five-spice and white pepper.
  6. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 150 C (300 F). Lower in the legs skin-side up and ladle hot oil continuously over the skin, letting the oil temperature fall to about 120 C (250 F) and holding it there. Cook for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until the meat by the bone reads 74 C (165 F). Lift onto a rack and rest at least 15 minutes.
  7. Raise the oil to 190 C (375 F). Fry the legs again for 2 to 3 minutes, ladling oil over the skin, until it is deep mahogany and crackling. This hot second fry blisters the dried skin.
  8. Drain on the rack for 5 minutes, then chop through the bone into pieces. Serve with the salt dip, lemon wedges and prawn crackers.

Common mistakes

  • Skin that is still damp when it meets the oil spatters and turns leathery instead of crisp.
  • Frying only at high heat colors the skin before the meat is cooked; start moderate and finish hot.

Authentic tips

  • The overnight air-dry is what makes the skin glass-crisp; a fan or an open fridge shelf speeds it up.
  • Warm the maltose jar in hot water so it pours easily; honey works but browns less deeply.

Substitutions

  • An air fryer at 200 C (400 F) for 20 minutes gives crisp skin with far less oil.
  • Roast on a rack at 220 C (425 F) for 35 minutes if you would rather not deep-fry.

Storage

Leftovers: Best within an hour; leftovers keep 2 days and recrisp in a 200 C (400 F) oven for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended; the skin turns leathery on thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Prawn crackers and steamed rice
  • Soup: Watercress and pork soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Chrysanthemum tea
