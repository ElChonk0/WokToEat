Thai Basil Chicken, Chicken Thigh Version (Pad Krapow Gai)

▾

Surprise me with

One of Thailand's most-eaten everyday dishes, pad krapow stir-fries minced meat with holy basil, chili, and garlic, traditionally topped with a fried egg — found at nearly every street-food stall across the country.

Method

  1. Pound the garlic and chilies together in a mortar into a coarse paste; pounding releases more flavor than chopping. Mix the fish sauce, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar with a tablespoon of water.
  2. Fry the eggs Thai-style: heat 1 cm (1/2 in) of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and fry each egg, spooning hot oil over it, for 1 to 2 minutes until the edges are crisp and lacy and the yolk is still runny. Drain.
  3. Pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil and fry the garlic-chili paste over medium heat for 20 to 30 seconds until fragrant. The fumes are strong; turn on the fan.
  4. Raise the heat to high, add the chicken and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes, breaking it up, until cooked through and starting to brown.
  5. Add the sauce and toss for 30 seconds until it coats the chicken and mostly evaporates. Turn off the heat and stir in the holy basil until just wilted.
  6. Serve over jasmine rice with a fried egg on top, and fish sauce with sliced chilies (prik nam pla) on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the basil too early and overcooking it, which turns it dark and loses its distinctive peppery aroma.
  • Substituting sweet Italian basil for Thai holy basil without adjusting expectations — the flavor is quite different.

Authentic tips

  • Thai holy basil (bai krapow) has a more peppery, clove-like flavor than Thai sweet basil — seek it out if possible.
  • Very high heat and quick cooking are essential; this dish should take only a few minutes total once you start cooking.

Substitutions

  • Thai sweet basil works if holy basil isn't available, though the flavor will be milder.
  • Ground turkey or ground pork are common substitutes for chicken in this dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the basil loses some vibrancy.

Freezing: Not recommended — fresh basil doesn't hold up.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice topped with a fried egg
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
