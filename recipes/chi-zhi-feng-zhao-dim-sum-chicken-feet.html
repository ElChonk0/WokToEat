Chǐ Zhī Fèng Zhǎo (Dim Sum Chicken Feet in Black Bean Sauce) 豉汁凤爪

▾

Surprise me with

Steamed chicken feet in black bean sauce, called phoenix claws, are a fixture of the Cantonese yum cha table; they are fried, soaked and steamed so the skin puffs and turns soft and gelatinous.

Method

  1. Put the feet in a pot of cold water with the blanching ginger and wine, bring to a boil and cook for 2 minutes. Drain and rinse. Blanching first firms the skin so it does not shrink back when you trim it.
  2. Clip off the claw tips with kitchen shears and cut away any rough patches. Brush the feet with the maltose thinned with a spoon of hot water, which helps them brown, then pat them completely dry and leave on a rack for at least 1 hour. Any water left on them makes the oil spit violently.
  3. Heat the frying oil in a Dutch oven to 175 C (350 F). Fry the feet in small batches with a lid held over the pot, turning after 2 to 3 minutes, for 5 to 6 minutes in all, until deep golden and blistered and the oil has calmed down.
  4. Drop the fried feet straight into a large bowl of ice water and soak for at least 1 hour, or up to overnight in the refrigerator. The sudden cold puffs and wrinkles the skin, which is what gives dim sum chicken feet their soft, spongy texture. Drain and cut each foot in half at the joint.
  5. Mix the black bean garlic sauce, oyster sauce, soy sauces, hoisin, sugar and wine in a bowl. Heat the braising oil in the cleaned pot over high heat and fry the ginger for 30 seconds, then the garlic, shallot, chilies, star anise and most of the black beans for 30 seconds until fragrant. Add the sauce and stir for 45 seconds.
  6. Add the feet and the hot water so they are barely covered. Bring to a boil, then cover and braise over medium-low heat for 45 minutes, stirring now and then, until the skin is tender and the sauce has reduced and coats the feet.
  7. Pile the feet into small heatproof bowls, top with the remaining black beans and the red pepper and steam over boiling water in a flat-bottom wok or steamer for 10 to 15 minutes, until the skin slips easily from the bones. Serve hot in the bowls.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Frying feet that are still damp causes violent spattering; dry them thoroughly and keep a lid handy.
  • Skipping the ice-water soak leaves the skin flat and tough instead of puffy.

Authentic tips

  • Restaurants rest the sauced feet overnight before steaming so the flavor soaks in.
  • Steam in small dishes so each one can come to the table hot.

Substitutions

  • No maltose: honey gives the same color.
  • Pressure cooker: after soaking, cook the sauced feet on high for 15 minutes instead of steaming.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; re-steam for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes well after the final steam for 2 months; re-steam from frozen for 20 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow or siu mai
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
