Chǐ Zhī Fèng Zhǎo (Dim Sum Chicken Feet in Black Bean Sauce) 豉汁凤爪

Steamed chicken feet in black bean sauce, called phoenix claws, are a fixture of the Cantonese yum cha table; they are fried, soaked and steamed so the skin puffs and turns soft and gelatinous.

Method

  1. Clip off the claw tips with kitchen shears, scrub the feet with the salt and rinse. Blanch for 3 minutes, drain, brush with the maltose thinned with a little hot water, and air-dry for at least 1 hour; they must be completely dry before frying.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 180 C (350 F). Fry the feet in batches, partly covered with a lid because they spit hard, for 8 to 10 minutes until deep golden and blistered.
  3. Drop the fried feet straight into a large bowl of ice water and soak for 1 hour, until the skin puffs and wrinkles. Drain.
  4. Simmer the feet with the star anise and water to cover for 30 minutes, then drain. Toss with the black beans, garlic, oyster sauce, both soy sauces, sugar, wine, cornstarch and chili.
  5. Spread in shallow heatproof bowls and steam over high heat for 45 minutes, until the skin is meltingly soft and falls from the bones.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Frying feet that are still damp causes violent spattering; dry them thoroughly and keep a lid handy.
  • Skipping the ice-water soak leaves the skin flat and tough instead of puffy.

Authentic tips

  • Restaurants rest the sauced feet overnight before steaming so the flavor soaks in.
  • Steam in small dishes so each one can come to the table hot.

Substitutions

  • No maltose: honey gives the same color.
  • Pressure cooker: after soaking, cook the sauced feet on high for 15 minutes instead of steaming.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; re-steam for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes well after the final steam for 2 months; re-steam from frozen for 20 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow or siu mai
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
