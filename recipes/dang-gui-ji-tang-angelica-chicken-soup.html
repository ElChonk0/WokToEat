Dāng Guī Jī Tāng (Angelica Root Chicken Soup) 當歸雞湯

▾

Surprise me with

Dang gui, angelica root, is a warming herb used in Taiwanese tonic soups, especially in winter and after childbirth.

  • 1 kg2.2 lbChicken (or bone-in thighs), chopped into pieces
  • 15 g1/2 ozDang gui (angelica root) slices
  • 5 g1/6 ozChuan xiong (lovage root) slices (optional)
  • 15 g2 tbspGoji berries
  • 88Dried red dates (jujubes)
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, sliced
  • 60 ml1/4 cupRice wine (mijiu)
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater
  • 6 g1 tspSalt

Method

  1. Blanch the chicken in boiling water for 3 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Rinse the herbs briefly. Put the chicken, dang gui, chuan xiong, red dates and ginger in a Dutch oven with the water.
  3. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1 hour, until the chicken is tender and the broth fragrant. Dang gui is strong and slightly bitter; a little goes a long way.
  4. Add the goji berries and rice wine and simmer for 10 minutes more.
  5. Season with salt and serve. In Taiwan it is a winter tonic, often eaten in the cold months.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the soup cloudy and bitter.
  • Too much dang gui makes the broth medicinal; use measured amounts.

Authentic tips

  • Avoid in pregnancy without advice from your doctor.
  • Serve with rice or noodles.

Substitutions

  • Skip the dang gui if you dislike the medicinal taste and use more ginger.
  • Chicken drumsticks only shorten the simmer by 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sesame oil noodles
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Warm ginger tea
