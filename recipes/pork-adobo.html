Pork Adobo

▾

Surprise me with

A variation of the same adobo technique used for chicken earlier this book, pork adobo is often considered even richer thanks to the fat rendered from pork belly or shoulder during the long simmer.

  • Prep 10 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 1.5 hr

Method

  1. Marinate the pork with the soy sauce, half the garlic, bay leaves and peppercorns for at least 1 hour.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Lift the pork from the marinade and brown it in batches for 5 to 6 minutes, rendering some fat. Add the remaining garlic for 1 minute.
  3. Pour in the marinade, vinegar and water. Bring to a boil and let it bubble for 2 to 3 minutes without stirring, to mellow the vinegar.
  4. Cover and simmer over low heat for 1 to 1.25 hours, until the pork is tender.
  5. Uncover and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, until the sauce reduces and the pork starts to fry in its own fat. Some cooks let it crisp slightly at this stage (adobong tuyo). Stir in the sugar and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Simmering covered for the entire cooking time, which never lets the vinegar's sharpness mellow or the sauce reduce to the right consistency.
  • Using a lean cut and expecting the same richness as pork belly — shoulder works fine but tastes noticeably less rich; a fair trade for a leaner version.

Authentic tips

  • As with chicken adobo, this tastes even better the next day — consider making it a day ahead.
  • For a richer variation (adobo sa gata), stir in a splash of coconut milk in the last 5 minutes of cooking.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can replace pork using the earlier Chicken Adobo recipe's shorter cooking time.
  • Hard-boiled eggs can be added in the last 10 minutes to simmer in the sauce, a common addition in Filipino households.

Storage

Leftovers: Excellent shredded into adobo fried rice or adobo flakes the next day.

Freezing: Freezes excellently for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
