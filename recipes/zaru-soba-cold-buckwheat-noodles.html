Zaru Soba (Cold Buckwheat Noodles) ざるそば

▾

Surprise me with

Zaru soba, chilled buckwheat noodles with a dipping sauce, is the classic Japanese summer dish, and eating it noisily is traditionally regarded as a sign of appreciation.

  • 400 g14 ozDried soba noodles
  • 240 ml1 cupKombu and shiitake dashi
  • 60 ml1/4 cupSoy sauce
  • 60 ml1/4 cupMirin
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 4 g2 sheetsNori, shredded
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion, finely sliced
  • 10 g2 tspWasabi

Method

  1. Make the tsuyu dipping sauce: boil the mirin for 30 seconds to cook off the alcohol, add the soy sauce, sugar and dashi and simmer for 1 minute. Chill it well.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil (no salt) and add the soba, stirring so it does not stick. Cook according to the packet, usually 4 to 5 minutes, adding a splash of cold water if it threatens to boil over.
  3. Drain and rinse the noodles under cold running water, rubbing them between your hands to wash off the surface starch, which makes them sticky. Then plunge them into ice water to firm up.
  4. Drain very well and pile onto bamboo zaru trays or plates. Top with shredded nori.
  5. Serve with the chilled tsuyu, green onion and wasabi to mix in. Dip a mouthful of noodles at a time, and slurp.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the rinse leaves gummy noodles.
  • Overcooking makes them mushy.

Authentic tips

  • Save the cooking water (sobayu) to dilute the leftover dip.
  • Slurp noisily.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought mentsuyu replaces homemade dipping sauce.
  • Somen replaces soba.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; rinse before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Tempura
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
