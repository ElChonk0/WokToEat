Xi Hu Niu Rou Geng (West Lake Beef Soup) 西湖牛肉羹

West Lake beef soup, a thick soup of minced beef, egg white and cilantro, is a fixture of Cantonese banquets and restaurant menus; its thick, egg-ribboned body is typical of Cantonese geng (thickened soups).

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the soy sauce, cornstarch and 2 tablespoons of water. Blanch it in boiling water for 30 seconds, stirring to separate, then drain; this keeps the soup clear.
  2. Bring the stock to a boil with the mushrooms and tofu and simmer for 5 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.
  3. Stir in the cornstarch mixed with the water and simmer for 1 minute until it lightly coats a spoon.
  4. Add the beef. Lower the heat, stir the soup slowly in one direction and drizzle in the egg whites so they form white ribbons.
  5. Stir in the cilantro and sesame oil and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding raw beef straight to the soup clouds it with grey scum.
  • Boiling hard after adding the egg whites breaks them into specks.

Authentic tips

  • Hand-mincing the beef with a cleaver gives a better texture than ground beef.
  • A drizzle of Worcestershire sauce at the table is a popular Hong Kong touch.

Substitutions

  • Ground beef works if you blanch it the same way.
  • Use whole eggs instead of whites.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended; the thickened soup turns watery.

Serve with
