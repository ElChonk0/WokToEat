Dōngběi Lā Pí (Cold Mung Bean Starch Sheet Salad) 东北拉皮

▾

Surprise me with

La pi, translucent mung bean starch sheets tossed with cucumber, carrot and sesame sauce, is a popular cold dish in Dongbei, served in summer.

  • 300 g10 ozMung bean starch sheets (la pi), soaked and cut into strips
  • 250 g9 ozCucumber and carrot, julienned
  • 80 g3 ozRed cabbage or wood ear, shredded (optional)
  • 22Eggs, made into thin omelets and julienned
  • 30 g2 tbspSesame paste
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 20 ml4 tspRice vinegar
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic, crushed
  • 5 g1 tspChinese mustard
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 15 g1/2 ozChili oil and cilantro

Method

  1. Soak the dried la pi sheets in hot water for 10 minutes until soft and translucent, then rinse in cold water and cut into wide strips.
  2. Thin the sesame paste with 2 to 3 tablespoons of water, then stir in the soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, garlic, mustard and sesame oil. The sharp mustard is the northeastern signature.
  3. Arrange the la pi on a platter with the vegetables and egg strips in separate piles, like spokes.
  4. Pour the dressing over at the table and add chili oil and cilantro.
  5. Toss everything together and serve cold, an essential summer and banquet appetizer in the northeast.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking makes the sheets mushy.
  • A thick sauce clumps; thin it with water.

Authentic tips

  • Serve chilled.
  • Add a spoonful of chili oil if you like heat.

Substitutions

  • Cold glass noodles replace the sheets.
  • Peanut butter replaces sesame paste.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; dress just before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed buns
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
