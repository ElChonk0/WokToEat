Idli Sambar (Steamed Rice Cakes with Lentil Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

Idli, steamed fermented rice and lentil cakes, are the staple South Indian breakfast, served with sambar and coconut chutney across Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.

Method

  1. If making the batter, soak 2 parts idli rice and 1 part urad dal separately for 6 hours, grind each smooth, mix with salt and leave in a warm place for 8 to 12 hours until bubbly and doubled. Fermentation makes idlis soft and spongy.
  2. Cook the toor dal with turmeric and 500 ml (2 cups) water until mushy, then whisk smooth. Simmer the vegetables in 400 ml (1.75 cups) water with the tamarind, tomato, sambar powder, jaggery and salt for 10 minutes until tender, then stir in the dal and simmer for 5 minutes.
  3. For the tempering, heat the oil and splutter the mustard and fenugreek seeds, then add the chilies, curry leaves and asafoetida for 10 seconds. Pour it over the sambar.
  4. Grease an idli stand, fill each mold three-quarters full with batter without stirring it (stirring knocks out the air) and steam over boiling water, covered, for 10 to 12 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  5. Rest for 2 minutes, then ease the idlis out with a wet spoon. Serve hot with sambar and coconut chutney, the classic South Indian breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Opening the steamer early deflates the idli.
  • Too-thin batter gives flat idlis.

Authentic tips

  • Ferment the batter overnight in a warm place.
  • Serve hot.

Substitutions

  • Ready-made batter saves time.
  • Idlis can be steamed in a muffin tin.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; re-steam for 5 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze idlis for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Coconut chutney
