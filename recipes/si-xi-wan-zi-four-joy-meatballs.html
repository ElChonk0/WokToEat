Sì Xǐ Wán Zi (Four Joy Meatballs) 四喜丸子

▾

Surprise me with

Si xi wan zi, four large braised meatballs symbolizing good fortune, joy, wealth and longevity, are a Beijing banquet dish eaten at New Year and weddings.

Method

  1. Mix the pork with the ginger, green onion, meatball soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and salt, then add the ginger-scallion water a little at a time, stirring in one direction until absorbed. Add the egg, water chestnuts and cornstarch and slap the mixture against the bowl 20 times until springy.
  2. Divide into 4 large balls; four for the 'four joys' of fortune, wealth, long life and happiness. Wet your hands and toss each between your palms until smooth.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry the meatballs for 4 to 5 minutes, turning, until golden all over. This sets the outside so they keep their shape.
  4. Put them in a pot with the sauce soy sauces, sugar, star anise and stock, bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 40 minutes, turning once.
  5. Arrange the meatballs on the cabbage. Thicken the braising liquid with the slurry and pour it over. A dish for weddings and the New Year.

Common mistakes

  • Overworking the pork makes the meatballs dense.
  • Boiling hard breaks them up.

Authentic tips

  • Chilling the mixture makes shaping easier.
  • Serve over rice with the cabbage and sauce.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken thigh replaces some of the pork for a lighter version.
  • Napa cabbage can be replaced by bok choy.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat in the sauce.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months; thaw overnight and reheat gently.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
