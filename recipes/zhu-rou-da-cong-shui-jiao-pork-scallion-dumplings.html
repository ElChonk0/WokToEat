Zhū Ròu Dà Cōng Shuǐ Jiǎo (Pork and Scallion Dumplings) 猪肉大葱水饺

▾

Surprise me with

Large scallions are a Shandong specialty, and pork and scallion dumplings are the classic filling in the province, eaten at New Year.

  • 60 ml1/4 cupBlack vinegar and garlic, for dipping

Method

  1. Chop the thick scallions finely and toss them with the sesame oil first; the oil coats them so they stay juicy and do not weep.
  2. Mix the pork with the ginger, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, salt and pepper. Add the Sichuan pepper water a tablespoon at a time, stirring hard in one direction, until it is absorbed and the filling turns sticky and springy. This makes juicy dumplings. Fold in the vegetables and sesame oil.
  3. Put a heaping teaspoon of filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it in half, pinch the middle and pleat or press the edges to seal tightly.
  4. Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and add 15 to 20 dumplings, stirring gently so they do not stick. When it returns to a boil, add 250 ml (1 cup) of cold water; repeat twice. They are done when they float and look puffed, 6 to 8 minutes.
  5. Lift out with a slotted spoon and serve with black vinegar, garlic and chili oil. Shandong is famous for its dumplings, made thick-skinned and generously filled.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the scallions early makes the filling watery.
  • Overfilling splits the wrappers.

Authentic tips

  • Shandong's large scallions are sweet and mild; regular scallions work too.
  • Dumplings freeze well uncooked.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken thigh replaces the pork.
  • Green onion tops replace large scallions.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; pan-fry leftovers.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; boil from frozen for 8 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black vinegar dip
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
