Guō Bāo Ròu (Northeastern Sweet and Sour Pork) 锅包肉

▾

Surprise me with

From China's northeastern Dongbei region, guo bao rou batters and fries pork slices before tossing them in a tangy, less tomato-forward sweet-sour sauce than the Cantonese version, finished with fresh cilantro and carrot.

Method

  1. Coat pork slices in egg, then dredge thickly in potato starch.
  2. Heat oil to 175°C (350°F) and fry pork in batches until golden and crisp, 4–5 minutes; drain and rest 5 minutes.
  3. Re-fry briefly for extra crispness; drain again.
  4. Whisk vinegar, sugar, and soy sauce for the sauce; bring to a simmer in a separate pan until slightly thickened.
  5. Add carrot and green onion to the sauce, cooking 1 minute.
  6. Toss the fried pork through the sauce quickly and serve immediately, garnished with cilantro.

Common mistakes

  • Tossing the pork in sauce too far ahead of serving, which quickly softens the crispy coating.
  • Using too little vinegar — this dish should taste distinctly sour before the sugar rounds it out.

Authentic tips

  • The thick potato starch coating and double-fry are what give guo bao rou its distinctively crackling crust.
  • This dish should be assembled and served immediately once sauced, unlike some braises that improve with rest.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh strips take the same batter.
  • Rice vinegar plus a little ketchup mimics the sauce if needed.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crust softens within minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with
