Bì Fēng Táng Chǎo Xiè (Typhoon Shelter Crab) 避风塘炒蟹

Typhoon shelter crab is a Hong Kong dish from the boat people of Causeway Bay: crab is fried and stir-fried with a crispy topping of garlic, chili and fermented black beans.

  • Prep 25 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 1 kg2.2 lbCrab, cleaned and cut into pieces
  • 40 g1/3 cupCornstarch
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 12 cloves12 clovesGarlic, minced
  • 30 g1 ozFermented black beans, rinsed
  • 10 g1/3 ozDried chilies, snipped
  • 30 g1 ozFresh red chilies, sliced
  • 60 g1 cupPanko or fried bread crumbs
  • 40 g1.5 ozGreen onion, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper
  • 5 g1 tspSugar

Method

  1. Pat the crab pieces dry and dust with the cornstarch.
  2. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) and fry the crab in two batches for 3 to 4 minutes until the shells turn bright red, then drain.
  3. Pour off all but 45 ml of the oil, fry the crumbs over medium heat for 2 minutes until golden, then lift out.
  4. In the same oil, fry the garlic for 2 minutes until golden and crisp, add the black beans and both chilies for 1 minute.
  5. Return the crab, add the wine, salt, pepper and sugar, and toss over high heat for 2 minutes until coated.
  6. Add the crumbs and green onion, toss for 30 seconds, and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the garlic turns the dish bitter; fry it slowly until golden.
  • Wet crab makes the oil spit; dry it well.

Authentic tips

  • Mud crab or blue swimmer crab both work.
  • Use the crumb and garlic topping on prawns or squid too.

Substitutions

  • Large prawns replace crab.
  • Crushed fried shallots replace the crumbs.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched greens
  • Soup: Clear winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea or cold beer
