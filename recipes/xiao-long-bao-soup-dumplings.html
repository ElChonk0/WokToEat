Xiǎo Lóng Bāo (Soup Dumplings) 小笼包

▾

Surprise me with

Xiao long bao, steamed dumplings filled with pork and gelatinized broth that melts into soup, originated in Jiangnan and are the signature dish of Shanghai and Nanxiang.

  • 250 g2 cupsFlour
  • 130 ml1/2 cupWarm water
  • 400 g14 ozPork, ground
  • 200 g7 ozPork skin aspic, chopped (jellied stock)
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Mix the flour with the warm water into a firm dough, knead 5 minutes and rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork with the ginger, scallion, soy sauce and sesame oil, stir in one direction until sticky, then fold in the chopped aspic. Chill for at least 1 hour so it stays cold.
  3. Divide the dough into 24 pieces, roll each into a thin 8 cm circle with slightly thinner edges, and fill each with about 1 tablespoon of filling.
  4. Pleat the edges up and around the filling in 14-18 folds and pinch closed at the top.
  5. Line a steamer with parchment or cabbage and steam the dumplings 2 cm apart over high heat for 10 minutes.
  6. Serve immediately with black vinegar and julienned ginger.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the filling warm up melts the aspic early and makes folding messy.
  • Overstuffing or thin, torn wrappers lose the soup in the steamer.

Authentic tips

  • Make the aspic by simmering pork skin or trotters, straining and chilling until set; chicken stock set with gelatin works too.
  • Eat each dumpling by biting a small hole and sipping the soup first.

Substitutions

  • Unflavored gelatin dissolved in hot stock replaces pork skin aspic.
  • Ground chicken thigh replaces some of the pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; leftovers keep 1 day and re-steam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; steam from frozen for 14 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Ginger and black vinegar dip
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
