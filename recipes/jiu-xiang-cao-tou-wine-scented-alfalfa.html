Jiǔ Xiāng Cǎo Tóu (Wine-Scented Clover Shoots) 酒香草头

▾

Surprise me with

Cao tou, tender clover or alfalfa shoots, is a Shanghai spring vegetable, stir-fried with a splash of Shaoxing wine to bring out its aroma.

  • Cook 5 min

  • 400 g14 ozCao tou (clover shoots)
  • 45 ml3 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt
  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Pick over the cao tou (clover shoots), keeping only the tender tips, and wash and drain well.
  2. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat until it just smokes. Add the garlic for 5 seconds.
  3. Tip in the greens all at once and toss rapidly for 20 to 30 seconds, until they just begin to wilt.
  4. Pour the Shaoxing wine around the hot edge of the wok so it flares and steams, season with the salt and sugar and toss for 10 seconds more. The 'wine-scented' (jiu xiang) aroma comes from the wine hitting the very hot wok.
  5. Serve at once, while the greens are still bright green. A Shanghai spring dish; snow pea shoots work the same way.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the tender shoots limp; they need under two minutes.
  • Wet greens make the dish watery.

Authentic tips

  • The sizzling wine is the signature; use a very hot wok.
  • Spinach or pea shoots work if you cannot find cao tou.

Substitutions

  • Pea shoots or spinach replace the clover shoots.
  • A splash of rice vinegar can balance the wine.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; eat within the hour.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
