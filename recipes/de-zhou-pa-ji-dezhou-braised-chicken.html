Dézhōu Pá Jī (Dezhou Braised Chicken) 德州扒鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Dezhou braised chicken, simmered for hours in a spice-and-soy stock until the meat falls from the bone, is the most famous chicken dish of Shandong, a specialty of the city of Dezhou.

  • 1.5 kg3.3 lbWhole chicken
  • 90 ml6 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 80 g5 tbspGinger, scallion, garlic
  • 15 g1/2 ozStar anise, cinnamon, cloves, fennel
  • 60 ml4 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 2 L8 cupsWater

Method

  1. Pat the chicken dry and brush the skin with a spoonful of the dark soy sauce; sear in a little oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat until golden all over, 8-10 minutes.
  2. In a pot just large enough for the bird, bring the water, remaining soy sauces, rock sugar, wine, ginger, scallion, garlic and spices to a boil and simmer 10 minutes.
  3. Lower in the chicken, breast side up, and add water if needed to come almost to the top; cover and cook on the lowest heat for 90 minutes, turning once, until the meat nearly falls from the bone.
  4. Switch off the heat and cool the chicken in the stock for 30 minutes so it stays moist and takes up flavor.
  5. Lift out carefully and serve whole, pulling the meat from the bones at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard breaks the skin and toughens the meat; the stock should barely tremble.
  • Lifting the chicken out hot makes it fall apart; cool it in the stock first.

Authentic tips

  • The stock is a master sauce; strain and freeze it for the next batch.
  • Serve cold or at room temperature with steamed buns.

Substitutions

  • Chicken legs only cook in 75 minutes.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the simmer to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; tear and serve cold or at room temperature.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months in its braising liquid.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed buns or rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
