Ròu Suì Zhēng Dàn (Steamed Egg with Minced Pork) 肉碎蒸蛋

▾

Silky steamed egg topped with seasoned minced pork is a Cantonese home favorite, cooked on a plate in the steamer alongside the rice.

  • 150 g5 ozGround pork
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 8 g1 tbspCornstarch
  • 1 g1/4 tspSalt, for the pork
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspGround white pepper
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater, for the pork
  • 10 ml2 tspNeutral oil
  • 33Eggs
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the eggs
  • 270 ml1 cup plus 2 tbspWarm water or stock
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce, to finish
  • 3 ml1/2 tspSesame oil
  • 1 stalk1 stalkGreen onion, finely chopped

Method

  1. Mix the pork with the oyster sauce, cornstarch, salt, pepper and water until sticky, then stir in 1 teaspoon of the oil.
  2. Beat the eggs with the salt until smooth but not foamy. Stir in the warm water gently, about 1.5 parts water to 1 part egg by volume. Warm, not hot, water keeps the custard smooth; hot water starts cooking the egg.
  3. Grease a shallow, straight-sided heatproof dish about 2.5 cm (1 in) deep with the remaining oil and spread the pork over the bottom in a thin, even layer.
  4. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer over boiling water, put in the dish, cover and steam over medium heat for 3 to 5 minutes, until the pork is mostly cooked and has released some juice.
  5. Pour the egg mixture slowly over the pork, through a sieve if you want an extra-smooth top, and skim off any bubbles. Cover the dish with a plate so condensation cannot drip onto the custard.
  6. Steam over medium heat for 5 to 6 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave covered for 3 to 4 minutes. It is done when the custard is set with only a slight wobble. Strong steam makes it pitted and spongy, so keep the heat moderate; if it is still liquid, give it another minute.
  7. Drizzle with the soy sauce and sesame oil, scatter with green onion and serve hot with rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Steaming on high heat makes the egg pocked and spongy.
  • Leaving it uncovered lets condensation drip and dimple the surface.

Authentic tips

  • A ratio of about 1 part egg to 1.5 parts water gives the silkiest custard.
  • Prop the steamer lid open a crack to keep the temperature gentle.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken replaces pork.
  • Add a salted egg or century egg, diced, to the custard.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic gai lan
  • Soup: Spinach tofu soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
