Pho Bo (Beef Noodle Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Vietnam's most famous export, pho bo simmers beef bones and spices for hours into a fragrant, clear broth, served over rice noodles with thinly sliced raw beef that cooks in the hot broth tableside.

Method

  1. Char onion and ginger under a broiler or in a dry cast-iron skillet over high heat until blackened in spots; rinse off any loose char.
  2. Blanch beef bones and brisket in boiling water 5 minutes; drain and rinse.
  3. Combine bones, brisket, charred onion and ginger, star anise, and cinnamon in a large pot with plenty of water.
  4. Bring to a boil, skim thoroughly, then reduce to a very low simmer, partially covered, for 2.5–3 hours.
  5. Strain the broth, season with fish sauce, and slice the cooked brisket thin.
  6. Cook rice noodles, divide among bowls, top with cooked brisket and raw sliced sirloin, then ladle the very hot broth over top to cook the raw beef; serve with herbs and lime.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the charring step for onion and ginger, which is essential to pho's distinctive aromatic broth.
  • Boiling the broth hard instead of a gentle simmer, which makes it cloudy instead of clear.

Authentic tips

  • The broth must be genuinely very hot when ladled over the raw beef slices, since that heat alone is what cooks them.
  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a large batch and freeze portions.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can replace beef entirely for a pho ga variation, with a shorter cooking time.
  • Oxtail can be added to the bones for extra richness.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 4 days refrigerated; store noodles and raw beef separately.

Freezing: The broth freezes excellently for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Herbs, bean sprouts, lime and hoisin
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Vietnamese iced coffee
