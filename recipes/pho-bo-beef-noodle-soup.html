Pho Bo (Beef Noodle Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Vietnam's most famous export, pho bo simmers beef bones and spices for hours into a fragrant, clear broth, served over rice noodles with thinly sliced raw beef that cooks in the hot broth tableside.

Method

  1. Soak the bones in cold water for 1 hour, then blanch them with the oxtail in boiling water for 10 minutes. Drain, rinse every piece and scrub the pot. This is the single most important step for a clear broth.
  2. Char the onions and ginger, cut-side up, under the broiler or directly over a flame for 10 to 15 minutes until blackened in spots, then rinse off the loose char. Toast the star anise, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom, coriander and fennel in a dry pan for 2 minutes and tie them in a spice bag.
  3. Return the bones and oxtail to the pot with the water, bring to a boil, then lower to a bare simmer. Skim, add the brisket, charred onion and ginger. Never let it boil hard, or the broth turns cloudy.
  4. Lift out the brisket after 1.5 hours, when tender, and plunge it into cold water to stop it darkening. Add the spice bag for the last 1 hour of a 5 to 6 hour simmer, then remove it; longer and the spices turn bitter.
  5. Strain the broth and season with fish sauce, rock sugar and salt. It should be clear, fragrant and well seasoned, since the noodles and raw beef dilute it.
  6. Soak the noodles in warm water for 30 minutes, then dip them into boiling water for 10 to 20 seconds and divide among bowls. Top with sliced brisket and raw sirloin, ladle over the boiling broth to cook the sirloin pink, and add onion and green onion. Serve with herbs, sprouts, lime, chili and sauces on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the charring step for onion and ginger, which is essential to pho's distinctive aromatic broth.
  • Boiling the broth hard instead of a gentle simmer, which makes it cloudy instead of clear.

Authentic tips

  • The broth must be genuinely very hot when ladled over the raw beef slices, since that heat alone is what cooks them.
  • This broth freezes and reheats exceptionally well; make a large batch and freeze portions.

Substitutions

  • Chicken can replace beef entirely for a pho ga variation, with a shorter cooking time.
  • Oxtail can be added to the bones for extra richness.

Storage

Leftovers: The broth keeps 4 days refrigerated; store noodles and raw beef separately.

Freezing: The broth freezes excellently for up to 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Herbs, bean sprouts, lime and hoisin
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Vietnamese iced coffee
