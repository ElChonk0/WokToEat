Beef Tapa (Filipino Cured Beef, Tapsilog)

▾

Surprise me with

Tapa, thinly sliced beef cured in soy, vinegar and garlic, is the centerpiece of the Filipino breakfast "tapsilog" with garlic fried rice and egg.

Method

  1. Mix the soy sauce, calamansi, garlic, brown sugar and pepper and toss with the beef. Marinate overnight in the refrigerator; tapa is a cured style of beef, and the long marinade seasons it through.
  2. Make sinangag: fry the rice garlic in 1 tablespoon of oil over medium-low heat until golden, add the rice and salt and toss over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes. Keep warm.
  3. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Add the beef with a splash of the marinade and a few spoons of water and cook until the liquid evaporates, then keep frying for 2 to 3 minutes until caramelized at the edges.
  4. Fry the eggs sunny-side up in the remaining oil.
  5. Serve the tapa with the garlic rice and a fried egg: tap-si-log, from tapa, sinangag and itlog (egg). Spiced vinegar on the side is essential.

Common mistakes

  • Crowding the pan steams the beef.
  • Too much sugar burns.

Authentic tips

  • Pound the beef to tenderize.
  • A classic breakfast with a spiced vinegar dip.

Substitutions

  • Pork replaces beef for a porksilog.
  • Use thin, tenderized slices for the best texture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Freezes the marinated raw beef for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic rice, fried egg and spiced vinegar
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot coffee
