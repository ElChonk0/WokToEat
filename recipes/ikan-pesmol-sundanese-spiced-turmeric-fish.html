Ikan Pesmol (Sundanese Spiced Turmeric Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Pesmol is a West Javan preparation in which fried fish is simmered in a turmeric, nutmeg and tamarind sauce until the sauce is absorbed.

  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt, for the sauce

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the salt and lime juice and leave for 10 minutes. Pat dry and fry in the oil over medium-high heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side, until golden. Drain and pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, turmeric, ginger, candlenuts and chilies into a paste. Fry it in the reserved oil with the lemongrass and bay leaves over medium heat for 5 minutes, until fragrant.
  3. Add the tomato and cook for 2 minutes, then the tamarind, water, nutmeg and sauce salt. Simmer for 5 minutes until slightly thickened.
  4. Return the fish and spoon the sauce over it. Simmer for 2 to 3 minutes so it absorbs the flavor.
  5. Serve with rice. Pesmol is a Sundanese dish from West Java, sour and gently spiced.

Cook’s notes

Written for cod loin. Sundanese cooks use carp or tilapia.

Common mistakes

  • Too much stirring breaks the fish; baste instead.
  • Under-frying the paste leaves a raw spice taste.

Authentic tips

  • Pesmol should end with a thick, clinging sauce.
  • A light hand with nutmeg is enough.

Substitutions

  • Haddock replaces the cod.
  • Ground turmeric (2 g) replaces fresh turmeric.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Soup: Sayur asem
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
