Bánh Xèo Tôm (Crispy Shrimp Crepe)

▾

Surprise me with

Banh xeo, a crisp turmeric rice-flour crepe, is a southern Vietnamese street dish named for the sizzling sound of the batter hitting the pan.

  • Prep 15 min (plus 30 min resting)
  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, turmeric, salt, coconut milk and water into a thin batter, like cream, and stir in the green onion. Rest for 30 minutes. A thin batter is what makes the crepe lacy and crisp; banh xeo means 'sizzling cake', for the sound it makes in the pan.
  2. Make the nuoc cham: stir the sugar into the warm water until dissolved, then add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic and chili. It should taste sweet, sour and salty in balance.
  3. Heat a teaspoon of oil in a 25 cm (10 in) nonstick skillet over medium-high heat and fry a few slices of pork and onion for 1 minute, then add a few shrimp for 30 seconds.
  4. Stir the batter and pour in a ladleful, swirling at once to coat the pan thinly. Scatter with bean sprouts, cover and cook for 2 minutes.
  5. Uncover, drizzle a little oil around the edge and cook for 3 to 4 minutes more, until the bottom is deep golden and crisp and the edges pull away. Fold in half and slide out. Repeat.
  6. Eat by tearing off a piece, wrapping it in lettuce and herbs, and dipping in nuoc cham.

Common mistakes

  • Thick batter doesn't crisp.
  • Pan not hot enough means no sizzle.

Authentic tips

  • Wrap pieces in lettuce with herbs before dipping.
  • Beer in the batter adds crispness.

Substitutions

  • Beer in the batter adds crispness.
  • Bean sprouts and pork replace shrimp.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lettuce, herbs and nuoc cham
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
