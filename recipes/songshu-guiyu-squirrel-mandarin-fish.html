Sōngshǔ Guìyú (Squirrel Mandarin Fish) 松鼠桂鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Squirrel fish is a Suzhou banquet classic: the fish is scored, fried until its flesh fans out like a squirrel's tail, and sauced with sweet-and-sour tomato.

  • 15 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsNeutral oil for frying

Method

  1. Cut the fish in half lengthwise, remove the backbone and score the flesh in a 1 cm crosshatch, cutting almost to the skin.
  2. Coat the fish thoroughly in cornstarch, pressing into the cuts, and shake off the excess.
  3. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) and fry the fish, flesh-side down, for 4-5 minutes until golden and fanned out; drain.
  4. Simmer the ketchup, sugar, vinegar, garlic and 100 ml water until syrupy, then add the peas and carrots.
  5. Arrange the fish on a platter and pour the hot sauce over; serve immediately while crackling.

Common mistakes

  • Scoring too shallowly means the flesh will not open into the squirrel's tail.
  • A sauce that has stood makes the crust soggy; pour it over at the table.

Authentic tips

  • Dry the fish very well before coating.
  • Fry the fish twice for extra crispness.

Substitutions

  • Whole sea bass or tilapia replaces the mandarin fish.
  • Ketchup is a common modern shortcut; use tomato paste plus sugar for a deeper sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; the crust softens within 20 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
