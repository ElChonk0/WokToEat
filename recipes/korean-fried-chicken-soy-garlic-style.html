Korean Fried Chicken, Soy-Garlic Style 간장마늘치킨

▾

Surprise me with

Korean fried chicken is known for its exceptionally crackling, non-greasy double-fried crust; the soy-garlic (ganjang) glaze is one of the two most popular styles alongside the sweet-spicy yangnyeom version.

Method

  1. Season the chicken with the salt, pepper and ginger and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight; the dry seasoning gets into the meat and helps the skin crisp.
  2. Simmer the soy sauce, rice wine, sugar, honey, garlic, vinegar and sauce water over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes, until slightly thick and glossy. Turn off the heat.
  3. Whisk the flour, potato starch, baking powder and ice water into a smooth, thin batter. Less water gives a thicker crust; more gives a lighter, crackly one.
  4. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 160 to 165 C (320 to 330 F). Dip the chicken in the batter and fry in two batches for about 6 minutes, until cooked through and pale golden. Drain on a rack and rest for a few minutes.
  5. Raise the oil to 175 to 180 C (350 to 360 F) and fry all the chicken again for about 5 minutes, until deep golden and very crisp. Resting between fries lets moisture rise to the surface so the second fry can drive it out.
  6. Brush the sauce onto the hot chicken, or toss it quickly in a large bowl, and sprinkle with sesame seeds and green onion. Brushing rather than drenching keeps the crust crackling. Serve with pickled radish.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the double-fry, which is essential to Korean fried chicken's non-greasy, extra-crisp texture.
  • Tossing the chicken in glaze too far ahead of serving, which softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Potato starch gives a crispier, more shatter-prone crust than flour — a hallmark of Korean fried chicken specifically.
  • The glaze should be thick enough to coat the chicken without pooling; reduce it a little further if it seems thin.

Substitutions

  • Chicken wings are the more traditional cut, if you'd like to try this style beyond thighs.
  • For a spicier version, whisk 1–2 tbsp gochujang into the glaze.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1–2 days but loses crispness.

Freezing: Freezes well, fried and unglazed, for up to a month; re-fry from frozen before glazing.
