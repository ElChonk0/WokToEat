Korean Fried Chicken, Soy-Garlic Style 간장마늘치킨

Korean fried chicken is known for its exceptionally crackling, non-greasy double-fried crust; the soy-garlic (ganjang) glaze is one of the two most popular styles alongside the sweet-spicy yangnyeom version.

  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Toss chicken pieces in potato starch to coat evenly.
  2. Heat oil to 170°C (340°F) and fry chicken in batches 5–6 minutes until light golden; drain and rest 5 minutes.
  3. Increase oil heat to 190°C (375°F) and fry all the chicken again together 2 minutes until deeply golden and extra crisp.
  4. Meanwhile, simmer soy sauce, garlic, ginger, sugar, and vinegar in a small pan until slightly thickened, 3–4 minutes.
  5. Toss the hot fried chicken in the glaze until evenly coated.
  6. Garnish with sesame seeds and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the double-fry, which is essential to Korean fried chicken's non-greasy, extra-crisp texture.
  • Tossing the chicken in glaze too far ahead of serving, which softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Potato starch gives a crispier, more shatter-prone crust than flour — a hallmark of Korean fried chicken specifically.
  • The glaze should be thick enough to coat the chicken without pooling; reduce it a little further if it seems thin.

Substitutions

  • Chicken wings are the more traditional cut, if you'd like to try this style beyond thighs.
  • For a spicier version, whisk 1–2 tbsp gochujang into the glaze.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1–2 days but loses crispness.

Freezing: Freezes well, fried and unglazed, for up to a month; re-fry from frozen before glazing.
