Black Pepper Beef 黑椒牛柳

▾

Surprise me with

A Cantonese stir-fry with clear Western influence via Hong Kong's cha chaan teng (tea restaurant) culture, black pepper beef pairs seared beef and onion with a bold, coarsely cracked black pepper sauce.

Method

  1. Rinse the beef strips in cold water for 1 minute, drain and squeeze out the moisture. Massage in the marinade pepper, cornstarch, oyster sauce, soy sauce, wine, water and sesame oil for 30 to 60 seconds, then the marinade oil. Mix the sauce ingredients in a cup.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes, then add the oil. Add the beef and stir-fry for about 1 minute, just until it changes color, and lift it out. It finishes in the sauce, so do not cook it through now.
  3. Fry the garlic for 20 to 30 seconds until fragrant, then add the onion and pepper and stir-fry for about 90 seconds, keeping them crisp. Avoid watery vegetables such as bok choy, which thin the sauce.
  4. Return the beef for 45 to 60 seconds, then stir the sauce and pour it in. Toss for about 1 minute until glossy; if the dish looks wet, use only part of the sauce.
  5. Add the butter if using, then scatter the finishing pepper around the hot edge of the wok so it toasts, and toss for 45 to 60 seconds until the pepper smells sharp and aromatic. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Using pre-ground black pepper instead of freshly cracked, which lacks the coarse texture and aroma the dish relies on.
  • Overcooking the beef, which turns it tough — sear it quickly and remove promptly.

Authentic tips

  • Coarsely cracking whole black peppercorns (with a mortar and pestle or the flat of a knife) gives noticeably better texture and aroma than pre-ground pepper.
  • The touch of butter at the end is a distinctly Hong Kong cha chaan teng flourish that adds richness.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh takes the same sauce and cooks in the same time.
  • Without bell pepper, extra onion alone is fine.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat quickly in a hot pan so the beef stays tender.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month without the vegetables, but the beef is best fresh.
