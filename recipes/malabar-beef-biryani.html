Malabar Beef Biryani

▾

Surprise me with

Malabar biryani, the Kerala version with short-grain kaima rice and tender beef cooked on dum, is a signature of Kozhikode's Mappila Muslim cooking.

Method

  1. Fry the onions in the oil over medium heat for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring, until deep golden and crisp. Lift them out. Fry the cashews and raisins in the same oil for 1 minute.
  2. Pressure-cook or simmer the beef with half the ginger-garlic-chili mix, turmeric and a little salt and water until tender, 1 to 1.5 hours.
  3. In a wide pot, heat half the ghee and fry the remaining crushed ginger, garlic and chilies for 2 minutes, then the tomatoes for 5 minutes. Add the yogurt, garam masala, salt, half the fried onions, half the herbs and the cooked beef with its juices. Simmer for 10 minutes until thick. Add the lemon juice.
  4. Meanwhile, heat the rest of the ghee in another pot, fry the whole spices for 30 seconds, add the drained rice and toss for 2 minutes. Add 750 ml (3 cups) boiling water and the rice salt and cook covered over low heat until the water is absorbed and the rice is just tender, about 12 minutes.
  5. Spread the rice over the beef masala in the wide pot and top with the remaining fried onions, cashews, raisins and herbs. Seal the lid with foil or dough.
  6. Cook over very low heat (dum) for 20 to 25 minutes, ideally on a heat diffuser, so the steam marries the layers. Mix gently from the side when serving. Malabar biryani uses short-grained, fragrant kaima rice.

Common mistakes

  • Mushy rice if boiled too long.
  • A poor seal lets the steam out.

Authentic tips

  • Kaima (jeerakasala) rice is the traditional grain.
  • Serve with raita and pickle.

Substitutions

  • Basmati replaces kaima rice.
  • A pressure cooker for the beef cuts 30 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat covered.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Raita and pickle
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Pudding
  • Beverage: Black tea
