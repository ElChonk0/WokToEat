Lo Mai Gai (Sticky Rice Chicken in Lotus Leaf) 糯米鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Lo mai gai, glutinous rice with chicken, mushrooms and Chinese sausage steamed inside a lotus leaf, is a staple of Cantonese dim sum and a common breakfast in Hong Kong.

Method

  1. Drain the rice, spread it on a cloth in a steamer and steam over high heat for 30 minutes until tender, then mix in the soy sauces, oyster sauce, sugar and 1 tablespoon of oil. Soak the lotus leaves in hot water for 30 minutes until pliable.
  2. Mix the chicken with the oyster sauce, soy sauce and cornstarch. Heat the remaining oil in a flat-bottom wok and stir-fry the sausage, mushrooms and shrimp for 2 minutes, then add the chicken and cook for 3 minutes until just done.
  3. Lay a lotus leaf half shiny side down. Spread a quarter of the rice in the center, top with a quarter of the filling and an egg yolk, then cover with a little more rice.
  4. Fold the leaf into a tight rectangular parcel. Repeat to make 4.
  5. Steam the parcels seam-side down over high heat for 40 minutes. Open at the table.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Unsoaked glutinous rice stays hard; it needs at least 6 hours in water.
  • Wrapping loosely lets steam in and makes the rice soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Dim sum houses make smaller versions called jan ju gai, "pearl chicken".
  • Make a batch and freeze the parcels; they reheat perfectly by steaming.

Substitutions

  • No lotus leaves: wrap in parchment and foil, though the tea-like aroma will be missing.
  • Leave out the salted yolks if you cannot find them.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; re-steam for 15 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes wrapped for 3 months; steam from frozen for 30 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and steamed greens
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
