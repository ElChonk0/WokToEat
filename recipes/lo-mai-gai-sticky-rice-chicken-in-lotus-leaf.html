Lo Mai Gai (Sticky Rice Chicken in Lotus Leaf) 糯米鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Lo mai gai, glutinous rice with chicken, mushrooms and Chinese sausage steamed inside a lotus leaf, is a staple of Cantonese dim sum and a common breakfast in Hong Kong.

Method

  1. Soak the lotus leaves in warm water for 2 hours, weighting them down, until soft and pliable. Wipe them clean, cut each into quarters and trim off the hard center stem.
  2. Rinse the rice three times and cook it in a rice cooker or covered pot with the cooking water until just tender, then fluff it. Cooking the rice first, rather than steaming it raw in the parcel, gives an evenly soft, sticky result.
  3. Soak the shiitake in warm water for 15 minutes, keeping the liquid. Mince half and slice the rest thickly. Mix the chicken with the cornstarch and water.
  4. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it just smokes, add 1 tablespoon of oil and stir-fry the chicken for 30 seconds, then add the shallots for 30 seconds and the mushrooms and filling water for 1 minute.
  5. Lower the heat and add the filling oyster sauce, soy sauces, sugar and salt. Stir in the slurry cornstarch mixed with 1 teaspoon of water and cook for 2 minutes until thick and glossy, then add the sesame oil. Spread the filling on a plate to cool; a hot filling makes the rice too wet to wrap.
  6. Stir the remaining oil, the rice oyster sauce, soy sauce and mushroom liquid into the rice until every grain is coated. The oil keeps the rice from sticking to the leaf. Let it cool slightly and divide it into 8 portions.
  7. Overlap two leaf quarters shiny side down. Press half a portion of rice into a thin rectangle in the center, add a spoonful of filling, a slice or two of sausage and char siu and half an egg yolk, then cover with the other half portion of rice and press gently.
  8. Fold the bottom of the leaf over the filling, then the sides, then roll it up into a tight, flat rectangle. Repeat to make 8 parcels.
  9. Steam the parcels seam-side down in a single layer, not stacked, over a full boil in a flat-bottom wok or steamer for 20 to 25 minutes, until the rice is soft and fragrant with the leaf. Serve in the leaves and open at the table.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Unsoaked glutinous rice stays hard; it needs at least 6 hours in water.
  • Wrapping loosely lets steam in and makes the rice soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Dim sum houses make smaller versions called jan ju gai, "pearl chicken".
  • Make a batch and freeze the parcels; they reheat perfectly by steaming.

Substitutions

  • No lotus leaves: wrap in parchment and foil, though the tea-like aroma will be missing.
  • Leave out the salted yolks if you cannot find them.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; re-steam for 15 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes wrapped for 3 months; steam from frozen for 30 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and steamed greens
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
