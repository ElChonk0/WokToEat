Indonesian Sweet and Sour Pork

▾

Surprise me with

Reflecting Indonesia's significant Chinese-Indonesian culinary influence, this version of sweet and sour pork adds local touches like extra chili and kecap manis to the familiar Cantonese-derived template.

Method

  1. Toss pork chunks in egg, then dredge in cornstarch to coat.
  2. Heat oil to 175°C (350°F) and fry pork in batches until golden and cooked through, 5–6 minutes; drain.
  3. Whisk kecap manis, vinegar, and ketchup with 60 ml water for the sauce.
  4. Stir-fry bell pepper, onion, and chili in a little oil until just tender-crisp, 2–3 minutes.
  5. Add the sauce and pineapple, bringing to a simmer until slightly thickened.
  6. Toss the fried pork through the sauce just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Tossing the pork in sauce too far ahead of serving, which softens the crispy coating.
  • Skipping the chili, which is the distinguishing element that separates this from the standard Cantonese version.

Authentic tips

  • Frying twice (rest, then re-fry briefly) gives an extra-crisp coating that holds up better once sauced.
  • Fresh pineapple gives a brighter, less syrupy flavor than canned, if available.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works well with the same method for a lighter version.
  • No kecap manis: dark soy sauce with a little brown sugar approximates it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the coating softens on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended — fried texture doesn't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Es jeruk
