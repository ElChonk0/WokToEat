Dòu Fu Yuán Zi (Hui-Style Fried Tofu Balls) 豆腐圆子

▾

Surprise me with

Dou fu yuan zi, golden fried balls of mashed tofu, are a Huizhou holiday dish, often simmered afterward in a savory broth; Anhui is closely associated with the invention of tofu, traditionally credited to Liu An of Huainan.

Method

  1. Wrap the tofu in a clean cloth and squeeze out as much water as possible; mash it until fine.
  2. Mix the tofu with the carrot, shiitake, scallion, ginger, egg, cornstarch, salt and pepper into a firm, sticky mixture.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and shape the mixture into walnut-size balls with wet hands.
  4. Fry in batches for 4-5 minutes, turning, until deep golden and crisp; drain on a rack.
  5. Serve hot with soy sauce for dipping, or simmer the balls in a light broth for 3 minutes.

Common mistakes

  • Wet tofu makes balls that fall apart in the oil; squeeze it very dry.
  • Oil that is too hot browns the outside before the center is cooked.

Authentic tips

  • Fry twice, the second time briefly at 190 C (375 F), for an extra-crisp crust.
  • Traditionally the balls also appear in braised dishes and soups at holiday meals.

Substitutions

  • Use bean sprouts or minced water chestnut in place of carrot.
  • Add minced pork for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheat in a hot oven to recrisp.

Freezing: Freeze cooked balls on a tray, then bag; reheat from frozen at 200 C (400 F).

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable broth
  • Dessert: Orange slices
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
