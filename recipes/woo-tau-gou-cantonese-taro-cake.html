Woo Tau Gou (Cantonese Taro Cake) 芋头糕

Steamed taro cake studded with Chinese sausage, dried shrimp and mushroom is a dim sum and Lunar New Year staple in Guangdong and Hong Kong, sliced and pan-fried to serve.

  • Prep 30 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr

Method

  1. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the sausage, shrimp, mushrooms and shallots for 3 minutes until fragrant. Add the taro and fry 5 minutes, then season with the five-spice, salt, sugar and pepper.
  2. Whisk the rice flour with the water until smooth, pour it into the wok and stir over low heat for 2 to 3 minutes until it thickens to a heavy paste.
  3. Scrape into an oiled 20 cm (8 in) square pan and smooth the top.
  4. Steam over high heat for 50 to 60 minutes until a chopstick comes out clean, topping up the water. Scatter with green onion and cool completely, ideally overnight.
  5. Cut into slices 1 cm (1/2 in) thick and pan-fry in the remaining oil over medium heat for 3 minutes per side until golden and crisp.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the cake warm makes it crumble; let it set fully.
  • Under-steaming leaves a raw, pasty center.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with XO sauce or chili oil, as dim sum restaurants do.
  • The cake keeps well, so make it a day ahead of a gathering.

Substitutions

  • Daikon instead of taro makes lo bak go (turnip cake).
  • Leave out the sausage and shrimp for a vegetarian cake.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated, wrapped; slice and pan-fry as needed.

Freezing: Freezes in slices for 3 months; pan-fry from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and siu mai
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
