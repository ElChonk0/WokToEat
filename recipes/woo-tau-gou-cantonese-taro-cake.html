Woo Tau Gou (Cantonese Taro Cake) 芋头糕

▾

Surprise me with

Steamed taro cake studded with Chinese sausage, dried shrimp and mushroom is a dim sum and Lunar New Year staple in Guangdong and Hong Kong, sliced and pan-fried to serve.

Method

  1. Soak the dried shrimp and mushrooms in hot water for 10 minutes, then mince them. Peel the taro wearing gloves, as the raw sap can itch, and dice it into peanut-size pieces. Stir the rice flour into the batter water until smooth.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok over high heat until it just smokes, add the oil and fry the garlic, ginger and shallots for 20 seconds, the shrimp for 40 seconds, then the sausage and mushrooms for 45 seconds, until fragrant. Keep a spoonful of the sausage for the top.
  3. Add the taro and stir-fry for 1 minute, then season with the oyster sauce, five-spice, salt and sugar and stir 1 minute more so the taro is coated.
  4. Pour in the boiling water, stir and cover. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes until it boils again and the taro edges start to soften. Oil a 20 cm (8 in) cake pan.
  5. Lower the heat, stir the rice flour mixture and pour it in with the pepper and sesame oil, stirring for about 1.5 minutes until it is evenly thick and pasty. Spread it in the pan and smooth the top. Wash out the wok.
  6. Set a rack in the wok with boiling water just below the pan. Steam covered over high heat for 40 to 50 minutes, topping up with boiling water, until a chopstick pushed into the center comes out clean. Scatter the reserved sausage and green onion on top for the last minute.
  7. Cool for a few hours, then refrigerate until firm. Serve in slices, steamed again to warm, or pan-fried in a little oil over medium-high heat for 3 to 5 minutes per side until golden and crisp.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the cake warm makes it crumble; let it set fully.
  • Under-steaming leaves a raw, pasty center.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with XO sauce or chili oil, as dim sum restaurants do.
  • The cake keeps well, so make it a day ahead of a gathering.

Substitutions

  • Daikon instead of taro makes lo bak go (turnip cake).
  • Leave out the sausage and shrimp for a vegetarian cake.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated, wrapped; slice and pan-fry as needed.

Freezing: Freezes in slices for 3 months; pan-fry from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Har gow and siu mai
  • Soup: Not needed at yum cha
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Pu-erh tea
