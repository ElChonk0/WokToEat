Ayam Bakar (Indonesian Grilled Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Ayam bakar simmers chicken in a sweet soy (kecap manis) and spice mixture before finishing on the grill, a technique that ensures the chicken is fully seasoned and tender before it ever touches the fire.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, candlenuts, coriander and turmeric into a paste. Fry it in the oil in a wide pan over medium heat with the lemongrass, bay leaves and galangal for 5 to 6 minutes, until fragrant.
  2. Add the chicken and turn to coat, then add the water, half the kecap manis, the tamarind, palm sugar and salt. Simmer, covered, for 20 minutes, then uncover and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes more, turning, until the sauce is thick and the chicken cooked through. Braising first (ungkep) seasons the meat to the bone.
  3. Mix the remaining kecap manis with a few spoons of the reduced sauce for basting.
  4. Grill the chicken over hot charcoal or under a broiler for 4 to 5 minutes per side, basting often, until sticky and charred in spots.
  5. Serve with rice, sambal and cucumber.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the simmering step and grilling raw chicken directly, which risks burning the exterior before the inside cooks through.
  • Using regular soy sauce instead of kecap manis, which is thicker and considerably sweeter — the flavor won't be the same.

Authentic tips

  • Simmering first, then finishing on the grill, is the technique that gives ayam bakar its deep flavor and reliable doneness.
  • Basting repeatedly while grilling builds up a beautifully lacquered, caramelized exterior.

Substitutions

  • Macadamia nuts replace candlenuts.
  • Bone-in drumsticks work with 10 extra minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in an oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.
