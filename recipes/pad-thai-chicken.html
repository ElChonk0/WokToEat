Pad Thai (Chicken)

▾

Surprise me with

Pad Thai was promoted as a national dish in the 1930s-40s as part of a campaign to build Thai identity around rice-noodle dishes instead of imported rice — it has since become the single most internationally recognized Thai dish.

Method

  1. Soak the noodles in room-temperature water for 1 to 2 hours, until limp but still firm; hot water makes them mushy. Drain. Simmer the tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar for 2 minutes until the sugar dissolves, to make the sauce.
  2. Heat half the oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat. Fry the shallots, garlic, tofu, radish and dried shrimp for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant and the tofu is golden. Add the chicken and stir-fry for 2 to 3 minutes until cooked through.
  3. Add the noodles and about two-thirds of the sauce and toss with tongs for 2 to 3 minutes. Add splashes of water as needed until the noodles are soft and chewy and have absorbed the sauce. Cook pad thai in batches of two servings; a crowded wok steams the noodles into clumps.
  4. Push the noodles aside, add the remaining oil and crack in the eggs. Let them set for 30 seconds, scramble, then fold them through the noodles.
  5. Add the bean sprouts, chives and half the peanuts and toss for 30 seconds, just to warm them; they should stay crunchy. Taste and add the remaining sauce if needed.
  6. Serve with the rest of the peanuts, lime wedges, chili flakes, sugar and extra bean sprouts so each person can season their own.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the noodles, which makes them mushy and prone to breaking apart during stir-frying.
  • Adding the sauce before the wok is properly hot, which steams rather than sears the noodles.

Authentic tips

  • The noodles should still have a slight bite when they go into the wok — they finish softening as they absorb the sauce.
  • Tamarind paste's sourness is essential; bottled pad thai sauces often skip it in favor of ketchup, which tastes quite different from the real thing.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or tofu alone both work well in place of chicken for a pescatarian or vegetarian version.
  • No tamarind paste: a mix of lime juice and a touch of brown sugar approximates the sour-sweet balance.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; noodles soften further on reheating.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture doesn't survive freezing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Lime wedges, peanuts and chili flakes
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
