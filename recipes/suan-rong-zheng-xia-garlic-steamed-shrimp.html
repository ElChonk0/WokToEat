Suàn Róng Zhēng Xiā (Garlic Steamed Shrimp with Glass Noodles) 蒜蓉粉丝蒸虾

▾

Surprise me with

Butterflied shrimp steamed under a blanket of fried garlic on a bed of glass noodles is a Cantonese banquet and home favorite; the same garlic treatment is used for scallops and lobster.

Method

  1. Soak the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes, snip them shorter, drain and pat dry. Toss them with the noodle oyster sauce, soy sauce and salt and spread them over a rimmed heatproof plate.
  2. Put the garlic, ginger and green onion whites in a heatproof bowl. Heat the oil until it just smokes, 30 to 40 seconds, and pour it over so the garlic sizzles and turns fragrant. Stir in the garlic salt, sugar, pepper, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sesame oil, then the cornstarch, which helps the garlic cling to the shrimp.
  3. Trim the shrimp legs, cut along the back through the shell and devein, opening the cut a little to make a pocket. Spoon 1/2 to 1 teaspoon of the garlic into each one; too much and the garlic overwhelms the shrimp.
  4. Arrange the shrimp in a ring on the noodles and dot any leftover garlic between them.
  5. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer and bring the water to a rolling boil. Steam the plate covered over high heat for 6 to 7 minutes, until the shells are red and the flesh is just opaque; any longer and they turn rubbery.
  6. Scatter with the green onion greens and serve on the plate, with the juice-soaked noodles underneath.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Steaming longer than 6 minutes makes the shrimp tough.
  • Browning all the garlic makes it bitter; keep half raw.

Authentic tips

  • The noodles soak up the garlic and shrimp juices; serve them with the shrimp.
  • Use the same topping for scallops on the half shell.

Substitutions

  • Peeled shrimp work; steam 4 minutes.
  • Lobster tails, split, steam 8 to 10 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon seafood soup
  • Dessert: Sweet tofu pudding
  • Beverage: Chilled white wine or jasmine tea
