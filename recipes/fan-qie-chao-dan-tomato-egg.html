Fan Qie Chao Dan (Cantonese Tomato and Scrambled Eggs) 番茄炒蛋

Tomato and egg is the most common home-cooked dish across China; the Cantonese version is typically less sweet and cooked with a touch of ginger.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 66Eggs
  • 400 g14 ozTomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 30 g2 tbspScallions
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Beat the eggs with the wine and a pinch of salt until no streaks of white remain.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over medium-high heat and pour in the eggs; let them set for 10 seconds, then push into large soft curds, about 40 seconds. Slide onto a plate while still slightly wet.
  3. Add the remaining oil and fry the tomato wedges for 3 minutes, pressing so they release their juice into a loose sauce.
  4. Stir in the sugar and the rest of the salt, then return the eggs and fold gently for 20 seconds.
  5. Scatter over the scallion and serve over rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the eggs.
  • Using underripe tomatoes.

Authentic tips

  • Ripe tomatoes give the best sauce.
  • Add a splash of water for a saucier dish to pour over rice.

Substitutions

  • Cherry tomatoes work year round when large tomatoes are bland.
  • Add a spoonful of ketchup if the tomatoes lack sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; best over fresh hot rice.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
