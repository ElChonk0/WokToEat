Fān Qié Chǎo Dàn (Cantonese Tomato and Scrambled Eggs) 番茄炒蛋

▾

Surprise me with

Tomato and egg is the most common home-cooked dish across China; the Cantonese version is typically less sweet and cooked with a touch of ginger.

Method

  1. For silkier sauce, score a cross in the base of each tomato, drop them in boiling water for 1 to 2 minutes, then slip off the skins. Cut each tomato into 8 wedges. Beat the eggs with their salt vigorously until frothy, and mix the slurry.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok or skillet over high heat until hot but not smoking. Add 2 tablespoons of oil, pour in the eggs and scramble in large, loose folds for about 35 seconds, until about 80% set and still glossy. Lift them out; they finish cooking in the sauce.
  3. Add the remaining oil and the ginger, then the tomatoes, and stir-fry over high heat for 30 seconds. Add the water, salt and sugar and boil for 2 to 3 minutes, covering if needed, until the tomatoes soften and release their juice. High heat keeps the sauce bright rather than watery.
  4. Re-stir the slurry, as it settles, and add it gradually until the sauce is glossy and thick enough to coat a spoon.
  5. Return the eggs and fold for about 50 seconds until heated through. Turn off the heat, stir in the ketchup and scallions and serve over rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the eggs.
  • Using underripe tomatoes.

Authentic tips

  • Ripe tomatoes give the best sauce.
  • Add a splash of water for a saucier dish to pour over rice.

Substitutions

  • Cherry tomatoes work year round when large tomatoes are bland.
  • Add a spoonful of ketchup if the tomatoes lack sweetness.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; best over fresh hot rice.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
