Léi Chá Fàn (Hakka Thunder Tea Rice) 擂茶饭

▾

Surprise me with

Lei cha, a green paste of tea leaves, herbs, peanuts and sesame pounded in a mortar, is a Hakka tradition of western Fujian and Guangdong, drunk as a restorative or poured over rice with vegetables.

Method

  1. Pound the tea leaves, herbs, peanuts and sesame seeds in a large mortar (or blend them) into a coarse green paste. Lei cha ('pounded tea') is a Hakka tradition.
  2. Stir in the hot water and salt to make a thick, fragrant green soup.
  3. Stir-fry each vegetable separately in a little oil with garlic and a pinch of salt for 1 to 2 minutes, keeping them crisp. Dry-fry the pickled radish.
  4. Arrange the rice in bowls with small piles of tofu, green beans, cabbage and radish on top.
  5. Pour the tea soup over the rice at the table and mix everything together.

Common mistakes

  • Over-processing makes the paste bitter; pound to a coarse texture.
  • Using cold water gives a gritty drink.

Authentic tips

  • A mortar and pestle (a ridged lei bowl) is the traditional tool.
  • The soup is also drunk on its own as a restorative.

Substitutions

  • Matcha plus herbs replaces the traditional tea paste.
  • Toasted sesame seeds can replace some peanuts.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat the tea soup separately.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the rice bowl is the meal
  • Soup: Hot tea soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
