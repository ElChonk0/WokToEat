Cong Kao Xue Yu (Scallion-Braised Cod) 葱烤鳕鱼

Cong kao, slowly braising fish in a sweet soy sauce with large amounts of scallion, is a Shanghai cold dish and a banquet appetizer.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozIcelandic cod fillet, in 5 cm pieces
  • 200 g7 ozScallions, cut in 6 cm lengths
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 25 g2 tbspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice vinegar
  • 150 ml2/3 cupWater

Method

  1. Pat the cod dry. Heat half the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat and fry the cod for 2 minutes per side until golden; lift out gently.
  2. Add the remaining oil, the scallions and ginger and cook over medium heat for 4 minutes until the scallions are soft and golden.
  3. Add the wine, both soy sauces, sugar, vinegar and water and bring to a boil.
  4. Return the fish on top of the scallions, cover, and simmer 5 minutes over low heat.
  5. Uncover, spoon the sauce over the fish, and reduce for 3 minutes until syrupy. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Cook’s notes

Written for Icelandic cod fillet. The Shanghai original is cong kao fish, braised with plenty of scallions, usually using crucian carp.

Common mistakes

  • A heavy hand on the stirring breaks the cod; baste instead.
  • Too little sugar makes the sauce sharp and flat.

Authentic tips

  • The scallions should be soft and sweet, almost caramelized.
  • It tastes even better at room temperature the next day.

Substitutions

  • Haddock or black cod replaces the cod.
  • Leeks replace some of the scallions.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator and is good cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and cold cucumber
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
