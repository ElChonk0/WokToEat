Chā Shāo Bāo (Cantonese Barbecue Pork Buns) 叉烧包

▾

Surprise me with

Cha shao bao are Cantonese dim sum buns of soft steamed dough around a sweet, saucy filling of char siu barbecue pork, a staple of Hong Kong tea houses.

  • 12 g1 tbspSugar, for the filling
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 25 ml1.5 tbspOyster sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 120 ml1/2 cupChicken stock
  • 15 g2 tbspAll-purpose flour, for the filling
  • 220 g1.5 cupsChar siu (barbecue pork), diced

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water in a large bowl or mixer. Add the flour and cornstarch, sugar and dough oil and mix on low speed or by hand into a smooth ball. Cover with a damp cloth and leave for 2 hours. The cornstarch is what gives the buns their snowy white, pull-apart crumb.
  2. Make the filling: heat the oil in a pan over medium-high heat and cook the shallots for 1 minute. Lower the heat to medium-low and add the sugar, soy sauces, oyster sauce and sesame oil, stirring until it bubbles.
  3. Add the stock and filling flour and cook for 2 minutes, stirring, until very thick. Off the heat, fold in the char siu. Cool completely, chilling it if you can; warm filling softens the dough and the buns will not seal.
  4. Knead the baking powder into the rested dough until fully mixed, adding 1 to 2 teaspoons of water if it feels dry, then knead until smooth and rest for 15 minutes. Cut ten 10 cm (4 in) squares of parchment and set up a steamer with water.
  5. Divide the dough into 10 pieces and roll each into a ball. Press each into a disc about 11 cm (4.5 in) across, thicker in the center and thin at the edge, so the base holds the filling and the top does not end up doughy.
  6. Put a heaping tablespoon of filling in the center and pleat the edge up and around it, pinching and twisting the top closed. Set each bun on a parchment square, seam-side up.
  7. Bring the steamer water to a full boil before the buns go in; the sudden burst of steam is what makes the tops split open. Steam in two batches over high heat for 12 minutes, with the water well below the buns so it cannot splash them.
  8. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 1 minute before opening, so the buns do not collapse. They are done when snowy white and cracked open on top. Serve warm.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • A warm filling makes the dough tear and the filling leak; chill it fully.
  • Opening the lid during steaming collapses the buns.

Authentic tips

  • The filling can be made a day ahead.
  • Use high-gluten flour for a smoother skin if you have it.

Substitutions

  • Roast pork or leftover pork shoulder replaces char siu.
  • Hoisin replaces some of the oyster sauce for a vegetarian-style filling with mushrooms.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; resteam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; steam from frozen for 18 minutes.
