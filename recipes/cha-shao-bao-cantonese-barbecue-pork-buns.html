Cha Shao Bao (Cantonese Barbecue Pork Buns) 叉烧包

Cha shao bao are Cantonese dim sum buns of soft steamed dough around a sweet, saucy filling of char siu barbecue pork, a staple of Hong Kong tea houses.

  • Prep 1 hr 30 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Hard

Method

  1. Mix the yeast and 10 g of the sugar into the warm water, rest for 5 minutes, then knead into the flour with the oil until smooth. Cover and proof for 1 hour until doubled.
  2. Fry the shallot in the filling oil for 1 minute, add the oyster sauce, soy sauce, hoisin and sugar and bubble for 1 minute.
  3. Stir the cornstarch into the water, pour into the pan and cook for 1 minute until a glossy thick sauce forms. Fold in the char siu and sesame oil and chill completely.
  4. Knead the baking powder and remaining sugar into the dough, divide into 8 pieces and roll each into a round with thinner edges.
  5. Put a spoonful of the cold filling in each round, pleat and pinch the top closed, and set each bun on a square of parchment.
  6. Rest the buns for 20 minutes, then steam over high heat for 12 minutes without lifting the lid, until the tops split slightly.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • A warm filling makes the dough tear and the filling leak; chill it fully.
  • Opening the lid during steaming collapses the buns.

Authentic tips

  • The filling can be made a day ahead.
  • Use high-gluten flour for a smoother skin if you have it.

Substitutions

  • Roast pork or leftover pork shoulder replaces char siu.
  • Hoisin replaces some of the oyster sauce for a vegetarian-style filling with mushrooms.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; resteam for 8 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; steam from frozen for 18 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili oil
  • Soup: Hot Chinese tea
  • Dessert: Egg tarts
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
