Fuzhou Bian Rou Wonton Soup 扁肉

▾

Surprise me with

Fuzhou’s bian rou are tiny wontons whose filling is pounded until the pork turns springy; a bowl of them with a plate of peanut-sauce noodles (ban mian) is the city’s standard breakfast.

Method

  1. Pound or process the pork with baking soda and salt, adding the iced water gradually, until it is a smooth, sticky paste.
  2. Season with fish sauce. Place 1/2 tsp of filling on each wrapper and pinch the wrapper closed around it in one squeeze.
  3. Bring the stock to a simmer and season it with salt and white pepper.
  4. Boil the wontons in plain water 2 minutes until they float; lift into bowls.
  5. Ladle the hot stock over and top with fried shallots, green onion and a drop of sesame oil.

Common mistakes

  • Filling the wrappers generously; bian rou are meant to be mostly silky wrapper with a small bouncy centre.

Authentic tips

  • The pounding and iced water are what give the filling its bounce; a food processor works if you pulse in short bursts.

Substitutions

  • Pair with noodles tossed in peanut butter, soy and black vinegar for the full Fuzhou breakfast.

Storage

Leftovers: Keep cooked wontons and broth separate; best eaten fresh.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked wontons on a tray, then bag; cook from frozen in 3 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled radish
  • Soup: Not needed; the wonton soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
