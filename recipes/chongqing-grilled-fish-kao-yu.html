Chongqing Grilled Fish 重庆烤鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Chongqing grilled fish emerged in Wanzhou in the 1990s: a whole river fish is roasted, then simmered in a spicy mala sauce with vegetables in a shared pan over a flame.

Method

  1. Butterfly the fish through the back so it opens flat, and score the thick flesh. Rub with salt, white pepper and Shaoxing wine and scatter with ginger and green onion. Leave for 20 minutes, then pat dry.
  2. Heat the oven to 220 C (425 F) with a rack under the broiler. Brush the fish with oil, sprinkle with cumin and chili and roast on an oiled tray for 15 to 20 minutes, then broil for 3 to 5 minutes until the skin is crisp and blistered. Charcoal grilling is traditional.
  3. Meanwhile, blanch the lotus root and potato for 2 minutes and drain.
  4. Heat the remaining oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium heat and fry the doubanjiang for 2 minutes until the oil is red. Add the garlic, ginger, dried chilies and peppercorns for 1 minute, then the celery and onion.
  5. Add the stock, soy sauce and sugar and the blanched vegetables and bean sprouts, and simmer for 5 minutes.
  6. Lay the grilled fish in a deep tray set over a burner, pour the vegetables and spicy broth over and around it, and simmer at the table for 5 minutes. Scatter with cilantro and sesame seeds.

Common mistakes

  • Under-roasting the fish, which leaves the skin flabby in the sauce.
  • Adding too much stock, which dilutes the concentrated flavor.

Authentic tips

  • Sturdy vegetables go in first; add leafy ones last.

Substitutions

  • Tilapia or catfish replace carp.
  • Reduce the chilies for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; the skin softens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
