Níng Méng Jī (Hong Kong Lemon Chicken) 柠檬鸡

Crisp fried chicken with a bright lemon sauce is a Hong Kong restaurant classic that travelled with Cantonese cooks to Britain, Canada and the United States.

  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Flatten the thighs slightly, season with the salt and wine, then coat with the beaten egg and press into the cornstarch until thickly covered. Rest 10 minutes so the coating hydrates.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 170 C (340 F) and fry the thighs two at a time for 6 to 7 minutes, until golden and cooked through. Drain on a rack, then refry all of them at 190 C (375 F) for 1 minute until very crisp.
  3. In a small pan bring the lemon juice, zest, sugar, water and salt to a simmer. Stir in the cornstarch mixed with a spoon of water and cook for 1 minute until glossy and pourable. Stir in the butter and lemon slices.
  4. Cut the chicken into strips and arrange on a plate.
  5. Pour the sauce over just before serving, or serve it alongside, and sprinkle with sesame seeds.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the lemon juice for long turns it bitter; simmer briefly.
  • Pouring the sauce on too early softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Some Hong Kong kitchens use a little custard powder in the sauce for color and body.
  • Fry the chicken flat, then slice, for neat restaurant-style strips.

Substitutions

  • Air fryer: spray the coated thighs with oil and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 15 to 18 minutes, turning once.
  • Chicken breast works; fry 1 minute less.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated with the sauce separate; re-crisp the chicken in a hot oven.

Freezing: The fried chicken freezes for 2 months; make the sauce fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Iced lemon tea
