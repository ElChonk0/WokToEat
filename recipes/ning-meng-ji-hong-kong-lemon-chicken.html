Níng Méng Jī (Hong Kong Lemon Chicken) 柠檬鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Crisp fried chicken with a bright lemon sauce is a Hong Kong restaurant classic that travelled with Cantonese cooks to Britain, Canada and the United States.

Method

  1. Slice any thick part of the thighs horizontally so the pieces are an even 1.5 cm (1/2 in) thick, then cut them into broad strips. Even pieces cook through before the crust overbrowns.
  2. Mix the marinade oyster sauce, salt, sugar, pepper, wine, cornstarch and water, toss with the chicken and stir in half the beaten egg. Leave while you make the sauce base and batter.
  3. Stir the lemon juice, zest, sauce sugar, sauce water and salt in a small pan until the sugar dissolves. Taste it now: it should be sharp but balanced, as the sugar softens the sourness and the salt brightens it. Mix the sauce cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water.
  4. Whisk the flour, batter cornstarch, baking powder and salt, then add the cold water a tablespoon at a time until the batter falls from the whisk in thin ribbons that melt back in, like pancake batter. Stir in the rest of the egg and the batter oil. If it runs like water, whisk in a little more cornstarch; if it drops in clumps, add water. The baking powder makes the crust light and puffy.
  5. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 165 C (325 F). Coat the chicken in the batter just before frying and lower the pieces in one at a time so they do not stick together. Fry in batches without crowding for about 3 minutes, turning once, until the crust is firm and pale yellow. Raise the heat slightly after each batch goes in, as the oil temperature drops. Drain on a rack.
  6. Raise the oil to 200 C (390 F). Fry the pieces again in two batches for 20 to 40 seconds, until deep golden and audibly crisp, and the chicken reads 74 C (165 F) inside. This hot second fry drives moisture out of the crust, which is what keeps it crunchy under the sauce. Drain on the rack.
  7. Bring the sauce base to a boil, lower the heat and stir in the slurry gradually. Raise the heat and stir for about 1 minute until it turns clear and glossy; boil it a little longer for a stickier sauce. Turn off the heat and stir in the butter.
  8. Arrange the lemon around a plate and the chicken in the middle, pour the sauce over or toss the chicken through it quickly, and sprinkle with sesame seeds. Serve at once: the crust starts absorbing the sauce straight away.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the lemon juice for long turns it bitter; simmer briefly.
  • Pouring the sauce on too early softens the crust.

Authentic tips

  • Some Hong Kong kitchens use a little custard powder in the sauce for color and body.
  • Fry the chicken flat, then slice, for neat restaurant-style strips.

Substitutions

  • Air fryer: spray the coated thighs with oil and cook at 200 C (400 F) for 15 to 18 minutes, turning once.
  • Chicken breast works; fry 1 minute less.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated with the sauce separate; re-crisp the chicken in a hot oven.

Freezing: The fried chicken freezes for 2 months; make the sauce fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Iced lemon tea
