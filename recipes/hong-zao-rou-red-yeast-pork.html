Hóng Zāo Ròu (Red Yeast Rice Pork) 红糟肉

▾

Surprise me with

Hong zao rou, pork marinated in red yeast rice lees and fried, is a signature Fuzhou dish whose color and wine scent reflect the region's dependence on red yeast rice.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 1 g1/2 tspFive-spice powder
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt
  • 45 g1/3 cupSweet potato starch
  • 750 ml3 cupsNeutral oil, for frying

Method

  1. Red wine lees (hong zao) is the sediment left from brewing Fujian red rice wine with red yeast rice. It gives Fuzhou dishes their deep red color and a sweet, winey, slightly fermented aroma. Mix the lees with the sugar, wine, garlic, five-spice and salt and massage it into the pork. Marinate for at least 4 hours, or overnight.
  2. Toss the pork in the sweet potato starch, pressing it on so it sticks to the marinade.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry the pork in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until cooked through and dark red-brown. The sugar in the lees burns easily, so keep the heat moderate.
  4. Drain and fry again at 190 C (375 F) for 30 seconds to crisp.
  5. Slice and serve hot as a snack or with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving excess lees on the meat burns it in the oil.
  • Frying at too low a heat makes the coating oily.

Authentic tips

  • Red yeast rice lees give a winey fragrance and a deep red color.
  • Marinating overnight gives the deepest flavor.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus a pinch of paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • Chicken thigh takes the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
