Hóng Zāo Ròu (Red Yeast Rice Pork) 红糟肉

▾

Surprise me with

Hong zao rou, pork marinated in red yeast rice lees and fried, is a signature Fuzhou dish whose color and wine scent reflect the region's dependence on red yeast rice.

  • 400 g14 ozPork belly or shoulder, sliced
  • 90 g6 tbspRed yeast rice lees
  • 20 g4 tspSugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 20 g1 tbspGarlic
  • 45 g1/3 cupSweet potato starch
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsNeutral oil, for frying

Method

  1. Cut the pork into 5 mm slices and mix with the lees, wine, sugar and garlic; refrigerate 2 hours or overnight.
  2. Wipe off excess lees (they burn) and toss the slices in the sweet potato starch, pressing it on.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry in two batches for 3-4 minutes until golden and crisp; drain.
  4. Refry for 30 seconds at 190 C (375 F) for an extra-crisp edge.
  5. Serve hot with a dipping sauce of garlic and vinegar.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving excess lees on the meat burns it in the oil.
  • Frying at too low a heat makes the coating oily.

Authentic tips

  • Red yeast rice lees give a winey fragrance and a deep red color.
  • Marinating overnight gives the deepest flavor.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus a pinch of paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • Chicken thigh takes the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
