Shī Mù Yú Dù Tāng (Milkfish Belly Soup) 虱目魚肚湯

▾

Surprise me with

Milkfish belly, the fattiest and most prized part, is simmered with ginger in a clear broth and is another Tainan breakfast staple.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, very finely julienned
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice wine
  • 5 g1 tspSalt

  • 20 g3/4 ozCelery, finely chopped
  • 10 g1 tbspFried shallots

  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce paste with ginger, for dipping

Method

  1. Cut the milkfish belly into 4 pieces. Milkfish is famously bony; buy deboned belly, or run your fingers over it and pull out any pin bones.
  2. Bring the water to a boil with half the ginger and the rice wine.
  3. Slide in the fish, lower to a gentle simmer and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, until the flesh turns white and the fat rendered from the belly floats in golden beads. Do not boil hard, or the rich belly breaks apart.
  4. Season with salt and white pepper.
  5. Ladle into bowls and top with the remaining ginger, celery, fried shallots and cilantro. Dip the fish in ginger-soy paste. A Tainan breakfast, often with rice or a fried dough stick.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard breaks the delicate belly.
  • Overcooking makes it dry.

Authentic tips

  • The belly is fatty and sweet.
  • Serve with congee or rice.

Substitutions

  • Fish belly from tilapia or sea bass replaces milkfish belly.
  • Add ginger and a splash of rice wine.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
