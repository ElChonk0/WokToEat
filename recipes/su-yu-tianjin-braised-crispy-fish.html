Sū Yú (Tianjin-Style Braised Crucian Carp) 酥鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Su yu is a slow-braised fish from Tianjin, near Beijing, cooked in a sweet-sour soy sauce until even the small bones turn soft, traditionally served cold on the Lunar New Year table.

  • Cook 4 hr

Method

  1. Rub the fish with salt and pat dry. Fry them in batches in oil at 190 C (375 F) for 4 to 5 minutes until deep golden and firm.
  2. Line a heavy Dutch oven with the cabbage leaves and half the ginger and green onion, which stops the fish sticking and scorching. Pack the fish in tightly in layers and top with the remaining aromatics.
  3. Mix the vinegar, sugar, soy sauces, Shaoxing wine, spices and water and pour it over to just cover the fish.
  4. Bring to a boil, then cover and cook over the lowest heat for 3 to 4 hours, without stirring. The vinegar and long cooking soften the bones until they can be eaten; su means 'crisp-tender'.
  5. Cool completely in the pot, then lift out carefully, drizzle with sesame oil and serve cold as an appetizer.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on too high a heat prevents the bones from softening.
  • Stirring breaks the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Su yu keeps for several days refrigerated.
  • A pressure cooker takes about 40 minutes.

Substitutions

  • A pressure cooker softens the bones in 40 minutes.
  • Small mackerel replaces crucian carp.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; served cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
