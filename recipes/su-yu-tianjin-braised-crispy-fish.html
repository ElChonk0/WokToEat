Sū Yú (Tianjin-Style Braised Crucian Carp) 酥鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Su yu is a slow-braised fish from Tianjin, near Beijing, cooked in a sweet-sour soy sauce until even the small bones turn soft, traditionally served cold on the Lunar New Year table.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 2 hr
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 1 kg2.2 lbSmall crucian carp or silver carp, cleaned
  • 90 ml6 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 60 ml4 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 60 g4 tbspSugar
  • 60 g4 tbspGinger and scallion
  • 5 g2 tspStar anise and cinnamon
  • 600 ml2.5 cupsWater

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry and fry in a little oil for 2 minutes per side until set and lightly golden.
  2. Layer the fish in a heavy pot with the ginger, scallion, star anise and cinnamon.
  3. Pour over the soy sauces, vinegar and sugar, then add 600 ml water to nearly cover.
  4. Bring to a boil, then cover and cook on the lowest heat for 2 hours without stirring, until the bones are soft.
  5. Cool in the liquid overnight; serve cold.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on too high a heat prevents the bones from softening.
  • Stirring breaks the fish.

Authentic tips

  • Su yu keeps for several days refrigerated.
  • A pressure cooker takes about 40 minutes.

Substitutions

  • A pressure cooker softens the bones in 40 minutes.
  • Small mackerel replaces crucian carp.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; served cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
