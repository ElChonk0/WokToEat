Siu Yuk (Crispy Roast Pork Belly) 脆皮烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

Siu yuk is prized above all for its crackling skin, achieved through a careful process of drying, scoring, and salt-crusting before high-heat roasting — a centerpiece of Cantonese roast meat shops.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 4 g1 tspGarlic salt
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, for the meat
  • 15 ml1 tbspGround bean sauce (mein see)
  • 4 g1 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 g1 tbspCoarse salt, for the skin
  • 10 ml2 tspWhite rice vinegar
  • 30 ml2 tbspMustard or white sugar, for serving

Method

  1. Bring a wide pan of water to a full boil. Lower the belly in skin-side down for 1 minute, until the skin tightens and changes color, then flip it meat-side down for 30 to 45 seconds. Plunge it into cold water. Blanching firms the skin so it can be scraped and pierced.
  2. Pat dry and scrape the skin with a knife held upright to remove any hairs and the thin waxy film. Prick the skin all over, very densely, with a skewer, meat tenderizer or fork, going through the skin but not deep into the fat. Those holes are what let the skin puff into crackling.
  3. Turn the belly over and score the meat side in a grid about 1 cm (1/2 in) deep, stopping before the skin. Mix the five-spice, both peppers, garlic salt and meat salt and rub it into the cuts. Stir the bean sauce, sugar and wine into what is left and spread it over the meat only, keeping it off the skin.
  4. Set the belly skin-side up on a sheet of foil and fold the foil up tightly around the sides, so only the skin is exposed. Rub the coarse salt into the skin, leave 5 minutes, then wipe it off and brush the skin with the vinegar.
  5. Refrigerate uncovered for at least 10 to 12 hours, or up to a day, until the skin is completely dry and hard to the touch.
  6. Heat the oven to 180 C (350 F). Put a rack in a roasting tray with a cup of water underneath and set the pork on it, skin-side up and as level as possible. Roast for 25 minutes to cook the meat.
  7. Raise the oven to 220 C (425 F) and roast for 30 to 35 minutes more, until the skin has puffed and blistered into a golden, crackling crust; turn the tray once if one side colors faster. If some spots stay flat, move the pork closer to the top of the oven for the last few minutes and watch closely.
  8. Rest for at least 30 minutes, ideally 1 hour, so the juices settle and the crackling sets. Turn it skin-side down and cut along the score lines with a heavy knife into bars, then into bite-size pieces. Serve with mustard or sugar for dipping.

Common mistakes

  • Not drying the skin thoroughly enough, which is the single biggest cause of failed crackling.
  • Cutting through the fat layer when scoring, which lets seasoning bleed into the fat and prevents proper crisping.

Authentic tips

  • The long, uncovered fridge-drying step is non-negotiable for good crackling — moisture is crackling's enemy.
  • The salt crust protects the skin from burning while it slowly dries out further during the first roast.

Substitutions

  • No time for the full process: a store-bought version from a Chinese BBQ shop is a completely reasonable substitute.
  • A hair dryer on the skin for a few minutes before roasting can help remove residual surface moisture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot oven to partially restore crackling.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably; the skin loses crispness but flavor holds.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and mustard
  • Soup: Lotus root and pork soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
