Siu Yuk (Crispy Roast Pork Belly) 脆皮烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

Siu yuk is prized above all for its crackling skin, achieved through a careful process of drying, scoring, and salt-crusting before high-heat roasting — a centerpiece of Cantonese roast meat shops.

Method

  1. Score the skin in a tight grid pattern, being careful not to cut into the meat.
  2. Rub the meat side (not skin) with salt, five-spice, white pepper, and wine; refrigerate uncovered, skin-side up, 8 hours or overnight to dry.
  3. Pat the skin very dry, then brush with vinegar and cover with a thick layer of coarse salt to form a crust.
  4. Roast at 220°C (425°F) for 45 minutes, then remove the salt crust.
  5. Increase oven to broil and blister the skin 8–10 minutes, watching very closely, until fully puffed and crackling.
  6. Rest 10 minutes before chopping into pieces.

Common mistakes

  • Not drying the skin thoroughly enough, which is the single biggest cause of failed crackling.
  • Cutting through the fat layer when scoring, which lets seasoning bleed into the fat and prevents proper crisping.

Authentic tips

  • The long, uncovered fridge-drying step is non-negotiable for good crackling — moisture is crackling's enemy.
  • The salt crust protects the skin from burning while it slowly dries out further during the first roast.

Substitutions

  • No time for the full process: a store-bought version from a Chinese BBQ shop is a completely reasonable substitute.
  • A hair dryer on the skin for a few minutes before roasting can help remove residual surface moisture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot oven to partially restore crackling.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably; the skin loses crispness but flavor holds.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and mustard
  • Soup: Lotus root and pork soup
  • Dessert: Fresh pineapple
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
