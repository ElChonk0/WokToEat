Xīng Huà Chǎo Mǐ Fěn (Xinghua Fried Rice Noodles) 兴化炒米粉

▾

Surprise me with

Xinghua rice noodles come from Putian, a coastal city in Fujian where thin dried rice vermicelli is stir-fried with vegetables and mushrooms into a signature local breakfast and festival dish.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 250 g9 ozDried Xinghua (Putian) rice vermicelli
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 33Shallots, sliced
  • 60 g2 ozShiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • 150 g5 ozCabbage, shredded
  • 100 g3.5 ozCarrot, julienned
  • 22Eggs, beaten
  • 300 ml1.25 cupsVegetable stock
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion

Method

  1. Soak the vermicelli in warm water for 5 minutes until just pliable, then drain.
  2. Scramble the eggs in 1 tablespoon of oil and lift them out.
  3. Fry the shallots in the remaining oil until golden, then the shiitake, cabbage and carrot for 2 minutes.
  4. Add the stock and soy sauce and bring to a boil, then add the vermicelli and toss with chopsticks for 3 to 4 minutes until it absorbs the liquid. Lifting rather than stirring keeps the fine strands long.
  5. Return the eggs with the green onion and sesame oil and toss to combine.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the noodles makes them mushy and clumped.
  • Crowding the wok steams rather than fries.

Authentic tips

  • Toss the noodles with chopsticks to keep them separate.
  • A splash of stock keeps them moist.

Substitutions

  • Tofu cubes add protein.
  • Thin dried rice noodles replace Xinghua vermicelli.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
