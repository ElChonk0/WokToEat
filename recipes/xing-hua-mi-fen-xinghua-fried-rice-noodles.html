Xīng Huà Chǎo Mǐ Fěn (Xinghua Fried Rice Noodles) 兴化炒米粉

Xinghua rice noodles come from Putian, a coastal city in Fujian where thin dried rice vermicelli is stir-fried with vegetables and mushrooms into a signature local breakfast and festival dish.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 250 g9 ozDried rice vermicelli
  • 60 g2 ozShiitake mushrooms
  • 250 g9 ozCabbage and carrots
  • 22Eggs
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 30 g2 tbspScallion
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Soak the vermicelli in hot water for 5 minutes until just pliable; drain well. Slice the mushrooms and shred the cabbage and carrots.
  2. Heat half the oil in a wok over high heat and fry the mushrooms for 2 minutes, then the cabbage and carrot for 2 minutes; remove.
  3. Add the remaining oil and scramble the eggs, then add the noodles and soy sauce and toss 2 minutes, adding a splash of water if dry.
  4. Return the vegetables and toss 1 minute.
  5. Finish with the sesame oil and scallion.

Common mistakes

  • Over-soaking the noodles makes them mushy and clumped.
  • Crowding the wok steams rather than fries.

Authentic tips

  • Toss the noodles with chopsticks to keep them separate.
  • A splash of stock keeps them moist.

Substitutions

  • Tofu cubes add protein.
  • Thin dried rice noodles replace Xinghua vermicelli.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the noodles are the meal
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
