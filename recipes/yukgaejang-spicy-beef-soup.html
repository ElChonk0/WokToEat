Yukgaejang (Spicy Beef Soup) 육개장

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty, spicy shredded beef soup loaded with vegetables and gochugaru, yukgaejang was traditionally made for royal households and is now a beloved comfort food, especially popular for its warming, restorative qualities.

Method

  1. Simmer the gosari in plenty of water, covered, for about 30 minutes until tender, then rinse, drain and cut it into 10 cm (4 in) lengths. Soak the shiitake.
  2. Bring the beef, water, onion and radish to a boil, skimming off the foam, then cover and simmer over medium heat for about 1 hour, until the beef pulls apart easily. Lift out the beef and shred it along the grain; strain the broth and keep about 1.8 L (7.5 cups). Skim off the fat.
  3. Blanch the bean sprouts for 1 minute and drain. Cut the green onion into long pieces.
  4. Warm the sesame oil in a large pot over low heat, stir in the gochugaru and take it off the heat as soon as the oil turns red and the flakes go pasty, about 20 seconds. Toss the beef, gosari, shiitake, garlic and 1 tablespoon of the soup soy sauce in this chili oil. Coating the meat in chili oil is what gives yukgaejang its deep red color and rounded heat.
  5. Add the broth, the rest of the soup soy sauce and the gochujang and boil, covered, over medium-high heat for 10 minutes.
  6. Add the bean sprouts and green onion and boil 10 minutes more, until the green onion is soft and sweet. Season with salt and pepper, drizzle in the eggs if using, and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the step of seasoning the shredded beef separately before returning it to the broth, which results in a less flavorful final soup.
  • Rushing the initial beef simmer, which doesn't give it enough time to become tender enough to shred easily.

Authentic tips

  • Seasoning the shredded beef directly with the gochugaru mixture before returning it to the broth builds much deeper flavor than seasoning the broth alone.
  • This soup freezes and reheats exceptionally well, making it a great batch-cooking candidate.

Substitutions

  • Dried bracken can be skipped; add extra bean sprouts.
  • Shredded chicken replaces beef.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the soup improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
