Yán Jiān Ròu (Sichuan Salt-Fried Pork) 盐煎肉

▾

Surprise me with

Yan jian rou, thin pork belly slices stir-fried with garlic sprouts and doubanjiang, is a Sichuan home dish related to twice-cooked pork but made from raw meat.

  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

4servings (recipe serves 4)

  • 400 g14 ozPork belly, thinly sliced
  • 200 g7 ozGarlic sprouts or leeks
  • 20 g1.5 tbspDoubanjiang
  • 10 g2 tspFermented black beans
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 2 g1 tspSichuan pepper
  • 5 g1 tspSugar

Method

  1. Slice the pork belly 3 mm thick and cut the garlic sprouts into 4 cm lengths on the diagonal, keeping the whites and greens separate.
  2. Heat a dry wok over medium-high heat and fry the pork for 4-5 minutes, stirring, until the fat renders and the edges curl and brown.
  3. Push the pork aside, add the doubanjiang, black beans and Sichuan pepper to the rendered fat and fry 30 seconds until the oil turns red.
  4. Add the whites of the sprouts and toss for 1 minute, then the greens, soy sauce and sugar for another minute.
  5. Serve immediately with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Crowding the wok steams the pork instead of browning it; use a large pan or cook in two batches.
  • Burning the doubanjiang makes the dish bitter, so add it after the fat has rendered and keep stirring.

Authentic tips

  • Freezing the belly for 30 minutes makes it easier to slice thinly.
  • Leeks or scallions can stand in for garlic sprouts.

Substitutions

  • Thick-cut bacon is a workable shortcut.
  • Leeks replace the garlic sprouts when those are out of season.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot pan.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Jasmine tea
