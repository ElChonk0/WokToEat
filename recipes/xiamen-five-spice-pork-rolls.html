Five-Spice Pork Rolls 五香卷

Called ngo hiang in Hokkien, these bean-curd-skin rolls of five-spice pork and water chestnut are a Xiamen and Quanzhou festival food that travelled with Hokkien emigrants to Singapore and Malaysia.

  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Mix pork, shrimp, water chestnut, shallot, five-spice, soy, sugar, egg and cornstarch until sticky.
  2. Wipe the bean curd skins with a damp cloth to soften, cut into 20 × 25 cm pieces, and spread a log of filling along one edge.
  3. Roll up tightly, fold in the ends, and seal with a dab of cornstarch paste; make 8 rolls.
  4. Steam the rolls 12 minutes to set the filling, then cool 5 minutes.
  5. Shallow-fry in batches, turning, until blistered and golden, 5–6 minutes.
  6. Slice on the diagonal and serve with sweet chili sauce or Xiamen sweet sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Frying raw rolls straight away, which burns the skin before the filling is cooked; steam first.

Authentic tips

  • Steamed, unfried rolls keep in the freezer; fry from thawed when you need a quick dinner.

Substitutions

  • No bean curd skin: spring roll wrappers, though they turn crisper and less chewy.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days; re-crisp in a hot pan or oven.

Freezing: Freeze steamed, unfried rolls for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sweet chili sauce and rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
