Pancit Bihon

▾

Surprise me with

Pancit, from the Hokkien word for noodles, reflects centuries of Chinese influence on Filipino cuisine and is traditionally served at birthdays and celebrations as a symbol of long life.

Method

  1. Poach the chicken in the water for 15 minutes, then shred it. Keep 400 ml (1.75 cups) of the broth.
  2. Soak the bihon in cold water for 10 minutes until pliable, then drain. Soaking in cold rather than hot water keeps the noodles from breaking.
  3. Heat the oil in a large flat-bottom wok and saute the garlic and onion for 2 minutes, then the sausage, chicken, carrot and green beans for 2 minutes. Add the cabbage for 1 minute. Lift out half the vegetables for topping.
  4. Add the broth, soy sauce, oyster sauce and pepper and bring to a boil. Add the noodles and toss with tongs for 3 to 5 minutes until they absorb all the liquid.
  5. Top with the reserved vegetables and green onion and serve with calamansi to squeeze over. Pancit, from Hokkien for 'convenient food', is served at birthdays for long life.

Common mistakes

  • Adding too much liquid: the noodles should absorb the stock, not swim in it — add gradually and adjust.

Authentic tips

  • Pre-soaking the noodles just until pliable (not fully soft) keeps them from turning mushy once they finish cooking in the pan.

Substitutions

  • No bihon: thin rice noodles or even angel hair pasta work in a pinch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days; reheats well with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Calamansi wedges and a fried lumpia
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
