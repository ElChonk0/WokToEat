Pancit Bihon

▾

Surprise me with

Pancit, from the Hokkien word for noodles, reflects centuries of Chinese influence on Filipino cuisine and is traditionally served at birthdays and celebrations as a symbol of long life.

Method

  1. Soak rice vermicelli in warm water until pliable, about 10–15 minutes; drain.
  2. Heat oil in a wok or large pan; cook garlic and onion until fragrant.
  3. Add chicken and cook until just done, 4–5 minutes.
  4. Add carrot, cabbage, and snow peas; stir-fry 2–3 minutes until just tender.
  5. Add soy sauce and stock, then add the drained noodles, tossing to absorb the liquid evenly.
  6. Cook, tossing frequently, 5–6 minutes until the noodles are tender and the liquid is mostly absorbed.
  7. Serve with calamansi or lime wedges.

Common mistakes

  • Adding too much liquid: the noodles should absorb the stock, not swim in it — add gradually and adjust.

Authentic tips

  • Pre-soaking the noodles just until pliable (not fully soft) keeps them from turning mushy once they finish cooking in the pan.

Substitutions

  • No bihon: thin rice noodles or even angel hair pasta work in a pinch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days; reheats well with a splash of water.

Freezing: Not recommended — noodle texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Calamansi wedges and a fried lumpia
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
