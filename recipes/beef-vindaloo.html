Beef Vindaloo

The beef version of Goa's famous Portuguese-derived curry, vindaloo marinates meat in vinegar and chili before a long braise — among the most fiery and vinegar-forward curries in the Indian repertoire.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 2 hr marinating)
  • Cook 1.5 hr

Method

  1. Toast dried chilies, cumin, mustard seeds, and cinnamon in a dry pan until fragrant; grind into a powder.
  2. Blend the spice powder with garlic, ginger, and vinegar into a paste.
  3. Coat beef thoroughly and marinate at least 2 hours, or overnight for best flavor.
  4. Fry onion in oil until golden, then add the marinated beef with its marinade.
  5. Add enough water to just cover, bring to a simmer, cover, and braise 1 to 1.25 hours until tender.
  6. Uncover and reduce 10 minutes; stir in sugar and adjust seasoning before serving.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little vinegar, which is central to vindaloo's distinctive sharp, tangy character.
  • Rushing the beef braise, since chuck needs real time to become properly tender.

Authentic tips

  • This curry is traditionally very spicy and sour — don't tone down the vinegar or chili too much if aiming for authenticity.
  • Like most curries, this dish tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have melded.

Substitutions

  • Cider vinegar replaces malt vinegar.
  • Pork or lamb take the same marinade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the flavor deepens.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pao or steamed rice
  • Soup: Kachumber salad
  • Dessert: Bebinca
  • Beverage: Cold beer
