Dakjjim 닭찜

▾

Surprise me with

A hearty Korean chicken stew braised in soy sauce and gochugaru with root vegetables and glass noodles, dakjjim is a common dish for family gatherings and celebrations, often made in a large pot to share.

Method

  1. Soak the noodles in warm water for about 30 minutes until pliable, then drain. Keep the cut potatoes in cold water so they do not brown. Trim the fat from the chicken and cut it into small pieces so it cooks quickly and absorbs the sauce.
  2. Put the chicken, water, soy sauce, oyster sauce, mirin, sugar, syrup and pepper in a Dutch oven. Bring to a boil over high heat and boil uncovered for about 10 minutes, skimming off the foam.
  3. Add the drained potatoes, carrot, mushrooms, onion, dried chilies, garlic and ginger. Cover and cook over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until the potatoes are just tender. There will still be plenty of liquid.
  4. Add the noodles, fresh chilies and green onion and cook uncovered for about 3 minutes, stirring, until the noodles are glossy and soft. The potatoes and noodles soak up the liquid and thicken it into a rich glaze.
  5. Stir in the sesame oil, scatter with sesame seeds and serve family style with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the glass noodles too early, which makes them overcook and turn mushy.
  • Skimping on the gochugaru, which is what gives this stew its characteristic red color and gentle heat.

Authentic tips

  • Soaking the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes before adding them ensures they cook evenly in the short remaining time.
  • This dish is traditionally served family-style directly from the cooking pot, set at the center of the table.

Substitutions

  • Sweet potato can replace regular potato for a different sweetness.
  • No glass noodles: thin rice noodles work, though texture differs slightly.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; store noodles and stew together, as they'll absorb more sauce over time.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably without the noodles; add fresh noodles when reheating.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and kimchi
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
