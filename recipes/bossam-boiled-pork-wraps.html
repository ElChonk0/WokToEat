Bossam (Boiled Pork Wraps) 보쌈

▾

Surprise me with

Bossam poaches pork belly in aromatics until tender, then serves it sliced with lettuce wraps, kimchi, and salted shrimp — a communal dish popular for gatherings and celebrations across Korea.

Method

  1. Put the water, doenjang, coffee, salt, onion, green onion, garlic, ginger, peppercorns and bay leaves in a pot just large enough to hold the pork and boil over medium-high heat for 5 minutes, stirring to dissolve the paste. The doenjang and coffee take away the porky smell and give the meat a deep savoriness and color.
  2. Add the pork, bring back to a boil and boil uncovered for about 5 minutes, skimming off the scum.
  3. Cover and cook at a steady medium simmer for 45 to 50 minutes, turning the pieces once, until a chopstick slides through the thickest part with no resistance. Check a slice at 40 minutes.
  4. Turn off the heat and leave the pork to cool in its liquid for at least 15 minutes, which keeps it moist and tender. Store any leftovers in the cooking liquid too.
  5. Lift out the pork and slice it thinly against the grain, about 6 mm (1/4 in) thick, just before serving.
  6. Serve warm on a platter with the cabbage or lettuce leaves, radish salad, kimchi, salted shrimp and ssamjang. Wrap a slice of pork with a little of each in a leaf and eat it in one bite.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the pork immediately after cooking without resting, which makes it harder to get clean slices.
  • Skipping the doenjang in the poaching liquid, which is what gives the meat its characteristic savory background flavor.

Authentic tips

  • A little coffee or coffee grounds in the poaching liquid is a traditional Korean trick for tenderizing and reducing gaminess in the pork.
  • This dish is meant to be a communal, build-your-own-wrap meal — set out plenty of accompaniments.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder can substitute for belly for a leaner version.
  • No doenjang: white miso is a reasonable, if less traditional, substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; great sliced into a rice bowl.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Napa kimchi, lettuce and salted shrimp
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Soju or barley tea
