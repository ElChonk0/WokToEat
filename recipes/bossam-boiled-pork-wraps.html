Bossam (Boiled Pork Wraps) 보쌈

Bossam poaches pork belly in aromatics until tender, then serves it sliced with lettuce wraps, kimchi, and salted shrimp — a communal dish popular for gatherings and celebrations across Korea.

  • Cook 1 hr

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil with doenjang, ginger, garlic, green onion, and coffee grounds if using.
  2. Add the whole pork belly piece, reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook 50–60 minutes until very tender.
  3. Remove pork and rest 10 minutes, then slice thin.
  4. Arrange sliced pork on a platter with lettuce leaves, kimchi, and ssamjang.
  5. Serve family-style, wrapping pork and accompaniments in lettuce leaves to eat.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the pork immediately after cooking without resting, which makes it harder to get clean slices.
  • Skipping the doenjang in the poaching liquid, which is what gives the meat its characteristic savory background flavor.

Authentic tips

  • A little coffee or coffee grounds in the poaching liquid is a traditional Korean trick for tenderizing and reducing gaminess in the pork.
  • This dish is meant to be a communal, build-your-own-wrap meal — set out plenty of accompaniments.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder can substitute for belly for a leaner version.
  • No doenjang: white miso is a reasonable, if less traditional, substitute.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; great sliced into a rice bowl.

Freezing: Freezes well, cooked, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Napa kimchi, lettuce and salted shrimp
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced pear
  • Beverage: Soju or barley tea
