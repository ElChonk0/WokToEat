Teriyaki Salmon

▾

Surprise me with

"Teriyaki" describes a cooking technique — grilling or pan-searing with a glossy, sweet-savory glaze — rather than one specific recipe, and became one of Japan's most internationally recognized styles of cooking salmon in particular over the 20th century.

Method

  1. Pat the salmon dry and season lightly with salt. Leave for 10 minutes, then pat off the moisture and dust the fillets lightly with flour, which helps the sauce cling. Mix the soy sauce, mirin, sake and sugar.
  2. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat and lay the fillets in skin-side down. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the skin is crisp.
  3. Turn, cover and cook for 2 to 3 minutes until almost opaque in the center.
  4. Wipe out the excess oil with a paper towel, pour in the sauce and simmer over medium heat, spooning it over the fish, for 1 to 2 minutes until it thickens into a glossy glaze.
  5. Plate the salmon, spoon the glaze over and sprinkle with sesame seeds. Serve with rice and a green vegetable.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the sauce too early, before the fish has a chance to sear — this steams the fish and prevents color from developing.
  • Using a sauce that's too thin and never properly glazing; let it reduce until it visibly coats the back of a spoon.

Authentic tips

  • A non-stick or well-seasoned cast-iron pan makes a real difference for crisp salmon skin without sticking.
  • Make extra glaze and keep some on the side, since the pan version reduces and intensifies quickly.

Substitutions

  • Any oily fish works — mackerel or trout are excellent substitutes.
  • No mirin? Use 2 tbsp sake plus 1 extra tsp sugar.

Storage

Leftovers: Flake into a grain bowl or onigiri filling the next day.

Freezing: Freeze raw salmon in the marinade for up to 2 months; thaw and cook as directed.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and spinach ohitashi
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
