Cōng Shāo Hǎi Shēn (Scallion-Braised Sea Cucumber) 葱烧海参

▾

Scallion-braised sea cucumber is the most celebrated Shandong banquet dish, in which rehydrated sea cucumber is simmered in a sauce of caramelized scallion.

Method

  1. Slice the sea cucumbers lengthwise and blanch them with the ginger, green onion and blanching wine for 3 minutes to remove any fishy smell. Drain.
  2. Fry the scallion whites in the oil over medium-low heat for 6 to 8 minutes, turning, until golden brown and fragrant. Lift them out and keep the scallion oil. This cong you is the soul of the dish.
  3. Fry the ginger in 2 tablespoons of the scallion oil, add the sauce wine, oyster sauce, soy sauces, sugar and stock, and bring to a boil.
  4. Add the sea cucumbers and fried scallions and simmer gently over low heat for 10 to 15 minutes, so the sea cucumbers absorb the flavor; they have none of their own.
  5. Thicken with the slurry and finish with a spoon of scallion oil for gloss. Cong shao hai shen is Shandong's most celebrated banquet dish.

Common mistakes

  • Poorly rehydrated sea cucumber is rubbery; buy prepared if you are not sure.
  • Burning the scallions makes the sauce bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Dried sea cucumber takes several days to rehydrate; ready-to-cook is a good shortcut.
  • Serve hot as a banquet dish.

Substitutions

  • Pre-rehydrated sea cucumber saves 2 days of soaking.
  • Add braised mushrooms to stretch the dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
