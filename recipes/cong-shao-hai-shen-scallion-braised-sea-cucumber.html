Cōng Shāo Hǎi Shēn (Scallion-Braised Sea Cucumber) 葱烧海参

▾

Surprise me with

Scallion-braised sea cucumber is the most celebrated Shandong banquet dish, in which rehydrated sea cucumber is simmered in a sauce of caramelized scallion.

  • 150 g5 ozScallions, cut into long pieces
  • 20 g1 tbspGinger
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 30 ml2 tbspOyster sauce
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 250 ml1 cupChicken stock
  • 30 ml2 tbspCornstarch slurry
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Cut the rehydrated sea cucumbers into 4 cm pieces and blanch 2 minutes with ginger; drain.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok over medium heat and fry the scallions until deep golden and fragrant, about 4 minutes; set half aside.
  3. Add the ginger, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and stock to the pan and bring to a simmer.
  4. Add the sea cucumbers and simmer gently for 10 minutes so they absorb the sauce.
  5. Thicken with the slurry, then top with the reserved fried scallions.

Common mistakes

  • Poorly rehydrated sea cucumber is rubbery; buy prepared if you are not sure.
  • Burning the scallions makes the sauce bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Dried sea cucumber takes several days to rehydrate; ready-to-cook is a good shortcut.
  • Serve hot as a banquet dish.

Substitutions

  • Pre-rehydrated sea cucumber saves 2 days of soaking.
  • Add braised mushrooms to stretch the dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
