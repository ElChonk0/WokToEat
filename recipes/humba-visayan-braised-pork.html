Humba (Visayan Braised Pork Belly)

▾

Surprise me with

Humba, pork belly braised in soy sauce, vinegar, black beans and brown sugar, is a Visayan dish with Chinese influences, a sweeter relative of adobo.

  • Cook 2 hr

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and brown the pork for 6 to 8 minutes, rendering some fat. Pour off all but 2 tablespoons.
  2. Saute the garlic and onion in the fat for 3 minutes.
  3. Add the soy sauce, vinegar, brown sugar, pineapple juice, black beans, star anise, bay leaves, peppercorns and water. Bring to a boil without stirring for 2 minutes to cook off the vinegar's sharpness.
  4. Cover and simmer over low heat for 1.5 hours, until the pork is meltingly tender. Add the banana blossoms for the last 15 minutes.
  5. Uncover and reduce for 10 to 15 minutes until the sauce is thick and sticky. Humba is the sweeter, Chinese-influenced Visayan cousin of adobo.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring right after adding vinegar leaves a raw sour edge.
  • Boiling hard toughens the pork.

Authentic tips

  • Add fried saba bananas or boiled eggs.
  • Better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.
  • Fried saba bananas are a classic addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
