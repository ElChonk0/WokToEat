Shuǐzhǔ Yú (Sichuan Boiled Fish) 水煮鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Shuizhu yu, "water-boiled fish," is a Sichuan classic that became a national restaurant staple from the 1980s; despite the name, the fish poaches in a fiery broth of chili and Sichuan pepper and is finished with sizzling oil.

Method

  1. Marinate the fish with egg white, cornstarch, salt and a splash of Shaoxing wine for 15 minutes.
  2. Blanch the cabbage or sprouts, drain, and spread in a deep serving bowl.
  3. Fry the doubanjiang, ginger and garlic in 2 tablespoons of oil until the oil turns red, then add stock and bring to a boil.
  4. Slide in the fish slices one at a time and poach gently for 2 minutes until just opaque; pour fish and broth over the vegetables.
  5. Scatter the dried chilies and peppercorns on top, then heat the remaining oil until smoking and pour it over to bloom them.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the fish hard, which breaks the delicate slices apart.
  • Skipping the egg white and starch coating, which keeps the fish silky.

Authentic tips

  • Use the bones and head to make the stock for a deeper broth.
  • Most of the oil stays in the bowl; eat the fish and vegetables and leave the oil.

Substitutions

  • Cod, basa or catfish fillet all work well in place of tilapia.
  • Use fewer dried chilies for a milder version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps one day in the fridge; the fish softens, so reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended; the fish texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is soupy
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
