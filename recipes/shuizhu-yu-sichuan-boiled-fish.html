Shuǐzhǔ Yú (Sichuan Boiled Fish) 水煮鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Shuizhu yu, "water-boiled fish," is a Sichuan classic that became a national restaurant staple from the 1980s; despite the name, the fish poaches in a fiery broth of chili and Sichuan pepper and is finished with sizzling oil.

  • 10 ml2 tspSalt and Shaoxing wine, for the fish

  • 15 g2 tbspCornstarch
  • 300 g10 ozNapa cabbage, celery or bean sprouts
  • 45 g3 tbspPixian doubanjiang
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger and garlic, minced
  • 500 ml2 cupsStock or water
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce
  • 25 g1 ozDried red chilies
  • 10 g1 tbspSichuan peppercorns
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion and garlic, for the topping
  • 120 ml1/2 cupNeutral oil, for frying and the final pour

Method

  1. Toss the fish slices with a pinch of salt, the Shaoxing wine (or a splash of water), the egg white and the cornstarch until coated in a thin film. Velveting keeps the delicate fish whole and silky in the broth.
  2. Toast the dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns in a dry flat-bottom wok over low heat for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant and darker, then cool and chop them finely.
  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil over high heat and stir-fry the vegetables with a pinch of salt for 1 to 2 minutes, then lay them in a deep bowl.
  4. Add 2 more tablespoons of oil, lower the heat and fry the doubanjiang for 1 to 2 minutes until the oil is red. Add the ginger and garlic for 30 seconds, then the stock and soy sauce and bring to a boil.
  5. Lower to a gentle simmer and slide in the fish slices one at a time. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes without stirring, until just opaque, and pour fish and broth over the vegetables.
  6. Top with the chopped chilies, peppercorns, green onion and garlic. Heat the remaining oil until smoking and pour it over so the spices sizzle. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the fish hard, which breaks the delicate slices apart.
  • Skipping the egg white and starch coating, which keeps the fish silky.

Authentic tips

  • Use the bones and head to make the stock for a deeper broth.
  • Most of the oil stays in the bowl; eat the fish and vegetables and leave the oil.

Substitutions

  • Cod, basa or catfish fillet all work well in place of tilapia.
  • Use fewer dried chilies for a milder version.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps one day in the fridge; the fish softens, so reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended; the fish texture suffers.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the dish is soupy
  • Dessert: Chilled watermelon
  • Beverage: Cold beer
