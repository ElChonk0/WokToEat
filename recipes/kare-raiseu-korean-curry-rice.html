Kare-raiseu (Korean Curry Rice) 카레라이스

▾

Surprise me with

Korean curry, a mild yellow curry of pork, potato and carrot made with curry powder roux, arrived via Japan and spread with the Ottogi curry powder launched in 1969; it is a school-lunch and family staple.

Method

  1. Cut the pork small and season it lightly with salt and pepper. Dice the onion and cut the potatoes and carrot into chunks of a similar size.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 to 6 minutes, until translucent and lightly browned; this sweetness is the base of the sauce.
  3. Add the pork, garlic and ginger and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until the pork is no longer pink. Add the potatoes, carrot and mushrooms and stir-fry for 2 minutes more, until the potato edges turn translucent.
  4. Pour in the water, bring to a boil, then simmer for 10 to 15 minutes until the pork and vegetables are tender.
  5. Turn the heat to low, break the curry roux into pieces and stir it in until dissolved, then add the peas. Simmer over medium-low for 5 minutes, stirring often so it does not catch, until thick and glossy. Thin with a little water if it gets too thick.
  6. Spoon the curry over the rice and serve with kimchi or pickled radish.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the curry mix as a dry powder makes lumps; dissolve it first.
  • Not stirring after thickening lets it scorch.

Authentic tips

  • Korean curry mixes are milder and more turmeric-yellow than Japanese roux blocks.
  • Kimchi on the side is essential in Korea.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or beef replace pork.
  • Japanese curry roux works for a darker, sweeter curry.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; it thickens, so loosen with water.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months; potatoes soften slightly.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Kimchi and danmuji
  • Soup: Miyeokguk
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
