Thit Kho (Caramelized Braised Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

A staple of Vietnamese home cooking and a Lunar New Year fixture, thit kho braises pork belly in a dark caramel-fish sauce base, often with boiled eggs added, until deeply savory and glossy.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork for 3 minutes, rinse and marinate it with the fish sauce, shallots, garlic and pepper for 30 minutes.
  2. Make the caramel: heat the sugar with the oil and a tablespoon of water in a Dutch oven over medium heat, without stirring, until it turns deep amber, 4 to 5 minutes.
  3. Add the pork and toss for 3 to 4 minutes until coated in the caramel and lightly browned.
  4. Pour in the coconut water to just cover the pork, bring to a boil and skim, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1 hour. Coconut water, not plain water, gives thit kho its sweet, clean flavor.
  5. Add the eggs and the finishing fish sauce and simmer uncovered for 30 to 45 minutes, until the pork is tender and the sauce is amber and slightly thickened. Skim the fat.
  6. Serve with rice and pickled mustard greens. A Tet (Lunar New Year) dish, it keeps well for days.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the caramel burn before adding the pork, which makes the whole dish bitter.
  • Stirring too vigorously during braising, which can break up the pork — spoon the sauce over instead.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is traditionally made in a clay pot; a heavy Dutch oven distributes heat just as gently and works on gas, electric and induction.
  • Flavor deepens considerably overnight — this is an ideal make-ahead dish.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.
  • Coconut water is traditional; water plus a spoonful of sugar works.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickled mustard greens

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
