Pork Momos (Himalayan Steamed Dumplings)

▾

Surprise me with

Momos, steamed dumplings that came to India with Tibetan and Nepali communities, are a street food of Darjeeling, Sikkim and Delhi.

Method

  1. Knead the flour and water into a firm, smooth dough. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork with the onion, garlic, ginger, herbs, soy sauce, salt, pepper, water and oil until sticky and juicy. The water and onion make the filling burst with juice.
  3. Make the chutney: char the tomatoes in a dry pan or over a flame until blistered, then blend with the soaked chilies, garlic, timur and a pinch of salt.
  4. Roll the dough into thin rounds 8 cm (3 in) across, thinner at the edges. Fill each with a heaping teaspoon of filling and pleat into a round purse or a half-moon.
  5. Steam on oiled racks over boiling water for 10 to 12 minutes, until the wrappers are slightly translucent and the filling is cooked.
  6. Serve hot with the tomato chutney. Momos came to Darjeeling, Sikkim and the Northeast from Tibet and Nepal.

Common mistakes

  • Dry filling makes dry momos; add the water.
  • Overfilling splits the wrappers.

Authentic tips

  • Wrappers must be thin; roll them yourself if possible.
  • Fry leftovers.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken replaces pork.
  • Chopped cabbage lightens the filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; re-steam or pan-fry.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; steam from frozen for 15 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Spicy tomato chutney
  • Soup: Clear broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Butter tea
