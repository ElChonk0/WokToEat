Siu Ngap (Cantonese Roast Duck) 烧鸭

▾

Surprise me with

Cantonese roast duck is a siu mei dish of whole duck with a spiced cavity and a dried, lacquered skin, roasted until the skin is crisp and the meat stays juicy, and chopped to order in Hong Kong barbecue shops.

Method

  1. Pat the duck dry inside and out and trim off the fat around the cavity opening and any loose neck skin. Rub the cavity lightly with salt and rinse it once if the duck smells strong.
  2. Mix the hoisin, bean sauce, soy sauce, wine, sugar, five-spice and cavity salt into a paste. Spoon it into the cavity with the garlic, ginger, shallots, star anise, tangerine peel and green onion, and rub it around the inside. Keep it off the skin, which must stay clean to crisp.
  3. Close the cavity tightly with a bamboo skewer, weaving it through the skin like a stitch, or sew it with kitchen string, so the juices stay inside and steam the meat from within as it roasts.
  4. Set the duck on a rack in the sink and ladle the boiling water slowly over every part of the skin, turning it, until the skin tightens, shrinks and turns pale. Scalding tightens the pores so the skin roasts thin and crisp.
  5. Stir the maltose, both vinegars and hot water until smooth and brush it evenly over the warm skin. Hang the duck or stand it upright on a rack in the refrigerator, uncovered, for 12 to 24 hours, brushing on a second coat after a few hours. The skin is ready when it feels dry, tight and papery; damp skin stays rubbery.
  6. Take the duck out 1 hour before roasting. Heat the oven to 220 C (425 F). Set it breast-side up on a rack over a tray holding 2 cm (1 in) of water, which stops the dripping fat from smoking.
  7. Roast for 20 minutes, then turn the oven down to 180 C (350 F), turn the duck breast-side down and roast for 30 minutes. Turn it breast-side up again and roast 20 to 30 minutes more, until the skin is deep mahogany and crackly and the thigh reads 74 C (165 F). Cover any spots that darken too fast with small pieces of foil.
  8. Rest for 15 minutes. Remove the skewer over a bowl and pour out the cavity juices; skim off the fat to make the sauce. Chop the duck through the bone into pieces and serve with the juices and plum sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the air-drying gives soft, leathery skin; give it at least 12 hours.
  • A closed cavity that is not sealed lets the marinade leak and the flavor escape.

Authentic tips

  • A fan blowing on the duck in the refrigerator dries it faster.
  • Roast on a rack so the fat drips away and the skin crisps all round.

Substitutions

  • Two duck legs and thighs replace a whole duck: roast 50 minutes.
  • Honey replaces maltose.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; crisp in a 200 C oven for 10 minutes.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; thaw overnight.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh orange wedges
  • Beverage: Hot pu-erh tea
