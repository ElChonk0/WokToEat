Shanghai Red-Braised Pork Belly 红烧肉

The dish that defines hong shao (red braising) as a technique, Shanghai-style red-braised pork belly is sweeter and glossier than its Hunanese cousin, built on rock sugar caramelization and a long, gentle simmer.

Method

  1. Blanch pork belly chunks in boiling water 3 minutes; drain and pat dry.
  2. Melt rock sugar in a dry pot over medium heat until deep amber.
  3. Add pork and turn to coat in the caramel, 2–3 minutes.
  4. Add both soy sauces, wine, ginger, green onion, and star anise, stirring to coat.
  5. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a very gentle simmer, cover, and braise 45 minutes.
  6. Uncover and reduce over medium heat 10–15 minutes until the sauce is thick and glossy, coating the pork.

Common mistakes

  • Simmering too vigorously, which can toughen the meat instead of gently melting it tender.
  • Rushing the final reduction, which leaves the sauce thin instead of the intended thick, clinging glaze.

Authentic tips

  • This is a classic slow-and-low dish — patience during the simmer is what makes the pork meltingly tender.
  • Like most braises, this dish tastes even better reheated the next day.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish; simmer 20 minutes longer.
  • Hard-boiled eggs added for the last 20 minutes soak up the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently so the fat doesn't separate.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Blanched greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
