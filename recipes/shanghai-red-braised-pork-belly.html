Shanghai Red-Braised Pork Belly 红烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

The dish that defines hong shao (red braising) as a technique, Shanghai-style red-braised pork belly is sweeter and glossier than its Hunanese cousin, built on rock sugar caramelization and a long, gentle simmer.

Method

  1. Put the pork belly in a pot of cold water, bring it to a boil and boil for 3 minutes. Drain and rinse off the scum; starting in cold water draws out more of the blood.
  2. Heat a tablespoon of oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion and star anise for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the rock sugar and stir over medium-low heat until it melts into an amber caramel, then add the pork belly and turn it until glossy and evenly colored. Caramelized sugar, not just dark soy, gives Shanghai red-braising its lacquered color.
  4. Pour in the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the pot so it sizzles, then the soy sauces and enough hot water to almost cover. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 1 hour (add the eggs halfway), until the pork is soft enough to cut with a spoon.
  5. Uncover, raise the heat to medium-high and reduce the sauce for 5 to 10 minutes, turning and basting, until it is thick, glossy and clings to the meat. This final reduction (shou zhi) is what makes the dish 'nong you chi jiang': rich in oil, dark in sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Simmering too vigorously, which can toughen the meat instead of gently melting it tender.
  • Rushing the final reduction, which leaves the sauce thin instead of the intended thick, clinging glaze.

Authentic tips

  • This is a classic slow-and-low dish — patience during the simmer is what makes the pork meltingly tender.
  • Like most braises, this dish tastes even better reheated the next day.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish; simmer 20 minutes longer.
  • Hard-boiled eggs added for the last 20 minutes soak up the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat gently so the fat doesn't separate.

Freezing: Freezes well for 3 months.
