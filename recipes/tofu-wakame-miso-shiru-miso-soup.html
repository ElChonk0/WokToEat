Tofu to Wakame no Misoshiru (Tofu and Wakame Miso Soup) 豆腐とわかめの味噌汁

▾

Miso soup is the cornerstone of the Japanese breakfast; tofu and wakame is the most common version, found in homes across the country.

  • 10 g1/3 ozKombu, for the dashi
  • 33Dried shiitake, for the dashi
  • 900 ml3.75 cupsWater
  • 200 g7 ozSilken or soft tofu, cut into 1 cm (1/2 in) cubes
  • 6 g2 tbspDried wakame
  • 60 g4 tbspMiso paste (awase or white)
  • 30 g1 ozGreen onion, finely sliced

Method

  1. Soak the kombu and shiitake in the water for at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the refrigerator. Heat slowly and remove the kombu just before it boils, then simmer the shiitake for 5 minutes and remove them. This is the vegan dashi.
  2. Soak the wakame in cold water for 5 minutes until it expands, then drain.
  3. Add the tofu to the simmering dashi and heat for 2 minutes.
  4. Turn off the heat. Put the miso in a ladle or strainer, lower it into the soup and stir with chopsticks until it dissolves completely. Taste and add more if you like.
  5. Add the wakame and warm the soup gently, just until steaming; boiling it after the miso goes in dulls the aroma. Serve topped with green onion.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling after adding the miso dulls its aroma.
  • Too much wakame swells and takes over.

Authentic tips

  • Use a mix of red and white miso.
  • Serve at the start of a meal.

Substitutions

  • White miso is milder; red miso is saltier.
  • Instant dashi powder replaces kombu dashi.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently without boiling.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
