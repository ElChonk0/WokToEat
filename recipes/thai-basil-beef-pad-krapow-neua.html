Thai Basil Beef (Pad Krapow Neua)

The beef version of Thailand's ubiquitous holy basil stir-fry, pad krapow neua pairs the peppery herb with quickly seared beef for a dish nearly as popular as its more common pork and chicken counterparts.

Method

  1. Pound or mince garlic and chilies together into a rough paste.
  2. Stir-fry the garlic-chili paste in hot oil over high heat until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  3. Add beef and stir-fry until just cooked through, 3–4 minutes.
  4. Add fish sauce, oyster sauce, soy sauce, and sugar; toss to combine.
  5. Stir in basil leaves at the very end, off heat, just until wilted.
  6. Fry eggs sunny-side up with crispy edges and serve on top of the beef over jasmine rice.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the basil too early and overcooking it, which turns it dark and loses its distinctive peppery aroma.
  • Substituting sweet Italian basil for Thai holy basil without adjusting expectations — the flavor is quite different.

Authentic tips

  • Thai holy basil (bai krapow) has a more peppery, clove-like flavor than Thai sweet basil — seek it out if possible.
  • Very high heat and quick cooking are essential; this dish should take only a few minutes total.

Substitutions

  • Thai sweet basil works if holy basil isn't available, though the flavor will be milder.
  • Ground beef and sliced beef both work — ground is faster, sliced gives more texture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the basil loses some vibrancy.

Freezing: Not recommended — fresh basil doesn't hold up.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice topped with a fried egg
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
