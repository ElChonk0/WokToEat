Niú Ròu Cháng Fěn (Beef Rice Noodle Rolls) 牛肉肠粉

▾

Surprise me with

Cheung fun, steamed rice-noodle rolls, are a Cantonese dim sum staple; the beef version is a breakfast favorite in Guangzhou and Hong Kong.

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, wheat starch, salt, batter oil and room-temperature water until smooth, then stir in the boiling water. Rest the batter for at least 30 minutes; rushed batter makes sheets that crack when rolled.
  2. Mix the beef with the cilantro, green onion, cornstarch, soy sauce, baking soda, sugar and sesame oil, then beat in the cold water a little at a time until the mixture turns pasty and smooth. Divide it into 6 portions.
  3. Simmer the sweet soy ingredients for 1 minute until the sugar dissolves. Oil two 23 cm (9 in) square metal pans. In a wide pot with a tight lid, set a small rack and 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of water and bring it to a full boil.
  4. Warm one pan in the steamer, stir the batter, pour in about 120 ml (1/2 cup) and tilt to coat the base evenly. Cover and steam for 1 minute until the sheet is just set.
  5. Shape a beef portion into a flat strip about 6 mm (1/4 in) thick and lay it across one end of the sheet. Cover and steam for 4 to 5 minutes more, until the beef is cooked through and the sheet is translucent and slightly puffed.
  6. Lift the pan out onto a towel and put the second pan in to start the next sheet. Loosen the sheet with a spatula and roll it up around the beef, then slide it onto a plate. Oil the pan again before each sheet.
  7. Cut the rolls into pieces and drizzle with the sweet soy just before serving. Eat them warm; the sheets firm up as they cool.

Common mistakes

  • A batter that has settled makes thick, rubbery sheets, so stir it before every ladle.
  • Pouring too thick a layer gives chewy rolls; the sheets should be almost translucent.

Authentic tips

  • A flat-bottomed pan in a steamer with a cloth-wrapped lid stops condensation dripping onto the rolls.
  • Cheung fun are best eaten within minutes of rolling.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or char siu make a classic alternative filling.
  • Chopped choy sum and mushrooms give a vegetarian filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten within an hour; leftovers keep 1 day and re-steam for 3 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended; the rice rolls turn hard.
