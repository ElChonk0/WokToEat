Niú Ròu Cháng Fěn (Beef Rice Noodle Rolls) 牛肉肠粉

Cheung fun, steamed rice-noodle rolls, are a Cantonese dim sum staple; the beef version is a breakfast favorite in Guangzhou and Hong Kong.

  • Prep 30 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Whisk the rice flour, wheat starch, 450 ml water and a pinch of salt until smooth; rest 20 minutes and stir again before using.
  2. Season the ground beef with 1 tablespoon of the soy sauce and half the sesame oil.
  3. Oil a shallow heatproof tray. Ladle in a thin layer of batter (about 3 mm), scatter over 2 tablespoons of beef and some scallion, and steam over high heat 3-4 minutes until the sheet is translucent and the beef is cooked.
  4. Loosen the edge with a scraper and roll the sheet into a loose log; repeat with the remaining batter, oiling the tray each time.
  5. Mix the remaining soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil and 60 ml hot water and pour over the rolls just before serving.

Common mistakes

  • A batter that has settled makes thick, rubbery sheets, so stir it before every ladle.
  • Pouring too thick a layer gives chewy rolls; the sheets should be almost translucent.

Authentic tips

  • A flat-bottomed pan in a steamer with a cloth-wrapped lid stops condensation dripping onto the rolls.
  • Cheung fun are best eaten within minutes of rolling.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or char siu make a classic alternative filling.
  • Chopped choy sum and mushrooms give a vegetarian filling.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten within an hour; leftovers keep 1 day and re-steam for 3 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended; the rice rolls turn hard.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Congee
  • Soup: Clear pork bone soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
