Fuzhou Drunken Ribs 醉排骨

▾

Surprise me with

A Fuzhou banquet classic, these ribs are fried crisp and then tossed in a tangy glaze sharpened with rice wine and vinegar; the "drunk" refers to the wine-soaked finish.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 30 min marinating)
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying

Method

  1. Marinate the ribs with soy, wine, egg and a pinch of salt for 30 minutes, then toss in potato starch.
  2. Fry at 170°C (340°F) for 6 minutes until cooked through; drain. Raise to 190°C (375°F) and fry 1 minute more until crisp.
  3. In a clean wok, fry the garlic, add vinegar, sugar and rice wine and bubble until slightly syrupy.
  4. Toss the ribs through the glaze over high heat until coated and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the second, hotter fry, which leaves the coating soft once glazed.

Authentic tips

  • Ask for spare ribs cut across the bone into small pieces; they cook evenly and are easy to eat.

Substitutions

  • Air-fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15–18 minutes, shaking twice, instead of deep-frying.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; re-crisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freeze the fried, unsauced ribs for up to a month; re-fry or oven-crisp, then glaze.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
