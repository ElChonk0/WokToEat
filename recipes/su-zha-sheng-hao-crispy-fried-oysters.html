Sū Zhà Shēng Háo (Crispy Fried Oysters) 酥炸生蚝

▾

Surprise me with

Plump oysters in a light, crisp batter are a Cantonese restaurant and banquet dish, especially in winter when oysters are best.

Method

  1. Toss the oysters very gently with the cleaning cornstarch, then rinse them in cold water three times and drain in a colander. The starch lifts off grit and slime. Toss them with the ginger and wine and leave for 5 minutes.
  2. Whisk the flour, batter cornstarch, baking powder, garlic salt, fish sauce, pepper and ice water until the batter flows from the whisk in a steady ribbon. Stir in the batter oil just before using. Spread the cabbage on a platter.
  3. Pat the oysters dry on paper towels; wet oysters spit and the batter slides off. Heat the frying oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok to 165 C (330 F); a drop of batter should float straight up.
  4. Coat the oysters a few at a time and fry them in batches over medium heat for about 1 minute, until set and pale golden. Drain on a rack.
  5. Raise the oil to 200 C (390 F) and fry them all again for about 20 seconds, until deep golden and crisp. Drain and pile on the cabbage. Serve at once with lemon or sweet chili sauce.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the quick blanch lets the oysters release water and spatter in the oil.
  • Over-frying shrinks the oysters to nothing.

Authentic tips

  • The flour scrub is the Cantonese way to clean oysters.
  • Keep the batter cold for the crispest shell.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or squid in the same batter.
  • Serve on shredded lettuce with lemon.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Sweet red bean soup
  • Beverage: Cold beer
