Jì Cài Hún Tún (Shepherd's Purse Wontons) 荠菜馄饨

▾

Surprise me with

Shepherd's purse wontons, filled with the spring wild green ji cai, are a seasonal Shanghai favorite and a typical breakfast.

  • Cook 8 min

Method

  1. Blanch the shepherd's purse for 30 seconds, cool it in cold water, squeeze it very dry and chop finely. Excess water would make the wrappers soggy.
  2. Mix the greens with the tofu, chopped egg, shiitake, ginger, soy sauce, salt, sugar and sesame oil. The sesame oil binds the meatless filling.
  3. Put a tablespoon of filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it in half, then bring the two corners together and press to seal into the large 'ingot' shape typical of Shanghai.
  4. Boil the wontons in plenty of water for 4 to 5 minutes, until they float and the wrappers are tender.
  5. Put laver, a drop of sesame oil, a pinch of salt and green onion in each bowl, ladle in the hot stock and add the wontons.

Common mistakes

  • Wet greens make the filling watery; squeeze thoroughly.
  • Overfilling splits the wrappers.

Authentic tips

  • Shepherd's purse has a distinctive wild-green flavor; spinach with a little chive is a substitute.
  • Add a few drops of sesame oil to finish.

Substitutions

  • Spinach with a little chopped dill replaces shepherd's purse.
  • Mushrooms replace tofu.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; store wontons and broth separately.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked wontons for 2 months; boil from frozen for 5 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pan-fried buns
  • Soup: Not needed; the wonton soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Orange slices
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
