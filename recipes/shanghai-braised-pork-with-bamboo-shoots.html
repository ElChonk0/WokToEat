Shanghai Braised Pork with Bamboo Shoots 笋尖烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

A springtime Jiangnan favorite, this dish braises pork belly with fresh bamboo shoots, which soak up the sweet soy braising liquid while contributing their own delicate crunch.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 40 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 700 g1.5 lbPork belly, cut into chunks
  • 300 g10.5 ozBamboo shoots, sliced (fresh or canned)
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 30 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 1 knob1 knobGinger, sliced
  • 400 ml1.75 cupsWater

Method

  1. If using fresh bamboo shoots, blanch 5 minutes to remove bitterness; drain.
  2. Blanch pork belly 3 minutes; drain.
  3. Brown pork in a little oil, add ginger, stirring briefly.
  4. Add both soy sauces, rock sugar, and wine, stirring to coat.
  5. Add water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 25 minutes.
  6. Add bamboo shoots and continue simmering uncovered 10 minutes until the sauce reduces and thickens.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the blanching step for fresh bamboo shoots, which leaves an unpleasant bitter taste.
  • Adding bamboo shoots too early, which can make them mushy by the time the pork is tender.

Authentic tips

  • Canned bamboo shoots (rinsed well) are a perfectly fine, widely available substitute for fresh.
  • The pork should be quite tender by the end — give it the full simmering time before adding the shoots.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder can replace belly for a leaner version.
  • Shiitake mushrooms can be added alongside bamboo shoots for extra umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably, though bamboo shoots soften somewhat.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
