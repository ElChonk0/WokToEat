Xián Dàn Huáng Xiā (Salted Egg Yolk Shrimp) 咸蛋黄虾

▾

Surprise me with

Salted egg yolk shrimp is a Hong Kong and Cantonese tze char style dish in which fried shrimp are tossed in a crumbly, buttery salted egg yolk coating.

Method

  1. Steam the salted yolks over boiling water for 8 to 10 minutes until firm, then mash them with a fork into a fine, sandy crumb with no lumps. Lumpy yolk will not foam in the butter.
  2. Pat the shrimp very dry, season with the salt and pepper and toss in the cornstarch until evenly coated, shaking off the excess.
  3. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven to 180 C (350 F) and fry the shrimp in batches for 1.5 to 2 minutes until crisp and pink. Drain on a rack.
  4. Pour out the oil and wipe the wok. Melt the butter over low heat and fry the curry leaves, chilies and garlic for 30 seconds until crisp and fragrant.
  5. Add the mashed yolk and sugar and stir constantly over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until the mixture foams up into a frothy, golden sauce. Keep the heat low; high heat makes the yolk grainy and dull.
  6. Return the shrimp and toss quickly until every piece is coated in the foamy yolk. Serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the yolk brown makes it bitter; keep the heat at medium and move quickly.
  • Wet shrimp make the coating gummy, so dry them thoroughly before starching.

Authentic tips

  • The yolk should froth into fine bubbles; that is what gives the sandy texture.
  • Fry the curry leaves in the oil for a few seconds first for extra fragrance.

Substitutions

  • Squid or chicken pieces take the same sauce.
  • Unsalted cooked egg yolks mashed with 1/2 teaspoon salt stand in for salted yolks.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the coating softens within an hour.

Freezing: Not recommended.
