Ròu Mò Mǐ Xiàn (Minced Pork Rice Noodles) 肉末米线

▾

Surprise me with

Rice noodles topped with a savory minced pork sauce are a standard Kunming breakfast, sold at small shops around the city.

  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozFresh round rice noodles (mi xian)
  • 300 g10 ozPork, ground
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic and ginger, minced
  • 20 ml4 tspSweet soy sauce or dark soy with sugar
  • 20 ml4 tspLight soy sauce
  • 1.2 L5 cupsPork or chicken stock
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 80 g3 ozPickled mustard greens, chopped
  • 30 ml2 tbspChili oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGreen onion, mint and cilantro

Method

  1. Fry the pork in the oil with the garlic and ginger over medium-high heat for 5 minutes until browned and crumbly. Add the soy sauces and cook for 2 minutes until glossy.
  2. Season the stock with salt and keep it boiling.
  3. Blanch the rice noodles for 30 seconds to 1 minute.
  4. Divide the noodles among bowls, top with the minced pork and pickled greens and ladle over the hot stock.
  5. Finish with chili oil and herbs. Known as xiao guo mi xian when cooked in small copper pots.

Common mistakes

  • Using lukewarm stock makes the bowl flat.
  • Overcooked noodles break apart.

Authentic tips

  • The pickles supply plenty of salt; season the stock last.
  • Kunming locals eat this for breakfast.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken replaces pork.
  • Fresh rice noodles replace dried.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the pork topping keeps 4 days.

Freezing: Freeze the topping for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodle soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Pu'er tea
