Xiāng Cháng (Taiwanese Sweet Pork Sausage) 香腸

▾

Surprise me with

Taiwanese sausage, sweet and garlicky with a hint of rice wine, is sold from night-market grills and eaten with raw garlic and pickles.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 400 g14 ozTaiwanese sausages (sweet pork sausages)
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice wine
  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil
  • 60 g2 ozRaw garlic cloves, peeled
  • 100 g3.5 ozPickled cucumber or green chilies
  • 10 g1/3 ozCilantro

Method

  1. Put the sausages in a cold skillet with the rice wine and 60 ml (1/4 cup) of water, cover and simmer over medium-low heat for 5 minutes. Gentle poaching cooks the sugary sausages through before they burn.
  2. Uncover, add the oil and cook over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes, rolling them often, until the liquid has gone and the skins are glossy and caramelized. Or grill them over medium charcoal, the night-market way.
  3. Rest for 2 minutes, then slice on an angle.
  4. Serve with the raw garlic cloves, pickled cucumber and cilantro.
  5. Eat a bite of sausage with a clove of raw garlic; the sharp garlic balances the sweet, fatty sausage.

Common mistakes

  • High heat burns the sugary casing before the inside cooks.
  • Pricing too much lets the fat out.

Authentic tips

  • Raw garlic is the traditional pairing.
  • Grill over charcoal for the full night-market flavor.

Substitutions

  • Chinese lap cheong in a pinch, cooked 10 minutes.
  • Add a drizzle of rice wine for the last minute.
  • No grill: bake at 190°C (375°F) for 20 minutes on a rack, turning once, then broil 2 minutes to brown; or pan-sear over medium heat.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a pan.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months raw or cooked.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Raw garlic and pickled cucumber
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
