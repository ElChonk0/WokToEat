Mi Quang (Turmeric Noodles with Pork and Shrimp)

▾

Surprise me with

A specialty of Vietnam's Quang Nam province, mi quang tosses wide turmeric-stained rice noodles in just enough rich, peanut-topped broth to coat rather than submerge them — a deliberately drier style than most Vietnamese noodle soups.

Method

  1. Marinate pork and shrimp separately in turmeric, garlic, shallot, and fish sauce for 15 minutes.
  2. Stir-fry pork in oil until browned and just cooked through; remove and set aside.
  3. Stir-fry shrimp in the same pan until just pink; remove and set aside.
  4. Add remaining aromatics to the pan, frying briefly, then add stock and simmer 10 minutes to build a light, concentrated broth.
  5. Cook rice noodles according to package directions; divide among bowls.
  6. Top noodles with pork, shrimp, and a small ladle of broth (just enough to moisten, not submerge); garnish with crushed peanuts, rice crackers, and fresh herbs.

Common mistakes

  • Adding too much broth, which turns this into a soup rather than the intended lightly-sauced noodle dish.
  • Skipping the turmeric in the marinade, which is what gives the dish its characteristic golden color and earthy flavor.

Authentic tips

  • This dish is meant to be much drier than pho or bun bo — use only a small amount of broth per bowl.
  • Rice crackers (banh da) are a traditional crunchy accompaniment, broken up and scattered over the top.

Substitutions

  • Chicken replaces pork.
  • Fresh wide rice noodles replace turmeric noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and the pork topping separately; the topping keeps 3 days.

Freezing: Freeze the topping for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Rice crackers, herbs and chili
  • Soup: The dish has a little broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Iced tea
