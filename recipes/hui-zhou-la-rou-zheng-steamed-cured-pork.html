Huī Zhōu Là Ròu (Huizhou Steamed Cured Pork) 徽州腊肉

▾

Surprise me with

Cured pork belly, smoked and air-dried over winter, is a staple of Huizhou in southern Anhui and across the mountainous south, steamed and sliced as a rich accompaniment to rice.

  • Cook 45 min

  • 400 g14 ozCured pork belly (la rou)
  • 30 g2 tbspGinger slices
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 g2 tbspScallions
  • 10 g2 tspChili (optional)

Method

  1. Rinse the cured pork under warm water, scrubbing the surface, and soak for 10 minutes.
  2. Place the pork on a plate skin-side up with the ginger, sugar and wine.
  3. Steam over high heat for 40 minutes until tender and translucent.
  4. Cool slightly, slice 3 mm thick and arrange on a plate.
  5. Garnish with scallion and chili.

Common mistakes

  • Not rinsing leaves it too salty.
  • Under-steaming leaves the fat chewy.

Authentic tips

  • Slice while still warm for clean cuts.
  • Serve in small portions with plenty of rice.

Substitutions

  • Thick-cut smoked bacon replaces cured pork belly, steamed 25 minutes.
  • Add dried bamboo shoots under the pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; serve cold or re-steam.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, in small portions
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
