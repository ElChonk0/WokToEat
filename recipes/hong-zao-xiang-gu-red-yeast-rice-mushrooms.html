Hóng Zāo Xiāng Gū (Red-Yeast Rice Mushrooms) 红糟香菇

▾

Surprise me with

Red yeast rice lees (hong zao) is the defining Fuzhou seasoning; mushrooms cooked with it gain a wine-scented, savory depth.

  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 500 g1.1 lbMixed mushrooms (shiitake, king oyster, oyster), sliced
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g3/4 ozGarlic, minced
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, minced
  • 60 g1/4 cupRed wine lees (hong zao)
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing or Fujian red rice wine
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 g1/2 ozGreen onion

Method

  1. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over high heat and cook the mushrooms for 4 to 5 minutes, without stirring at first, until browned and their water has evaporated. Lift them out.
  2. Add the remaining oil and fry the garlic, ginger and red wine lees over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant and darker.
  3. Add the wine, sugar and soy sauce and bubble for 30 seconds.
  4. Return the mushrooms and toss until coated in the red sauce.
  5. Scatter with green onion and serve. Check that your hong zao has no added fish products; traditional lees are made only from rice and red yeast.

Common mistakes

  • Crowding the pan steams the mushrooms.
  • Overcooked lees turn bitter, so keep the heat moderate.

Authentic tips

  • Brown the mushrooms well for the best flavor.
  • Use a mix of mushrooms for texture.

Substitutions

  • Red fermented bean curd plus paprika approximates red yeast lees.
  • King oyster mushrooms give a meaty texture.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Tofu soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
