Pork Sinigang

▾

Surprise me with

Sinigang na baboy is one of the Philippines' most beloved comfort dishes, a sour tamarind soup loaded with pork and vegetables — often cited as the national dish alongside adobo.

Method

  1. Put the pork in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil and skim off all the scum.
  2. Add the onion, tomatoes and fish sauce and simmer, covered, for 45 to 60 minutes, until the pork is tender. Add the taro for the last 20 minutes; it melts and thickens the broth.
  3. Boil the fresh tamarind in a cup of the broth until soft, mash and strain it back into the pot, or stir in the sinigang mix. The broth should be boldly sour.
  4. Add the radish for 5 minutes, then the eggplant, long beans, okra and chilies for 3 to 4 minutes.
  5. Add the kangkong for the last minute. Season with fish sauce and serve with rice, with fish sauce and chilies on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the leafy greens too early, which overcooks them and dulls their color.
  • Under-seasoning the sourness — sinigang is meant to have a pronounced tang; taste and add more tamarind base as needed.

Authentic tips

  • Packaged sinigang mix is a reliable, widely used shortcut even in many Filipino households.
  • This soup welcomes almost any vegetable on hand — okra and taro are also traditional additions.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp or beef short ribs can replace or supplement pork for variety.
  • No kangkong: spinach or bok choy work as substitutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the vegetables soften further.

Freezing: Freezes acceptably without the leafy greens; add fresh greens when reheating.

Serve with
