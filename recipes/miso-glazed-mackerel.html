Miso-Glazed Mackerel

▾

Surprise me with

Saba shioyaki and miso-marinated mackerel are staples of Japanese home cooking, prized for being inexpensive, fast to cook, and rich in omega-3s — mackerel's strong flavor pairs especially well with miso's funk and sweetness.

Method

  1. Whisk miso, mirin, sake, and sugar into a smooth marinade.
  2. Coat the mackerel fillets in the marinade and let sit at least 30 minutes (longer deepens the flavor, up to a few hours).
  3. Wipe most of the marinade off the fillets before cooking — miso burns easily, and too much left on the surface will scorch before the fish cooks through.
  4. Heat oil in a pan over medium heat and place the fillets skin-side down.
  5. Cook 3–4 minutes until the skin is crisp and the marinade caramelizes slightly at the edges.
  6. Flip and cook another 2–3 minutes until just cooked through.
  7. Garnish with green onion and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving too much marinade on the fish before cooking — it burns before the fish is done, leaving bitter dark spots.
  • Cooking over heat that's too high, since the sugar in the marinade scorches quickly; medium heat gives time to cook through without charring.

Authentic tips

  • A broiler works just as well as a stovetop pan — broil 4–5 minutes per side, watching closely near the end.
  • Mackerel's strong flavor stands up well to extra grated ginger in the marinade if you'd like to brighten it further.

Substitutions

  • Any oily fish — salmon, trout, sardines — works with this same miso marinade.
  • Red (aka) miso gives a bolder, saltier glaze if that's what's on hand instead of white miso.

Storage

Leftovers: Flake into rice with a little extra miso and green onion for a quick next-day lunch.

Freezing: Marinate and freeze raw for up to 2 months; thaw fully before cooking.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and pickles
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
