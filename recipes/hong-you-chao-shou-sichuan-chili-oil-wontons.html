Hóng Yóu Chāo Shǒu (Sichuan Chili Oil Wontons) 红油抄手

▾

Surprise me with

Hong you chao shou, wontons in a spicy red chili oil sauce, is a classic Sichuan snack; chao shou is the Sichuan word for wonton.

Method

  1. Mix the pork with the ginger, green onion, Shaoxing wine, filling soy sauce, salt, white pepper and sesame oil. Add 3 tablespoons of water a little at a time, stirring in one direction until the filling is sticky and holds the water. This makes juicy, springy wontons.
  2. Put a teaspoon of filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it into a triangle, then bring the two side corners together and press them to seal, so it looks like folded arms (chao shou).
  3. Mix the chili oil, sauce soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, garlic and Sichuan pepper and divide among four bowls.
  4. Boil the wontons in plenty of water for 3 to 4 minutes, adding a splash of cold water whenever it returns to a boil, until they float and the filling is cooked.
  5. Lift them out with a slotted spoon into the bowls, add a spoonful of the cooking water, and top with green onion. Toss gently to coat.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling the wontons makes them burst; use no more than a teaspoon.
  • Boiling them at a hard boil breaks the wrappers; keep it at a medium boil.

Authentic tips

  • Stir the filling in one direction so it turns bouncy and holds together.
  • The sauce keeps 1 week in the refrigerator.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or shrimp replaces pork.
  • Plain chili flakes in hot oil replace store-bought chili oil.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days in the refrigerator without the sauce; reheat in boiling water.

Freezing: Uncooked wontons freeze for 3 months; boil from frozen for 7 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cucumber salad
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
