Guō Bāo Niú Ròu (Dongbei Sweet-Sour Fried Beef) 锅包牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Guo bao rou, a Dongbei dish of crisp battered pork in a tangy sauce, is adapted to beef in some restaurants for a chewier texture.

Method

  1. Slice the beef into large, thin pieces about 5 mm (1/4 in) thick. Mix the potato starch with just enough water to make a thick, sticky paste (it should drip slowly from a spoon), and coat the slices in it.
  2. Mix the vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and a pinch of salt in a bowl.
  3. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) in a Dutch oven and fry the beef in batches for 2 to 3 minutes until pale gold. Drain. Raise the oil to 200 C (390 F) and fry everything again for 1 minute until crisp and golden. The double fry is what gives guo bao rou its shattering crust.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Fry the ginger, garlic and carrot over medium heat for 20 seconds, then pour in the sauce and bubble until it turns syrupy, about 30 seconds.
  5. Toss in the beef, green onion and cilantro for 10 seconds and serve at once. The dish was created in Harbin in the early 1900s for Russian guests, who liked it sweet and sour.

Common mistakes

  • Saucing early makes the crust soggy.
  • A thick batter on the beef makes it heavy.

Authentic tips

  • The sauce should be tangy; add a splash more vinegar if sweet.
  • Pork is the traditional version.

Substitutions

  • Pork tenderloin is the classic version.
  • Rice vinegar plus ketchup replaces Chinkiang vinegar.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crust softens within minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
