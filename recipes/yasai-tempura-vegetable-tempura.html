Yasai Tempura (Vegetable Tempura) 野菜天ぷら

▾

Surprise me with

Vegetable tempura follows the same Edo-period technique as shrimp tempura, and is a staple of soba shops and home cooking.

  • 150 g5 ozSweet potato, sliced 5 mm (1/4 in) thick
  • 11Japanese eggplant, sliced and fanned
  • 150 g5 ozKabocha squash, sliced 5 mm (1/4 in) thick
  • 44Shiitake mushrooms
  • 80 g3 ozGreen beans or shishito peppers
  • 44Shiso leaves (optional)
  • 100 g3/4 cupCake flour (or all-purpose with 10% cornstarch)
  • 11Egg, cold
  • 150 ml2/3 cupIce-cold water
  • 1 L4 cupsNeutral oil for frying
  • 120 ml1/2 cupKombu dashi, for the tentsuyu
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce, for the tentsuyu
  • 30 ml2 tbspMirin, for the tentsuyu
  • 60 g2 ozGrated daikon

Method

  1. Simmer the kombu dashi, soy sauce and mirin for 1 minute to make the tentsuyu. Cut the vegetables, pat them very dry and prick the shishito peppers so they do not burst.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven to 170 C (340 F) for the dense vegetables (sweet potato, kabocha) and 180 C (350 F) for the quick ones (eggplant, mushrooms, beans, shiso).
  3. Make the batter at the last moment: beat the egg with the ice water, add the flour all at once and stir just a few strokes with chopsticks, leaving it lumpy. Keep the bowl over ice.
  4. Dust each piece lightly with flour, dip it in the batter and slide it into the oil. Fry the sweet potato and kabocha for 3 to 4 minutes until tender, and the others for 1 to 2 minutes, until the batter is crisp and pale gold. Fry a few at a time so the oil stays hot.
  5. Skim out the loose batter bits between batches and drain the tempura on a rack.
  6. Serve at once with the tentsuyu and grated daikon, or a little salt.

Common mistakes

  • Overmixing makes the batter heavy.
  • Wet vegetables make the batter slide off.

Authentic tips

  • Fry in small batches.
  • Sweet potato and kabocha take a little longer.

Substitutions

  • Zucchini, shiso and mushrooms are good alternatives.
  • Sparkling water in the batter keeps it light.

Storage

Leftovers: Best within minutes; leftovers recrisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Hot soba or rice
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold beer
