Yú Piàn Zhōu (Fish Slice Congee) 鱼片粥

▾

Surprise me with

Fish slice congee is a Cantonese breakfast: thin slices of raw fish are cooked by ladling boiling congee over them in the bowl.

  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil, for the fish

Method

  1. Slice the fish on a sharp angle into broad pieces about 6 mm (1/4 in) thick. Mix it with the cornstarch, salt, pepper and water, then the egg white, then the oil, which keeps the slices from sticking together. The coating keeps them silky in the hot congee.
  2. Rinse the rice three times until the water runs nearly clear. Bring the water to a boil in a Dutch oven over high heat, add the rice and stir once.
  3. When it returns to a boil, add the rice oil, lower the heat, set the lid ajar and simmer for 25 to 30 minutes without stirring much, until the grains have burst.
  4. Whisk hard for 1 to 2 minutes to break the rice into a creamy, smooth congee, and thin with boiling water if it is thicker than heavy cream.
  5. Bring it back to a gentle bubble, add the fish slices one by one and stir very gently; they cook in about 1 minute and should stay in whole pieces. Stir in the ginger, salt and fish sauce and turn off the heat.
  6. Ladle into bowls, add a few drops of sesame oil and top with scallions, cilantro and youtiao.

Cook’s notes

Written for haddock fillet. Cantonese cooks use grass carp or dace; any mild white fish works.

Common mistakes

  • Congee that is not boiling hard leaves the fish raw in the middle.
  • Cutting the slices too thick means they cook unevenly.

Authentic tips

  • Freeze the fish for 20 minutes to slice it thin.
  • Century egg, chopped and stirred in for the last 10 minutes, is the other classic Cantonese pairing (pei daan yu pin juk).

Substitutions

  • Cod, tilapia or any firm white fish replaces the haddock.
  • Leftover cooked rice and extra water make a quicker version in 20 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Congee keeps 2 days in the refrigerator without the fish; thin with water when reheating.

Freezing: Freeze plain congee for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried dough sticks
  • Soup: The congee is the soup
  • Dessert: Soy milk
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
