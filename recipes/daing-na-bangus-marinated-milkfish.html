Daing na Bangus (Marinated Fried Milkfish)

▾

Surprise me with

Daing na bangus, milkfish butterflied and marinated in garlic and vinegar, is the fish of the Filipino "silog" breakfast, served as bangsilog with garlic rice and egg.

Method

  1. Lay the butterflied milkfish skin-side down in a dish and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Pour the vinegar over and scatter with the crushed garlic, then refrigerate, covered, overnight. The vinegar cures the fish lightly, the 'daing' style.
  3. Lift out the fish and pat it very dry; wet fish spatters fiercely. Keep a few of the garlic cloves.
  4. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the fish skin-side down first for 4 to 5 minutes, then flesh-side for 3 to 4 minutes, until golden and crisp at the edges. Fry the garlic alongside until golden.
  5. Serve with garlic rice, a fried egg, sliced tomatoes and spiced vinegar for a classic bangsilog breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Wet fish spatters.
  • Moving the fish too soon tears the skin.

Authentic tips

  • Bangsilog is bangus with garlic rice (sinangag) and egg (itlog).
  • Buy boneless to save time.

Substitutions

  • Tilapia or sea bass replace milkfish.
  • A nonstick pan helps the skin crisp.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes the marinated raw fish for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic rice, fried egg and tomato
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot coffee
