Kerala Pork Roast (Pork Ularthiyathu)

▾

Surprise me with

A Kerala Syrian Christian specialty, pork ularthiyathu slow-cooks pork with black pepper and coconut pieces until deeply dark and dry-roasted, traditionally a festive dish served at Christmas and family celebrations.

Method

  1. Combine pork with half the shallot, ginger, garlic, black pepper, coriander, and vinegar; marinate 20 minutes.
  2. Cook the marinated pork in a pot with a splash of water, covered, over medium heat 25 minutes until tender.
  3. Uncover and continue cooking until most of the liquid evaporates.
  4. In a separate pan, fry remaining shallot, ginger, garlic, and curry leaves in oil until golden.
  5. Add coconut pieces and fry until lightly browned, then add the cooked pork, stirring to coat and dry-roasting everything together 8–10 minutes until dark and glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the final dry-roasting stage, which is what develops this dish's characteristic dark color and concentrated flavor.
  • Using too little black pepper — this dish is meant to be assertively peppery, a defining Kerala flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The small pieces of fried coconut add texture and nutty sweetness that's central to this dish's character.
  • Patience during the dry-roasting stage is what separates a good ularthiyathu from an ordinary pork curry.

Substitutions

  • Dried coconut slivers fried in oil replace fresh coconut.
  • Beef is the common alternative in Kerala.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Parotta or steamed rice
  • Soup: Cucumber raita
  • Dessert: Payasam
  • Beverage: Black tea
