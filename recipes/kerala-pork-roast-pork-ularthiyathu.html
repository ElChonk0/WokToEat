Kerala Pork Roast (Pork Ularthiyathu)

▾

Surprise me with

A Kerala Syrian Christian specialty, pork ularthiyathu slow-cooks pork with black pepper and coconut pieces until deeply dark and dry-roasted, traditionally a festive dish served at Christmas and family celebrations.

Method

  1. Mix the Pork shoulder, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) chunks with the turmeric, chili, coriander, half the pepper, half the garam masala, the ginger, garlic, green chilies, vinegar and salt. Marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Pressure-cook the Pork shoulder, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) chunks with 60 ml (1/4 cup) water for 4 to 5 whistles, or simmer it covered in a Dutch oven over low heat for 1 hour, until tender and the liquid has almost gone. It cooks in its own juices.
  3. Heat the coconut oil in a wide, heavy skillet or cast-iron pan over medium heat and fry the coconut slivers for 3 to 4 minutes until golden. Add the shallots and curry leaves and fry for 8 to 10 minutes until the shallots are deep brown.
  4. Add the cooked Pork shoulder, cut into 2.5 cm (1 in) chunks with any remaining juices and the fennel. Ularthiyathu means 'dry-roasted': fry it over medium-low heat, stirring and scraping every few minutes, for 20 to 30 minutes, until the meat turns dark brown and the masala clings to it.
  5. Stir in the remaining pepper and garam masala for the last 5 minutes and add another sprig of curry leaves.
  6. Serve hot with Kerala parotta, appam or rice. Kerala Christians and Muslims cook this for feasts.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the final dry-roasting stage, which is what develops this dish's characteristic dark color and concentrated flavor.
  • Using too little black pepper — this dish is meant to be assertively peppery, a defining Kerala flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The small pieces of fried coconut add texture and nutty sweetness that's central to this dish's character.
  • Patience during the dry-roasting stage is what separates a good ularthiyathu from an ordinary pork curry.

Substitutions

  • Dried coconut slivers fried in oil replace fresh coconut.
  • Beef is the common alternative in Kerala.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
