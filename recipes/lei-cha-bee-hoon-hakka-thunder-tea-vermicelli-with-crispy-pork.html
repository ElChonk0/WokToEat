Lei Cha Bee Hoon (Hakka Thunder Tea Vermicelli with Crispy Pork) 擂茶米粉

Lei cha, Hakka thunder tea, is a herb-and-nut paste ground and mixed into a green broth; in Singapore it is served over rice or vermicelli with chopped vegetables and crispy pork.

  • Cook 30 min

  • 300 g11 ozRice vermicelli (bee hoon)
  • 300 g11 ozPork belly, in one piece
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 5 g1 tspSalt, for the pork
  • 10 g1/3 ozGreen tea leaves
  • 30 g1 ozMint leaves
  • 30 g1 ozThai basil leaves
  • 60 g1/2 cupRoasted peanuts
  • 20 g2 tbspWhite sesame seeds
  • 1 L4 cupsWater
  • 150 g5 ozLong beans, chopped
  • 150 g5 ozFirm tofu, diced
  • 150 g5 ozCabbage, shredded
  • 40 g1.5 ozPreserved radish, diced
  • 4 g3/4 tspSalt, for the broth

Method

  1. Boil the pork belly in water for 25 minutes until just tender, dry it completely, rub the skin with salt, and chill for 1 hour.
  2. Cut the pork into 1 cm cubes and fry in 170 C (340 F) oil for 5 to 6 minutes until the skin blisters and the pieces are golden. Drain.
  3. Pound or blend the tea leaves, mint, basil, peanuts and sesame seeds with 200 ml of the water into a rough green paste.
  4. Bring the remaining water to a boil, stir in the paste and the broth salt, and simmer gently for 5 minutes.
  5. Soak the vermicelli in hot water for 5 minutes, drain, and divide into bowls. Stir-fry the long beans, tofu, cabbage and radish in a little oil for 4 minutes and spoon over the noodles.
  6. Top with the crispy pork and pour the hot green broth over.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the paste hard turns it bitter; simmer gently.
  • Frying wet pork makes the oil spit; dry it well first.

Authentic tips

  • Grind the paste to a rough texture, not a smooth purée, for the traditional bite.
  • Serve the broth separately so the crispy pork stays crisp.

Substitutions

  • Roast pork replaces fried pork belly.
  • Mung beans or sunflower seeds replace some of the peanuts.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; store the broth and pork separately.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Chili-soy dip
  • Soup: The broth is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
