Cá Kho Tộ (Caramel Braised Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Cá kho tộ is a classic Vietnamese clay-pot braised fish, built on the same caramel-and-fish-sauce technique as thịt kho but suited to fish's faster cooking time — a staple of everyday Vietnamese family meals, traditionally cooked in an actual clay pot (tộ).

Method

  1. In a Dutch oven or heavy pot, melt the sugar over medium heat until deep amber, watching closely.
  2. Add the fish pieces carefully (the caramel will sizzle) and turn gently to coat.
  3. Add garlic, shallot, fish sauce, and black pepper.
  4. Pour in the coconut water and bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Reduce heat to low, cover, and braise 12–15 minutes, spooning the sauce over the fish occasionally rather than stirring, which keeps the pieces intact.
  6. Uncover for the last few minutes to let the sauce reduce and thicken to a glossy glaze.
  7. Garnish with green onion and chili; serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the fish vigorously during braising, which breaks it apart — spoon the sauce over instead of mixing.
  • Letting the caramel burn before adding the fish, which makes the whole dish bitter rather than pleasantly deep and sweet.

Authentic tips

  • A clay pot holds heat gently, but a Dutch oven does the same job on any stove, including induction, where an ordinary clay pot may crack.
  • Firmer fish (catfish, snapper, halibut) holds together through braising far better than delicate, flaky fish.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly can be braised the same way if you'd rather make thịt kho instead.
  • Regular water with an extra pinch of sugar works if coconut water isn't available.

Storage

Leftovers: The flavor deepens overnight; reheat gently to avoid breaking up the fish further.

Freezing: Freezes reasonably well; the texture softens slightly but the flavor holds up.
