Huá Dàn Niú Ròu Fàn (Slippery Egg and Beef Rice) 滑蛋牛肉饭

▾

Surprise me with

Hua dan niu rou, soft scrambled egg over seared beef on rice, is a Hong Kong cha chaan teng classic eaten at breakfast and lunch.

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the marinade oyster sauce, wine, sugar, cornstarch, baking soda and water and leave for 15 minutes. The baking soda keeps the thin slices soft.
  2. Heat a flat-bottom wok or skillet over high heat with the oil. Sear the beef in one layer for 30 to 45 seconds, turning once, just until it loses its pink color. Lift it out; it finishes cooking in the gravy.
  3. Lower the heat to medium and fry the garlic for 20 seconds. Pour in the gravy wine, scraping up the browned bits, then add the stock, oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar and bring to a simmer.
  4. Stir the slurry and add it gradually, stirring, until the gravy is thick, glossy and smooth. It needs to be thick before the eggs go in, or they scramble into lumps rather than silky ribbons.
  5. Turn the heat to low. Drizzle in the eggs in a thin stream, leave them for 10 seconds to set, then stir gently in large strokes so they form soft, slippery ribbons. Stop while the egg is still a little runny.
  6. Return the beef and its juices, stir once, add the sesame oil and green onion and turn off the heat. Spoon over hot rice and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the eggs until firm makes the dish dry; they finish cooking on the hot rice.
  • Marinating the beef too long in soy sauce makes it salty and dark.

Authentic tips

  • Stir a tablespoon of water into the eggs for extra softness.
  • Serve immediately; every minute of waiting firms the eggs.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or shrimp work with the same silky egg.
  • Add blanched choy sum on the side to round it out.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the egg sauce turns rubbery when reheated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the rice is included
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Hong Kong milk pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
