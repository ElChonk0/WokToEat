Hunan Smoked Chicken Thigh Stir-Fry 烟熏鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Smoked and cured meats are a Hunan signature, born from a mountainous region where preservation by smoking was traditional; this dish approximates that smoky depth with a quick stovetop char before stir-frying.

Method

  1. Toss sliced chicken with smoked paprika and half the soy sauce; let sit 10 minutes.
  2. Heat a dry wok or cast-iron pan very hot and sear the chicken without oil until lightly charred at the edges, 3–4 minutes; remove.
  3. Add oil to the pan, then stir-fry garlic and red chili until fragrant.
  4. Add celery and stir-fry 1–2 minutes until just tender-crisp.
  5. Return chicken to the pan with remaining soy sauce, wine, and sugar; toss to combine over high heat.
  6. Serve hot immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the dry char step, which is what stands in for the traditional smoking process and adds depth.
  • Overcrowding the pan during searing, which steams the chicken instead of charring it.

Authentic tips

  • A well-seasoned cast-iron pan gets closest to the smoky char this dish is named for.
  • Smoked paprika is a reasonable modern shortcut for the traditional smoking process.

Substitutions

  • Bell pepper can replace celery for a sweeter, less peppery crunch.
  • A drop of liquid smoke in the marinade intensifies the smoky character if desired.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended — the charred texture doesn't hold up.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh orange
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
