Táng Cù Lǐ Jǐ (Sweet and Sour Pork Tenderloin) 糖醋里脊

▾

Surprise me with

Tang cu li ji, crisp strips of pork tenderloin in a sweet-sour sauce, is a Shandong and Beijing banquet dish and a favorite among children.

Method

  1. Cut the pork into 1 cm strips and toss with a pinch of salt. Beat the egg with the cornstarch and 3 tablespoons of water into a thick batter and coat the pork.
  2. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry in batches for 3 minutes until pale gold; drain.
  3. Raise the heat to 190 C (375 F) and refry for 30 seconds until crisp.
  4. Simmer the sugar, vinegar, ketchup, garlic and ginger with 60 ml water for 3 minutes until syrupy.
  5. Toss the pork in the sauce and serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Saucing early makes the crust soggy.
  • Frying once leaves it soft; the second fry gives crunch.

Authentic tips

  • Cut the strips evenly so they cook in the same time.
  • Serve immediately for the crispest texture.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast strips take the same batter.
  • Rice vinegar replaces Chinkiang for a lighter flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crust softens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
