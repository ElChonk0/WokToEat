Omurice (Japanese Omelet Rice) オムライス

Omurice, ketchup-seasoned chicken fried rice wrapped in or topped with a soft omelet, is a yoshoku (Western-style) dish that appeared in Tokyo and Osaka restaurants in the early 1900s.

Method

  1. Heat the oil and half the butter in a large skillet over medium-high heat and cook the chicken for 4 minutes until browned, then the onion and mushrooms for 3 minutes.
  2. Add the ketchup and cook 1 minute until it darkens slightly, then add the rice, soy sauce, salt and pepper and toss until evenly red and hot. Divide among 4 plates and shape into ovals.
  3. For each omelet, beat 2 eggs with 1 tablespoon of milk and a pinch of salt. Melt a little butter in a 20 cm (8 in) non-stick pan over medium heat.
  4. Pour in the eggs and stir quickly with chopsticks for 20 to 30 seconds until softly set but still wet on top, then slide or fold the omelet over a mound of rice.
  5. Repeat with the rest and finish each with a line of ketchup.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the egg until firm loses the soft, custardy texture.
  • A pan that is too hot browns the omelet; it should stay pale yellow.

Authentic tips

  • For the split omelet style, roll it into a firm-outside, runny-inside log, lay it on the rice and slit it open.
  • Demi-glace sauce is the classic alternative to ketchup on top.

Substitutions

  • Ham or shrimp replace chicken.
  • Japanese curry sauce makes omu-curry.

Storage

Leftovers: The rice keeps 3 days; make the omelet fresh.

Freezing: The ketchup rice freezes for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Green salad with sesame dressing
  • Soup: Corn potage or miso soup
  • Dessert: Purin (custard pudding)
  • Beverage: Iced barley tea
