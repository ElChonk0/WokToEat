Wat Daan Ha Yan Ho (Shrimp Chow Fun with Silky Egg Gravy) 滑蛋虾仁河

▾

Surprise me with

Seared rice noodles under a silky egg and shrimp gravy is a Hong Kong cha chaan teng and dai pai dong favorite.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 600 g1.3 lbFresh wide rice noodles (ho fun)
  • 300 g10 ozShrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 g1/4 tspSalt, for the shrimp
  • 5 g2 tspCornstarch, for the shrimp
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 10 ml2 tspDark soy sauce
  • 10 ml2 tspLight soy sauce
  • 400 ml1 2/3 cupsChicken stock
  • 15 ml1 tbspOyster sauce
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt, for the gravy
  • 2 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 0.5 g1/4 tspGround white pepper
  • 25 g2.5 tbspCornstarch, for the gravy
  • 45 ml3 tbspWater, for the slurry
  • 33Eggs, beaten
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, chopped

Method

  1. Toss the shrimp with the salt and cornstarch. Peel the noodles apart into single strands; if they are cold and stiff, microwave them covered for 1 minute first so they do not break. Mix the gravy cornstarch with its water.
  2. Heat a dry flat-bottom wok over high heat, add 1 tablespoon of oil, then lower to medium. Spread the noodles out and fry undisturbed for about 1 minute until lightly charred, turn and fry 30 to 40 seconds more. Add the soy sauces and toss gently until evenly brown, then spread on a deep platter.
  3. Add the remaining oil and stir-fry the shrimp over high heat for 60 to 90 seconds, until pink and just opaque. Lift them out.
  4. Pour in the stock with the oyster sauce, salt, sugar and pepper and bring to a boil. Stir the slurry and add it gradually until the gravy is thick and glossy, then return the shrimp.
  5. Turn the heat to low and drizzle in the eggs in a thin stream. Leave for 10 seconds, then stir gently in big strokes so they form silky ribbons. The gravy must be thick and only just simmering, or the egg turns lumpy.
  6. Pour the egg gravy over the noodles, scatter with green onion and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the eggs over high heat curdles them into lumps.
  • A thin gravy makes the noodles soggy.

Authentic tips

  • Turning the heat off before the egg goes in is the key to the "wat" (silky) texture.
  • Use the same gravy with beef for wat daan ngau ho.

Substitutions

  • Beef or char siu replace shrimp.
  • Dried wide rice noodles, soaked.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled vegetables
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Mango pudding
  • Beverage: Hong Kong milk tea
