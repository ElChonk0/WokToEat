Miso Ramen (Sapporo-Style Miso Ramen) 味噌ラーメン

▾

Surprise me with

Miso ramen was created in Sapporo in the 1950s, at the Aji no Sanpei shop; the rich, wok-fried miso broth with corn and butter became the signature of Hokkaido.

Method

  1. Mix the miso, sesame paste, soy sauce and sugar.
  2. Heat the oil in a pot over high heat and fry the pork 3 minutes, then the garlic and ginger, then the sprouts and cabbage for 2 minutes until lightly charred.
  3. Add the stock and bring to a boil, then lower the heat and whisk in the miso mixture; keep it just below a boil.
  4. Boil the noodles according to the packet and drain well.
  5. Divide the noodles into bowls, ladle the broth and vegetables over, and top with corn, a pat of butter, a halved egg and green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the broth after adding miso dulls its aroma.
  • Stir-frying the vegetables over low heat loses the smoky Sapporo flavor.

Authentic tips

  • The wok-frying of pork and vegetables before adding stock is the Sapporo technique.
  • Add a spoon of chili bean paste for spicy miso ramen.

Substitutions

  • Thick curly noodles are traditional; any ramen noodle works.
  • Chashu slices replace ground pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Store broth separately for 3 days; cook noodles fresh.

Freezing: The broth freezes for 2 months.
