Niú Ròu Jiā Mó (Braised Beef in Flatbread) 牛肉夹馍

▾

Surprise me with

Meat tucked into a flatbread (mo) is a staple of north-central China, and Anhui's northern plains share the tradition with beef braised in a spiced sauce.

  • 40 g1.5 ozCilantro and green onion, chopped

Method

  1. Blanch the beef, then simmer it in the water with the aromatics, spices, soy sauces and salt for 2 hours, until tender. Keep 120 ml (1/2 cup) of the braising liquid.
  2. Cool the beef slightly and chop it finely with a cleaver.
  3. Toss the chopped beef with the cumin, chili, green pepper and herbs and moisten it with the braising liquid so it is juicy.
  4. Warm the flatbreads in a dry skillet until crisp outside and soft inside, then split them open.
  5. Stuff generously with the beef and eat hot. Bozhou, in northern Anhui, shares this style with neighboring Henan.

Common mistakes

  • Dry beef makes a dry sandwich; moisten with plenty of braising liquid.
  • Cold bread cools the filling; warm it.

Authentic tips

  • Chop the beef by hand for good texture.
  • Serve with a bowl of soup.

Substitutions

  • Brisket replaces shank.
  • Store-bought flatbreads or pita replace homemade mo.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; reheat the meat and warm the bread fresh.

Freezing: Freezes the braised beef for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Not needed; the sandwich is the meal
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
