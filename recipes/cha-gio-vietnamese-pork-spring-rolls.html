Cha Gio (Vietnamese Pork Spring Rolls)

▾

Surprise me with

Crispy fried spring rolls filled with pork, shrimp, and vegetables, cha gio (called nem in northern Vietnam) are a party and celebration staple, traditionally wrapped in rice paper and fried until shatteringly crisp.

  • Cook 15 min

Method

  1. Combine ground pork, shrimp, carrot, wood ear mushroom, glass noodles, shallot, fish sauce, and egg; mix until well combined.
  2. Dampen a rice paper wrapper briefly, place a spoonful of filling near one edge, and roll tightly, folding in the sides.
  3. Repeat with remaining filling and wrappers.
  4. Heat oil to 165°C (330°F) and fry rolls in batches, turning occasionally, 8–10 minutes until deeply golden and crisp.
  5. Drain and serve with a dipping sauce of fish sauce, lime, sugar, and chili.

Common mistakes

  • Frying at too high a temperature, which browns the wrapper before the filling cooks through.
  • Over-soaking the rice paper, which makes it too fragile to roll tightly.

Authentic tips

  • Frying at a lower, steadier temperature for longer gives the crispest, least greasy result.
  • These rolls freeze exceptionally well uncooked, making them a great make-ahead party food.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken can replace pork for a lighter filling.
  • Rice paper can be swapped for spring roll pastry wrappers, which fry up even crispier.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1–2 days but loses crispness.

Freezing: Freezes excellently uncooked for up to 3 months; fry directly from frozen with a couple extra minutes.

Serve with
