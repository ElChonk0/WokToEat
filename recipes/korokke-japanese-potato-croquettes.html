Korokke (Japanese Potato and Beef Croquettes) コロッケ

▾

Surprise me with

Korokke, panko-crusted croquettes of mashed potato and ground beef, came from the French croquette in the Meiji era and became one of Japan's most popular butcher-shop and home foods.

Method

  1. Boil the whole potatoes in salted water for 20 to 25 minutes until a skewer slides in, drain, return to the pot over low heat for a minute to dry, then mash. Dry mash is what keeps korokke from bursting in the oil.
  2. Heat the oil and butter in a skillet over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 minutes until soft, then add the beef and cook for 4 to 5 minutes until browned. Season with salt, pepper, sugar and soy sauce.
  3. Mix the beef into the potatoes, cool completely and shape into 8 to 10 oval patties about 2 cm (3/4 in) thick. Chill for 30 minutes so they hold their shape.
  4. Coat each patty in flour, then beaten egg, then panko, pressing it on firmly.
  5. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) in a Dutch oven and fry 3 or 4 at a time for 2 to 3 minutes, turning once, until deep golden. The filling is already cooked, so you are only crisping and heating it.
  6. Drain on a rack and serve hot with tonkatsu sauce and shredded cabbage.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Shaping warm filling makes the croquettes burst in the oil.
  • Wet mash: dry the boiled potatoes in the pot for a minute before mashing.

Authentic tips

  • Chilling the shaped croquettes helps them hold together.
  • Oven: spray with oil and bake at 220 C (425 F) for 20 minutes, using toasted panko.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork replaces beef.
  • Add corn or cream (cream korokke) variations.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days; re-crisp in a hot oven.

Freezing: Freeze breaded, uncooked croquettes for 2 months; fry from frozen at 170 C.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Shredded cabbage and rice
  • Soup: Miso soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
