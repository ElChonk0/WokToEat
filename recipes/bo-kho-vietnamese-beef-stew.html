Bo Kho (Vietnamese Beef Stew)

▾

Surprise me with

A French-influenced Vietnamese beef stew flavored with lemongrass, star anise, and a touch of tomato, bo kho is traditionally eaten with crusty bread or rice noodles, often for breakfast.

Method

  1. Marinate the beef with the marinade fish sauce, sugar, five-spice, ginger, half the garlic and half the shallots for at least 1 hour.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat and sear the beef in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until browned. Lift it out.
  3. Fry the remaining garlic and shallots, the lemongrass, star anise and cinnamon for 1 minute, then the tomato paste for 2 minutes until it darkens to brick red.
  4. Return the beef with the coconut water and stock, bring to a boil, skim, then cover and simmer over low heat for 2 hours, until the beef is nearly tender. Coconut water gives bo kho its gentle sweetness.
  5. Add the carrots and simmer uncovered for 30 minutes until tender and the sauce has thickened. Season with fish sauce.
  6. Remove the lemongrass and spices. Serve with Thai basil, cilantro and onion, with baguette for dipping (a breakfast favorite) or over rice noodles.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the braise, since chuck or shank needs real time to become properly tender.
  • Skipping the lemongrass, which is a key aromatic that distinguishes this stew from more generic beef braises.

Authentic tips

  • This stew, like most braises, tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have melded.
  • Serving with a crusty baguette for dipping reflects Vietnam's French colonial culinary influence.

Substitutions

  • Short rib replaces chuck.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the stew to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Baguette or rice noodles
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Vietnamese iced coffee
