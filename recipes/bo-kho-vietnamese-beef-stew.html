Bo Kho (Vietnamese Beef Stew)

A French-influenced Vietnamese beef stew flavored with lemongrass, star anise, and a touch of tomato, bo kho is traditionally eaten with crusty bread or rice noodles, often for breakfast.

  • Cook 1.5 hr

Method

  1. Blanch beef in boiling water 3 minutes; drain.
  2. Fry garlic and shallot in oil until fragrant, then add tomato paste, cooking 2 minutes.
  3. Add beef, lemongrass, and star anise, stirring to coat.
  4. Add fish sauce and stock; bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and braise 1 to 1.25 hours.
  5. Add carrot and continue simmering 20–25 minutes until the beef and carrot are tender.
  6. Serve with crusty baguette for dipping or over rice noodles.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the braise, since chuck or shank needs real time to become properly tender.
  • Skipping the lemongrass, which is a key aromatic that distinguishes this stew from more generic beef braises.

Authentic tips

  • This stew, like most braises, tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have melded.
  • Serving with a crusty baguette for dipping reflects Vietnam's French colonial culinary influence.

Substitutions

  • Short rib replaces chuck.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the stew to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Baguette or rice noodles
  • Soup: Not needed; the stew is its own soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Vietnamese iced coffee
