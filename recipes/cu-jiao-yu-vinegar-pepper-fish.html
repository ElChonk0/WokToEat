Cù Jiāo Yú (Vinegar-Pepper Fish Soup) 醋椒鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Cu jiao yu, fish simmered in a sour, peppery broth, is an old Beijing and Shandong-influenced dish that pairs white pepper with plenty of vinegar for a warming, appetizing soup.

  • Cook 20 min

  • 900 g2 lbWhole crucian carp or grass carp, cleaned and scored
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt, for the fish
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 40 g1.5 ozGinger, sliced, and green onion
  • 30 ml2 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 1.2 L5 cupsBoiling water
  • 5 g1 tspSalt, for the soup
  • 3 g1.5 tspWhite pepper, ground
  • 60 ml1/4 cupChinkiang or rice vinegar
  • 30 g1 ozCilantro, chopped
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil

Method

  1. Rub the fish with salt and pat dry. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium-high heat and fry the fish for 2 minutes per side until lightly golden.
  2. Add the ginger and green onion, pour the Shaoxing wine around the edge, then pour in the boiling water.
  3. Boil hard for 10 to 12 minutes; pouring boiling water onto seared fish and boiling vigorously turns the broth milky white.
  4. Season with salt, then stir in the white pepper and vinegar off the heat. The soup should be peppery-hot and sharply sour, a northern remedy for colds.
  5. Scatter with cilantro and a few drops of sesame oil and serve the fish in its soup.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the vinegar too early dulls its aroma.
  • Boiling hard breaks the fish.

Authentic tips

  • The pepper should be noticeable but not harsh; add more to taste.
  • Boiling water, added to the hot pan, gives a milky broth.

Substitutions

  • Cod or sea bass replace the carp.
  • Fresh coriander is traditional; add generously.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day refrigerated; reheat gently.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
