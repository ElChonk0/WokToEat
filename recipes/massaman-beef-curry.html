Massaman Beef Curry

▾

Surprise me with

Massaman curry blends Thai technique with spices introduced through historical trade with Persian and Indian merchants; the beef version is especially popular for long, slow braises that tenderize tougher cuts.

Method

  1. Simmer the beef in one can of coconut milk with 250 ml (1 cup) water, the cinnamon, cardamom and bay leaves in a covered Dutch oven over low heat for 1.5 to 2 hours, until fork-tender.
  2. Meanwhile, heat the thick cream from the second can in a skillet over medium heat and simmer, stirring, for 3 to 5 minutes, until it thickens and the oil starts to separate ('cracking'). Fry the curry paste in it for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring constantly, until deeply fragrant and the oil turns red. Frying the paste in separated coconut fat is what develops a Thai curry's flavor.
  3. Scrape the fried paste into the beef with the rest of the coconut milk, the potatoes, onion and peanuts. Simmer uncovered for 20 to 25 minutes until the potatoes are tender.
  4. Season with fish sauce, palm sugar and tamarind, adjusting until the curry tastes sweet, salty and gently sour in balance. Massaman, shaped by Persian and Indian trade, is the mildest and sweetest Thai curry.
  5. Rest for 15 minutes before serving with jasmine rice; it tastes better still the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial beef braise, since chuck needs real time to become properly tender before the vegetables are added.
  • Skipping the tamarind, which provides the subtle sourness that balances the dish's richness.

Authentic tips

  • Massaman is meant to be milder and sweeter than other Thai curries — resist adding extra chili if aiming for authenticity.
  • This curry, like many braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Short rib adds richness.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the simmer to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with
