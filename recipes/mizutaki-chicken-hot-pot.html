Mizutaki (Chicken Hot Pot) 水炊き

▾

Surprise me with

Mizutaki, chicken simmered with vegetables in a plain broth and dipped in ponzu, is a Fukuoka specialty and a popular winter hot pot across Japan.

Method

  1. Soak the kombu in the water for at least 30 minutes. Blanch the bone-in chicken in boiling water for 1 minute, then rinse it in lukewarm water; cold water would set the fat on it.
  2. Add the bone-in and boneless chicken, sake and ginger to the kombu water in a Dutch oven or donabe and bring to a boil over medium heat, skimming off the foam. Remove the kombu just before it boils.
  3. Cover and simmer over medium-low heat for 30 minutes, until the broth turns rich and slightly cloudy. Discard the ginger. The broth is left unseasoned; the ponzu seasons each bite.
  4. At the table, on a portable burner if you have one, add the cabbage, tofu, mushrooms, leeks and carrot and cook, covered, for 8 to 10 minutes. Add the mizuna last, just to wilt.
  5. Ladle chicken, vegetables and broth into small bowls and dip them in ponzu mixed with grated daikon, green onion and yuzu kosho.
  6. To finish, add the rice to the remaining broth, season with a pinch of salt, simmer for 3 minutes and stir in beaten eggs for a comforting zosui.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the broth cloudy and the chicken stringy.
  • Leaving the kombu in the boiling water makes the broth slimy; remove it when the pot boils.

Authentic tips

  • Fukuoka's version uses a long-simmered milky broth.
  • Season the leftover broth with salt and cook rice in it.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in thighs and drumsticks give the best broth.
  • Bottled ponzu with a squeeze of fresh citrus replaces homemade.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; the broth becomes rice porridge (zosui).

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Cooked rice or udon for the final broth
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Warm sake or sencha
