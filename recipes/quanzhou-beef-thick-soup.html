Quanzhou Beef Thick Soup 牛肉羹

▾

Surprise me with

Beef is rare in most Fujian cooking, but Quanzhou has a long tradition of beef shops, and niu rou geng is their staple: starch-coated slivers of beef poached in a peppery broth until silky, finished with black vinegar.

  • Prep 20 min (plus 20 min marinating)
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • to tasteto tasteCelery leaves or cilantro, and fried shallots

Method

  1. Mix the beef with fish sauce, soy, sugar, baking soda and 2 tbsp water; rest 20 minutes.
  2. Add the sweet potato starch and mix hard until each strip is coated in a thick, sticky paste.
  3. Bring the stock and ginger to a gentle simmer.
  4. Drop the beef in, a few strips at a time, and simmer 3–4 minutes until they float and turn glossy.
  5. Thicken the broth lightly with 1 tbsp starch stirred into water; season with white pepper and vinegar.
  6. Serve topped with celery leaves and fried shallots.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the coated beef all at once, so it clumps into one mass; drop it in strip by strip.

Authentic tips

  • Sweet potato starch gives the slippery, slightly chewy coating that defines this soup; cornstarch is softer.

Substitutions

  • Pork replaces beef for a traditional variation.
  • Cornstarch replaces sweet potato starch.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the starch thickens more when cold.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or noodles
  • Soup: Not needed; this is a thick soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
