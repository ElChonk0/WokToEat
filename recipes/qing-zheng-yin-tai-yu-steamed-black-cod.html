Qing Zheng Yin Tai Yu (Steamed Black Cod) 清蒸银鳕鱼

Cantonese steaming with ginger, scallion and hot oil is the standard way to cook very fresh fish; black cod, sold in Hong Kong as silver cod, suits it because its richness stays moist.

  • Cook 10 min

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozBlack cod fillets, about 150 g each
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 2 g1/3 tspSalt
  • 30 g1 ozGinger, cut in fine strips
  • 40 g1.5 ozScallions, cut in fine strips
  • 45 ml3 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 30 ml2 tbspWater
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil
  • 10 g1/3 ozCilantro
  • 1 g1/4 tspWhite pepper

Method

  1. Pat the fillets dry and rub them with the wine, salt and white pepper; lay them skin-side down on a heatproof plate on a rack above boiling water.
  2. Cover and steam over high heat for 8 to 10 minutes until the flesh is opaque and flakes at the thickest part.
  3. Tip away the liquid on the plate and scatter half the ginger and scallion over the fish.
  4. Warm the soy sauce, sugar and water together in a small pan until the sugar dissolves and pour around the fish.
  5. Heat the oil in a small pan until it just smokes, pour it over the ginger and scallions so they sizzle, then add the cilantro and serve at once.

Cook’s notes

Written for black cod fillet (sablefish). Cantonese restaurants sell it as silver cod.

Common mistakes

  • Over-steaming dries the fish; start checking at 7 minutes.
  • Leaving the steaming liquid on the plate makes the dish taste fishy.

Authentic tips

  • Water must be at a full boil before the fish goes in.
  • Pour the oil while it is hot enough to hiss.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin or Chilean sea bass replaces the black cod.
  • Add 1 tablespoon fermented black beans for a stronger flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator; reheat by steaming 2 minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched choy sum
  • Soup: Pork and watercress soup
  • Dessert: Tong sui sweet soup
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
