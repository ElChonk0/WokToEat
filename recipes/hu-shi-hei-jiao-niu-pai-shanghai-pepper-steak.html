Hù Shì Hēi Jiāo Niú Pái (Shanghai Pepper Steak) 沪式黑椒牛排

▾

Surprise me with

Shanghai's Western-style restaurants adapted steak with a black pepper sauce to local tastes in the 20th century, giving rise to a sizzling-plate steak popular today.

  • 600 g1.3 lbBeef sirloin steaks
  • 6 g2 tspBlack pepper, crushed
  • 30 ml2 tbspSoy sauce
  • 30 g2 tbspButter
  • 100 g3.5 ozOnion
  • 120 ml1/2 cupStock
  • 15 ml1 tbspCornstarch slurry

Method

  1. Pat the steaks dry, season with salt and half the crushed black pepper and let come to room temperature for 20 minutes.
  2. Heat a heavy pan over high heat with a little oil and sear the steaks 3 minutes per side for medium-rare; rest on a plate for 5 minutes.
  3. In the same pan, melt half the butter, fry the onion for 2 minutes and the remaining pepper for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the soy sauce and stock and bubble 2 minutes, then stir in the slurry and remaining butter until glossy.
  5. Slice the steak, return it to a hot plate or serve whole, and spoon the sauce over.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the rest makes the juices run out.
  • Burning the pepper makes the sauce bitter.

Authentic tips

  • For the sizzling effect, serve on a preheated cast-iron plate.
  • Crush the peppercorns coarsely in a mortar.

Substitutions

  • Ribeye or strip steak replaces the sirloin.
  • Coarsely crushed pepper gives more bite than ground.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; reheat gently so the steak doesn't overcook.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fries or rice
  • Soup: Russian-style borscht
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot black tea
