Dāng Guī Yā (Dang Gui Duck Soup) 当归鸭

▾

Surprise me with

Dang gui duck is a Chinese herbal soup of duck simmered with angelica root, goji berries and red dates, found at herbal stalls and restaurants in Malaysia and Singapore.

Method

  1. Trim the excess fat and skin from the duck, then blanch the pieces in boiling water for 5 minutes. Drain and rinse. This removes the scum and much of the fat, so the soup stays clear and light.
  2. Rinse the dang gui, dang shen and red dates quickly in cold water. Dang gui is strong and slightly bitter, so use it sparingly.
  3. Put the duck, herbs, red dates, ginger and water in a large pot and bring to a boil, then skim and turn down to a gentle simmer.
  4. Simmer, partly covered, for 1.5 to 2 hours, until the duck is very tender and the broth is dark and fragrant. Skim off the fat as it rises.
  5. Add the goji berries and wine for the last 10 minutes, season with the salt and serve the soup with the duck, often alongside mee sua or rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Too much dang gui makes the soup bitter; keep to 25 g.
  • Boiling hard clouds the broth; keep it at a bare simmer.

Authentic tips

  • Chill and skim the fat for a cleaner soup.
  • A small spoon of rice wine at serving brightens the aroma.

Substitutions

  • Chicken replaces duck.
  • Skip the codonopsis if you cannot find it.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched greens
  • Soup: The dish is a soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot water or ginger tea
