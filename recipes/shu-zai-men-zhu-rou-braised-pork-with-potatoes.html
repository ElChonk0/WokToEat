Shǔ Zǎi Mèn Zhū Ròu (Cantonese Braised Pork with Potatoes) 薯仔焖猪肉

▾

Surprise me with

Pork belly or shoulder braised with potatoes in soy, oyster sauce and a little fermented bean curd is a thrifty everyday Cantonese family dish.

Method

  1. Keep the cut potatoes in cold water until needed, then drain and dry them well. Pat the pork dry and keep the pieces at least 3 cm (1.25 in) thick so they do not fall apart in the braise.
  2. Heat a Dutch oven over high heat with 1 teaspoon of the oil and fry the onion for 30 seconds, then lift it out. Add another teaspoon of oil and the potatoes and fry for 2 to 3 minutes until golden in spots; they only need color now.
  3. Add the last of the oil and the pork. Sear without moving for 1 minute per side, until browned and some fat has rendered, then add the ginger and garlic for 20 seconds.
  4. Turn the heat down to low and stir in the bean sauce, bean curd, wine, oyster sauce and soy sauces, then add the sugar and cook for 30 seconds until it smells caramelized. Taking the heat down stops the sauces scorching.
  5. Pour in enough boiling water to nearly cover, bring to a boil, cover and simmer over medium-low heat for 40 minutes, until the pork is tender.
  6. Add the potatoes and carrot, cover and cook for 15 to 20 minutes, until a chopstick slides easily into a potato. Taste and add the salt. The potatoes soak up liquid, so add a splash of water if it gets too dry; the sauce should be thick and glossy, not thin.
  7. Turn off the heat and stir in the onion, sesame oil and green onion. Serve over rice.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the potatoes at the start turns them to mush.
  • Skipping the blanch leaves a cloudy, scummy sauce.

Authentic tips

  • Red fermented bean curd gives the reddish color and a savory, almost cheesy depth.
  • Pork belly makes it richer; shoulder keeps it meaty.

Substitutions

  • Taro replaces the potatoes for a more festive version.
  • No nam yu: add 1 extra tablespoon of oyster sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months; potatoes soften slightly.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and garlic bok choy
  • Soup: Spinach tofu soup
  • Dessert: Orange segments
  • Beverage: Hot tea
