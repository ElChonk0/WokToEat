Gān Shāo Yú (Dry-Braised Fish) 干烧鱼

Gan shao, or "dry-braise," is a Sichuan technique that simmers the main ingredient in a seasoned sauce until it reduces to a thick glaze; gan shao yu is a home and banquet favorite built on doubanjiang, fermented rice and minced pork.

  • Cook 25 min

  • 900 g2 lbWhole carp or sea bass, cleaned and scored
  • 100 g3.5 ozMinced pork
  • 45 g3 tbspPixian doubanjiang
  • 30 g2 tbspFermented rice (jiuniang)
  • 50 g4 tbspGinger, garlic and scallion, minced
  • 350 ml1.5 cupsStock
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspChinkiang vinegar
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Pat the fish dry and pan-fry it in oil for 2 minutes per side until lightly golden; set aside.
  2. Fry the pork until browned, then add the doubanjiang, ginger and garlic and stir until the oil turns red.
  3. Add the fermented rice, stock, sugar and fish; simmer for 10 minutes, spooning the sauce over.
  4. Turn the fish once, then reduce the sauce until thick and clinging.
  5. Finish with vinegar and scallion and transfer to a platter.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the fish too much, which tears the skin.
  • Reducing the sauce too far and burning it; keep the heat moderate near the end.

Authentic tips

  • The sauce should be rich, savory, slightly sweet and only lightly saucy.

Substitutions

  • Two whole trout or six thick cod fillets can replace the whole fish.
  • One tablespoon of rice wine plus 1 teaspoon sugar replaces the fermented rice.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently in the sauce.

Freezing: Not recommended for whole fish.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
