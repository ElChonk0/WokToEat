Chicken Adobo

▾

Surprise me with

Adobo predates Spanish colonization as an indigenous Filipino preservation technique using vinegar and salt, later incorporating soy sauce through Chinese trading influence — it's widely considered the unofficial national dish of the Philippines.

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken with the soy sauce, half the garlic, bay leaves and peppercorns for at least 1 hour.
  2. Put the chicken and marinade in a Dutch oven with the vinegar and water. Bring to a boil and let it bubble for 2 to 3 minutes without stirring; this cooks off the sharpness of the vinegar, which is why Filipino cooks say not to stir adobo after adding vinegar.
  3. Cover and simmer over low heat for 30 minutes, turning the chicken once, until tender.
  4. Lift out the chicken and pat the skin dry. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and fry the chicken and the remaining garlic, skin-side down, for 4 to 5 minutes until browned and crisp.
  5. Meanwhile reduce the braising liquid by half with the sugar, until slightly syrupy.
  6. Return the chicken to the sauce, turn to coat and serve with plenty of rice. Adobo tastes even better the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring or disturbing the vinegar too early in cooking — simmering it uncovered for a bit cooks off its harshness; doing this too vigorously before that happens leaves a sharp, raw taste.
  • Using too little vinegar out of caution — adobo is meant to taste distinctly tangy; the long simmer mellows it considerably from how it smells at the start.

Authentic tips

  • Letting adobo sit overnight and reheating the next day is traditional and genuinely improves the flavor — many households consider day-old adobo the better version.
  • Cane vinegar (sukang maasim) gives a more traditional flavor than plain white vinegar, if available.

Substitutions

  • Pork belly or pork shoulder makes pork adobo with the same method (see this week's Friday recipe).
  • Coconut milk can be stirred in at the end for a richer adobo sa gata variation.

Storage

Leftovers: Improves with a day in the fridge; shred extra chicken for adobo fried rice.

Freezing: Freezes excellently for up to 3 months; the vinegar-based sauce holds up well.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
