Bakso (Indonesian Beef Meatball Soup)

▾

Surprise me with

Bakso, bouncy beef meatballs in a clear broth with noodles, is Indonesia's most popular street food, sold from carts across the country.

Method

  1. Pulse the cold beef with the crushed ice in a food processor until it forms a smooth, sticky paste; keeping it ice-cold is what makes bakso springy. Add the tapioca starch, ground fried garlic and shallots, salt, pepper, sugar, baking powder and egg white and process until fully combined.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to just below a simmer, about 80 C (175 F). With oiled hands, squeeze the paste through your fist between thumb and forefinger to form balls the size of a walnut, scooping each one into the water with a spoon.
  3. Poach the meatballs for 10 minutes, until they float and are cooked through. Keep the water below a boil; boiling makes them rough and cracked. Lift them out.
  4. Bring the broth to a simmer with the fried garlic and salt and drop in the meatballs to keep warm.
  5. Blanch the noodles and greens and divide them among bowls. Add the meatballs, ladle over the hot broth and top with fried shallots, celery leaves and green onion. Season at the table with sambal, kecap manis and lime.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard makes the meatballs tough.
  • Warm beef gives a dense, rubbery texture; keep everything cold.

Authentic tips

  • Ice keeps the mix cold and bouncy.
  • Add a boiled egg or fried tofu.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought beef meatballs save time.
  • Chicken stock replaces beef broth.

Storage

Leftovers: Store meatballs and broth separately; the broth keeps 4 days.

Freezing: Freeze meatballs for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sambal and fried shallots
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
