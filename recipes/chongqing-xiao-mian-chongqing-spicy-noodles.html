Chóngqìng Xiǎo Miàn (Chongqing Spicy Noodles) 重庆小面

▾

Surprise me with

Chongqing xiao mian, literally "small noodles", is a breakfast and street noodle of Chongqing: wheat noodles in a bowl pre-loaded with chili oil, Sichuan peppercorn and soy sauce, topped with minced pork and greens.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a wok over medium-high heat, add the pork and stir-fry for 4 minutes until browned, then add the doubanjiang and 15 ml soy sauce and cook for 1 minute until the oil turns red. Set aside.
  2. Divide the chili oil, bowl soy sauce, dark soy sauce, vinegar, Sichuan pepper, garlic, sugar and sesame oil among 4 serving bowls.
  3. Bring the stock to a simmer and keep it hot.
  4. Boil the noodles in a large pot for 2 to 3 minutes until just tender, adding the greens for the last 30 seconds, then lift them into the bowls.
  5. Ladle 120 ml of the hot stock into each bowl and toss the noodles through the seasoning.
  6. Top with the pork, green onion and peanuts, and eat while hot.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles makes them mushy because they keep softening in the hot stock; pull them slightly firm.
  • Adding the stock before the noodles dilutes the seasoning; put the sauce in the bowl first.

Authentic tips

  • Use fresh noodles with a bit of alkaline bite.
  • Add the chili oil last if you prefer to control the heat.

Substitutions

  • Noodles of any kind work, including udon.
  • Crumbled firm tofu replaces pork for a lighter bowl.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; keep leftover pork topping 3 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: The pork topping freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens
  • Soup: Not needed; the noodles come with broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea or soy milk
