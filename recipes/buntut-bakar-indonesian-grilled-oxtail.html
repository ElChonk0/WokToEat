Buntut Bakar (Indonesian Grilled Oxtail)

▾

Surprise me with

Buntut bakar is an Indonesian dish of oxtail simmered until tender, then glazed with sweet soy sauce and grilled; it is usually served with sambal and rice.

Method

  1. Blanch the oxtail in boiling water for 10 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, candlenuts, coriander and white pepper into a paste. Fry it in the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes, until fragrant and darker, with the lemongrass, bay leaves and galangal.
  3. Add the oxtail and water, bring to a boil and simmer, partly covered, for 2.5 to 3 hours, until the meat is very tender but still on the bone. Season the broth with salt; serve it as soup alongside.
  4. Lift out the oxtail and toss it with the kecap manis, lime juice and a few spoons of the reduced broth.
  5. Grill over hot charcoal or under a broiler for 4 to 5 minutes per side, basting with the sweet soy mixture, until sticky and charred at the edges.
  6. Serve with sambal, rice and the bowl of oxtail broth.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Grilling before the meat is tender gives chewy oxtail; make sure it falls from the bone first.
  • Brushing the kecap manis too early burns it; glaze near the end.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the broth: it makes a soup the next day.
  • Chill the cooked oxtail overnight so it holds together when grilled.

Substitutions

  • Beef short ribs replace oxtail.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the simmer to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; regrill or oven-roast to reheat.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice, fresh cucumber and sambal
  • Soup: Sayur asem
  • Dessert: Pisang goreng
  • Beverage: Es teh manis
