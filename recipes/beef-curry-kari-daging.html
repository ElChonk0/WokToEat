Beef Curry (Kari Daging)

▾

Surprise me with

A Malaysian home-style beef curry reflecting the country's Indian and Malay culinary fusion, kari daging simmers beef in a fragrant spice paste and coconut milk until tender — a staple at Malay family gatherings.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic and ginger into a paste. Mix the curry and chili powders with the water into a thick paste; blooming the powder as a paste stops it scorching.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the cinnamon, star anise, cardamom and curry leaves for 30 seconds, then the shallot paste for 3 to 4 minutes.
  3. Add the curry paste and fry, stirring, for 5 to 8 minutes, until it darkens and the oil separates. This step decides the depth of the curry.
  4. Add the beef and stir to coat for 5 minutes, then the tomato and water. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer gently for 1 hour, until the beef is nearly tender.
  5. Add the potatoes and coconut milk and simmer, partly covered, for 25 to 30 minutes more, until the beef is fork-tender, the potatoes are soft and the gravy is thick. Season with salt and sugar. Serve with rice, roti or bread.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the spice-frying step, which is essential for developing the curry's full aroma before liquid is added.
  • Boiling the curry hard, which can cause the coconut milk to separate into an oily texture.

Authentic tips

  • Toasting the curry paste in oil until the oil separates slightly is a key step for deep flavor.
  • This curry, like many braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Short rib adds richness.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or roti canai
  • Soup: Acar
  • Dessert: Ais kacang
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
