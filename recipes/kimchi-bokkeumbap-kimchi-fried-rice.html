Kimchi Bokkeumbap (Kimchi Fried Rice) 김치볶음밥

▾

Surprise me with

Kimchi fried rice grew out of the Korean home habit of using up well-fermented, sour kimchi and leftover rice; topped with a fried egg it is one of the most common quick meals in Korea.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or large skillet over medium-high heat and fry the pork belly for 4 to 5 minutes until browned and its fat has rendered.
  2. Add the onion and half the green onion and cook for 2 minutes, then add the kimchi, gochugaru and sugar and stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until the kimchi turns soft and slightly caramelized.
  3. Stir in the gochujang, soy sauce and kimchi juice, then add the rice, breaking up clumps. Stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until every grain is red and hot. Stir in the butter and sesame oil.
  4. In a separate pan fry the eggs sunny side up with crisp edges and runny yolks.
  5. Spoon the rice into bowls, top each with an egg, the seaweed, sesame seeds and the rest of the green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Fresh, mild kimchi gives a flat result; use kimchi that has turned sour.
  • Freshly cooked warm rice turns mushy; use cold rice from the day before.

Authentic tips

  • Press the rice flat at the end and leave it 1 minute for crisp scorched bits on the bottom.
  • Spam is a very common alternative to pork belly in Korean homes.

Substitutions

  • Spam or tuna in place of the pork belly.
  • Make it vegetarian with tofu and vegan kimchi.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a pan and add a fresh egg.

Freezing: Freezes for 1 month without the egg.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Mayak gyeran (soy-marinated eggs) and seasoned spinach
  • Soup: Doenjang jjigae
  • Dessert: Sliced Korean pear
  • Beverage: Barley tea
