Babi Pongteh (Peranakan Braised Pork)

▾

Surprise me with

A Nyonya (Peranakan) specialty blending Chinese and Malay techniques, babi pongteh braises pork with fermented soybean paste (taucheo) and mushrooms until deeply savory — a dish traditionally served at Peranakan family gatherings.

Method

  1. Pound or blend the shallots and garlic into a coarse paste. Put the pork in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-low heat and fry the shallot paste for 4 to 5 minutes until golden and sweet-smelling, then add the taucheo and cinnamon and fry for 2 minutes more. Frying the fermented bean paste brings out its savory, slightly sweet depth, the signature of pongteh.
  3. Add the pork and mushrooms and stir for 3 to 4 minutes until coated. Add the soy sauces, palm sugar, pepper and the liquid to just cover.
  4. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 45 minutes, stirring now and then.
  5. Add the potatoes and simmer, partly covered, for 25 to 30 minutes more, until the pork is meltingly tender and the potatoes are soft. The gravy should be dark and slightly thick. Taste for salt; like most braises, it is better the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little taucheo, which is the defining flavor of this dish, distinct from plain soy-based braises.
  • Skipping the mushroom soaking liquid, which can be strained and added to the braise for extra depth.

Authentic tips

  • Reserve the mushroom soaking liquid and use some of it in place of plain water for extra flavor.
  • This dish, like most braises, tastes even better the next day.

Substitutions

  • Chicken replaces pork for a halal version.
  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
