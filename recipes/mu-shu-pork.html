Mu Shu Pork 木须肉

▾

Surprise me with

A Shandong-originated dish now popular across northern China and abroad, mu shu pork stir-fries shredded pork with egg, wood ear mushroom, and day lily buds, traditionally wrapped in thin mandarin pancakes.

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the marinade soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and cornstarch.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a flat-bottom wok over medium-high heat and scramble the eggs into soft, fluffy curds. Lift them out.
  3. Add the remaining oil and stir-fry the pork for 2 minutes until it turns white.
  4. Add the wood ear, daylily and cabbage and stir-fry for 1 to 2 minutes, then return the eggs with the sauce soy sauce and green onion and toss for 30 seconds. Finish with sesame oil.
  5. Serve with warm pancakes and hoisin, the American-Chinese way, or with rice as in Shandong and Beijing, where mu xu rou began.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the egg, which turns it rubbery instead of soft curds within the finished dish.
  • Skipping the day lily buds, which contribute a distinctive slightly tangy flavor central to the dish.

Authentic tips

  • Cooking the egg, pork, and vegetables in separate stages and combining at the end keeps each component's texture intact.
  • No mandarin pancakes: warmed flour tortillas or even lettuce cups work as a substitute wrap.

Substitutions

  • Chicken or tofu replaces the pork.
  • Thin wheat pancakes, tortillas or lettuce cups replace the Mandarin pancakes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Thin Mandarin pancakes and hoisin
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
