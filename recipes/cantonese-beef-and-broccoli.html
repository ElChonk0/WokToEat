Cantonese Beef and Broccoli 芥兰牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

A dim sum and takeout staple recognized worldwide, beef and broccoli stir-fries tender beef with broccoli in a glossy oyster sauce — simple, quick, and a reliable weeknight favorite across the Cantonese diaspora.

Method

  1. Rinse the beef in water, drain and squeeze dry. Mix it with the baking soda, marinade oyster sauce, soy sauce, water, cornstarch, sugar and wine until absorbed, stir in the marinade oil and leave for 30 minutes. Mix the sauce ingredients in a cup.
  2. Blanch the broccoli in boiling water with a pinch of salt and a splash of oil for about 2 minutes, until bright green and just tender. Drain it very well; water left on the florets makes the sauce watery.
  3. Heat a flat-bottom wok over the highest heat, add 2 tablespoons of oil and swirl. Lower the heat to medium-high, spread the beef out and sear for 30 to 60 seconds until browned underneath, then turn and cook 60 to 90 seconds more until mostly browned with a little pink. Lift it out.
  4. Add the remaining oil and fry the garlic and ginger for 20 to 30 seconds, then the green onion and carrot for 20 to 30 seconds. Add the broccoli and toss for 30 to 40 seconds.
  5. Return the beef, stir the sauce and pour it in, then toss for about 1 minute until it thickens and coats everything. Add a spoon of water if it gets too thick.
  6. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the beef, which turns it tough rather than tender — sear it quickly over very high heat.
  • Skipping the blanching step for broccoli, which can leave it undercooked and dull in color.

Authentic tips

  • Slicing the beef thinly against the grain is the single most important step for tenderness in this dish.
  • Velveting the beef (marinating with cornstarch) further improves tenderness, a standard Cantonese technique.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thigh works with the same method for a lighter version.
  • Gai lan (Chinese broccoli) is the more traditional vegetable; blanch the stems 30 seconds longer.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot wok, not the microwave, to keep the beef tender.

Freezing: Not recommended; the broccoli goes limp.
