Hakka Stuffed Tofu

▾

Surprise me with

Stuffed tofu is a signature dish of Hakka cuisine, a Chinese subgroup known for resourceful, hearty home cooking — filling fried tofu pockets with seasoned meat reflects a tradition of stretching protein further across a family meal.

Method

  1. Simmer the whole tofu block in salted water for 5 minutes, then drain and cool. This firms it so it holds the filling and seasons it lightly. Pat dry and cut into 8 squares or triangles.
  2. Mix the pork, shrimp, mushrooms and green onion with the soy sauce, salt, sugar, pepper, cornstarch and water, stirring in one direction for 1 to 2 minutes until the mixture turns sticky and springy. Stir in the sesame oil.
  3. With a small spoon, scoop a shallow pocket from the center of each tofu piece without breaking the walls. Mash the scooped tofu into the filling. Dust the pockets with a little cornstarch so the filling sticks, then press the filling in, mounding it slightly and smoothing the top.
  4. Heat the oil in a flat-bottom wok or skillet over medium heat. Fry the tofu filling-side down for 3 to 4 minutes, without moving, until the meat is browned and set, then turn and brown the other sides for 2 minutes. Lower the heat if it spits.
  5. Add the garlic for 15 seconds, then the stock, oyster sauce and soy sauce. Cover and braise over medium-low heat for 6 to 8 minutes, until the filling is cooked through and the tofu has soaked up some of the sauce.
  6. Lift the tofu onto a plate. Stir the sauce cornstarch with 1 tablespoon of water into the pan and simmer for 30 seconds until glossy, then pour it over the tofu.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling the tofu pockets, which causes the filling to pop out during frying — pack firmly but don't overstuff past the tofu's edges.
  • Frying at too high a heat, which browns the tofu before the filling has a chance to start cooking through; medium heat followed by a covered simmer finishes the job evenly.

Authentic tips

  • Patting the hollowed tofu cavity dry before stuffing helps the filling stick and stay in place during frying.
  • This dish reheats and even freezes better than most tofu dishes, since the meat filling helps the tofu hold its structure.

Substitutions

  • Ground chicken can replace pork for a leaner filling.
  • Make it pescatarian by using all shrimp or a mix of shrimp and white fish in place of pork.

Storage

Leftovers: Reheats well in a covered pan with a splash of water or stock.

Freezing: Freezes well, uncooked or cooked, for up to 2 months — one of the few tofu dishes that holds up to freezing thanks to the meat filling.

Serve with
