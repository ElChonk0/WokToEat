Gyu Shabu-Shabu (Beef Shabu-Shabu) 牛しゃぶしゃぶ

▾

Surprise me with

Shabu-shabu, thin beef swished in a pot of kombu broth, was inspired by Chinese hot pot and became popular in Osaka in the 1950s; the name is onomatopoeic for the sound of swishing.

Method

  1. Soak the kombu in the water in a donabe or Dutch oven for at least 30 minutes, then heat it slowly and remove the kombu just before the water boils. Boiling kombu turns the broth slimy and bitter.
  2. Whisk the sesame paste, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and a few spoons of water into a pourable sesame sauce. Mix the ponzu with the grated daikon and green onion.
  3. Bring the pot to the table on a portable burner and keep it at a gentle simmer. Add the carrot, cabbage stems, mushrooms, tofu and negi first, since they take longest, and cook for 5 minutes.
  4. Pick up a slice of beef with chopsticks and swish it through the broth for 10 to 20 seconds, until just pink. The name imitates that swishing sound.
  5. Dip the beef in sesame sauce or ponzu and eat at once. Add the cabbage leaves as you go and skim off foam between rounds.
  6. When the meat is finished, cook the udon in the rich broth, season with a pinch of salt and serve it as the last course.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the beef.
  • Crowding the pot cools the broth.

Authentic tips

  • Freeze the beef for 30 minutes for easier thin slicing.
  • Season the leftover broth for a final soup.

Substitutions

  • Thin pork slices replace beef.
  • Frozen beef sliced on a deli slicer is a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the leftover broth is an excellent soup.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Udon or rice for the end
  • Soup: Not needed; the pot is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot sencha
