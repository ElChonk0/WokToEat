Kakuni (Braised Pork Belly) 角煮

▾

Surprise me with

Kakuni ("squared simmer") braises cubed pork belly in soy, sake, and mirin until meltingly tender, a dish with roots in Nagasaki's historical trade with China, closely related to Chinese red-braised pork.

Method

  1. Boil pork belly cubes in plain water 5 minutes to remove impurities; drain and rinse.
  2. Simmer the pork with ginger and green onion in fresh water, covered, 1 hour until quite tender.
  3. Drain, discarding the aromatics, and return pork to the pot.
  4. Add soy sauce, mirin, sake, sugar, and 600 ml fresh water; bring to a simmer.
  5. Cover and braise 25–30 minutes more, adding boiled eggs in the last 15 minutes if using, until the sauce reduces and the pork is glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the initial plain simmer, which is what tenderizes the pork before it ever meets the flavoring liquid.
  • Adding the eggs too early, which can make them overcooked and rubbery by the time the dish finishes.

Authentic tips

  • The two-stage cooking (plain water first, then seasoned liquid) is what gives kakuni its signature melt-in-the-mouth texture without being overly salty.
  • This dish, like most braises, improves with an overnight rest in the refrigerator.

Substitutions

  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 45 minutes.
  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the fat solidifies and can be lifted off.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months in the sauce.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice with karashi mustard

  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
