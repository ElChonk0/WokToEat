Pork Rendang (Balinese / Chinese-Indonesian Style)

▾

Surprise me with

A pork adaptation of the famous Minangkabau beef rendang, this version is popular among Bali's Hindu and Indonesia's Chinese communities, slow-braised in coconut milk and toasted spice paste until nearly dry.

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, ginger, galangal and turmeric into a smooth paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or wide wok over medium heat and fry the spice paste (bumbu) with the lemongrass and lime leaves, stirring often, for 8 to 10 minutes, until it darkens, smells cooked rather than raw and the oil starts to separate. Under-fried bumbu tastes sharp and grassy; this step is the base of Indonesian cooking.
  3. Add the pork and stir for 5 minutes until coated, then add the coconut milk and bring to a boil. Simmer uncovered over low heat for 1.5 hours, stirring every 15 minutes, until the pork is tender and the sauce has reduced.
  4. Stir in the kerisik, tamarind, palm sugar and salt and cook for 30 to 45 minutes more, stirring often, until the sauce is thick and the coconut oil separates.
  5. Keep frying in that oil, stirring constantly, for 5 to 10 minutes until the paste darkens and clings to the meat. Pork shoulder's fat makes it richer than beef rendang. Serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the long, slow reduction — rendang is defined by patience; the sauce needs real time to darken and concentrate.
  • Skipping the kerisik, which gives rendang its distinctive nutty depth and helps thicken the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • True rendang should end up almost dry, with the sauce clinging to the meat rather than pooling.
  • Kerisik can be bought ready-made or made by slowly toasting grated coconut in a dry pan until deep golden brown.

Substitutions

  • Beef is the more traditional protein if you'd like to try the classic version.
  • No fresh galangal or lemongrass: dried or paste versions work, though fresh gives noticeably better aroma.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; flavor deepens considerably.

Freezing: Freezes exceptionally well for up to 3 months.
