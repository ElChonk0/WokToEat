Huī Shì Hóng Shāo Ròu (Hui-Style Red-Braised Pork) 徽式红烧肉

▾

Surprise me with

Red-braised pork belly is a staple across China; the Hui version is darker and saltier, with dried bamboo and local soy sauce.

  • Cook 1.5 hr

  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 900 ml4 cupsHot water

Method

  1. Blanch the pork belly in boiling water for 3 minutes, starting from cold water, then drain and rinse.
  2. Heat a tablespoon of oil in a Dutch oven or clay pot over medium heat and fry the ginger, green onion, garlic and star anise for 1 minute.
  3. Add the rock sugar and stir over low heat until it melts into an amber caramel, then turn the pork belly in it until glossy. Hui cooking is known for 'heavy oil, heavy color and attention to the fire'.
  4. Pour the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the pot, add the soy sauces and hot water to almost cover. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer over low heat for 1 hour, until the pork is meltingly tender.
  5. Uncover and reduce over medium-high heat for 5 to 10 minutes, basting, until the sauce is thick and glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Burning the sugar makes the dish bitter; take it off the heat when amber.
  • Skipping the blanching clouds the sauce.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the heat low for tender pork.
  • Serve with plain rice and greens.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder gives a leaner dish.
  • Hard-boiled eggs added for the last 20 minutes soak up the sauce.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried greens
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
