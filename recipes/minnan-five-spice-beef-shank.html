Five-Spice Braised Beef Shank 卤牛腱

▾

Surprise me with

Soy-braised beef shank, sliced thin and served cold, is a shared Chinese dish; in southern Fujian it is part of the lu wei (braised meats) counter, seasoned with five-spice and a lighter, sweeter soy.

  • Prep 15 min (plus overnight chilling)
  • Cook 2 hr

Method

  1. Blanch the shank 5 minutes; rinse.
  2. Combine everything except the beef in a pot, bring to a boil and simmer 10 minutes.
  3. Add the shank, cover and simmer gently 1.5–2 hours until a chopstick slides in with light resistance.
  4. Cool the beef in the braising liquid, then chill overnight in the liquid.
  5. Slice thinly across the grain and serve cold with a drizzle of the braising liquid, chili oil and garlic.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing it warm; shank must be fully chilled to slice thin without shredding.

Authentic tips

  • Keep the strained braising liquid (lu shui) in the freezer and reuse it; it deepens each time.

Substitutions

  • Brisket works but slices less neatly.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated in the braising liquid; slice cold.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
