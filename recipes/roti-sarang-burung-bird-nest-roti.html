Roti Sarang Burung (Bird's Nest Roti)

Roti sarang burung, "bird's nest roti", is a Malaysian and Singaporean mamak dish of flaky roti dough shaped into a bowl and filled with a fried egg.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, water, sugar, salt, milk and 15 g of the ghee into a soft dough, knead for 8 minutes until smooth, and divide into 4 balls.
  2. Coat the balls in the oil, cover and rest for at least 2 hours until very soft and elastic.
  3. Stretch each ball on an oiled surface into a thin, translucent sheet, brush with ghee, then gather the edges inward and fold into a flat square with a small hollow in the middle.
  4. Heat a flat griddle or pan over medium heat, add a little ghee, and place the roti on it. Crack an egg into the hollow and cook, covered, for 3 minutes.
  5. Flip and cook for 2 more minutes until golden and crisp on the base, with the egg set to your liking.
  6. Fluff the roti by clapping it between your hands and serve with the dhal.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Stretching dough that has not rested tears; give it 2 hours.
  • Cooking over very high heat burns the base before the egg sets.

Authentic tips

  • Rest the dough in oil: the oil makes it easy to stretch thin.
  • Ready-made frozen roti canai can be used for the base.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought frozen roti replaces homemade dough.
  • A sunny-side egg or scrambled egg both work.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the dough keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: The dough balls freeze for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Dhal or curry gravy
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Not needed
  • Beverage: Hot teh tarik
