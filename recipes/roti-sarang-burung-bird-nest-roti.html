Roti Sarang Burung (Bird's Nest Roti)

▾

Surprise me with

Roti sarang burung, "bird's nest roti", is a Malaysian and Singaporean mamak dish of flaky roti dough shaped into a bowl and filled with a fried egg.

Method

  1. Knead the flour, water, sugar, salt and evaporated milk into a very soft, smooth dough, then divide it into 4 balls. Coat them well with melted ghee and oil and rest them, covered, for at least 4 hours or overnight.
  2. On an oiled surface, flatten a ball and stretch it with oiled hands into a large, paper-thin sheet. Fold it into a long rope, coil it loosely, and flatten the coil slightly, leaving a hollow in the center like a nest.
  3. Heat a flat griddle or large skillet over medium heat with a little ghee and fry the roti for 2 minutes until golden underneath.
  4. Crack an egg into the hollow, cover with a lid for 1 minute until the white starts to set, then flip carefully and cook for 1 to 2 minutes more, until the roti is crisp and the egg is cooked to your liking.
  5. Serve hot with dhal or curry gravy.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Stretching dough that has not rested tears; give it 2 hours.
  • Cooking over very high heat burns the base before the egg sets.

Authentic tips

  • Rest the dough in oil: the oil makes it easy to stretch thin.
  • Ready-made frozen roti canai can be used for the base.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought frozen roti replaces homemade dough.
  • A sunny-side egg or scrambled egg both work.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the dough keeps 2 days in the refrigerator.

Freezing: The dough balls freeze for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Dhal or curry gravy
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Not needed
