Hetian Poached Chicken 白斩河田鸡

Hetian in Changting county, heartland of the Fujian Hakka, is famous for its free-range yellow chickens; the Hakka serve them simply poached and chilled, with a sharp ginger-garlic dip, at every festival table.

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water with the ginger, green onions and salt to a boil; there should be enough to submerge the chicken.
  2. Holding the chicken by the neck, dip it in and out three times so the cavity fills with hot water, then lower it in.
  3. Return to a bare simmer, cover, cook 15 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave covered 20–25 minutes.
  4. Check the thickest part of the thigh reads 74°C (165°F); plunge the chicken into iced water for 10 minutes.
  5. For the dip, put ginger, garlic and green onion in a heatproof bowl, pour the smoking oil over, then stir in the soy sauce.
  6. Chop the chicken through the bone into pieces and serve at room temperature with the dip.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard, which tightens the meat and tears the skin; the chicken should cook mostly in residual heat.

Authentic tips

  • The ice bath is what gives Hakka white-cut chicken its firm, slightly gelatinous skin; don’t skip it.
  • Keep the poaching stock for soup or for cooking rice.

Substitutions

  • Use 1.2 kg bone-in thighs instead of a whole bird; simmer 10 minutes and rest 15.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; serve cold or let it come to room temperature.

Freezing: The poaching stock freezes well; the chicken is best fresh.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain rice with ginger-scallion dip
  • Soup: Clear chicken broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
