Zi Cai Dan Hua Tang (Seaweed and Egg Drop Soup) 紫菜蛋花汤

A five-minute soup of dried laver and egg ribbons is one of the most common everyday soups in Chinese homes.

  • Prep 5 min
  • Cook 10 min
  • Difficulty Easy
  • Draft Cantonese Tofu & Vegetarian LunchDinnerSoupVegetarian30 min or lessEgg

Ingredients

4servings (recipe serves 4)

  • 10 g1/3 ozDried laver (zi cai)
  • 1.2 L5 cupsVegetable stock
  • 10 g1/3 ozGinger, julienned
  • 22Eggs, beaten
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 2 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 1 g1/2 tspGround white pepper
  • 5 ml1 tspSesame oil
  • 2 stalks2 stalksGreen onion, chopped

Method

  1. Rinse the laver briefly and tear into pieces.
  2. Bring the stock and ginger to a boil and simmer 3 minutes.
  3. Add the laver and soy sauce and simmer 1 minute.
  4. Lower the heat and drizzle in the eggs while stirring slowly so they form ribbons.
  5. Season with salt and pepper and finish with sesame oil and green onion.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Made With Lau YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the laver for long makes it slimy.
  • Pouring the egg into a hard boil breaks it into specks.

Authentic tips

  • Add a handful of dried shrimp for the classic non-vegetarian version.
  • A few drops of vinegar brighten it.

Substitutions

  • Roasted nori sheets work, added at the very end.
  • Chicken stock is common if it does not need to be vegetarian.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keeps 1 day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fried rice
  • Soup: This is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
