Japanese Curry

Japanese curry arrived via British naval cooking in the late 1800s and was adapted into a thick, mildly sweet roux-based stew now considered a national comfort food, usually served over rice.

  • 15ml1 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Heat oil in a pot and brown the meat lightly; remove and set aside.
  2. Add onion and cook until softened and lightly golden, 8–10 minutes — don't rush this, it builds sweetness.
  3. Add carrot and potato, then return the meat to the pot.
  4. Add water, bring to a simmer, and cook covered 15–20 minutes until vegetables are tender.
  5. Remove from heat, break the curry roux into pieces, and stir in until fully dissolved.
  6. Return to low heat and simmer 5 more minutes, stirring, until thickened.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the roux to a boiling pot: it can clump — remove from heat first, then stir it in.

Authentic tips

  • Letting the onions cook low and slow until truly golden makes a noticeably better curry than rushing them.

Substitutions

  • No Japanese curry roux: a roux of butter, flour, curry powder, ketchup, and soy sauce approximates the flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Tastes even better the next day; keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes very well; a great make-ahead freezer meal.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Fukujinzuke pickles
  • Soup: Green salad
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
