Japanese Curry

▾

Surprise me with

Japanese curry arrived via British naval cooking in the late 1800s and was adapted into a thick, mildly sweet roux-based stew now considered a national comfort food, usually served over rice.

Method

  1. Season the chicken with salt and pepper. Heat half the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat and brown the chicken skin-side down for 3 to 4 minutes until golden, then lift it out.
  2. Add the remaining oil and the onions and cook over medium heat for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and golden brown. Caramelized onions give the curry its deep sweetness. Add the garlic and ginger for 1 minute.
  3. Return the chicken with the carrots and potatoes, add the sake and stock and bring to a boil. Skim off the foam, then cover and simmer for 15 to 20 minutes, until the potatoes and carrots are tender.
  4. Turn off the heat and break the roux into the pot, stirring until it fully dissolves. Adding roux off the heat prevents lumps and scorching.
  5. Return to low heat and simmer, stirring often, for 5 to 10 minutes until thick and glossy. Stir in the soy sauce and ketchup to round out the flavor. Serve over rice with fukujinzuke; like most stews, it is even better the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the roux to a boiling pot: it can clump — remove from heat first, then stir it in.

Authentic tips

  • Letting the onions cook low and slow until truly golden makes a noticeably better curry than rushing them.

Substitutions

  • No Japanese curry roux: a roux of butter, flour, curry powder, ketchup, and soy sauce approximates the flavor.

Storage

Leftovers: Tastes even better the next day; keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes very well; a great make-ahead freezer meal.
