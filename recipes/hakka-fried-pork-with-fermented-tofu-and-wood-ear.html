Hakka Fried Pork with Fermented Tofu and Wood Ear

▾

Surprise me with

A Hakka festival dish in which pork is deep-fried and then stewed with fermented bean curd and wood ear fungus; it is served at Lunar New Year.

  • Cook 1 hr

  • 600 g1.3 lbPork belly, cut into 3 cm chunks
  • 60 g2 ozFermented bean curd
  • 45 ml3 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 15 g2 tbspCornstarch
  • 500 ml2 cupsNeutral oil, for frying
  • 15 g1/2 ozDried wood ear mushrooms, soaked
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced
  • 4 cloves4 clovesGarlic, crushed
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 10 g2 tspSugar
  • 400 ml1 2/3 cupsWater

Method

  1. Mash half the fermented bean curd with 15 ml of the wine, toss with the pork and cornstarch, and marinate for 10 minutes.
  2. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) and fry the pork in two batches for 5 minutes each, until golden, then drain.
  3. Pour off all but 15 ml of the oil, fry the ginger and garlic for 30 seconds, and add the remaining bean curd, mashed.
  4. Return the pork, add the wine, soy sauce, sugar, wood ear and water, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 40 minutes.
  5. Uncover and cook for 5 minutes, until the sauce clings to the pork.

Common mistakes

  • Frying at too low a temperature makes the pork greasy; wait until the oil shimmers.
  • Overcrowding the oil cools it, so fry in batches.

Authentic tips

  • Rinse the wood ear well and trim the hard knobs.
  • Red fermented bean curd gives a richer color.

Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder replaces belly for a leaner dish.
  • Shiitake replaces wood ear.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days in the refrigerator; reheat with a splash of water.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Clear mustard green soup
  • Dessert: Fresh tangerines
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
