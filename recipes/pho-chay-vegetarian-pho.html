Phở Chay (Vegetarian Pho)

▾

Surprise me with

Pho chay, vegetarian pho with a clear broth of charred onion, ginger, star anise and mushrooms, is a staple of Vietnam's Buddhist vegetarian restaurants, which serve it on the 1st and 15th days of the lunar month.

Method

  1. Char the onions and ginger under the broiler or over a flame until blackened in spots, and rinse off the loose char. Toast the spices in a dry pan for 2 minutes and tie them in a spice bag.
  2. Put the daikon, carrots, shiitake, apple, charred onion and ginger in a pot with the water and simmer, covered, for 1 hour. The daikon, apple and shiitake build the sweetness and depth that bones give a meat pho.
  3. Add the spice bag and simmer for 30 to 45 minutes more, then strain.
  4. Season with soy sauce, rock sugar and salt. Slice the rehydrated shiitake and keep them for topping.
  5. Soak the noodles in warm water for 30 minutes, dip in boiling water for 15 seconds and divide among bowls. Top with tofu and shiitake, ladle over the boiling broth and serve with herbs, sprouts, lime and chili.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the charring gives a flat broth.
  • Boiling hard makes it cloudy.

Authentic tips

  • Toast the spices first.
  • Add dried shiitake for depth.

Substitutions

  • Dried shiitake add depth to the broth.
  • Rice stick noodles replace flat rice noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Store noodles and broth separately; the broth keeps 5 days.

Freezing: Freeze the broth for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Herbs, bean sprouts and lime
  • Soup: Not needed; the soup is the meal
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
