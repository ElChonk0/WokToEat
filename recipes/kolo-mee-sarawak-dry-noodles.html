Kolo Mee (Sarawak Dry Noodles)

▾

Surprise me with

Kolo mee is a Sarawak dry noodle dish of springy egg noodles tossed in shallot oil with minced pork and char siu; it is a breakfast staple in Kuching.

  • 15 ml1 tbspBlack vinegar
  • 150 g5 ozChoy sum
  • 20 g3/4 ozGreen onion, sliced
  • 1.5 L6 cupsWater

Method

  1. Make shallot oil: fry the shallots in the oil over medium-low heat for 6 to 8 minutes, stirring, until evenly golden. Strain, keeping both the crisp shallots and the fragrant oil. Do not let them go dark brown, or they turn bitter.
  2. Mix the pork with the soy sauce and wine and stir-fry it in a spoonful of the shallot oil over medium-high heat for 3 to 4 minutes, breaking it up, until cooked and lightly browned.
  3. In each serving bowl, mix a tablespoon of shallot oil, the light and dark soy, white pepper and a splash of black vinegar. Kolo mee is about the springy noodles and this simple, fragrant oil.
  4. Bring the water to a rolling boil and blanch the choy sum for 1 minute; lift it out. Cook the noodles for 30 to 40 seconds, loosening them with chopsticks, dip them briefly in cold water and back into the hot water for a few seconds, then drain well.
  5. Toss the noodles in the sauce in each bowl until glossy and evenly colored. Top with the minced pork, sliced char siu, choy sum, crisp shallots and green onion. Serve with a small bowl of soup and pickled green chilies.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles makes them mushy; they cook in about a minute.
  • Burning the shallots makes the oil bitter; fry them slowly and lift them out as they turn golden.

Authentic tips

  • Shake the noodles dry before saucing so the sauce is not diluted.
  • A spoonful of pork lard in the sauce gives the classic richness.

Substitutions

  • Roast pork replaces char siu.
  • Thin wonton noodles replace the egg noodles.

Storage

Leftovers: Best eaten at once; the shallot oil keeps 2 weeks in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended for the noodles; the pork freezes for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled green chilies
  • Soup: Clear pork bone broth
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot kopi
