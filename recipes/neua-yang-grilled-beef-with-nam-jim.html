Neua Yang (Thai Grilled Beef with Jaew Dipping Sauce)

▾

Surprise me with

Neua yang, grilled marinated beef, is eaten across Thailand, especially Isaan, with a spicy tamarind-chili dipping sauce called nam jim jaew.

Method

  1. Pound the garlic, cilantro root and white pepper into a paste and mix with the soy sauce, oyster sauce, palm sugar and oil. Rub it into the beef and marinate for at least 1 hour, or overnight.
  2. Make the nam jim jaew: stir the fish sauce, lime juice, tamarind and palm sugar until dissolved, then add the toasted rice powder, chili flakes, shallot and cilantro. It should be sour, salty and smoky.
  3. Heat a grill or cast-iron grill pan over high heat. Grill the beef for 3 to 5 minutes per side for medium-rare, until charred at the edges. The sugar in the marinade caramelizes, so turn it before it burns.
  4. Rest the beef for 5 minutes so the juices settle.
  5. Slice thinly against the grain and serve with the jaew, sticky rice and raw vegetables.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking too long makes the beef tough.
  • Slicing with the grain leaves it chewy.

Authentic tips

  • Add fresh herbs to the dip.
  • Flank is traditional.

Substitutions

  • Flank steak takes the marinade best.
  • No grill: broil on a rack about 8 cm (3 in) from the element for 3 to 4 minutes per side for medium-rare, then rest 5 minutes before slicing; a hot ridged pan also works.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; slice cold into salad.

Freezing: Freezes cooked beef for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sticky rice and papaya salad
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Singha beer
