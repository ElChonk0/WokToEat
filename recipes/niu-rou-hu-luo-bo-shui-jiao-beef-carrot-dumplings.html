Niú Ròu Hú Luó Bo Shuǐ Jiǎo (Beef and Carrot Dumplings) 牛肉胡萝卜水饺

▾

Beef dumplings are a staple of Beijing's Hui Muslim community, often paired with carrot or celery for sweetness.

  • Cook 8 min

  • 15 ml1 tbspNeutral oil, for the carrots
  • 60 ml1/4 cupBlack vinegar and garlic, for dipping

Method

  1. Toss the grated carrots with the oil instead of salting and squeezing; the oil coats them so they stay sweet and do not leak water into the filling.
  2. Mix the beef with the ginger, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and pepper. Add the Sichuan pepper water a tablespoon at a time, stirring in one direction, until it is absorbed and the filling turns sticky and springy. Fold in the vegetables, green onion and sesame oil.
  3. Put a heaping teaspoon of filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it in half and pinch the middle, then pleat or press the edges to seal tightly.
  4. Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and add 15 to 20 dumplings, stirring so they do not stick. When the water returns to a boil, add 250 ml (1 cup) of cold water; repeat twice more. They are done when they float and look puffed, 6 to 8 minutes.
  5. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and serve with black vinegar, crushed garlic and chili oil. Dumplings (jiaozi) are the Lunar New Year food of northern China.

Common mistakes

  • Overmixing makes the filling tough.
  • Overfilling splits the dumplings.

Authentic tips

  • Carrot adds sweetness and moisture.
  • Chill the filling before wrapping if it is sticky.

Substitutions

  • Ground lamb replaces beef for a more traditional version.
  • Grated daikon can replace some of the carrot.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; pan-fry leftovers.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked on a tray for 2 months; boil from frozen for 8 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Black vinegar and chili oil dip
  • Soup: Hot and sour soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
