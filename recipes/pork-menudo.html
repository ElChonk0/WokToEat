Pork Menudo

▾

Surprise me with

A Spanish-influenced Filipino stew similar to afritada but with the distinctive addition of tomato sauce, liver, and raisins, pork menudo is a common dish at Filipino celebrations and potlucks.

Method

  1. Marinate the pork in the soy sauce and calamansi for 30 minutes.
  2. Fry the potatoes and carrot in the oil in a Dutch oven for 4 to 5 minutes until lightly browned and lift them out.
  3. Saute the garlic and onion for 3 minutes, then add the pork with its marinade and cook for 5 minutes until no longer pink. Add the liver for 2 minutes.
  4. Add the tomato sauce, stock and bay leaf, bring to a boil, cover and simmer for 40 to 45 minutes, until the pork is tender.
  5. Return the potatoes and carrot with the bell pepper, chickpeas, raisins and hot dogs and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes until thick. Season with fish sauce and sugar; menudo is savory with a little sweetness from the raisins.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the liver too early, which can make it overcooked and grainy — add it near the end.
  • Cutting the vegetables too large, which won't cook through in the same time as the pork.

Authentic tips

  • This stew is a great make-ahead dish, since the flavor deepens as it sits.
  • Green peas are a common addition alongside the other vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Omit the liver for a lighter dish.
  • Hot dogs are a common addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Leche flan
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
