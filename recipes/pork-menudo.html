Pork Menudo

▾

Surprise me with

A Spanish-influenced Filipino stew similar to afritada but with the distinctive addition of tomato sauce, liver, and raisins, pork menudo is a common dish at Filipino celebrations and potlucks.

Method

  1. Brown pork cubes in oil until golden; set aside.
  2. In the same pot, fry onion and garlic until softened.
  3. Add tomato sauce and cook 2–3 minutes until slightly darkened.
  4. Return pork to the pot with fish sauce and stock; bring to a simmer.
  5. Cover and braise 15 minutes, then add potatoes and carrot; continue simmering 10 minutes.
  6. Stir in raisins and pork liver if using in the last 5 minutes; serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the liver too early, which can make it overcooked and grainy — add it near the end.
  • Cutting the vegetables too large, which won't cook through in the same time as the pork.

Authentic tips

  • This stew is a great make-ahead dish, since the flavor deepens as it sits.
  • Green peas are a common addition alongside the other vegetables.

Substitutions

  • Omit the liver for a lighter dish.
  • Hot dogs are a common addition.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; better the next day.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Leche flan
  • Beverage: Calamansi juice
