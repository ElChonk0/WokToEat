Gaeng Som Pla (Southern Thai Sour Curry with Fish)

▾

Surprise me with

Gaeng som, a sour and spicy curry with no coconut milk, is a staple of Thai home cooking, especially in the south and central regions.

  • 600 g1.3 lbFirm white fish steaks or fillets (snapper, cod)
  • 60 g1/4 cupSour curry paste (gaeng som / gaeng leung)
  • 60 g2 ozFish, extra piece for thickening (optional)

  • 45 ml3 tbspTamarind paste

  • 15 g1 tbspPalm sugar
  • 15 ml1 tbspLime juice (optional, to sharpen)
  • 200 g7 ozDaikon or green papaya, sliced
  • 100 g3.5 ozLong beans, cut into lengths
  • 100 g3.5 ozCabbage or cha-om omelet (optional)

Method

  1. Poach the extra piece of fish in a little of the water for 3 minutes, then pound it with the curry paste in a mortar. Traditional gaeng som is thickened with fish pounded into the paste rather than coconut milk.
  2. Bring the rest of the water to a boil and dissolve the paste in it. Simmer for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the daikon and simmer for 5 minutes until almost tender, then the long beans and cabbage for 2 minutes.
  4. Season with tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar. It should taste sour first, then salty, with only a little sweetness.
  5. Slide in the fish and simmer gently for 4 to 5 minutes, without stirring, until it flakes. Taste, add lime juice if you want it sharper, and serve with rice.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the fish dry.
  • Adding the tamarind too early makes it bitter.

Authentic tips

  • Add cabbage or cauliflower.
  • Southern versions use turmeric and are fiery.

Substitutions

  • Shrimp replaces fish.
  • Cauliflower and cabbage replace daikon.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freeze the broth only.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Jasmine rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the curry is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
