Suan Rong Jie Lan (Garlic Chinese Broccoli) 蒜蓉芥蓝

Gai lan stir-fried or blanched with plenty of garlic is the standard Cantonese green vegetable, eaten at home and at dim sum and banquets alike.

  • Prep 5 min
  • Cook 8 min
  • Difficulty Easy

  • 500 g1.1 lbChinese broccoli (gai lan)
  • 30 g2 tbspGarlic, minced
  • 10 g2 tspGinger
  • 15 ml1 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 5 g1 tspSugar
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil

Method

  1. Trim the stem ends and cut thick stalks in half lengthwise so they cook as quickly as the leaves.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to a boil with a pinch of salt and a few drops of oil; blanch the gai lan 1 minute and drain well.
  3. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat, fry the garlic and ginger 10 seconds until fragrant but not browned.
  4. Add the gai lan, wine, sugar and salt and toss over high heat for 1 minute until glossy.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking until the stems turn gray.
  • Burning the garlic.

Authentic tips

  • Peel thick stems for even cooking.
  • A pinch of sugar offsets the natural bitterness.

Substitutions

  • Broccolini or choy sum are good substitutes.
  • A splash of vegetarian oyster sauce gives a savory glaze.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day; keeps 1 day and loses its color.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Steamed tofu with soy sauce
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
