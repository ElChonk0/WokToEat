Suàn Róng Jiè Lán (Garlic Chinese Broccoli) 蒜蓉芥蓝

▾

Surprise me with

Gai lan stir-fried or blanched with plenty of garlic is the standard Cantonese green vegetable, eaten at home and at dim sum and banquets alike.

Method

  1. Rinse the gai lan and trim the stem ends so the stalks are all about the same length. Peel any very thick stems and slit them lengthwise 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) so they cook as fast as the leaves.
  2. Bring a wok or large pot of water to a hard boil and add the blanching oil and salt, which keep the greens glossy and green. Add the gai lan, push it under and cover. Cook 1 to 2 minutes after the water returns to a boil, turning it once.
  3. Bite a stem: it should be tender but still snap. Cook 1 to 2 minutes more if you prefer it softer. Drain well and lay it on a platter, snipping long stalks in half with scissors.
  4. Wipe the wok dry and heat the oil over medium-low heat. Fry the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds, until golden and fragrant but not dark. Add the wine, mushroom sauce, sugar and water and bubble for a few seconds.
  5. Pour the garlic sauce over the greens and serve at once.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking until the stems turn gray.
  • Burning the garlic.

Authentic tips

  • Peel thick stems for even cooking.
  • A pinch of sugar offsets the natural bitterness.

Substitutions

  • Broccolini or choy sum are good substitutes.
  • A splash of vegetarian oyster sauce gives a savory glaze.

Storage

Leftovers: Best the same day; keeps 1 day and loses its color.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Steamed tofu with soy sauce
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot jasmine tea
