Lion's Head Meatballs 狮子头

▾

Surprise me with

Named for their resemblance to a lion's mane amid surrounding greens, these oversized, tender pork meatballs are a Jiangsu classic, traditionally braised gently in a clay pot until custard-soft.

Method

  1. Combine ground pork, water chestnuts, ginger, green onion, egg, cornstarch, soy sauce, and wine; mix gently until just combined (don't overwork).
  2. Form into 4 large meatballs, packing loosely.
  3. Sear the meatballs in a little oil until browned on all sides; remove.
  4. Line the bottom of a pot with napa cabbage, place meatballs on top, and pour in stock.
  5. Cover and simmer gently 30–35 minutes until the meatballs are cooked through and tender.
  6. Serve the meatballs and cabbage together with the braising broth spooned over.

Common mistakes

  • Overworking the meat mixture, which produces dense, tough meatballs instead of the intended tender, custardy texture.
  • Simmering too hard, which can cause the large meatballs to break apart.

Authentic tips

  • Packing the meatballs loosely, without over-compacting, is key to their signature light, tender texture.
  • A Dutch oven is the easy modern stand-in for the traditional clay pot and holds a gentle simmer on any stove.

Substitutions

  • A mix of pork and shrimp is a traditional variation for added texture and flavor.
  • Ground chicken can be used for a lighter version, though the classic texture relies on pork's fat content.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; flavor improves.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; the napa cabbage broth serves as soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot green tea
