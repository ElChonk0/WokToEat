Shuàn Niú Ròu (Beijing Beef Hot Pot) 涮牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Instant-boiled meat hot pot with a copper pot is Beijing's signature winter meal, usually lamb but beef is a common variation in Hui restaurants.

  • 600 g1.3 lbBeef, paper-thin slices (or lamb, the Beijing classic)
  • 2 L8 cupsWater
  • 50 g2 ozGinger, green onion, dried shiitake and goji berries, for the broth
  • 250 g9 ozNapa cabbage

  • 100 g3.5 ozGlass noodles, soaked

  • 15 g1 tbspFermented bean curd, mashed
  • 15 g1 tbspChive flower sauce (jiu hua jiang), optional
  • 15 ml1 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspChili oil
  • 30 g1 ozCilantro and green onion, chopped

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil in a copper hot pot or Dutch oven with the ginger, green onion, shiitake and goji berries. Beijing shuan rou uses a plain, clear broth; all the flavor comes from the dip.
  2. Make the dip: thin the sesame paste with water a spoonful at a time, stirring until smooth and pourable, then stir in the fermented bean curd, chive flower sauce and soy sauce. Each person adds chili oil, cilantro and green onion to taste.
  3. Bring the pot to the table on a portable burner and keep it at a rolling boil.
  4. Swish each slice of beef in the boiling broth for 10 to 15 seconds, until it just changes color, then dip it in the sesame sauce.
  5. Cook the cabbage, tofu and glass noodles in the broth as you go, and finish by drinking the enriched broth.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking makes the thin slices tough; seconds are enough.
  • Slicing too thick makes the beef chewy.

Authentic tips

  • Freeze the beef for 30 minutes before slicing very thin.
  • Serve with sesame flatbread.

Substitutions

  • Lamb is the classic meat for Beijing hot pot.
  • Add enoki mushrooms and spinach.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; use leftover broth for noodle soup the next day.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sesame buns
  • Soup: Not needed; the hot pot broth serves as soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
