Cōng Yóu Bǐng (Taiwanese Scallion Pancake) 蔥油餅

Scallion pancakes, flaky layered flatbreads, are among Taiwan's most popular breakfast and street foods, often served with egg or basil.

  • Prep 40 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 300 g2.5 cupsFlour
  • 180 ml3/4 cupHot water
  • 100 g3.5 ozScallions, chopped
  • 60 ml4 tbspNeutral oil
  • 5 g1 tspSalt
  • 45 ml3 tbspSoy sauce dip

Method

  1. Mix the flour with the hot water into a dough, knead 5 minutes and rest, covered, 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 4, roll each thin, brush with oil and scatter with scallions and salt.
  3. Roll up like a rope, coil and flatten gently.
  4. Pan-fry in a little oil over medium heat for 3-4 minutes per side until golden and flaky.
  5. Crush gently to open the layers and serve with soy dip.

Common mistakes

  • Cold water gives a tough dough.
  • High heat burns the outside before the layers cook.

Authentic tips

  • Hot water gives a tender, layered pancake.
  • Add an egg for a Taiwanese breakfast version.

Substitutions

  • Store-bought frozen pancakes are a good shortcut.
  • Whole wheat flour replaces some flour.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days; reheat in a hot dry pan.

Freezing: Freeze uncooked rounds between parchment for 3 months; cook from frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Add an egg or basil
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot soy milk
