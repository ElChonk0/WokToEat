Fū Qī Fèi Piàn (Husband and Wife Beef Slices) 夫妻肺片

Fu qi fei pian, thin slices of braised beef and offal in a chili-sesame sauce, was created by a Chengdu couple in the 1930s and is a Sichuan cold appetizer classic.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 90 min
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 50 g2 ozRoasted peanuts and cilantro
  • 40 g3 tbspGinger and scallion

Method

  1. Cover the beef (and tongue, if using) with cold water, bring to a boil, boil 5 minutes, then drain and rinse.
  2. Return to the pot with fresh water, the star anise, cinnamon, Sichuan pepper, ginger and scallion and simmer gently for 80 minutes until a skewer slides in easily.
  3. Let the meat cool in the liquid, then chill for at least 1 hour.
  4. Slice the beef as thinly as possible against the grain and layer on a platter.
  5. Whisk the chili oil, soy sauce, ground Sichuan pepper and 3 tablespoons of the cooking liquid, pour over, and scatter with the peanuts and cilantro.

Common mistakes

  • Slicing the beef while warm tears it; chill first for neat, thin slices.
  • Over-boiling the shank makes it dry; keep the liquid at a gentle simmer.

Authentic tips

  • The braising liquid can be reused as a master stock for later batches.
  • Beef tongue or tripe makes it authentic, but shank alone is excellent.

Substitutions

  • Beef shank alone is a fine, simpler version.
  • Use less chili oil for a milder dressing.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated in the braising liquid; dress just before serving.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or steamed buns
  • Soup: Cucumber salad
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Barley tea
