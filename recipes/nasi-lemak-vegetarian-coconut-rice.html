Nasi Lemak (Vegetarian Coconut Rice Plate)

▾

Surprise me with

Nasi lemak, coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts and egg, is Malaysia's unofficial national dish and a standard breakfast; this version replaces the anchovies with tempeh.

Method

  1. Put the rice in a pot or rice cooker with the coconut milk, water, pandan, lemongrass, ginger and salt. Bring to a boil, cover and cook on low for 15 minutes, then rest covered for 10 minutes. Fluff gently.
  2. For the vegetarian sambal, blend the soaked chilies, shallots and garlic. Fry the paste in the oil over medium-low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring, until dark red and the oil separates; long, slow frying makes a sambal that is rich rather than harsh.
  3. Add the tamarind, sugar and onion rings and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until the onion softens. It should be sweet, spicy and tangy.
  4. Fry the tempeh until golden and crisp, and toast the peanuts in a dry pan.
  5. Mound the rice on plates with a spoonful of sambal, the tempeh, egg, peanuts and cucumber around it.

Common mistakes

  • Too much coconut milk makes the rice greasy.
  • Skipping the pandan loses the fragrance.

Authentic tips

  • The classic version uses fried anchovies.
  • Banana-leaf packets are the street style.

Substitutions

  • Anchovies are the traditional topping.
  • Coconut milk powder replaces canned.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; the rice firms up.

Freezing: Freezes the rice for 1 month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Sambal and fried peanuts
  • Soup: Not needed
  • Dessert: Kuih
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
