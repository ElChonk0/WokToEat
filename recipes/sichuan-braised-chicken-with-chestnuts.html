Sichuan Braised Chicken with Chestnuts 板栗烧鸡

A homestyle Sichuan braise where chestnuts soak up the doubanjiang-spiked sauce as they simmer, balancing the dish's heat with natural sweetness — a popular autumn and winter dish when fresh chestnuts are at their best.

Method

  1. Brown chicken pieces in a little oil over medium-high heat, skin-side down first; set aside.
  2. In the same pot, fry doubanjiang, garlic, and ginger until the oil turns red and fragrant.
  3. Return chicken to the pot; add soy sauce, wine, sugar, and star anise, stirring to coat.
  4. Add stock, bring to a simmer, cover, and braise 20 minutes.
  5. Add chestnuts and continue simmering uncovered 10–12 minutes until the sauce reduces and thickens.
  6. Taste and adjust seasoning; serve hot.

Common mistakes

  • Adding chestnuts too early, which can make them fall apart into the sauce by serving time.
  • Skimping on the doubanjiang-frying step, which is essential to building the dish's depth of flavor.

Authentic tips

  • Vacuum-packed cooked chestnuts (widely available) make this dish quick without sacrificing much authenticity.
  • The sauce should reduce to a glossy coating, not remain thin and watery — keep the last stretch uncovered.

Substitutions

  • Sweet potato chunks can replace or supplement chestnuts for a different starchy sweetness.
  • Bone-in thighs can be swapped for drumsticks with a similar braising time.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated; flavor improves the next day.

Freezing: Freezes well for up to 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Stir-fried pea shoots
  • Dessert: Fresh pear
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
