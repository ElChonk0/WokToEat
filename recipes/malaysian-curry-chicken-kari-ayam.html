Malaysian Curry Chicken (Kari Ayam)

▾

Surprise me with

A everyday Malaysian curry built on curry powder and coconut milk, kari ayam is a fixture of both home cooking and mamak (Indian-Muslim) eateries, often eaten with roti canai for dipping.

Method

  1. Mix the curry powder and chili powder with the water into a thick paste. Blend the shallots, garlic and ginger into a second paste.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the cinnamon, star anise and cardamom for 30 seconds, then the onion and curry leaves for 3 minutes until soft.
  3. Add the shallot paste and fry for 3 minutes, then the curry paste and lemongrass, stirring for 5 to 8 minutes, until it darkens and the oil separates. Blooming the spices fully is what gives Malaysian curry its depth.
  4. Add the chicken and stir to coat for 3 to 4 minutes, then the water and potatoes. Bring to a boil and simmer, covered, for 20 minutes.
  5. Stir in the coconut milk, salt and sugar and simmer uncovered for 10 to 15 minutes, until the chicken and potatoes are tender and the gravy is thick and glossy. Keep it at a gentle bubble so the coconut milk does not split. Serve with rice, roti or bread.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the curry powder too briefly, which leaves a raw, dusty taste rather than a toasted, fragrant one.
  • Boiling the coconut milk too vigorously, which can cause it to separate.

Authentic tips

  • Good quality Malaysian curry powder (a specific blend, distinct from Indian curry powder) makes a real difference here.
  • This curry is traditionally eaten with roti canai for dipping — worth trying if you can find frozen roti.

Substitutions

  • Bone-in thighs add flavor; simmer 10 minutes longer.
  • Add tofu puffs for the last 5 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; the curry improves.

Freezing: Freezes for 2 months without the potatoes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice or roti canai
  • Soup: Acar
  • Dessert: Ais kacang
  • Beverage: Teh tarik
