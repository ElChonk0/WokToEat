Steamed Chicken Thighs with Black Bean & Ginger 豉汁蒸鸡

▾

Surprise me with

A staple of Cantonese home cooking, this dish relies on fermented black beans for savory depth and a quick, high-heat steam that keeps the chicken tender — a dish nearly every Cantonese household makes on a weeknight.

  • Cook 15 min

  • 15 g1 tbspGinger, julienned

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the wine, soy sauce, oyster sauce, oil, sugar, salt, pepper and sesame oil. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight (halve the salt if marinating overnight). Take it out 30 minutes to 1 hour before cooking so it steams evenly.
  2. Just before steaming, mix in the black beans, garlic, ginger, cornstarch and water. The cornstarch thickens the juices into a light sauce as the chicken steams. Use fermented black beans, not canned black beans; they give the dish its savory depth.
  3. Spread the chicken in a single layer in a rimmed heatproof dish, such as a pie plate, that fits your steamer and is deep enough to hold the juices.
  4. Set a rack in a flat-bottom wok or steamer with water below it and bring to a full boil over high heat. Set the dish in, cover and steam on high for 10 minutes.
  5. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 2 minutes more, so the chicken finishes gently. It is done when the pieces are opaque all the way through and the juices are clear.
  6. Lift the dish out with a towel, scatter with green onion and chili and serve at once with rice, spooning the black bean juices over.

Common mistakes

  • Steaming over low or medium heat, which stretches the cooking time and dries the chicken out.
  • Not draining excess liquid that pools on the plate, which can leave the dish watery.

Authentic tips

  • Cutting the chicken into small, even pieces ensures fast, even cooking under high steam heat.
  • A thin layer of cornstarch in the marinade gives the sauce that pools on the plate a silky, glossy texture.

Substitutions

  • Chicken breast works but turns rubbery faster — watch the steam time closely if substituting.
  • No fermented black beans: a little extra soy sauce plus a pinch of miso approximates the umami.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat by re-steaming for a few minutes.

Freezing: Not recommended — the delicate steamed texture doesn't hold up well frozen.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice to soak up the juices
  • Soup: Egg drop soup
  • Dessert: Fresh lychee or longan
  • Beverage: Hot oolong tea
