Zhǐ Bāo Jī (Paper-Wrapped Chicken) 纸包鸡

Paper-wrapped chicken is a Cantonese dish in which marinated chicken pieces are sealed in greaseproof paper parcels and deep-fried, so the meat cooks in its own juices.

  • Cook 25 min
  • Difficulty Medium

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the soy sauce, oyster sauce, wine, sugar, ginger, garlic, sesame oil, white pepper and cornstarch, and marinate for 30 minutes.
  2. Cut 16 squares of greaseproof paper or parchment, about 15 cm each.
  3. Put 3 or 4 pieces of chicken in the center of each square with a piece of green onion and a sprig of cilantro.
  4. Fold the paper over the filling into a rectangle, then fold in the sides and roll them over to seal into a tight envelope.
  5. Heat the oil to 160 C (320 F) and fry the parcels in batches for 5 to 6 minutes until the paper turns golden and the chicken is cooked through.
  6. Drain and serve in the paper, letting each person unwrap their own.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is too hot browns the paper before the chicken cooks; keep it near 160 C.
  • Loosely folded parcels leak oil and juices; fold them tightly.

Authentic tips

  • Use greaseproof paper or foil-lined paper, never plain paper, because it can burn in the oil.
  • Serve with a small dish of Worcestershire sauce or plum sauce for dipping.

Substitutions

  • Foil replaces paper if you bake at 200 C (400 F) for 20 minutes.
  • Chicken breast works if you cut it small and shorten the fry to 4 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the paper softens and the chicken cools quickly.

Freezing: The uncooked wrapped parcels freeze for 1 month; fry from frozen for 8 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
