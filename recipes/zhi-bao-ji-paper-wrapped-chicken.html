Zhǐ Bāo Jī (Paper-Wrapped Chicken) 纸包鸡

▾

Surprise me with

Paper-wrapped chicken is a Cantonese dish in which marinated chicken pieces are sealed in greaseproof paper parcels and deep-fried, so the meat cooks in its own juices.

  • Prep 30 min (plus 1 hr marinating)
  • Cook 25 min

Method

  1. Pat the chicken dry. Mix it with the soy sauce, oyster sauce, hoisin, wine, sugar, five-spice, ginger, garlic, shallot, sesame oil and pepper, then stir in the cornstarch so the marinade thickens and clings. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or up to overnight.
  2. Cut 16 squares of greaseproof paper or parchment, about 15 cm (6 in) across. Lay one square on the board as a diamond, with a corner pointing toward you.
  3. Put 3 or 4 pieces of chicken just below the center with a piece of green onion and a sprig of cilantro. Fold the bottom corner up over the filling, then fold the left and right corners in so they overlap.
  4. Fold the parcel up toward the top corner, then tuck that corner into the pocket formed by the side folds to lock it closed, like an envelope. The parcel must be sealed tightly, or oil gets in and the juices leak out. Repeat with the rest.
  5. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or flat-bottom wok to 160 C (320 F). Fry the parcels in batches of 4 or 5 for 5 to 6 minutes, turning once, until the paper is golden and the parcels feel firm. Keep the oil from getting hotter, as the paper scorches before the chicken is cooked. Check one: the chicken should be opaque all the way through.
  6. Drain the parcels upright on a rack for a minute so excess oil runs off, then serve in the paper, letting each person tear open their own to release the steam and juices inside.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by dishes featured on the Get Fed series (Overkill Singapore) and on Alderic's channel.

Common mistakes

  • Oil that is too hot browns the paper before the chicken cooks; keep it near 160 C.
  • Loosely folded parcels leak oil and juices; fold them tightly.

Authentic tips

  • Use greaseproof paper or foil-lined paper, never plain paper, because it can burn in the oil.
  • Serve with a small dish of Worcestershire sauce or plum sauce for dipping.

Substitutions

  • Foil replaces paper if you bake at 200 C (400 F) for 20 minutes.
  • Chicken breast works if you cut it small and shorten the fry to 4 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Eat at once; the paper softens and the chicken cools quickly.

Freezing: The uncooked wrapped parcels freeze for 1 month; fry from frozen for 8 minutes.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and blanched choy sum
  • Soup: Winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot Chinese tea
