Dēng Yǐng Niú Ròu (Sichuan Lantern-Shadow Beef) 灯影牛肉

▾

Surprise me with

Deng ying niu rou, beef sliced so thin that light shines through, then fried crisp with chili and Sichuan pepper, is a specialty of Dazhu, Sichuan.

  • Prep 30 min (plus 4 hr drying)
  • Cook 60 min

Method

  1. Partly freeze the beef and slice it as thinly as you can along the grain; the name means 'lamp-shadow beef', because the slices were thin enough to see lamplight through.
  2. Toss the slices with salt, Shaoxing wine and ginger and leave for 30 minutes. Spread them on racks and dry in an oven at its lowest setting (about 60 C / 140 F) with the door ajar for 3 to 4 hours, until dry and leathery.
  3. Steam the dried beef over boiling water for 10 minutes to soften it, then cool and tear into pieces.
  4. Heat the oil to 170 C (340 F) in a flat-bottom wok or Dutch oven and fry the beef in batches for 1 to 2 minutes, until crisp and translucent. Drain.
  5. Pour off all but 2 tablespoons of oil. Over low heat, fry the ground chili for 10 seconds, add the beef, Sichuan pepper, sugar and soy sauce and toss until the beef is coated and dry.
  6. Off the heat, toss with sesame oil and seeds. Cool completely; it crisps further as it cools and keeps for a week in an airtight jar.

Common mistakes

  • Slices that are too thick turn leathery rather than shattering.
  • Frying at too high a heat burns the chili before the beef is crisp.

Authentic tips

  • A partly frozen beef eye round slices thinnest.
  • Store in an airtight container for up to a week.

Substitutions

  • Pork loin works with the same slicing and drying.
  • Good store-bought beef jerky can be tossed in the spice oil as a shortcut.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 weeks in an airtight container.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months; thaw at room temperature.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Plain congee or rice
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
