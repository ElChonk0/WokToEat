Táng Cù Lǐ Yú (Sweet and Sour Yellow River Carp) 糖醋鲤鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Sweet-and-sour Yellow River carp is one of Shandong's signature dishes: a carp scored, fried and glazed with a tangy sauce, reflecting the carp's symbolism of good fortune and the river's role in northern Chinese cuisine.

Method

  1. Score the carp on both sides in a crosshatch pattern down to the bone, then coat thoroughly in the cornstarch, pressing into the cuts.
  2. Heat the oil to 180 C (350 F) in a wok large enough for the fish and fry for 6-8 minutes, spooning oil over the top, until golden and crisp; lift out and drain.
  3. Pour off the oil, add the ginger and garlic and fry 15 seconds, then the sugar, vinegar, ketchup and water.
  4. Simmer 3 minutes until syrupy.
  5. Arrange the fish on a platter, arched if you like, and pour the hot sauce over; serve immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Scoring too shallowly means the flesh will not open up.
  • A sauce that has been sitting makes the crust soggy.

Authentic tips

  • The traditional presentation arches the fish as if leaping.
  • Sea bass works if carp is unavailable.

Substitutions

  • Sea bass or trout replaces carp.
  • Rice vinegar plus ketchup replaces Chinkiang vinegar.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the crust softens.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Clear vegetable soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
