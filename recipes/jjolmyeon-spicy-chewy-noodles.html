Jjolmyeon (Spicy Chewy Cold Noodles) 쫄면

▾

Surprise me with

Jjolmyeon, very chewy wheat noodles in a sweet-sour gochujang sauce with crisp vegetables, was created by accident in Incheon in the 1970s and became a snack-bar favorite.

Method

  1. Mix the gochujang, gochugaru, vinegar, sugar, soy sauce, garlic and sesame oil into a sauce; a spoonful of pineapple juice or plum syrup softens the heat if you like.
  2. Blanch the bean sprouts, covered, for 3 to 4 minutes, then cool them in cold water and drain. Keep the lid on so they do not smell beany. Shred the cabbage and cut the cucumber and carrot into matchsticks.
  3. Separate the noodles by hand and boil them for about 5 minutes, or as the packet says, stirring so they do not stick.
  4. Drain and rinse them under icy water, rubbing them between your hands, until cold and no longer sticky. The icy rinse is what gives jjolmyeon its famously chewy, springy texture.
  5. Mound the noodles in bowls, top with the vegetables and half an egg, spoon the sauce over and sprinkle with sesame seeds. Mix well before eating.

Cook’s notes

Written independently, inspired by a dish featured on the Aaron and Claire YouTube channel.

Common mistakes

  • Not rinsing leaves the noodles sticky.
  • Undercooking makes them hard rather than chewy.

Authentic tips

  • The sauce improves if made a day ahead.
  • Fish cake or seasoned dried squid are common toppings.

Substitutions

  • Thick udon or naengmyeon noodles replace jjolmyeon.
  • Leave out the egg for a vegan version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; the sauce keeps a week refrigerated.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Gimbap
  • Soup: Eomuk-tang (fish cake soup)
  • Dessert: Bingsu
  • Beverage: Cold barley tea
