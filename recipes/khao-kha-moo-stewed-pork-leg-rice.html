Khao Kha Moo (Stewed Pork Leg Rice) ข้าวขาหมู

▾

Surprise me with

Khao kha moo, pork leg stewed in sweet soy broth and served over rice with a boiled egg, is a Thai-Chinese street-food staple of Bangkok.

Method

  1. Blanch the pork in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and rinse. Singe or scrape off any hairs from the skin.
  2. Heat the oil and palm sugar in a Dutch oven over medium heat until the sugar melts and turns a deep caramel, about 2 minutes. Add the pork and turn it in the caramel for 2 to 3 minutes until glossy and brown.
  3. Add the pounded garlic paste, star anise, cinnamon and five-spice and stir for 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the water, soy sauces and oyster sauce, bring to a boil, then simmer covered over low heat for 2.5 to 3 hours, until the skin is gelatinous and the meat falls off the bone. Add the eggs for the last hour.
  5. Pound the garlic and chilies and mix with vinegar and a pinch of salt and sugar for the dipping sauce. Blanch the Chinese broccoli.
  6. Slice the pork and serve over rice with a halved egg, the braising liquid, Chinese broccoli, pickled mustard greens and the chili-vinegar sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard toughens the meat.
  • Skipping the blanch gives a cloudy, greasy sauce.

Authentic tips

  • Serve with a sharp chili-garlic-vinegar sauce.
  • Hock gives the most gelatinous texture.

Substitutions

  • Pork hock is the easiest cut to find.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the braise to 50 minutes.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 5 days refrigerated; the sauce sets.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Pickled mustard greens and chili-garlic sauce
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Thai iced tea
