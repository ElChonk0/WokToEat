Babi Panggang (Grilled Pork, Balinese-Style)

▾

Surprise me with

A Balinese grilled pork dish marinated in a spice paste of shallot, garlic, chili, and turmeric, babi panggang reflects Bali's distinct Hindu culinary traditions within predominantly Muslim Indonesia.

  • Cook 20 min

Method

  1. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, turmeric, galangal, ginger and coriander into a paste and fry it in the coconut oil for 6 minutes until fragrant. Cool.
  2. Mix the paste with the tamarind, palm sugar, kecap manis and salt and massage it into the pork. Marinate for at least 2 hours, or overnight.
  3. Thread the pork onto skewers or lay it on a rack.
  4. Grill over medium charcoal or under a broiler for 12 to 15 minutes, turning and basting with the leftover marinade, until charred at the edges and cooked through (63 C / 145 F). Medium heat lets the fat render without the sugar burning.
  5. Rest for 5 minutes and serve with rice and sambal matah.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the paste-frying step before marinating, which leaves the flavor tasting raw and underdeveloped.
  • Grilling over too high heat, which chars the exterior before the pork cooks through.

Authentic tips

  • Frying the spice paste before using it as a marinade develops much deeper flavor than using it raw.
  • This dish pairs traditionally with a spicy sambal matah (raw shallot-chili relish) on the side.

Substitutions

  • No galangal: extra ginger works as a substitute, though the flavor is slightly less complex.
  • Chicken thigh works well with the same paste and method.
  • No grill: roast on a rack over a foil-lined sheet pan at 200°C (400°F) for 25 to 30 minutes, turning once, then broil 2 to 3 minutes for char.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 3 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes well, marinated and raw, for up to a month.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and lawar
  • Soup: Clear soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Es teh
