Niú Ròu Xiàn Bǐng (Taiwanese Pan-Fried Beef Pie) 牛肉餡餅

▾

Surprise me with

Pan-fried meat pies were brought to Taiwan by mainland soldiers after 1949, and beef pies are a street and breakfast food in Taipei.

  • 300 g2.5 cupsAll-purpose flour
  • 120 ml1/2 cupHot water (about 80 C / 175 F)
  • 60 ml1/4 cupCold water
  • 400 g14 ozBeef, ground (not too lean)
  • 10 g2 tspGinger, grated
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspRice wine
  • 3 g1/2 tspSalt
  • 3 g1/2 tspSugar
  • 1 g1/2 tspWhite pepper
  • 10 ml2 tspSesame oil
  • 60 ml1/4 cupScallion-ginger water (or plain water)
  • 100 g3.5 ozGreen onion, finely chopped
  • 45 ml3 tbspNeutral oil, for pan-frying

Method

  1. Stir the hot water into the flour with chopsticks, then the cold water, and knead into a soft, smooth dough. Hot water makes it tender and easy to stretch thin. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the beef with the ginger, soy sauce, rice wine, salt, sugar and pepper. Add the scallion-ginger water a little at a time, stirring in one direction, until the filling absorbs it and turns sticky. This makes the pies juicy. Stir in the sesame oil, then the green onion just before filling.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 pieces and roll each into a thin round. Put a generous portion of filling in the center, pleat the edge up and around to enclose it, and pinch closed.
  4. Turn each pie pleat-side down and press gently to flatten into a disc about 2 cm (3/4 in) thick.
  5. Heat the oil in a lidded skillet over medium-low heat and fry the pies for 4 to 5 minutes per side, covered for the first side, until deep golden and the filling is cooked through.
  6. Rest for 2 minutes before biting; the juices inside are very hot.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling tears the dough.
  • Cooking too fast burns the outside and leaves the inside raw.

Authentic tips

  • Cover the pan briefly to cook the filling through.
  • Serve with soy milk.

Substitutions

  • Ground pork replaces beef.
  • Chopped cabbage in the filling lightens the pie.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in a hot dry pan.

Freezing: Freeze cooked pies for 2 months.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Soy milk
  • Soup: Pickled vegetables
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot soy milk
