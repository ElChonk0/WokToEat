Clams in Chicken Broth 鸡汤汆海蚌

▾

Surprise me with

One of Fuzhou’s most refined banquet dishes: paper-thin slices of raw clam are placed in a bowl and cooked at the table by pouring clear, boiling chicken broth over them.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 1.5 hr
  • Difficulty Medium

  • 600 g1.3 lbLarge clams (geoduck, surf clam or Manila clams)
  • 800 g1.75 lbChicken carcass or wings
  • 150 g5 ozLean pork, minced (to clarify)
  • 20 g0.75 ozGinger, sliced
  • 30 ml2 tbspRice wine
  • to tasteto tasteSalt
  • 2 L8 cupsWater

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken and ginger in the water, barely bubbling, for 1 hour; strain.
  2. Stir the minced pork into the cooled broth, bring slowly to a simmer and let the pork rise and clarify the broth; strain through cloth.
  3. Shuck the clams, clean them, and slice large clams paper-thin (leave small clams whole).
  4. Blanch the clam slices in warm rice wine and water for 5 seconds; drain and divide among bowls.
  5. Season the broth with salt, bring to a hard boil and pour it over the clams at the table.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking the clams in the broth; they need only the heat of the poured broth or they turn rubbery.

Authentic tips

  • The pork "raft" trick gives the very clear broth this dish is judged on.

Substitutions

  • Manila clams left whole, briefly steamed open, work for an everyday version.

Storage

Leftovers: Best fresh; keep extra broth for up to 3 days.

Freezing: The broth freezes well; the clams don’t.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice
  • Soup: Not needed; this is the soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
