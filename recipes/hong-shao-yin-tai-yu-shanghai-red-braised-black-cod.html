Hong Shao Yin Tai Yu (Red-Braised Black Cod) 红烧银鳕鱼

▾

Surprise me with

Red braising in soy, sugar and wine is the signature technique of Shanghai home cooking, and a rich fish like black cod suits its short braise.

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 20 min
  • Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

  • 600 g1 lb 5 ozBlack cod, in 5 cm pieces
  • 30 ml2 tbspNeutral oil
  • 20 g3/4 ozGinger, sliced
  • 40 g1.5 ozScallions, cut in lengths
  • 45 ml3 tbspShaoxing wine
  • 30 ml2 tbspLight soy sauce
  • 15 ml1 tbspDark soy sauce
  • 25 g2 tbspRock sugar
  • 11Star anise
  • 200 ml3/4 cupWater

Method

  1. Pat the black cod dry. Heat the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-high heat and sear it for 2 minutes per side until golden. Lift it out.
  2. Fry the ginger and scallions in the same pan for 1 minute until fragrant, then add the rock sugar and let it melt for 30 seconds.
  3. Pour in the Shaoxing wine, soy sauces, star anise and water and bring to a simmer.
  4. Return the fish and simmer gently, covered, for 6 to 8 minutes, basting, until it flakes. Black cod is very rich and soft, so handle it gently.
  5. Uncover and reduce the sauce for 2 to 3 minutes until syrupy, spoon it over the fish and serve.

Cook’s notes

Written for black cod. Shanghainese cooks red-braise carp, hairtail or yellow croaker.

Common mistakes

  • Long braising breaks up the delicate flesh; keep the simmer short.
  • Moving the fish before it releases from the pan tears the skin.

Authentic tips

  • Tilt the pan to baste with the sauce instead of turning the fish.
  • Rock sugar gives a clearer gloss than white sugar.

Substitutions

  • Cod loin or Chilean sea bass replaces the black cod.
  • White sugar replaces rock sugar at 20 g.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 1 day in the refrigerator.

Freezing: Not recommended.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Steamed rice and stir-fried greens
  • Soup: Pork and winter melon soup
  • Dessert: Fresh fruit
  • Beverage: Hot tea
