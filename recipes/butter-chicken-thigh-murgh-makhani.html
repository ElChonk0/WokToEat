Butter Chicken Thigh (Murgh Makhani)

▾

Surprise me with

Invented in 1950s Delhi at the Moti Mahal restaurant as a way to use leftover tandoori chicken in a rich tomato-butter sauce, butter chicken has since become one of the most popular Indian dishes worldwide.

Method

  1. Marinate chicken in yogurt, half the garam masala, and half the garlic-ginger paste for at least 1 hour.
  2. Sear the marinated chicken in a hot pan until lightly charred and mostly cooked; set aside.
  3. In the same pan, melt butter and sauté onion with remaining garlic-ginger paste until softened.
  4. Add tomato puree and chili powder; simmer 10 minutes until thickened and deep red.
  5. Blend the sauce until smooth (optional but traditional for restaurant texture), then return to the pan.
  6. Add cream, remaining garam masala, sugar, and the seared chicken; simmer 8–10 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and sauce is glossy.
  7. Garnish with cilantro and serve.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the marination, which is what gives the chicken its characteristic tenderness and background flavor.
  • Adding the cream too early and letting it boil hard, which can cause it to split.

Authentic tips

  • Blending the tomato sauce until silky-smooth is what gives restaurant-style butter chicken its signature texture.
  • Kashmiri chili powder gives vibrant color without excessive heat — worth seeking out over regular chili powder.

Substitutions

  • Coconut cream replaces heavy cream for a dairy-light version.
  • Tandoori-style chicken can be roasted in an oven at 230 C (450 F).

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated; thin with a splash of water when reheating.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months; add the cream fresh after thawing.

Serve with

  • Side dish: Garlic naan and basmati rice
  • Soup: Cucumber raita
  • Dessert: Gulab jamun
  • Beverage: Mango lassi
