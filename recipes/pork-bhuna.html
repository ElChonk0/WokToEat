Pork Bhuna

▾

Surprise me with

Bhuna describes a slow, dry-roasting technique that concentrates spices into meat before adding liquid — this pork version produces a thick, deeply spiced curry with minimal sauce, distinct from wetter curries.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and fry the cumin, bay leaves, cardamom, cloves and cinnamon for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  2. Add the onions and cook for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden brown. Well-browned onions are the base of a bhuna; rushing them gives a pale, sweet sauce.
  3. Add the ginger-garlic paste and chilies for 1 minute, then the ground spices with a splash of water. Fry, stirring, for 2 minutes until the oil separates. Add the tomatoes and salt and cook for 6 to 8 minutes until they break down and oil pools at the edges again.
  4. Add the pork and 'bhuno' it: fry over medium-high heat, stirring and scraping constantly, for 10 minutes, until the meat is browned and coated in a thick, dark masala. Bhuna means this frying of spices and meat together.
  5. Lower the heat, add 250 ml (1 cup) of hot water, cover and cook for 45 to 60 minutes, stirring now and then and adding splashes of water only if it sticks, until the meat is tender.
  6. Uncover and cook over medium heat for 5 to 10 minutes, stirring, until the sauce is thick and clings to the meat. Stir in the garam masala and garnish with cilantro and ginger. Serve with roti or paratha.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the onion-browning step, which is the foundation of bhuna's deep flavor — it genuinely needs 10+ minutes.
  • Adding liquid to speed things up, which defeats the point of the dry, concentrated bhuna technique.

Authentic tips

  • The oil separating from the tomato-spice mixture is the visual cue that the base is properly cooked — wait for it before adding the pork.
  • This dish should end up thick and mostly dry, clinging to the pork rather than saucy.

Substitutions

  • Beef or lamb take the same method.
  • Reduce the chili powder for a milder dish.

Storage

Leftovers: Keeps 4 days refrigerated.

Freezing: Freezes for 3 months.
