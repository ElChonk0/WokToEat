Appendix A — Nutrition Estimate Methodology

Every recipe in this collection lists an estimated nutrition figure per serving — calories, protein, fat, carbohydrate, and sodium. These are practical estimates, not laboratory measurements, and a few things are worth knowing about how to read them.

Estimates are built from standard ingredient values for the specific cuts, brands, and forms named in each recipe (for example, skinless chicken thigh rather than a generic “chicken” figure). Where a recipe calls for “to taste” garnishes, those are excluded from the count, since amounts vary too much to estimate meaningfully.

Sodium is the figure most sensitive to brand choice. Soy sauce, fish sauce, and oyster sauce vary by as much as 30–40% in sodium content between brands, and the collection's estimates assume a mid-range, widely available product. If you've switched to the low-sodium swaps suggested in Part 1, your actual sodium intake will run noticeably lower than the listed figure — that's intentional, not an error in the recipe.

These figures are meant to help with general meal planning and to support the collection's heart-healthy goals — hitting fish twice a week, keeping sodium reasonable, favoring lean proteins — not to replace individualized dietary advice. If you're managing a specific health condition, working with a registered dietitian for precise targets will serve you better than any cookbook's estimates, including this one.

Carbohydrate figures for most recipes were derived from each recipe’s own calorie, protein and fat estimates (calories ≈ 4 × protein + 4 × carbs + 9 × fat), so the four numbers on every recipe agree with each other. Treat all of them as estimates within roughly 15–20%. Figures exclude rice or noodles served on the side unless the recipe itself contains them; the meal planner in the web version adds a measured rice portion where needed to reach a carb target.

Nutrition figures are per-serving estimates, not lab values. Side rice and noodles are excluded unless the dish contains them. See the guides for how they were calculated.
