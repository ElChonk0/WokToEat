How to Use This Site

This collection gathers 1185 weeknight dishes from 20 regional cuisines across China, East Asia, Southeast Asia and South Asia, organized by main ingredient (chicken, duck, pork, beef, seafood, and tofu & vegetarian) and by cuisine, so you can start from whatever is in the fridge.

Find a recipe. Use Browse to search by dish or ingredient and filter by cuisine, meal, difficulty and time, or go straight to a cuisine from the cuisine index. Every recipe lists calories, protein, carbs, fat and sodium per serving. The ingredient list shows metric and US amounts side by side, can be switched between them, and can be scaled to the number of servings you need.

Recipe status. Each recipe carries a label: Draft means it has been written but not yet checked by a person or cooked, Reviewed means someone has read and corrected it, and Tested means it has been cooked at home (often with cook's notes). Most recipes start as drafts, so treat their amounts, times and nutrition figures as a starting point. You can filter by status on the browse page.

Plan the week. The meal planner builds up to a week of breakfasts, lunches and dinners around the proteins and cuisines you choose and your daily macro targets. Lock the meals you like and rebuild the rest.

Read the guides. The pantry guide (Part 1) and the technique guide (Part 2) are reference material: read them once and every recipe will point back to them rather than re-explaining technique. The appendices cover nutrition methodology, a freezer quick-reference, and a glossary.

There's no need to read any of it first. Pick a dish, cook it, and see what your family likes.

Nutrition figures are per-serving estimates, not lab values. Side rice and noodles are excluded unless the dish contains them. See the guides for how they were calculated.

Recipes live as plain Markdown files, one per dish, and this site is generated from them.
