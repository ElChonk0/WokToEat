Appendix C — Glossary

A quick-reference glossary of ingredients and terms used throughout the collection. Fuller explanations for the starred (*) entries appear in Part 1.

Term Definition Term Definition
Black vinegar* Dark, malty Chinese vinegar (Chinkiang/Zhenjiang); used in dumpling sauces and some braises. Lemongrass A fibrous, citrusy stalk used in Vietnamese, Thai, and Malaysian cooking; mince very finely or bruise and remove before serving.
Calamansi A small, tart Filipino citrus fruit, similar to a cross between lime and mandarin. Mirin* Sweet Japanese rice wine used in glazes and simmered dishes.
Dashi* Japanese stock, typically from kombu and bonito flakes. Miso* Fermented soybean paste; white (shiro) is milder, red (aka) is bolder.
Doubanjiang* Sichuan fermented broad bean and chili paste; the base of mapo tofu. Nuoc cham Vietnamese dipping sauce of fish sauce, lime, sugar, water, garlic, and chili.
Five-spice powder A Chinese spice blend of star anise, cloves, cinnamon, Sichuan pepper, and fennel. Oyster sauce* Thick, savory-sweet Cantonese sauce made from oyster extract.
Galangal A rhizome related to ginger, with a more citrusy, piney flavor; common in Thai and Malaysian cooking. Palm sugar A unrefined sugar common in Southeast Asian cooking, with a deeper, slightly caramel flavor than white sugar.
Gochugaru* Korean coarse red chili flakes, fruitier and milder than generic chili flakes. Panko Japanese-style breadcrumbs, coarser and crispier than Western breadcrumbs.
Gochujang* Korean fermented chili paste; sweet, salty, and spicy. Shaoxing wine* Amber Chinese rice wine used in marinades and stir-fries.
Hoisin sauce A thick, sweet-savory Chinese sauce often used in glazes like char siu. Sichuan peppercorn* A citrusy, numbing spice (not a true pepper); central to málà flavor.
Kaffir lime leaves Aromatic citrus leaves used whole or sliced in Thai and Malaysian curries and soups. Tamarind A sour fruit pulp used in Southeast Asian soups and curries, such as sinigang and rendang.
Kecap manis Indonesian sweet soy sauce, thicker and sweeter than regular soy sauce. Turmeric A bright yellow rhizome used fresh or dried in Indonesian and Malaysian cooking, such as soto ayam.
Kombu Dried kelp, the base of Japanese dashi. Wok hei* The smoky, charred flavor from very high heat stir-frying, literally “breath of the wok.”
