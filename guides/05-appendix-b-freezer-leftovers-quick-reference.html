Appendix B — Freezer & Leftovers Quick Reference

Each recipe includes its own leftover and freezing notes, but a few patterns repeat across the whole collection. This page is a fast lookup for “can I freeze this?” without opening the specific recipe.

Category Freezes Well? Notes
Broths & stocks (pho, dashi, chicken stock) Yes — excellent Freeze flat in zip-top bags or in ice cube trays for small portions; keeps 3 months
Braises & stews (rendang, lu rou fan, char siu) Yes — excellent Often tastes better after a day or two anyway; freezes 2–3 months
Marinated raw meat (bulgogi, mackerel, lemongrass chicken) Yes Freeze in the marinade; thaw overnight in the fridge before cooking
Cooked rice Yes Cool completely before freezing; reheat with a splash of water to restore moisture
Uncooked dumplings & wontons Yes Freeze in a single layer until solid, then bag together to prevent sticking
Cooked fish & seafood No Texture turns mushy or rubbery; cook fish fresh and eat the same day where possible
Cooked rice noodles & fresh rice paper No Noodles turn gummy or separate; rice paper toughens
Tofu (in finished dishes) Not recommended Becomes spongier after freezing and thawing; freeze plain tofu only if planning to use it in a dish that doesn't depend on a silky texture
Fried or breaded items (katsu, spring rolls) Yes, before frying Freeze breaded but uncooked; fry from frozen, adding a few extra minutes
Fresh herbs & garnishes (basil, cilantro, scallion) No Add fresh after reheating any frozen component

Nutrition figures are per-serving estimates, not lab values. Side rice and noodles are excluded unless the dish contains them. See the guides for how they were calculated.
